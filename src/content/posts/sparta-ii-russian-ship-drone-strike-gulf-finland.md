A Russian cargo ship, the Sparta II, was still burning at anchor in Finnish waters on Sunday, November 22, a day after two drones struck it in the Gulf of Finland. The 121-metre ship had left St Petersburg for Russia's Baltic exclave of Kaliningrad. All 16 crew members were taken off safely. Finland's military said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine, which has not commented, according to Al Jazeera.

The ship belongs to Oboronlogistika, a company that provides logistics services to Russia's Ministry of Defence, and it is under US Treasury sanctions. Finland's Border Guard said it could not confirm what was on board, but did not rule out military equipment.

## What Happened on Saturday Morning

Estonia's Police and Border Guard Board (PPA) said its rescue centre first got word of a fire and possible explosions at 9:35 am local time (1:05 pm IST) on November 21. According to the PPA, quoted by Estonian broadcaster <a href="https://news.err.ee/1610202715/russian-cargo-ship-on-fire-in-gulf-of-finland-after-reported-drone-strike" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ERR</a>, the blasts happened in Estonia's exclusive economic zone, 11 nautical miles north of Vaindloo island, in an international shipping lane. The Finnish Defence Forces described the spot as international waters west of Russia's Gogland island.

Leningrad region governor Alexander Drozdenko said a Kaliningrad-bound cargo vessel was attacked by two drones three nautical miles from Russia's border. Russian media also reported that more than 60 drones were shot down over the region overnight, a figure later raised to 80.

After the strike, the bridge caught fire and the ship drifted out of Estonian waters around 10 am, ending up in Finnish territorial waters in the eastern part of the gulf. It is now anchored off the town of Loviisa. The crew moved to a nearby Russian ship and, as reported, will be taken back to Russia.

Orpo also wrote that Ukraine "has the right to fight back against the aggressor". Finland's Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen said a suspected Russian helicopter airspace violation may have been linked to rescue or tracking of the damaged ship.

## Why a Ship to Kaliningrad Matters

Kaliningrad sits between Poland and Lithuania, cut off from the rest of Russia by land. Ships sailing from St Petersburg must run the length of the Gulf of Finland, a narrow arm of the Baltic Sea with Finland to the north and Estonia to the south, both NATO members. A damaged Russian ship there can quickly end up in Finnish or Estonian waters, as the Sparta II did, and the rescue falls to them.

Risk analyst Robin Haggblom of Risk Intelligence told Al Jazeera there were unconfirmed signs the Sparta II was targeted on purpose. He warned that a leak or sinking could cause serious environmental damage, a risk raised by the poor condition of some "shadow fleet" tankers in the area. He also noted there is no ice on the gulf yet, which would make any clean-up harder later in winter. So far, the PPA says no marine pollution has been reported.

## A Busy Fortnight on NATO's Eastern Edge

The Sparta II strike is the latest in a run of incidents near the Baltic states in November 2026:

- **Earlier this month:** Finland's military detected a strike on a Russian warship in the same waters during Ukrainian drone attacks. Ukraine took credit, and Helsinki raised concerns with Kyiv.
- **November 20:** Russia complained of a "new Iron Curtain" as <a href="/estonia-closes-russia-border-crossings-december-1/">Estonia moved to shut its border crossings</a>.
- **November 21:** Russian missiles came down in Poland, raising questions about <a href="/russian-missiles-poland-nato-article-4-explained/">NATO's Article 4 and Article 5</a>.
- **November 21 to 22:** Sparta II hit, evacuated and still burning off Loviisa.

Ukraine has sent repeated waves of drones towards Russia's Leningrad region this year. Some have strayed into the airspace of nearby Baltic countries, which Finland's General Staff cited when it explained Saturday's traffic restrictions.

## What to Watch Next

Hakkanen said on Saturday the ship would be towed out of Finnish territorial waters that night, but on Sunday it was still anchored off Loviisa and burning. Three questions remain open: what the ship was carrying, whether its hull holds, and whether Ukraine confirms the attack. Estonia's navy chief has already said more attacks in the gulf cannot be ruled out.
