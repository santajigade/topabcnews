China is counting on artificial intelligence to create jobs rather than destroy them. At the opening of its annual parliament session in March 2026, Beijing presented a new five-year plan that pushes AI beyond factories into services, and Human Resources Minister Wang Xiaoping said the government would use AI to widen job options for the 12.7 million university graduates entering the market this year, Reuters reported. The backdrop is tough: a GDP growth target of 4.5% to 5%, the lowest since the 1990s, persistently high youth unemployment and about 300 million people due to retire over the next decade.

## Update (22 November 2026)

This story has moved on since March. In June 2026 the State Council, China's cabinet, issued a 2026 to 2030 implementation plan for its "employment-first" strategy, signed on 11 June and published on 17 June. It is the most detailed statement so far of how Beijing wants to handle AI and jobs together. According to Caixin, it:

- deepens the "AI Plus" initiative and encourages start-ups that build new AI applications;
- aims to create new jobs and occupations in emerging sectors, while using AI in traditional industries to improve safety and working conditions;
- sets up a support programme to help workers retrain and change careers;
- plans a monitoring system to track how AI creates and displaces jobs, how widely it is adopted and how skill demands are changing.

The plan dropped the hard target for new urban jobs that the previous five-year plan carried. It keeps a goal of surveyed urban unemployment below 5.5% a year and aims for skilled workers to make up 35% of the labour force by 2030. Major policies and projects will also need an assessment of their effect on employment before approval.

## Three Policy Papers Behind the "AI Jobs Plan"

There is no single document called the China AI Jobs Plan. The phrase covers several policies that build on each other, and it helps to see them side by side.

| Document | Issued | What it says about AI and work |
|---|---|---|
| State Council guideline on the AI Plus initiative | 26 August 2025 | Six focus areas, from science and industry to public services and governance. Targets AI agent and smart device penetration above 70% by 2027 and above 90% by 2030 |
| New five-year plan, presented to parliament | March 2026 | Spreads AI from manufacturing into services; leans on AI's job-creation effect to offset an ageing workforce |
| Employment-first implementation plan, 2026 to 2030 | Signed 11 June, published 17 June 2026 | New AI-related jobs, retraining support, an AI jobs monitoring system and an early-warning mechanism for jobs at risk |

The August 2025 guideline, published on the <a href="https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202508/27/content_WS68ae7976c6d0868f4e8f51a0.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chinese government's English portal</a>, is mostly about adoption and says little about jobs directly. According to Xinhua, it also asks for AI to be used to create new jobs and upgrade existing ones, and for innovation to be steered towards sectors that employ many people.

## The Job Numbers China Has Put Out

Officials have not published a figure for how many jobs AI will create overall. The numbers that do exist are narrower. In a January 2026 Xinhua report:

- the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security had identified 72 new occupations over five years, more than 20 of them linked to AI;
- each new occupation is expected to generate 300,000 to 500,000 jobs in its early stage;
- a skills campaign running to 2027 will give subsidised training to more than 30 million people;
- China's core AI industry is approaching 600 billion yuan, about $85.6 billion.

Schools have also been told to strengthen AI teaching from primary level upwards. At university level, ShanghaiTech University has started AI "micro-majors" to teach skills that AI cannot easily replace, its provost Yin Jie told Reuters.

## Where Companies Expect AI to Help

Some business leaders are upbeat. Changan Automobile chairman Zhu Huarong said he hoped AI could turn the car industry from a fading business into a "sunrise industry". Entrepreneurs are using AI agents to run e-commerce shopfronts as "one-person companies", and state media described ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 video model as a turning point for AI in film and television, according to Reuters.

## The Doubters, Including in China

Not everyone accepts the optimistic view. Labour economist Cai Fang wrote in a recent book that "job destruction often precedes and outweighs job creation," and called for more spending on human capital and social welfare. Alicia Garcia-Herrero of Natixis told Reuters that automation is pushing wages down and youth unemployment up. Shujing He of Plenum said Beijing treats AI progress as a bigger priority than heading off job losses, and that this leaves room for policy fixes later if needed.

There is a legal guardrail too. In 2025, a Beijing court ruled in a labour arbitration case that firing workers solely to replace them with AI is illegal.

The wider risk is not unique to China. An <a href="https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity" target="_blank" rel="noopener">IMF analysis</a> found that almost 40% of jobs worldwide are exposed to AI, rising to about 60% in advanced economies, 40% in emerging markets and 26% in low-income countries. The IMF stresses that exposure does not always mean job loss, since AI can complement some tasks as well as replace others.

## Tracking AI's Effect on 700 Million Workers

The June plan's monitoring system is the piece that could show whether the "create, not cut" bet is working. Bloomberg reported that authorities may use industrial electricity use, social insurance records and mobile payment data, though The Next Web noted this is reported intent, not published policy. China's workforce is over 700 million, and flexible employment is projected at about 320 million people this year, nearly half of it. The plan also covers platform workers, asking platform companies to make their algorithms more transparent and pay wages on time.
