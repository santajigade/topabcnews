India sold more goods to the world in October 2026 than in any month before. Data released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry on Friday, November 13, shows merchandise exports of $49.47 billion, up sharply from $34.11 billion in October 2025. But imports also surged to $89.35 billion, so the gap between what India buys and what it sells widened to $39.88 billion, the highest in a year according to a Reuters calculation.

The numbers point to two trends at once: strong demand for Indian goods abroad, led by petroleum products and electronics, and a heavier import bill driven by costly oil as conflict in the Middle East keeps energy prices high.

## October 2026 Trade Data at a Glance

Releasing the figures, Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said India "recorded its highest-ever monthly goods exports" in October. The table below sums up the official numbers from the <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2335914&reg=48&lang=2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Commerce Ministry's release on PIB</a>.

| Item | October 2026 | October 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Merchandise exports | $49.47 billion | $34.11 billion |
| Merchandise imports | $89.35 billion | $76.69 billion |
| Services exports (estimate) | $40.51 billion | $35.17 billion |
| Services imports (estimate) | $20.72 billion | $17.73 billion |
| Total exports (goods and services) | $89.99 billion | $69.27 billion |
| Total imports (goods and services) | $110.07 billion | $94.42 billion |
| Overall trade deficit | $20.09 billion | $25.15 billion |

Total exports, combining goods and services, grew 29.90% from a year earlier. Because total exports grew faster than total imports, the overall trade deficit actually narrowed in October compared with the same month last year.

## Why the Goods Trade Gap Widened From September

Month on month, the picture is different. In September, the merchandise trade deficit was $30.21 billion. In October it jumped by almost $10 billion, as imports climbed from $74.61 billion to $89.35 billion while exports rose from $44.40 billion to $49.47 billion. Economists polled by Reuters had expected a deficit of about $32.25 billion, so the actual figure was a clear surprise.

Oil is the main reason. India imports nearly 85% of the crude oil it uses, and disruptions in the Middle East have pushed up import costs. Based on the ministry's data, petroleum imports rose to roughly $22.8 billion in October, from about $14.8 billion a year earlier. A wider trade gap tends to put pressure on the rupee, a theme that also weighed on markets in our report on how <a href="/sensex-nifty-close-november-13-inflation-crude/">the Sensex fell on inflation and oil worries</a>.

## Which Exports Grew the Most

Growth was spread across many sectors. The biggest gains in October came from:

- **Petroleum products:** up 140.21%
- **Other cereals:** up 87.47%
- **Oil meals:** up 85.55%
- **Electronic goods:** up 62.70% to $6.64 billion
- **Handicrafts (excluding carpets):** up 55.22%
- **Gems and jewellery:** up 40.06% to $3.21 billion
- **Organic and inorganic chemicals:** up 36.20% to $2.89 billion
- **Engineering goods:** up 36.01% to $12.74 billion

Rice, tea, marine products and pharmaceuticals also posted gains. Non-petroleum exports, often seen as a better measure of underlying demand, rose to $40.58 billion from $30.40 billion.

### Top export markets

The United States remained a key buyer, with exports to it up 39.25% in October. Shipments to Spain, Singapore, Malaysia and China also rose strongly. On the import side, purchases from China, Russia, the US, Malaysia and Indonesia all increased. Reuters noted that India has put trade deals with the UK and New Zealand into effect this year, and an agreement with the European Union is expected to begin early next year.

## The Bigger Picture for April to October

For the first seven months of the financial year 2026-27, India's total exports of goods and services are estimated at $568.84 billion, up 16.66% from $487.59 billion a year earlier. Merchandise exports alone reached $309.06 billion, a rise of about 22%.

Imports grew at a similar pace, so the cumulative trade deficit for goods and services widened to $93.35 billion from $83.25 billion. Services continued to act as a cushion, with a surplus of about $124.80 billion over the period. The ministry noted that October services figures are estimates, and final data will come from the <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Reserve Bank of India</a>.

## What the Numbers Mean for the Rupee and Households

For ordinary Indians, the trade data matters in a few ways. A larger import bill means more demand for dollars, which can weaken the rupee and make imported items, from fuel to electronics, more expensive. On the other hand, record exports support jobs in manufacturing hubs and in sectors like engineering, electronics and textiles.

India's foreign exchange reserves provide a buffer against such pressures. They stood at $719.55 billion, as covered in our story on <a href="/india-forex-reserves-rise-719-billion-november-6/">the latest forex reserves data</a>. Brent crude had eased to around $92 a barrel at the time of the Reuters report, and the direction of oil prices in the coming weeks will play a big role in whether November's trade gap shrinks or grows.
