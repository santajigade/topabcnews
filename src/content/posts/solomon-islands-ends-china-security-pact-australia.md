Solomon Islands has told China it will not renew the security agreement the two countries signed in 2022. The five-year pact ends in **April 2027** and will be allowed to lapse. Australia's ABC confirmed the decision through its own sources on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, after Nine Newspapers broke the story. Prime Minister Matthew Wale arrived in Canberra the same afternoon and is due to sign a new treaty with Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese on **Wednesday, November 25**. Reuters reports the treaty would make Australia the country's main security partner.

China's foreign ministry had not commented by the time Reuters and the ABC published. Neither had Australia's foreign ministry or the Solomon Islands government.

## What the 2022 Pact Allowed, and What Replaces It

Then prime minister Manasseh Sogavare signed the China agreement in 2022, and its text has never been made public. This is how it compares with what is known so far about the Australian deal.

| Point | China pact (2022) | Australia treaty (to be signed Nov 25, 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Length | Five years, ending April 2027 | Not yet published |
| Policing | Lets China rotate police training teams; about two dozen Chinese police are in the country at any time (ABC) | Expected to make Australia the main security partner (Reuters) |
| Money | No figure public | $980 million in extra funding, according to the ABC |
| Known spending lines | None public | Leaked texts seen by the ABC list $142 million for Wale's free education policy and $47.8 million for visa improvements and Solomon Islands security requests |
| Public text | Covered by a non-disclosure clause, Wale said in June | Details not yet officially released (ABC) |

## From the Taiwan Switch to Non-Renewal: A Timeline

The ABC, Reuters and Pacific Media Network reports give this sequence:

- **2019:** Solomon Islands switches diplomatic recognition from Taiwan to China. Sogavare's government then builds closer ties with Beijing.
- **2022:** Sogavare signs the security agreement with China. Officials in Canberra, Wellington and Washington warn it could lead to a Chinese military foothold. Honiara and Beijing both deny any plan for a base.
- **May 2026:** Parliament elects Wale prime minister after a no-confidence vote removes Jeremiah Manele. Reuters says closer ties with China were among voters' concerns in the election.
- **June 2026:** On a visit to Canberra, Wale says his cabinet will review the pact. Back in Honiara, he says a legally binding non-disclosure clause stops him from releasing its text.
- **July 2026:** Australia and Solomon Islands pledge closer ties. They jointly criticise China's test of a ballistic missile fired from a nuclear-powered submarine in the Pacific.
- **November 23, 2026:** Sogavare, now opposition leader, says he believes Wale is about to drop the pact.
- **November 24, 2026:** Reports confirm Honiara has told Beijing it will not renew. Wale flies to Canberra with a 24-member delegation on a charter flight paid for by the Australian government.
- **April 2027:** The China agreement reaches the end of its term.

## How Canberra and Honiara's Critics See It

Australia has long worried that China could use policing to gain a security foothold in the Pacific. Solomon Islands lies about 1,600 km northeast of the Australian mainland. In the Senate on Tuesday, Foreign Minister Penny Wong repeated her view that the 2022 deal was Australia's "worst foreign policy blunder in the Pacific since the end of World War II". That pact was signed while Scott Morrison was prime minister.

Oliver Nobetau, who directs the Pacific Islands programme at the Lowy Institute, told the ABC the move was a huge win for Australia. He also cautioned that Wale has already survived one no-confidence motion and is likely to face trouble at home. Anna Powles of New Zealand's Massey University called the decision significant.

Sogavare, who negotiated the China pact, warned on Monday that the country should weigh what it would lose by ending it. His side has also accused Australia of chequebook diplomacy. Earlier this month the <a href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-24/china-solomon-islands-security-pact-australia/107421976" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ABC reported</a> that budget money meant for cyclone recovery and fuel shocks had been moved into MPs' constituency development funds.

## Questions Still Open Before April

1. **The treaty text.** Until it is released, nobody outside government knows whether it bars other foreign security forces or only adds Australian support.
2. **The Chinese police teams.** The pact allows them to rotate in. Neither government has said whether the teams will leave before April or at the end of the term.
3. **Beijing's reaction.** China has not commented. In June, Wale <a href="https://pmn.co.nz/read/pacific-region/solomons-pm-says-china-security-pact-must-stay-secret-despite-his-earlier-pledge-for-a-review" target="_blank" rel="noopener">told reporters in Honiara</a> that the country would not be a source of instability for the region. He also said future treaties should go before parliament.
4. **Wale's hold on power.** The pact does not lapse for another five months, and analysts have pointed to his narrow position in Honiara.

The signing in Canberra on November 25 is the first chance for the Australian treaty's terms to be made public. The questions above, on foreign security forces, the Chinese police teams and parliamentary oversight, are the ones the text will need to answer.
