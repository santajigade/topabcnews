India is set to become a full partner in the European Union's biggest research funding programme. On November 6, 2026, the European Commission and India announced that they had concluded negotiations on India's association with Horizon Europe, the EU's research and innovation programme worth 93.5 billion euros. Once the agreement is signed and ratified, Indian universities, laboratories and companies will be able to win EU grants directly and lead international research projects. The Commission expects the association to start in January 2027.

## What Horizon Europe Association Means for Indian Researchers

Horizon Europe runs from 2021 to 2027 and funds research across the EU and partner countries. Indian scientists have been able to join some projects in the past, but usually as partners without direct funding from the programme. Association changes that.

According to <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/artificial-intelligence/eu-and-india-conclude-negotiations-on-eur-93-5-billion-horizon-europe-programme" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tribune's report on the agreement</a>, Indian researchers, businesses and research organisations will be able to receive grants directly from the Commission and take part on the same terms as institutions from EU member states in the areas covered. In return, associated countries make a financial contribution to the programme budget. The size of India's contribution has not been made public.

Reports describe association as the closest form of research cooperation the EU offers to countries outside the bloc.

## Pillar II: Health, Climate, Space and Digital Research

The deal covers Pillar II of Horizon Europe, called "Global Challenges and European Industrial Competitiveness". This is the largest part of the programme. It funds research carried out by teams from several countries in areas such as:

- Health
- Digital technologies
- Industry
- Space
- Climate and energy
- Bioeconomy

Under the agreement, Indian entities will not only join these consortia but can also lead them. That gives Indian institutions a bigger say in setting research agendas, managing budgets and owning the results of large projects.

Ekaterina Zaharieva, the EU Commissioner for Startups, Research and Innovation, said the agreement "opens new opportunities for our businesses". She added that Europe wants India as a partner in building the technologies that will shape the future.

## How the India EU Talks Moved So Fast

The negotiations were quick by EU standards. Exploratory talks started on February 6, 2026, and formal negotiations began on July 17, 2026. Talks closed less than four months later.

The deal builds on several earlier steps:

| Milestone | Detail |
|---|---|
| 2001 | EU India Agreement for Scientific and Technological Cooperation signed, later renewed until 2030 |
| January 27, 2026 | Joint EU India Comprehensive Strategic Agenda towards 2030 adopted at the EU India Summit |
| February 6, 2026 | Exploratory talks on Horizon Europe association begin |
| July 17, 2026 | Formal negotiations open |
| November 6, 2026 | Negotiations concluded |
| January 2027 | Expected start of association |

The EU India Trade and Technology Council has also supported joint work on artificial intelligence, semiconductors, digital public infrastructure and high performance computing, according to The Pioneer.

## India Joins Canada, Japan and the UK

India will join 23 non EU countries that are already associated with Horizon Europe. These include the United Kingdom, Canada, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand, Switzerland and Ukraine. The Commission has also concluded talks with Australia, and that agreement is expected to be signed soon.

For India, the move fits a wider push to build research partnerships in fields such as clean energy, critical minerals and space. At home, the government recently backed a <a href="/critical-mineral-processing-scheme-lithium-nickel/">Rs 2,814 crore scheme for critical mineral processing</a>, and private players are entering areas like <a href="/cosmoserve-base-1-space-debris-hyderabad/">space debris removal</a>. Access to large European consortia could help Indian teams in such sectors find partners, data and funding.

## What Happens Next

Two formal steps remain. Both sides must sign the association agreement, and each must complete its own internal approval procedures. Only then can Indian applicants start bidding for Pillar II calls as associated participants.

Researchers who want to prepare can explore the EU's official <a href="https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/worldwide/india" target="_blank" rel="noopener">EURAXESS India portal</a>, which lists European research jobs, fellowships and funding information for people based in India. Since the current Horizon Europe programme runs only until 2027, how India fits into the EU's next research framework will also be worth watching.

## A Bigger Role for Indian Science in Europe

The Horizon Europe deal is one of the most significant science agreements between India and the EU so far. If the signing goes to plan, Indian institutions will move from being occasional partners to full participants with direct funding and the right to lead. For students, early career scientists and deep tech startups, that could open more chances to work on large international projects from India itself.
