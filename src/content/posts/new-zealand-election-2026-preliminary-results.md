New Zealand voted in its general election on Saturday, November 7, 2026, and the night ended without a clear winner. Preliminary figures from the Electoral Commission show the ruling National Party and the opposition Labour Party separated by only a few thousand party votes, each just above 29 percent. On current numbers the governing three party bloc of National, ACT and NZ First holds a slim majority, but hundreds of thousands of special votes are still to be counted, and the official result is not due until November 27.

## How Close the National and Labour Vote Really Is

According to the <a href="https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/2026-general-election-preliminary-results" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Electoral Commission's preliminary results</a>, Labour received 710,330 party votes (29.36 percent) and National received 706,680 (29.21 percent). That is the smallest gap between the two big parties in years, and both finished below 30 percent.

Yet the seat picture looks very different. National won 42 electorate seats, far more than Labour's 21, which gives it the largest group in parliament even with a slightly smaller party vote. Under New Zealand's mixed member proportional (MMP) system, a party that wins more electorates than its party vote would normally allow keeps the extra seats as "overhang" seats.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| National | 29.21 | 42 | 0 | 42 |
| Labour | 29.36 | 21 | 15 | 36 |
| Green | 13.30 | 2 | 14 | 16 |
| ACT | 10.92 | 1 | 12 | 13 |
| NZ First | 9.78 | 0 | 12 | 12 |
| Opportunity | 5.96 | 0 | 7 | 7 |
| Te Pati Maori | 0.57 | 5 | 0 | 5 |

The preliminary parliament has 131 seats, made up of 71 electorate seats and 60 list seats. A majority therefore needs 66 seats.

## The Coalition Maths for Luxon and Hipkins

Adding up the current figures, National (42), ACT (13) and NZ First (12) reach 67 seats, a majority of two. Labour, the Greens, Te Pati Maori and the Opportunity Party together reach 64.

Prime Minister Christopher Luxon told supporters it was "heartening" that National was again the largest party, but he added that negotiations and compromises would be needed and that a new government would only be formed after final results. Labour leader Chris Hipkins did not concede. He said every vote must now be counted, and that the Labour Party "is not going anywhere", ABC News reported in its <a href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-07/nz-election-2026-live-blog/107355448" target="_blank" rel="noopener">election night coverage</a>.

Luxon has repeatedly ruled out working with the Opportunity Party. Its leader, Qiulae Wong, said voters had chosen unity over division and that any arrangement would depend on her party's bottom lines. Political scientist Tom Jamieson told ABC that a new government might not be in place before Christmas.

## Why Special Votes Could Still Change the Result

Special votes include ballots cast by overseas voters, people who enrolled late and those who voted outside their own electorate. The Electoral Commission estimates about 399,365 special votes, including 96,947 overseas and dictation votes, and says that figure may change. The official count began on Sunday, November 8, and is expected to take about 20 days.

Special votes have shifted seats in past New Zealand elections, and with so many electorates decided narrowly, even a small move could matter. The Spinoff found that 37 of the 71 electorate winners won with a plurality rather than an outright majority. If Labour or its partners pick up a marginal seat, or if overhang numbers change, the two seat majority on the right could shrink or disappear.

### Key highlights

- Election held on Saturday, November 7, 2026
- About 2.43 million ordinary votes counted on election night
- Estimated turnout of 74.6 percent, down from 78.2 percent in 2023
- About 67.3 percent of ordinary votes were cast in advance
- Official results scheduled for Friday, November 27

## Smaller Parties Grow and Senior MPs Fall

The night was strong for smaller parties. The Greens, at 13.3 percent, recorded what ABC described as their best ever result. NZ First rose to about 9.8 percent from around 6 percent in 2023. The Opportunity Party crossed the 5 percent threshold and will enter parliament for the first time. According to The Spinoff, third parties together won a record share of the vote compared with past elections.

Te Pati Maori won only 0.57 percent of the party vote but led in five of the seven Maori electorates, which brings it overhang seats. The Spinoff counted seven overhang seats for National and four for Te Pati Maori, the most ever.

Several senior National figures lost their electorates, ABC reported, including deputy leader Nicola Willis, Paul Goldsmith and Speaker Gerry Brownlee. Labour gained urban seats such as Waitakere and Mt Roskill, while National took Tamaki from ACT.

## What It Means for India

New Zealand's next government will inherit the India New Zealand free trade agreement, which was signed in April 2026 and came into force on October 20. Under the deal, 57 percent of New Zealand exports to India became tariff free from day one, and a second round of tariff cuts is scheduled for January 1, 2027. Luxon has called the agreement one of the proudest achievements of his time in office. Two way trade was about NZ$3.99 billion in the year to June 2026, AAP reported. New Zealand is also home to a large Indian community and is a popular study destination for Indian students, so the policy direction of the next government will be watched closely in India.

## The Road to November 27

For now, both main parties are waiting. ABC analyst Stephen Dziedzic judged Luxon slightly more likely to stay in office, but the margin is thin and the special vote count will decide the final shape of parliament. Once the Electoral Commission releases the official results on November 27, the real bargaining over who governs New Zealand for the next three years can begin.
