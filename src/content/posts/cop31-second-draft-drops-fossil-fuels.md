Negotiators at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, released a second draft of the main political agreement on Thursday, November 19, and it no longer mentions fossil fuels at all. The first version, published on Monday, November 16, had two references to moving away from coal, oil and gas. The new text also drops the $120 billion figure for adaptation funding. The summit is due to close on Friday, November 20, and every country has to agree before a deal is adopted.

The document is called the cover decision. It is the political agreement meant to sum up what the summit decides.

## Side by Side: The November 16 and November 19 Drafts

The table compares what reporting from UN News and ThePrint says each version contained.

| Issue | First draft (Nov 16) | Second draft (Nov 19) |
|---|---|---|
| Fossil fuels | Two references, including a pledge on phasing them out | No mention of fossil fuels |
| Adaptation finance | Tripling by 2035, with a $120 billion target | Tripling goal kept, linked to "implementation needs"; $120 billion figure and the method of calculating the increase removed |
| Climate finance | Work programme and "legally binding financial obligations" on developed countries | Both references removed |
| Electrification | Electrification goals included | 35% by 2035 kept, but ThePrint reports the electrification goals were weakened |
| Renewables and efficiency | Not detailed in the reports we read | Tripling renewable capacity and doubling the efficiency improvement rate by 2030, presented as complementing the 35% target |
| Oceans | Work after 2028 and a high-level ministerial dialogue | Both removed; 18 ocean references remain and a synthesis report is due on June 1, 2028 |
| 1.5°C | Not detailed in the reports we read | Acknowledges the world will overshoot 1.5°C |

Our <a href="/cop31-draft-deal-adaptation-finance-120-billion/">report on the first COP31 draft</a> explains how the $120 billion goal was built and what developing countries had been asking for.

## Why the Missing Words Matter

Civil society groups in Antalya had been pressing for stronger action, and experts said the Thursday text backtracked from Monday's version. The reports do not name any country or bloc that pushed for the change.

Harjeet Singh of the Fill the Fund campaign told ThePrint the new text was "actively more regressive", saying it stripped out accountability, timelines and legal anchors. Teresa Anderson of ActionAid International told <a href="https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168839" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UN News</a> that announcements in the cover decision "won't cover up" how much the negotiations were struggling.

Australia's Chris Bowen, who leads the negotiations, said COP31 would be the first climate summit to admit that warming will go past 1.5°C. He also promised that talks would not end without consensus.

## The Clean Power Target That Survived

What remains on energy is Türkiye's "35 by 35" proposal: raising electricity's share of the world's final energy use to 35% by 2035. According to UN News, the COP31 Presidency's electrification package has support from more than 150 countries and the EU, 37 cities and over 260 businesses. The package points to more than 21,000 km of power grids built in 2025 and annual investment plans of at least $156 billion.

COP31 President Murat Kurum called ministers to a roundtable on Wednesday evening to break the deadlock. Some delegates described it as constructive, while ministers from vulnerable countries said little had moved. Finance, adaptation and the just transition were all still open as Friday's deadline approached.

## Reading the Numbers: How Far the World Has Got

The UN Climate Change Yearbook 2026, cited by UN News, tracked 96 climate indicators:

- 44 are moving in the right direction
- 19 show no progress
- 33 lack enough data to judge
- Renewable capacity grew 17% between 2024 and 2025
- Adaptation finance fell from $81.5 billion in 2022 to $64.1 billion in 2024

Adaptation funding fell by about $17.4 billion in those two years, and the new draft no longer puts a number on how far it should rise.

UN climate chief Simon Stiell told delegates that "across the board, we need to move far faster." More detail on the formal negotiating texts is posted on the <a href="https://unfccc.int" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UN Climate Change (UNFCCC) website</a>.

## Where India Fits In

India's government has not commented publicly on the second draft in the reports we read. Aarti Khosla, director of the research group Climate Trends, told ThePrint that the text "reinforces India's longstanding position" that climate ambition cannot be separated from equity. That is an independent analyst's reading, not an official Indian statement.

For Indian readers, the finance cuts may matter as much as the fossil fuel wording. The second draft drops the $120 billion figure, the climate finance work programme and the clause on legally binding obligations for developed countries, all of which concern how much money flows to developing economies.

Countries can still change the wording before a final text is adopted, and Bowen has said the talks will not end without one.
