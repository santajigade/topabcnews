Lakshya Sen has won the Kumamoto Masters Japan men's singles title, coming from a game down to beat home favourite Koki Watanabe 13-21, 21-18, 21-14 in the final on Sunday, November 15, 2026. The win, which took 64 minutes, is the Indian star's second BWF World Tour title in a row after his triumph at the Hylo Open in Germany earlier this month. It also lifts him into a strong position to qualify for the season-ending World Tour Finals in China.

## How Lakshya Sen Turned the Final Around

Watanabe, ranked world No. 15 and playing in front of his home crowd, controlled the first game and took it 21-13. Sen, ranked No. 13 and seeded eighth, then changed the pattern of the match. He edged a tight second game 21-18 to force a decider.

In the third game, Sen built a lead of 11-7 at the interval, according to Onmanorama, and Watanabe could not find a way back. Sen closed out the match 21-14 to take the title.

According to The Pioneer, he is the first Indian men's singles player to win this tournament. That is a big step up from last year, when his run in Kumamoto ended in the semi-finals after a quarter-final win over former world champion Loh Kean Yew, as reported by <a href="https://www.newsonair.gov.in/lakshya-sen-storms-into-semi-finals-of-kumamoto-masters-japan-tournament" target="_blank" rel="noopener">All India Radio News</a> at the time.

## Lakshya Sen's Route to the Title

Sen's strongest performance before the final came in the semi-final on Saturday, when he beat Indonesia's Alwi Farhan, the world No. 10, 21-12, 15-21, 21-11, Dainik Jagran English reported. Watanabe, meanwhile, reached the final by beating Thailand's Kunlavut Vitidsarn.

The week in Kumamoto continued a sharp turnaround in form. Earlier in November, Sen won the Hylo Open in Germany by beating Indonesia's Jonatan Christie in the final. Dainik Jagran English said that title ended a run of early exits in six straight tournaments. Two titles in two events have now put his season back on track.

The comeback in the final also followed a familiar pattern for the week. Against both Farhan and Watanabe, the matches went to three games, and Sen won the deciding game comfortably each time, 21-11 in the semi-final and 21-14 in the final. That ability to finish strongly after losing a game is what separated him from his opponents in Kumamoto, and it will matter again in the tough fields still to come this season.

## What the Win Means for the World Tour Finals

The title is worth 9,200 ranking points. The Pioneer reported that this takes Sen's tally in the qualification race from 64,980 to a projected 74,180 points, which is expected to lift him from 12th to sixth when the standings are updated.

Only the top eight eligible players qualify for the HSBC BWF World Tour Finals, which will be held in Hangzhou, China. The last qualifying event is the Hong Kong Open, scheduled for November 17 to 22, so Sen has one more chance to strengthen his place. Official standings and draws are published by the <a href="https://bwfbadminton.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Badminton World Federation</a> on its website.
