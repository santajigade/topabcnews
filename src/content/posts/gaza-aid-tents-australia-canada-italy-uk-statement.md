The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and the United Kingdom have jointly asked Israel to stop holding back shelter supplies for Gaza, saying about 150,000 tents and other essential goods are waiting at the border while families face the cold and rain of winter. The statement was issued on Sunday, November 15, 2026, a week after the season's first storm flooded tens of thousands of tents in displacement camps. Israel says it has already let in more than 93,000 tents and accuses Hamas of misusing aid.

## What the Four Ministers Asked Israel to Do

The joint statement, reported by <a href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/europe/2026/11/16/four-foreign-ministers-urge-israel-to-allow-rapid-and-unimpeded-aid-into-gaza/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The National</a> and Arab News, made several specific demands:

- Israel should end the delays and open the crossings so the tents held at the border can enter.
- Aid groups should get immediate, rapid and unimpeded access across the whole Gaza Strip.
- Dual-use items needed for humanitarian work and early recovery should be allowed in.
- All parties should carry out the Comprehensive Plan for Gaza, respect the ceasefire and follow international humanitarian law.

The ministers described conditions in the territory as dire and said recent flooding had added to the hardship of people who already lack proper shelter. "This was only the first storm of the winter," they warned, adding that there was still time to act before the colder months fully arrive.

More than two million people live in Gaza, and most of them are staying in makeshift shelters, according to The National's report on the statement.

## Floods, a Fatal Collapse and Rising Illness

The appeal follows a difficult start to the winter. On November 7 and 8, heavy rain flooded tens of thousands of tents in camps for displaced families. In central Gaza, a rain-soaked sand bank collapsed onto tents and killed five people, including three children. We covered that incident in our report on the <a href="/gaza-floods-tent-collapse-deir-el-balah/">Deir el-Balah tent collapse</a>.

The National also reported that a nine-month-old baby died last week from exposure to the cold, described as the first death linked to falling temperatures this season.

Health officials are worried about disease. A World Health Organization representative, Dr. Reinhilde Van De Weerdt, told reporters in Geneva that acute respiratory infections were running 33,000 cases higher than at the same point last year, according to the <a href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/dozens-of-nations-organizations-demand-israel-let-more-winter-supplies-into-gaza/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Times of Israel</a>. Respiratory illnesses now make up 11% of all medical consultations. The WHO said nearly one million displaced people live in areas exposed to flooding, and 400,000 of them are in places considered at extremely high risk. "The worst really is yet to come," she said.

The WHO also warned that floodwater raises the risk of contaminated drinking water, that standing water lets mosquitoes and pests breed, and that damp shelters encourage mould and bacteria, which is especially dangerous for young children.

## Israel's Position on Tents and Dual-Use Goods

Israel rejects the claim that it is blocking winter aid. COGAT, the Israeli Defence Ministry body that oversees civilian matters in Gaza, said on November 13 that more than 93,000 tents and over 8,600 trucks carrying winter supplies and other essential equipment had entered the territory. It also said it had stepped up sanitation work with international organisations.

Earlier, on November 9, the Israeli military said more than 850 requests for winter supplies had been approved, but that around half had not yet been carried out. It blamed this on logistical and budget problems faced by the UN and aid agencies.

Israel says it will not allow some equipment, including mobile homes, because of what it calls a known risk that Hamas could use such items to rebuild its capabilities. It also says reconstruction will not begin until Hamas disarms and gives up control of Gaza. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office said Israel was doing what it could to allow winter equipment in and blamed Hamas for not keeping its commitments. Israeli officials have argued that criticism of the aid flow distracts from Hamas's responsibility.

Municipal and civil defence officials in Gaza, which is run by Hamas, say they could not cope with the storm because of fuel shortages and the loss of vehicles, bulldozers and water pumps destroyed during the war.

## A Wider Diplomatic Push

The four-nation statement is part of a growing wave of pressure. On November 13, a group of 53 signatories, made up of countries and organisations and led by the UK, Qatar and Jordan, issued an appeal at the United Nations in New York. It asked Israel to immediately allow in critical items for shelter, fuel, water, sanitation and health, and to let aid move through all crossings and land routes, including corridors through Egypt and Jordan.

The UN humanitarian chief Tom Fletcher and officials of US President Donald Trump's Board of Peace have also criticised the pace of winter aid.

In Europe, Ireland's foreign minister Helen McEntee said she would press for a stronger EU statement calling for the aid corridors to open, at a meeting of EU foreign ministers on Monday. Germany's foreign minister has also urged Israel to allow more aid in before winter, according to Anadolu Agency.

The statement lands as Israel's domestic politics are in flux. The 53-signatory appeal was directed at Israel's incoming government, following the process described in our report on <a href="/herzog-taps-eisenkot-form-israel-government/">Herzog asking Gadi Eisenkot to form a government</a>.

## What Happens Next for Gaza's Shelter Crisis

The gap between the two sides is now mostly about which items can enter and how fast. Western and Arab governments say the tents and shelter materials are ready and only need clearance, while Israel says much of what it has approved is still waiting for aid agencies to deliver, and that it will keep restricting goods it sees as dual use. With more storms expected over the coming months, aid groups say the next few weeks will decide how many families get dry, warm shelter before the coldest part of the winter.
