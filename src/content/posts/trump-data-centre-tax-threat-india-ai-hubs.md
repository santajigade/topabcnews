US President Donald Trump said on Friday, November 20, that American companies building data centres outside the United States would face a "tremendous tax". He made the remark at a White House news conference held after his four-day Asia trip, and gave no rate, no start date and no list of companies or countries. Reuters reported him as saying, "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country." India is directly exposed: US firms have announced more than $32 billion of AI and cloud spending here between 2026 and 2030, much of it tied to data centres.

## What Trump Said, and What He Left Out

According to Reuters, Trump framed the move as a way to help the US artificial intelligence sector compete with China. Chinese firms, Reuters noted, rent computing capacity in countries that can legally import advanced chips. Newsweek quoted Trump saying "China will take all of them" and urging US communities to "make them pay your community".

Axios reported that Trump said he would either "block" such projects or tax them, and that he acknowledged he had not discussed the idea with his officials. Newsweek also reported he had not told his administration, and said the White House pointed reporters back to his remarks when asked for details.

Several things were not said:

- There is no tax rate, executive order or bill.
- He did not say whether data centres already built or under construction abroad would be covered.
- He did not say what legal route he would use. Axios pointed out that under the US Constitution, Congress holds the power to set federal taxes.

The Data Center Coalition, a US industry group, responded that its members are investing "hundreds of billions" of dollars in the United States, Newsweek reported. Microsoft declined to comment, and Newsweek said Amazon Web Services and Google had not replied.

The remark came the same week the US Commerce Department moved to restrict exports of advanced AI chips to several Southeast Asian countries, Reuters said. Earlier this week we explained the <a href="/us-remote-access-ai-chip-rule-india-impact/">US chip rule for overseas data centres and its India impact</a>.

## The US Data Centre Money Already Committed to India

The largest American AI investments in India were announced in late 2025, all running into the period Trump is now talking about.

| Company | Pledge | Period | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | About $15 billion | 2026 to 2030 | First AI hub in India at Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh: a gigawatt-scale data centre campus, a new subsea cable gateway, and energy and fibre networks, with AdaniConnex and Airtel as partners |
| Microsoft | $17.5 billion | 2026 to 2029 | Cloud and AI infrastructure, skilling and operations. A new Hyderabad cloud region (India South Central) and expansion of existing regions in Chennai, Hyderabad and Pune |
| Amazon | More than $35 billion | Through 2030 | All Amazon businesses in India, including AI-led digitisation, exports and jobs. Not a data centre figure on its own |

Google announced its plan on <a href="https://blog.google/intl/en-in/company-news/our-first-ai-hub-in-india-powered-by-a-15-billion-investment/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">its India blog on October 14, 2025</a>, describing the Vizag campus as capable of gigawatt-scale compute. <a href="https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2025/12/09/microsoft-invests-us17-5-billion-in-india-to-drive-ai-diffusion-at-population-scale/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Microsoft called its $17.5 billion pledge</a> its largest investment in Asia, on top of $3 billion announced in early 2025. Amazon's figure, announced on December 10, 2025, at its Smbhav summit in New Delhi, comes on top of nearly $40 billion it says it has already put into India. Google and Microsoft together account for the $32.5 billion of AI and cloud money in the first paragraph. Amazon's total is left out of that sum because it spans retail, logistics and other businesses.

## What Could Change for Indian Projects

Nothing has changed yet, because no measure exists. If what Trump described becomes policy, it would fall on US companies building data centres abroad, which is what the Google and Microsoft plans in India are. The effect depends on details nobody has published: the rate, whether it applies to projects already announced, and whether it targets only certain countries.

Newsweek reported that Trump had earlier criticised Google's roughly $15 billion data centre plan in Finland, and that he mentioned Finland again on Friday. Newsweek said it was not clear whether he was referring to Google. India was not named in any report of the remarks we read.

Three things will show whether this goes further:

1. A written proposal. Any tax would need a bill in Congress or a stated executive route.
2. Word from the companies. Google, Microsoft and Amazon had not commented on the remark in any report we read.
3. Chip export rules. The Commerce Department's move this week on chip exports to Southeast Asia shows the administration already has a separate tool that governs what hardware overseas data centres can buy.

## Markets and the Quad Backdrop

US stocks closed lower overnight with AI and chip shares among the decliners, South Korea's SBS reported on November 21. No report we read tied the fall to the data centre remark.

At the same news conference, Trump spoke about a Quad initiative with India, Japan and Australia to expand trusted AI systems across the Indo-Pacific, backed by more than $85 million in coordinated funding, the Sunday Guardian reported. The Quad leaders met in Manila on November 17 and 18; our <a href="/quad-manila-summit-2026-vs-2024-india/">Quad Manila summit report</a> has the background. Whether a tax on overseas data centres would treat partner countries differently is one of the questions Trump did not answer.
