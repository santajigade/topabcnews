India fast bowler Mohammed Shami has retired from Test cricket, bringing the curtain down on a red-ball career that began 13 years ago to the day. The 36-year-old announced the decision on Friday, November 6, through a post on Instagram. He finishes with 229 wickets in 64 Tests and has made it clear that he is not done with the game, as he will remain available for white-ball cricket, including the IPL and domestic matches.

## Shami's Farewell Message to the White Jersey

In his post, Shami said Test cricket had been a very special part of his life and that he was stepping away "with a heavy heart and unlimited beautiful memories." He also spoke about the setbacks along the way, saying he got hurt and suffered injuries but kept coming back.

"As far as international Test cricket is concerned, the journey ends here," he said, according to The Tribune. He added that he would continue to play white-ball cricket and turn out in domestic matches when needed to stay fit.

The announcement did not come as a complete surprise. Shami has not played a Test since the World Test Championship final against Australia at The Oval in 2023, and he has been out of the Indian side altogether since the 2025 Champions Trophy.

Shami was one of the main pillars of India's pace attack through a period when the team became a force in away Tests. His record, as compiled by The Tribune and Business Standard, reads as follows:

## From Eden Gardens Debut to the Oval Final

Shami made his Test debut against the West Indies at Eden Gardens, Kolkata, in November 2013. He announced himself straight away with figures of 4/71 and 5/47, helping India win by an innings and 51 runs.

His last Test was the 2021 to 2023 World Test Championship final against Australia. He took 2/122 and 2/39 as India lost by 209 runs. Injuries then kept him out of the Test team, and he never returned to the format.

## Memorable Spells and a Famous Fifty at Lord's

Several performances defined Shami's Test career:

- **Johannesburg, 2018:** He took 5/28 in the second innings as India pulled off a consolation win in South Africa.
- **Centurion:** On the following tour of South Africa, he claimed 8 wickets in the opening Test, which India won.
- **Lord's, August 2021:** Perhaps his most celebrated moment came with the bat. Shami struck an unbeaten 56 off 70 balls, with six fours and a six, and shared an unbroken 89-run stand for the ninth wicket with Jasprit Bumrah. He reached his fifty by hitting Moeen Ali for six. India declared at 298/8, set England 272, and then bowled them out for 120 to win by 151 runs.

That Lord's partnership swung the Test in India's favour and showed the fighting spirit Shami often brought to the side.

## Ravi Shastri Says Shami Deserved Another Chance

Former India head coach Ravi Shastri reacted to the news by saying Shami deserved another opportunity in Test cricket, according to reports on Saturday. The comment reflects a wider feeling among fans that a bowler of his skill could still have contributed in the longest format had he been fit and in contention.

## What Retirement Means for India's Pace Attack

With Shami gone from Tests, India's red-ball pace group will lean on the bowlers who have stepped up in his absence since 2023. For Shami himself, the focus now moves to white-ball cricket, the IPL and domestic tournaments, where he hopes to keep competing. ## A Career Built on Seam and Swing

Shami's Test career ends with 229 wickets, six five-wicket hauls and a place among the key members of India's pace attack of the last decade. He bowled through injuries, made repeated comebacks and played a big part in some of the country's most memorable overseas wins. He leaves the white jersey behind, but as he said himself, the memories tied to it stay with him.
