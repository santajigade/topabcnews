Mohammed Shami has retired from Test cricket. The 36-year-old India fast bowler announced it on Instagram on Friday, November 6, 2026, exactly 13 years after his Test debut against the West Indies at Eden Gardens on November 6, 2013. He finishes with 229 wickets in 64 Tests at an average of 27.71, with six five-wicket hauls and a best of 6/56. He is not leaving the game: he will keep playing white-ball cricket in domestic competitions and the IPL.

## How Shami Said Goodbye to the White Jersey

Shami posted the news along with a video recorded at his farmhouse in Sahaspur Alinagar village in Amroha district, Uttar Pradesh, according to a PTI report. In the post he wrote, as translated by PTI, "I am leaving the white jersey, but never the memories connected to it." He said he may already have bowled his last ball in Test cricket, and thanked his coaches, trainers, fans, the BCCI and the Cricket Association of Bengal.

His last Test was the 2023 World Test Championship final against Australia at The Oval, and he has not played for India in any format since the 2025 Champions Trophy final against New Zealand, which India won. He played seven Ranji Trophy games for Bengal last season and took 37 wickets, and before the announcement he had taken 14 wickets in three Ranji matches this season. PTI reported that he would still play the odd four-day game when fit, and that he had been named in Bengal's squad for the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy, which began on November 14.

## Where Shami Ranks Among India's Test Fast Bowlers

PTI placed Shami sixth among Indian pacers by Test wickets. The five above him are Kapil Dev (434), Zaheer Khan and Ishant Sharma (311 each), Javagal Srinath (236) and Jasprit Bumrah (234), who is still playing. Two things stand out. Shami has the best average and the best strike rate of these six: he took a wicket every 50 balls or so, about 10 to 14 balls quicker than Kapil, Zaheer, Ishant and Srinath. He also got to 229 wickets in 64 Tests, while Srinath needed 67 for 236. His one weaker column is five-wicket hauls: 6, against 10 or more for each of the four bowlers above him.

## Most of His Wickets Came Away From Home

Shami's record splits clearly by venue. Of his 64 Tests, 41 were played away from India:

| Venue | Tests | Wickets | Best innings | 5-wkt hauls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | 21 | 76 | 5/35 | 2 |
| Away | 41 | 145 | 6/56 | 4 |
| Neutral (WTC finals) | 2 | 8 | 4/76 | 0 |

That is 145 of his 229 wickets, or roughly 63 percent, taken in away Tests. PTI also noted that across India, South Africa and the West Indies combined, he took 131 wickets in 35 Tests at an average under 25.

## Spells India Will Remember

On debut at Eden Gardens he took four wickets in the first innings and five in the second, finishing with 9/118. Outlook India says only Narendra Hirwani, with 16 at Chennai in 1988, has taken more wickets on Test debut for India.

In South Africa he twice made a difference. He took 5/28 in the second innings at Johannesburg on the 2018 tour to set up a consolation win, and on the next tour he took eight wickets in the opening Test at Centurion.

His best-known Test moment came with the bat. At Lord's in August 2021 he made 56 not out and added an unbroken 89 for the ninth wicket with Jasprit Bumrah, reaching fifty with a heave off Moeen Ali, according to <a href="https://www.espncricinfo.com/print/story/1273659" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ESPNcricinfo's match report</a>. India declared at 298/8, set England 272, bowled them out for 120 and won by 151 runs.

## Ravi Shastri Says Shami Had More Test Wickets Left

Former India head coach Ravi Shastri, who worked with Shami from July 2017 to November 2021, called the exit sad. Speaking before India's second ODI against New Zealand, he said Shami had 30 to 40 more Test wickets in him and should have been picked in the last 12 months, Outlook India reported. Shastri dismissed the concerns about his fitness, pointing out that Shami had played every Bengal game and bowled 15 to 20 overs a match.

## What Comes Next for Shami and India's Pace Group

Shami has kept the door open in white-ball cricket. Outlook India linked his decision to stay available to the 2027 ODI World Cup, where he would want to build on the 2023 World Cup. In that tournament he was the leading wicket-taker with 24 wickets in seven matches, including 7/57 against New Zealand in the semi-final.

India's Test attack has already moved on without him since 2023.
