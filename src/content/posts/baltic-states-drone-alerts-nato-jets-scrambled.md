Residents in parts of Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia were told to take shelter early on Wednesday, November 11, after unidentified drones entered the region's airspace from the direction of Russia or Belarus. NATO fighter jets were activated in response, and the alerts were lifted a few hours later. No injuries were reported. The scare came as Ukraine and Russia traded drone strikes overnight, including Ukrainian attacks on Russian ports on the Baltic Sea.

## What Happened Overnight in the Baltic States

According to Reuters, Latvia and Lithuania warned people in several regions to seek shelter because of the threat from drones crossing into their airspace. One warning from Lithuania's National Crisis Management Centre covered Kaunas, the country's second biggest city with about 300,000 residents. Estonia also issued an alert.

Lithuanian Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevicius said four drones were detected across the region, calling it "a difficult night". Reuters reported that an attempt was made to shoot down a drone over northern Lithuania, though it was not immediately clear whether it was hit. Debris from a NATO missile was later found on the ground.

Finland's defence forces also placed temporary restrictions on airspace and sea traffic in the eastern Gulf of Finland as a precaution, and later withdrew them.

### Key facts from the night

- **Countries with alerts:** Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia
- **Drones detected:** four across the region, according to Lithuania's prime minister
- **Largest area covered:** Kaunas, Lithuania, home to about 300,000 people
- **Military response:** NATO air defence and fighter jets activated
- **Finland:** temporary airspace and maritime limits in the eastern Gulf of Finland
- **Casualties:** none reported

## Ukrainian Strikes on Russia's Baltic Ports

The alerts came during a night of heavy drone activity in Russia's northwest. As reported by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/11/russia-claims-interception-of-146-drones-in-leningrad-region" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>, Leningrad region governor Aleksandr Drozdenko said on Tuesday that 146 drones had been downed over the region. On Wednesday he reported a drone attack that caused a fire in the port area of Ust-Luga on the Baltic Sea. Reuters said three people were injured there.

A day earlier, on Monday, a Ukrainian drone hit the Kirishi oil refinery, described by Reuters as Russia's second largest, and local authorities said a woman was killed. In Russia's Smolensk region, the local governor said falling drone debris killed two people, according to Russian newswires.

Ukrainian officials, for their part, reported Russian attacks that knocked out power in Kryvyi Rih and injured people in Kharkiv and Zaporizhzhia. Reuters said it could not independently verify these reports from either side.

## Why Stray Drones Keep Reaching NATO Countries

This is not the first time in 2026 that drones linked to the war have crossed into the Baltic states and Finland. Ukraine has been targeting Russian oil export ports on the Baltic Sea, and some of its drones have missed their targets and drifted into neighbouring NATO countries, triggering security warnings. In Latvia, Reuters noted, such incidents contributed to the collapse of a government earlier this year.

The three sides describe the problem differently:

| Party | Position |
|---|---|
| Russia | Accuses Kyiv of using NATO airspace as cover for its attacks |
| Ukraine | Says some of its drones were pushed off course by Russian signal jamming |
| NATO members | Blame Russia, saying it started the war |

Reuters said the origin of the drones involved in the latest warnings had not been confirmed.

## How NATO Guards Baltic Skies

Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia do not have enough fighter aircraft of their own to police their airspace. Since they joined NATO in 2004, allies have rotated jets through the region under the <a href="https://ac.nato.int/missions/air-policing/baltics.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NATO Baltic Air Policing mission</a>, operating from Siauliai Air Base in Lithuania and, since 2014, from Amari Air Base in Estonia. Allied air forces rotate every four months. Wednesday's activation of jets is part of that standing arrangement.

## Talks in Abu Dhabi Continue in the Background

The overnight alerts overlapped with US-mediated talks between Russian and Ukrainian officials in Abu Dhabi, which began on Tuesday and entered their second and final day on Wednesday. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the first day "constructive and quite substantive". Our report on <a href="/abu-dhabi-talks-day-two-prisoner-swap-no-breakthrough/">day two of the Abu Dhabi talks</a> covers the reported prisoner exchange and the issues still unresolved.

## A Growing Risk at NATO's Edge

Wednesday's alerts ended without injuries, but they show how the war's long range drone campaign is reaching the doorstep of NATO members. As long as strikes on Russia's Baltic ports continue, governments in Vilnius, Riga and Tallinn are likely to keep facing nights like this one, with warnings issued at short notice while allied jets take to the air.
