Carbon dioxide released by burning fossil fuels is projected to fall by about 0.4% in 2026, according to the Global Carbon Budget 2026, the 21st annual assessment by the international Global Carbon Project. If confirmed, it would be one of the rare years when fossil emissions have declined, alongside the Covid year of 2020 and the financial crisis of 2009. But scientists say the dip is mostly the result of high oil prices linked to the Iran war, not a lasting shift, and total emissions including land use are still heading for a record. The findings were published this week as countries meet for the COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey.

## The Numbers Behind the 2026 Dip

The report projects fossil fuel and cement emissions of about 38.2 billion tonnes of CO2 in 2026. According to Business Today's reading of the dataset, that is down by around 157 million tonnes from 38.40 billion tonnes in 2025. The researchers give an uncertainty range of minus 1.5% to plus 0.6%, so a small rise cannot be ruled out.

The fall comes almost entirely from oil, while coal keeps growing.

| Fuel | Projected change in 2026 | Share of fossil CO2 |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | +0.6% | About 41% |
| Oil | -1.9% | About 33% |
| Gas | -0.3% (roughly flat) | About 21% |
| Cement | Not separately highlighted | About 2% |

According to <a href="https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-fossil-fuel-co2-emissions-set-to-dip-in-2026-as-iran-conflict-hits-oil-use" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Carbon Brief's analysis of the data</a>, written by Zeke Hausfather and Pierre Friedlingstein, the drop in oil use cut about 243 million tonnes of CO2.

## Why Oil Use Fell This Year

A related study led by Corinne Le Quéré and published in Nature Climate Change found that disruption of oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, tied to the Iran war, pushed up oil and gas prices around the world. That price shock is the main reason for the decline, the researchers said, and emissions would probably have grown this year without it.

Clean energy is helping, but not yet enough. The study estimates that if sales of electric vehicles and new solar and wind capacity keep growing at 2026 rates, they could offset about 63% of emissions growth between 2025 and 2028. Glen Peters of the CICERO climate research centre in Oslo told Science News that the energy transition is "not quite big enough to get emissions heading downward yet."

## India Bucks the Trend With a 4.4% Rise

Among major emitters, India is the only one projected to see a clear increase in 2026.

| Country or region | Projected change in 2026 | Share of global fossil CO2 |
|---|---|---|
| China | -0.9% | 32% |
| United States | -1.3% | 13% |
| India | +4.4% | 8% |
| European Union | -1.5% | 6% |
| Japan | -1.0% | 2.5% |
| Rest of the world | -0.7% | 38% |

India's rise is driven by coal, which is projected to grow 5.9%, while its oil emissions are expected to fall 0.9%. With an 8% share of the global total, India's growth stands out in a year when most other large economies are cutting back. In China, oil emissions are projected to fall 4.6% while coal edges up 0.3%. In the US, coal emissions are expected to drop 6.7%.

International aviation and shipping, together about 3% of the total, are each projected to fall around 1%.

## Land Fires Push Total Emissions to a Record

The picture changes when emissions from land use, such as deforestation and fires, are added. Land-use emissions are projected to jump to about 5.1 billion tonnes in 2026 from 4.4 billion tonnes in 2025, mainly because of peat fires and forest damage in South-east Asia linked to El Nino.

That takes total CO2 emissions to about 43.3 billion tonnes, a new record, up from 42.8 billion tonnes last year. Without the land-use spike, total emissions would have fallen to about 42.6 billion tonnes. We reported earlier on how a strengthening El Nino is also expected to make <a href="/wmo-2026-among-four-warmest-years-cop31/">2026 one of the four warmest years on record</a>.

## How Much Carbon Budget Is Left

The Global Carbon Project estimates how much more CO2 the world can release while keeping a 50% chance of limiting warming to certain levels. From the start of 2027:

- **1.5C:** 150 billion tonnes, which is less than four years of current emissions
- **1.7C:** 510 billion tonnes
- **2C:** 1,035 billion tonnes

Atmospheric CO2 is projected to average 427.7 parts per million in 2026, about 54% above pre-industrial levels, with a rise of about 2.2 ppm this year.

## Key Highlights

- Fossil CO2 projected at 38.2 billion tonnes in 2026, down about 0.4%
- Oil emissions fall 1.9%, coal still rises 0.6%
- India up 4.4%, China down 0.9%, US down 1.3%, EU down 1.5%
- Total CO2 including land use hits a record 43.3 billion tonnes
- 1.5C budget of 150 billion tonnes would be used up in under four years at current rates

## A Dip, Not Yet a Peak

Scientists are careful not to call 2026 the peak of global emissions. A single year of decline driven by an energy price shock can reverse quickly once prices ease. Still, Peters noted that the fact that researchers are discussing a peak at all suggests the world is getting close to it. Climate negotiators at <a href="https://unfccc.int/cop31/high-level" target="_blank" rel="noopener">COP31 in Antalya</a> will now have to decide how fast to push clean energy so that the dip becomes a lasting decline.
