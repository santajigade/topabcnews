India beat New Zealand by four runs on the DLS method in a rain hit second ODI at Hnry Stadium, Wellington, on Saturday, November 7, 2026, to take a 2-0 lead in the five match series. India's bowlers dismissed the hosts for just 134, their lowest all out total at home against India in ODIs. India's chase then wobbled badly, but when rain stopped play for good after 26 overs, India were 113 for 7, four runs ahead of the DLS par score of 109.

## How India's Bowlers Ripped Through New Zealand

India won the toss and chose to bowl after early rain cut the match to 47 overs a side. The decision paid off straight away. Tim Seifert fell for a duck, caught by Anshul Kamboj off Auqib Nabi, and Will Young went for 6 off 17 balls. Henry Nicholls was bowled by Kamboj for 11, and New Zealand were 29 for 3 at the end of the first powerplay.

The slide continued through the middle overs. The hosts slipped to 53 for 5 after Glenn Phillips was dismissed, and eight batters were out for single figures. New Zealand faced 176 dot balls, which shows how tightly India's attack kept the pressure on. Arshdeep Singh did not take a wicket but gave away only 16 runs in seven overs.

The standout was Gurnoor Brar, who finished with 4 for 30 from his 10 overs. Nabi, Kamboj and Axar Patel took two wickets each.

## Daryl Mitchell and Santner Stand Alone for the Hosts

Daryl Mitchell was the only New Zealand batter to settle. He scored a patient 55 off 104 balls, and captain Mitchell Santner added 31 off 38. Without that partnership, the total would have been even lower.

The 134 all out broke an old record. New Zealand's previous lowest home total against India in ODIs was 142 in Auckland in 1994. Other low scores in this fixture include 157 in Napier in 2019 and 168 in Wellington in 2003. New Zealand were already without opener Devon Conway, who was ruled out of the series with concussion before this match, as we reported in our [2nd ODI preview](/india-vs-new-zealand-2nd-odi-wellington/).

## A Nervy Chase and the DLS Finish

A target of 135 should have been simple, but India made it hard. Shubman Gill was out for a golden duck in the second over. Virat Kohli made 7 and Rohit Sharma scored 23 before Kyle Jamieson removed him. India were 47 for 4 at one stage.

Shreyas Iyer steadied things with 30 off 46 balls, and KL Rahul stayed unbeaten on 26 off 38, hitting two fours and two sixes. Wickets kept falling at the other end, with Naman Dhir and Kamboj both dismissed. When the rain returned after 26 overs, India were 113 for 7. Under the Duckworth Lewis Stern method, the par score at that point was 109, so India were declared winners by four runs.

## What the Result Means for the Series

The win builds on India's five wicket victory in the first ODI in Auckland, where New Zealand were bowled out for 185. Two matches in, India's bowlers have dismissed the hosts cheaply both times, which is a strong sign for a young attack on New Zealand pitches.

The batting, though, gives the team management something to think about. Losing four wickets for 47 while chasing a small total in tricky conditions is a warning before the bigger games. Rahul's calm unbeaten knock was the key reason India stayed ahead of the DLS par when the weather closed in. ## When and Where Is the 3rd ODI

The third ODI will be played on Tuesday, November 10, at Seddon Park, Hamilton, starting at 3:00 PM local time, which is 7:30 AM IST. A win there would give India an unbeatable 3-0 lead with two games to spare. New Zealand need a big batting improvement to keep the series alive.

For now, India's bowlers have done the heavy lifting, and one more strong all round display in Hamilton could seal the series.
