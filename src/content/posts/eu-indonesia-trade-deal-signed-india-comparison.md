The European Union and Indonesia signed their Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (IEU-CEPA) in Brussels on Saturday, November 21, 2026, after talks that began in July 2016. Both sides want it in force by **January 1, 2027**. Indonesia's ANTARA news agency reports that more than 90% of tariff lines on Indonesian goods entering the EU will go to zero. The <a href="https://commission.europa.eu/topics/trade/eu-indonesia-trade-agreement_en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">European Commission</a> says 98.5% of tariffs on EU exports to Indonesia will be removed, saving EU exporters an estimated €650 million a year in duties.

India finished its own deal with the EU on January 27, 2026, but has not signed it yet. A government official told PTI the signing is likely on December 16. So Indonesia's pact is on track to start before India's.

## What Each Side Gets

ANTARA reports that 90.4% of Indonesian tariff lines become duty-free from the first day. Another 8.37% are cut gradually over 3 to 15 years through tariff rate quotas. Indonesian textiles, clothing, footwear, food and crude palm oil, which now pay EU duties of 7% to 12%, go to zero.

On the EU side, the Commission lists Indonesian duties of up to 50% on cars, 25% on chemicals and 15% on machinery and medicines among those being removed. Most EU dairy products, including milk powder, cheese and infant formula, enter Indonesia duty-free, and 221 European geographical indications get protection. The EU sold €960 million of farm and food goods to Indonesia in 2025.

The EU kept some farm products out. There is no new access for Indonesian rice, sugar, eggs, fresh bananas or ethanol, and only small quotas for garlic, mushrooms, sweetcorn and manioc starch.

Alongside the trade deal, the two sides signed an Investment Protection Agreement covering legal protection for investors and state-to-state dispute settlement.

## The Palm Oil Quota in Numbers

The deal includes a separate Protocol on Palm Oil. According to Palm Oil Magazine, Indonesia gets a duty-free quota of **1.9 million tonnes** of crude palm oil and a further **140,000 tonnes** for processed palm products. The quotas rise each year and reach their ceiling in the ninth year.

The quota is not automatic. Exporters still need Indonesian export approval, must meet domestic market obligations where they apply, and must pay Indonesia's own export duty and levy. They also have to satisfy EU sustainability rules. Indonesia's Coordinating Minister Airlangga Hartarto said his country is ready to meet "mutually agreed sustainability standards", ANTARA reported.

The Commerce Ministry says Indian goods get preferential access across 97% of EU tariff lines, covering 99.5% of India's exports by value.

## Why the Timing Matters for India

Indonesia is racing a deadline of its own. Its EU trade preferences under the Generalised Scheme of Preferences expire on December 31, 2026, so Jakarta and Brussels set the signing date to let EU approval steps start on November 23. Indonesia plans to ratify by presidential regulation after consulting its parliament.

India has a longer road. Open Magazine, citing the Commission, lists the steps still pending: EU Council authorisation, signature, European Parliament consent, a final Council decision and India's own ratification. Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said on November 18, "We hope to sign it in December 2026," and that India is trying for entry into force in the first quarter of 2027.

If both timelines hold, Indonesian exporters could have duty-free EU access some weeks or months before Indian exporters of similar goods. ## What Happens Next in Brussels and Jakarta

The EU-Indonesia text now goes to the European Parliament and through Indonesia's ratification process. Both governments say they want it working by January 1, 2027, but the date depends on those votes. For India, the next marker is the signing expected during Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit to Europe in December.
