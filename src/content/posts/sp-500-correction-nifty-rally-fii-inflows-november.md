Wall Street's benchmark S&P 500 closed in a correction on Friday, November 20, after a 3.7% one-day fall left it more than 10% below its October 6 record. On the same day India's Nifty 50 ended at 25,801, its fourth straight weekly gain. For November so far, the S&P 500 is down 10.83% and the Nasdaq is down 11.08%, while the Nifty is up 11.92% and the Sensex 12.71%. Foreign investors have put a net Rs 1,11,949 crore into Indian shares this month, which Business Today describes as a record monthly inflow.

## How Friday Played Out on Wall Street

According to the <a href="https://www.inquirer.com/business/stock-market-sp-correction-ai-nvidia-anthropic-loss-20261120.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Associated Press market report</a>, the S&P 500 dropped 265.44 points to 6,910.39. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 949.73 points, or 1.9%, to 48,147.33, and the Nasdaq Composite sank 5.3% to 22,617.73, which puts it below where it started 2026. It was the S&P 500's worst week since April 2025, and the Nasdaq came close to its worst week since March 2020.

Chip stocks took the heaviest losses. Nvidia fell 13.8%, its ninth decline in ten sessions. Micron dropped 13.9%, AMD 13.3% and Broadcom 12.1%, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index lost 10.7%. Anthropic, which listed this week, fell 8.5% to $88.91, still above its $83 IPO price. Fortune reported that the S&P 500 had now fallen for seven sessions in a row, its longest losing run since February 2020, and that the VIX volatility index went above 30 for the first time since March.

Asia had already fallen hard. Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each lost more than 5% on Friday, and Taiwan's index had its steepest weekly fall since October 1997, according to LSEG data cited by Fortune.

## Why Investors Are Dumping AI Stocks

AP, Reuters and Fortune list several pressures:

- **Valuation worries.** AP said investors increasingly doubt that the market values of AI companies and their heavy spending can be justified.
- **US midterm results.** AP noted that voters favoured candidates who want to slow data centre construction.
- **Nvidia leadership.** Reuters, cited by Fortune, listed Jensen Huang's exit as Nvidia CEO among the worries. Our earlier report covers [Huang's resignation and Colette Kress's interim role](/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-colette-kress-interim/).
- **Higher borrowing costs.** Fortune said futures were pricing about a 95% chance of a Federal Reserve rate hike at the December 9 meeting, per CME FedWatch. The 10-year US Treasury yield ended at 4.93%, down from 5.04% a day earlier.
- **Data centre tax threat.** President Trump's warning of a "tremendous tax" on US companies that build data centres abroad added to the strain. We explained that proposal in [what the overseas data centre tax could mean for India](/trump-data-centre-tax-threat-india-ai-hubs/).

BNP Paribas strategist Benedicte Lowe told Fortune that investors were "panic selling". Independent analyst Luke Lango put it differently: "Money is leaving tech, not the market."

India's rally has been led by banks. Nifty Bank rose 5.9% last week to a record high of 64,681, with PSU banks up 6.7%. Nifty IT, the closest Indian link to the global tech trade, lost 1.9% and Infosys fell 3.8%.

## What the Foreign Money Is Buying

FIIs have been net buyers for 14 straight sessions, Upstox reported. NSDL data for November 1 to 15 shows where the money went: financial services drew Rs 42,936 crore, telecom over Rs 7,570 crore, autos Rs 7,551 crore, IT Rs 5,130 crore and FMCG nearly Rs 3,000 crore. IANS described part of the buying as rotation out of crowded AI trades.

Domestic institutions moved the other way, with net sales of Rs 61,786 crore this month, so foreign buyers are absorbing selling by Indian funds. The rupee gained about 1.6% last week and was the best performing emerging market currency, Business Today reported. Brent crude settled at $92.57 a barrel on Friday.

Not every part of the Indian market is rising. The Nifty Smallcap 100 fell 1.09% last week, and October retail inflation of 6.09% is above the RBI's upper tolerance limit, IANS noted. Holders of Indian IT stocks are having a very different month from those holding banks.
