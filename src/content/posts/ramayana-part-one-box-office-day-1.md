Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana: Part One, one of the most awaited Indian films in years, opened in theatres on Thursday, November 5, 2026. Early trade estimates show the film collected about Rs 64.85 crore net in India on its first day across five languages, according to figures from tracker Sacnilk reported by India TV and Outlook. The worldwide gross for the opening has been put at around Rs 127.50 crore. The number is big, but it falls a little short of the highest openings in recent years, and the trade is pointing at one clear reason: the film arrived in the slow days just before Diwali.

## Who is in Ramayana Part One

The film is a large scale retelling of the Ramayana, directed by Nitesh Tiwari, who earlier made Dangal. Ranbir Kapoor plays Lord Rama and Sai Pallavi plays Sita. Kannada star Yash plays Ravana, Sunny Deol plays Hanuman and Ravi Dubey plays Lakshman. The film is produced by Namit Malhotra's Prime Focus Studios along with DNEG and Yash's Monster Mind Creations, as per Outlook. Reports put the budget at a huge Rs 4,000 crore. The second part has been announced for Diwali 2028.

The film released in Hindi, Kannada, Telugu, Tamil and Malayalam, and in 3D as well as regular formats.

## Day 1 collection by language

The Sacnilk estimate, as reported by India TV, shows that the Hindi version brought in most of the money. Here is the language wise split of net collection in India on day 1:

| Language | Day 1 net (approx.) | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Hindi | Rs 47.50 crore | 20,678 |
| Kannada | Rs 7.25 crore | 1,197 |
| Telugu | Rs 6 crore | 1,776 |
| Tamil | Rs 2.60 crore | 992 |
| Malayalam | Rs 1.50 crore | 557 |
| **Total** | **Rs 64.85 crore** | **25,200** |

Pinkvilla's trade estimate is slightly different. It puts the all India net at around Rs 63.50 crore including 3D charges, with about Rs 45 crore from Hindi and around Rs 18.50 crore from the South dubbed versions. Without 3D charges, its estimate drops to about Rs 59.50 crore. Pinkvilla says counting 3D charges in collection figures is fast becoming normal practice, so readers may see both kinds of numbers being quoted.

All of these are trade estimates and not certified figures from the makers, so the final count may change by a small amount.

## How it compares with other big openings

Outlook compared the opening with some of the biggest Indian releases. Ramayana Part One stayed behind a few giants but beat several other major hits.

| Film | Day 1 net (as reported) |
|---|---|
| Dhurandhar 2 | Rs 102 crore |
| Adipurush | Rs 86.75 crore |
| Jawan | Rs 75 crore |
| **Ramayana Part One** | **Rs 64.85 crore** |
| Animal | Rs 63 crore |
| Drishyam 2 | Rs 62 crore |
| Pathaan | Rs 57 crore |

The comparison with Adipurush will be discussed a lot, since both films are based on the same epic. Adipurush had a stronger first day, but that film fell sharply after a poor response. For Ramayana Part One, the trend over the coming days will decide much more than the opening number.

Pinkvilla also noted that a trade projection of around Rs 65 crore for day 1 had been made before release, and the actual figure came in just under it.

## Pre Diwali timing hurt Hindi numbers

According to Pinkvilla, the Hindi collection looks low mainly because of the pre Diwali period, when many families are busy with shopping, cleaning and festival preparations. Since Ramayana is a devotional film made for family audiences, it was hit harder than a typical action film would be. The South and East regions did better because the pre Diwali effect is smaller there.

Karnataka was a big bright spot. Pinkvilla reported that the film earned around Rs 12 crore in the state on day 1, which it called an all time record for a Bollywood film, and it credited Yash's presence for this.

Before release, presales were strong. Sacnilk listed final advance booking at around Rs 42.96 crore gross, not counting blocked seats. Pinkvilla's related reports said worldwide presales crossed Rs 100 crore the day before release, and the film also posted record North American previews for a Bollywood title.

## Overseas and worldwide start

Outlook reported that overseas earnings on the first day were under 5.5 million US dollars, including about 1.5 million dollars from paid previews in North America. Together with the India gross of around Rs 77.50 crore, this takes the worldwide opening to roughly Rs 127.50 crore, according to India TV.

## What to expect over the Diwali weekend

Early audience response appears to be positive, says Pinkvilla. Its expectation for the next few days is:

- **Friday:** a dip, which is normal for a film that opens on Thursday
- **Saturday:** recovery with better numbers
- **Sunday (Diwali, November 8):** another dip, as families celebrate at home
- **Monday:** a strong turnout that will likely set the overall direction of the film

So the real test for Ramayana Part One will come after Diwali, when holiday crowds head to cinemas. If word of mouth stays good, the film could have a long run, much like other big festive releases. Earlier this year, Drishyam 3 showed how strong word of mouth can carry a film, with its Hindi net crossing Rs 379 crore as per Sacnilk.

## A Strong Start With a Bigger Test Ahead

Ramayana Part One has made a solid start at around Rs 64.85 crore net in India, despite releasing in one of the weakest weeks of the festive season. It did not break the biggest records, but its strong showing in Karnataka and a healthy worldwide figure point to wide interest. The days after Diwali will show whether this ambitious two part epic can turn its big opening into a historic run.
