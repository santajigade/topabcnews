Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana: Part One opened in theatres on Thursday, November 5, 2026, and collected Rs 64.85 crore net in India on its first day across five languages. That works out to about Rs 77.50 crore gross in India. With roughly Rs 50 crore from overseas markets, the worldwide opening came to about Rs 127.50 crore. These are estimates from tracker Sacnilk, as reported by <a href="https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/features/insight/ramayana-box-office-day-1-ranbir-kapoor-yash-film-braves-pre-diwali-lull-to-script-records-opens-less-than-adipurush" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Hollywood Reporter India</a> and Outlook.

The film played in 25,200 shows on day 1, and The Week reported that around 25.25 lakh tickets were sold. That is a big start. It still fell short of Adipurush and Dhurandhar 2, and the main reason given by the trade was timing: the film released in the slow days just before Diwali.

## Update (23 November 2026)

The opening turned out to be only the start. By its 18th day, Sunday, November 22, Ramayana Part One had reached:

| Hindi net | Rs 715.75 crore |
| India gross | Rs 1,126.50 crore |
| Overseas gross (last updated after day 17) | Rs 567 crore |
| Worldwide gross | Rs 1,693.50 crore |

These figures are Sacnilk data reported by <a href="https://www.businesstoday.in/trending/box-office/story/ramayana-box-office-ranbir-kapoor-starrer-hits-sunday-surge-takes-india-collections-nears-to-rs1000-crore-568987-2026-11-23" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Today</a> and The Week. The film added Rs 35.60 crore net on that third Sunday alone. Put another way, the opening day now makes up less than 7 percent of the India net total, which shows how much the film gained after the festival. For how it got here, read our reports on the [record opening weekend overseas](/ramayana-opening-weekend-overseas-record/) and the [day 8 crossing of Rs 1,000 crore worldwide](/ramayana-box-office-day-8-1000-crore-worldwide/).

## Day 1 Collection by Language

The Hindi version brought in nearly three quarters of the first day's money. Kannada was the strongest of the dubbed versions, which fits with Yash, one of Kannada cinema's biggest stars, playing Ravana.

| Language | Day 1 net (Rs crore) | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Hindi | 47.50 | 20,678 |
| Kannada | 7.25 | 1,197 |
| Telugu | 6.00 | 1,776 |
| Tamil | 2.60 | 992 |
| Malayalam | 1.50 | 557 |
| **Total** | **64.85** | **25,200** |

The cast is led by Ranbir Kapoor as Lord Rama, Sai Pallavi as Sita, Yash as Ravana, Sunny Deol as Hanuman and Ravie Dubey as Lakshman. Prime Focus Studios, DNEG and Yash's Monster Mind Creations produced the film, and Outlook reported its budget at Rs 4,000 crore. Part Two is set for Diwali 2028.

The Adipurush comparison needs context. That film opened on an ordinary Friday in mid June with no festival competing for attention, and its Telugu states alone gave it more than Rs 37 crore net on day 1, according to THR India. Ramayana's strength on day 1 was in Hindi, not Telugu.

If you count only the Hindi version, the picture changes. The Week listed Ramayana's Rs 47.50 crore Hindi net as the third biggest Bollywood opening, behind Dhurandhar 2 (Rs 99.10 crore) and Drishyam 3 (Rs 62 crore), and ahead of Border 2 (Rs 30 crore) and Mirzapur: The Movie (Rs 25.50 crore). It also became the best pre Diwali Hindi opening, beating Tiger 3 (Rs 43 crore), Krrish 3 (Rs 24 crore) and Housefull 4 (Rs 19.08 crore). THR India added that it is Ranbir Kapoor's biggest opening day and the third biggest of Yash's career, after KGF: Chapter 2 and Toxic.

## Net, Gross and 3D: Reading the Day 1 Numbers

Box office reports in India quote two different figures, and mixing them up is the most common reason readers see "different" collections for the same film.

**Gross** is the full amount buyers paid for tickets, tax included. **Net** is what remains after GST is taken out. After the GST revision announced in September 2025, cinema tickets priced up to Rs 100 carry 5 percent GST and tickets above Rs 100 carry 18 percent, as Business Today reported. Most multiplex tickets for a film like Ramayana cost more than Rs 100, so the higher rate applies to most of its sales.

The day 1 numbers fit this. The gap between Rs 77.50 crore gross and Rs 64.85 crore net is Rs 12.65 crore, about 16 percent of the gross. Dividing the gross by the number of tickets The Week reported gives an average of roughly Rs 307 per ticket before tax.

3D matters because a 3D ticket costs more. Bollywood Hungama's format breakdown for the first three days put 3D shows at 65.62 percent of the film's nett collection. Its estimates show average nett realisation of about Rs 328 per ticket in standard 3D against about Rs 242 in 2D, and about Rs 1,025 in IMAX 3D. So a film that sells most of its seats in 3D will post a larger rupee figure than a 2D film selling the same number of tickets. When you compare openings, check three things: net or gross, all languages or Hindi only, and the format mix.

## Why the Pre Diwali Date Held Back Day 1

THR India said advance bookings were strong, but walk in audiences on the day were thinner, and it put the softer domestic number down to the pre Diwali timing. The later run supports that reading. A film that opened at Rs 64.85 crore went on to cross Rs 940 crore net in India within 18 days.

## How the Overseas Opening Looked

Outlook reported that overseas earnings on day 1 were under 5.5 million US dollars, and that this included about 1.5 million dollars from paid previews in North America. THR India put overseas collections at about Rs 50 crore, roughly 39 percent of the worldwide opening. That share was an early sign of how well the film would travel. Sony Pictures handled its international release.

## What the Opening Day Told Us

Rs 64.85 crore net was not a record for an Indian film. It was a record for its release window, and the heavy 3D share and strong overseas start pointed to a film with wide appeal. Eighteen days later, with India net near Rs 943 crore, the first day reads as a cautious start in a slow week rather than a ceiling.
