D Gukesh begins the defence of his world chess title on Tuesday, November 24, when he sits down against Uzbekistan's Javokhir Sindarov in Geneva. Every game starts at 15:00 Central European Time, which is 7:30 PM in India. The match runs over a maximum of 14 classical games, and if the scores are level after that, the title will be settled by faster tiebreak games on December 12.

Both players are 20, and <a href="https://www.fide.com/salesforce-fide-world-championship-match-2026-schedule-format-and-prize-fund-confirmed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">FIDE's official match announcement</a> bills it as the youngest world championship contest ever held. Sindarov turns 21 on December 8, partway through the match.

With up to 150 minutes on each clock plus increments, a long game can stretch well past 11 PM in India. Games from 9 onwards are marked "if needed" because the match ends as soon as one player is out of reach.

The dates have moved since the venue was announced. <a href="https://www.espn.com/chess/story/_/id/49469254/geneva-host-gukesh-vs-sindarov-world-chess-championship-match" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ESPN's July report on the Geneva venue</a> gave a window of November 25 to December 15. World Chess later reported that FIDE's Council changed the schedule in late August without highlighting it, and FIDE's September notice confirmed the current November 22 to December 12 run.

## Time Controls, Draw Rules and the Tiebreak Ladder

The first player to 7.5 points wins. Each player gets 120 minutes for the first 40 moves and then 30 more minutes for the rest of the game, with 30 seconds added per move from move 41.

Players cannot offer a draw before Black's 40th move. Before that point, a game can end in a draw only by threefold repetition or stalemate, and the Chief Arbiter or Deputy Chief Arbiter has to confirm it.

At 7-7, the title goes to tiebreaks on December 12, in this order:

1. Four rapid games at 15 minutes each plus 10 seconds per move.
2. If still level, two rapid games at 10 minutes plus 5 seconds.
3. If still level, two blitz games at 3 minutes plus 2 seconds.
4. If still level, single blitz games in the same format until one player wins, with colours swapping after each game.

## How the $2.5 Million Prize Fund Is Shared

The prize fund is $2.5 million, the same as in 2024, and FIDE says the figure is net of local taxes. Each classical game won earns the winner $200,000, and the remaining money is split equally between the two players.

There is a floor for the winner. If the match ends 7.5-6.5 with 13 decisive games, or 8-6 with all 14 decisive, the champion takes $1.3 million and the runner-up $1.2 million. The same $1.3 million and $1.2 million split applies if the match goes to tiebreaks.

## Gukesh and Sindarov on Form

Gukesh won the title in Singapore in December 2024, beating China's Ding Liren 7.5-6.5. His 2026 has been difficult. He finished last at Norway Chess and at the Chennai Grand Masters, where he dropped to a rating of 2703. He won individual silver on board four at the Chess Olympiad in Samarkand, but lost his final game there.

Sindarov's form has gone the other way. He won the 2025 FIDE World Cup and then the 2026 Candidates Tournament in Cyprus, finishing unbeaten on 10 points out of 14. He also helped Uzbekistan win Olympiad gold without losing a game. Chess.com's November 10 ratings had him at 2782, fourth in the world, and Gukesh at 2710, ranked 26th. That gap of 72 points makes the challenger the higher rated player.

The sources we checked do not give a classical head-to-head record between the two. At the Olympiad their teams met in round six, but the board order kept them apart.

## What Top Players Are Predicting

Chess.com surveyed leading players in early November, and most of them lean towards the challenger. Magnus Carlsen called Sindarov a "significant favorite", and Hikaru Nakamura expects him to win cleanly. Fabiano Caruana described it as close but also picked Sindarov.

Garry Kasparov gave Gukesh a slight edge on experience, and Viswanathan Anand also backed the Indian. R Praggnanandhaa called it 50:50.

The last world title match in Switzerland was in Brissago in 2004. This one is at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny, on the edge of Geneva, with Salesforce as title sponsor. FIDE has said broadcast details will come through official event channels.
