Indian chess reached a landmark on Sunday, November 8, when 22-year-old International Master Anuj Shrivatri from Madhya Pradesh became the country's 100th Grandmaster. He earned the title by winning the 7th ASEAN Plus Individual Open Chess Championship in Misamis Occidental province in the Philippines. Shrivatri is also the first Grandmaster from Madhya Pradesh, and his success comes 38 years after Viswanathan Anand became India's first GM in 1988.

## How Anuj Shrivatri Won the Title in the Philippines

The tournament was played at the Asenso Global Youth Leadership and Sports Complex in Misamis Occidental. According to a PTI report published by <a href="https://theprint.in/sport/mps-anuj-shrivatri-becomes-indias-100th-grandmaster/3093757/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ThePrint</a>, Shrivatri lost his opening game but then hit back strongly to finish with 7 points out of a possible 8.

That comeback was enough to win the event outright, and the title victory completed his move from International Master to Grandmaster. The same championship has helped Indian players before: in November 2025, Raahul VS became India's 91st Grandmaster by winning the 6th edition of the ASEAN event with a round to spare, as reported by News On AIR.

## What the Grandmaster Title Means

Grandmaster is the top playing title in chess, awarded by FIDE, the world chess body. It sits above International Master, FIDE Master and Candidate Master in the ladder of titles, and it is the goal of almost every serious young player.

Most players climb this ladder one step at a time. They first become International Masters by scoring norms in strong tournaments and crossing a rating of 2400, as Raahul VS did in 2021 before his GM breakthrough. Shrivatri went into the Philippines event as an International Master, and the ASEAN Plus win lifted him to the top rung.

## First Grandmaster From Madhya Pradesh

For Madhya Pradesh, the result is historic. Chess in India has long been led by players from Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, Maharashtra and other traditional strongholds. Shrivatri's title shows that the game's talent base is spreading to states that had never produced a Grandmaster before.

Young players in Bhopal, Indore and other cities of Madhya Pradesh now have a home-grown role model to follow.

## India's Road From 1 to 100 Grandmasters

The journey to 100 has been long at first and very fast in recent years. After Anand's breakthrough in 1988, it took nine years for India to get its second Grandmaster, with Praveen Thipsay earning the title in 1997. By the end of 2011, India had 25 GMs, according to <a href="https://www.espn.com/chess/story/_/id/50420444/india-100-chess-grand-master-gm-anuj-shrivatri" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ESPN</a>.

In 2022, Anand said he hoped India would have 75 Grandmasters by the 75th year of independence. Just four years later, the country has added 25 more. The pace in 2026 alone has been remarkable, with India's 96th to 100th Grandmasters all earning their titles between July and November.

Just before Shrivatri, the most recent names on the list were Ethan Vaz, Harshvardhan GB, S Ashwath and V Rathnavel. Chess official Nitin Narang greeted the milestone on social media with a simple message: "Onwards to 200 Grandmasters!"

## Why the Boom in Indian Chess Is Happening

Anand is often described as the godfather of Indian chess. His own success inspired a generation, and his Westbridge-Anand Chess Academy has helped several young players move up the ranks. The steady flow of new titles also suggests that more Indian players are now getting regular chances to compete in international events where norms and titles can be earned.

Having a reigning world champion from India adds to the excitement, and players like Shrivatri now have clear examples of how far the path can lead. ## Gukesh's World Title Defence Is Next

The 100th GM milestone arrives just ahead of the biggest event on the chess calendar. Gukesh Dommaraju will defend his world title against challenger Javokhir Sindarov in Geneva, Switzerland. According to FIDE, the match runs from November 25 to December 15, 2026, over 14 classical games, with the first player to reach 7.5 points taking the crown and tiebreaks used if the score is level.

For Indian chess, the timing could not be better. A new Grandmaster from a first-time state and a world championship defence within the same month show how deep the country's chess talent now runs. Shrivatri's next goal will be to raise his rating and earn invitations to stronger events, while the rest of the country looks ahead to Geneva.
