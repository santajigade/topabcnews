Judges at the International Criminal Court (ICC) have closed the part of the court's Afghanistan investigation that could have examined the conduct of American troops and intelligence officers. The ruling, issued on Thursday and made public in court filings on Friday, narrows the inquiry to crimes allegedly committed by the Taliban since they took power in 2021. The judges said the prosecution faced serious limits in gathering evidence. The prosecutor's office says it has already appealed.

## What the ICC Judges Decided

According to an Associated Press report on the ruling by the <a href="https://www.icc-cpi.int/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">International Criminal Court in The Hague</a>, the judges ended the branch of the case that could have involved US military personnel. The Taliban part of the investigation will now be split off into a separate inquiry.

The judges gave a brief reason. They pointed to "significant limitations" on the prosecution's ability to investigate and secure evidence, but did not explain further in the public filing. Al Jazeera reported that the judges also closed the line of inquiry into the Islamic State affiliate in Khorasan Province, known as ISKP.

The prosecutor's office told both AP and Reuters that it had already lodged an appeal, so the decision may not be final.

## How the Afghanistan Investigation Began

The case has a long and difficult history. In 2020, ICC judges authorised an investigation into alleged crimes in Afghanistan dating back to 2002. It covered several groups: Afghan government forces, the Taliban, US troops and US foreign intelligence operatives.

In 2021, the court's then chief prosecutor, Karim Khan, said he would "deprioritise" the parts of the case involving American forces. He cited the difficulty of obtaining evidence. Since then, the US branch of the probe had been dormant, according to Al Jazeera. Khan himself was removed from the court earlier this year over sexual misconduct allegations.

## Focus Shifts to the Taliban

With the US branch closed, the inquiry centres on the Taliban's conduct since their return to power in Kabul in 2021. The court has already issued arrest warrants for four senior Taliban leaders, AP reported. They are accused of the crime against humanity of gender persecution against women, girls and LGBTQI+ people in Afghanistan. That part of the work is unaffected by this week's ruling and will continue as its own case.

## The Backdrop of US Pressure on the Court

The ruling comes at a time of intense friction between Washington and the court. The United States is not a member of the ICC and does not recognise its jurisdiction. However, the court's founding treaty allows it to prosecute atrocity crimes committed on the territory of member states.

The Trump administration has placed sanctions on the court and on 13 current and former ICC staff members. The sanctions are linked to the Afghanistan inquiry and to arrest warrants issued against Israeli officials. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his former defence minister face ICC warrants over alleged crimes in Gaza, accusations both men strongly reject.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio has said that "President Trump will never allow" the court to prosecute Americans. In an October statement reported by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/icc-stops-probe-into-alleged-crimes-by-us-troops-in-afghanistan" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>, he also vowed to keep working to dismantle the court "brick by brick" if necessary.

Neither AP nor Al Jazeera described the judges' ruling as a response to US pressure. Both reports noted that it is unclear whether the decision will change Washington's stance towards the court.

### Key highlights

- ICC judges closed the Afghanistan probe branch that could have covered US troops and intelligence officers.
- The ISKP line of inquiry was also closed, Al Jazeera reported.
- The investigation will now focus on Taliban conduct since 2021.
- Judges cited "significant limitations" in gathering evidence.
- The prosecutor's office has appealed the decision.
- The US remains under no ICC jurisdiction as a non-member, and has sanctioned 13 ICC staff.

## What Happens Next in The Hague

The appeal means the court's appeals judges may still review whether the US-related branch should stay closed. In the meantime, the Taliban inquiry will continue as its own case. For human rights groups and victims who had hoped to see every side of the Afghan conflict examined, the ruling is a setback. For Washington, it removes one of the main points of dispute with the court, even as other disputes, including the Israel warrants, remain.
