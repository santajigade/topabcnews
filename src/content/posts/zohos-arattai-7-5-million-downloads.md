<p>India’s own messaging app <strong>Arattai</strong>, built by software giant <a href="https://www.zoho.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Zoho</a>, is making big waves in the digital world. The app has now crossed <strong>7.5 million downloads</strong> across Google Play Store and Apple’s App Store, becoming one of the fastest-growing Indian apps <a href="https://topabcnews.com/technology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">technology </a>of recent times. What makes this story even more interesting is that Arattai is offering features that even global leader <strong>WhatsApp has not introduced yet in 2025</strong>.</p><p>One of the standout features is an <strong>Android TV version of the app</strong>, something WhatsApp users have been waiting for but still don’t have. This alone has given Arattai an edge, helping it climb to the <strong>number one spot on the App Store</strong>.</p>			<h4>What is Arattai?</h4>		<p>The word <strong>“Arattai” means “casual chat” in Tamil</strong>, which perfectly explains the app’s purpose: to make communication simple and easy for everyone.</p><p>Launched by <strong>Zoho Corporation</strong>, one of India’s most respected software companies, Arattai is designed to be <strong>fast, lightweight, and user-friendly</strong>. It is not just another messaging app trying to copy WhatsApp or Telegram. Instead, it is built with a clear vision to serve users in India and beyond who may not always have access to high-speed internet or expensive smartphones.</p><p>According to <strong>Sridhar Vembu</strong>, the founder of Zoho, Arattai is about <strong>inclusivity</strong>. He wanted an app that works even on <strong>basic phones and poor internet connections</strong>, so that no one is left behind in the digital world.</p>			<h4>Key Features That Make this App Special</h4>		<p>This App has many of the standard features you would expect from a messaging app texting, calling, file sharing, group chats, stickers, and more. But what makes it unique are the features that directly solve the challenges faced by many Indian users.</p><h5>1. Low Bandwidth Optimization</h5><p>It is designed to work smoothly even when the internet is weak or unstable. This is a big deal in rural areas and small towns where high-speed internet is not always available.</p><h5>2. Lightweight Design</h5><p>Unlike heavy apps that slow down cheaper phones, It is very light on memory and battery usage. This makes it accessible for people using budget-friendly smartphones.</p><h5>3. Seamless Performance</h5><p>Even though it is lightweight, It doesn’t compromise on speed or quality. Calls connect quickly, messages are delivered fast, and the app is simple to use.</p><h5>4. Android TV Support</h5><p>This is one feature that has caught everyone’s attention. <strong>It has an Android TV version</strong>, meaning you can chat or make calls from your television. WhatsApp still doesn’t offer this feature, giving Arattai a clear advantage.</p>			<h4>The Vision Behind this application</h4>		<p>Zoho’s founder Sridhar Vembu believes technology should not only serve the rich and urban population but also reach those with <strong>limited resources</strong>. He explained that Arattai was created with this goal in mind to <strong>bridge the digital gap</strong> in India and other developing countries.</p><p>In his own words, he reminded his team:</p>					
							“All of you have worked hard for over 5 years without expecting that the product would ever take off. Allow neither praise nor criticism nor fame to distract you—resolutely stay the course.”

				<p>This mindset of patience and persistence is what kept the Arattai team focused for years before the app became a success.</p><p>Vembu also explained that while Arattai may look like a simple app, it has <strong>deep technology running inside</strong>. The app is powered by a messaging and audio-video (AV) framework that Zoho has been perfecting for more than 15 years. This framework ensures <strong>crisp calls, quick connections, strong security, and reliable performance</strong>.</p>			<h4>The Growth Story</h4>		<p>This application growth has been nothing short of impressive. In a short span of time, it crossed <strong>7.5 million downloads</strong>, becoming one of the most downloaded messaging apps in India.</p><p>This growth has been supported by several factors:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Government support:</strong> Union ministers like <strong>Ashwini Vaishnaw, Piyush Goyal, and Dharmendra Pradhan</strong> have openly endorsed Arattai, encouraging citizens to use Indian-made apps.</p></li><li><p><strong>Social media buzz:</strong> Invitations to download Arattai have gone viral, ironically even spreading through WhatsApp groups.</p></li><li><p><strong>Industry endorsements:</strong> Business leaders like <strong>Anand Mahindra</strong> have also shown support. Mahindra tweeted that he downloaded Arattai “with pride” and wished it success.</p></li></ul><p>This combination of government backing, public excitement, and industry trust has helped Arattai quickly gain popularity.</p>			<h4>Why Arattai Matters for India</h4>		<p>India is on a mission to become <strong>digitally self-reliant</strong>. The government has been pushing for the use of <strong>“Swadeshi” (homegrown) software and platforms</strong>, especially in areas like cybersecurity, cloud services, and artificial intelligence.</p><p>Messaging apps are a critical part of daily communication, and until now, India mostly relied on foreign apps like WhatsApp and Telegram. Arattai changes that by giving Indians a strong, reliable, and locally built alternative.</p><p>Experts believe this is not just about one app—it’s about building <strong>trust in Indian technology</strong>.</p><h5>Expert Opinions</h5><ul><li><p><strong>Tarun Pathak</strong>, Research Director at Counterpoint, said the rise of Arattai is not just a short-term trend but part of a larger movement. He explained that India has very few homegrown alternatives in the AI era, and Arattai shows that it is possible to build strong local solutions.</p></li><li><p><strong>Faisal Kawoosa</strong>, Founder of Techarc, noted that leaders promoting Indian apps is a positive step. However, he also highlighted that small businesses (MSMEs) are very price-sensitive. For Arattai to succeed long-term, the government may need to provide <strong>incentives or tax benefits</strong> for using local software.</p></li></ul>			<h4>Challenges Ahead</h4>		<p>While Arattai is growing fast, the journey ahead will not be easy. Global giants like WhatsApp, owned by Meta, have <strong>deep pockets and huge resources</strong>. They can afford to lower their prices or add new features quickly to protect their market share.</p><p>Also, WhatsApp already has a massive user base, which makes it harder for a new app to convince people to switch.</p><p>However, Arattai’s strength lies in its <strong>local focus</strong>. By serving users in rural areas, low-bandwidth regions, and people who prefer lightweight apps, Arattai is targeting a niche that global apps have mostly ignored.</p>			<h4>The Future</h4>		<p>Looking ahead, Arattai is well-placed to grow even further. As more people in India’s <strong>rural and semi-urban areas</strong> come online, the demand for apps that work on low bandwidth will rise. Arattai is ready to fill that gap.</p><p>Zoho also has a strong background in <strong>research and development (R&amp;D)</strong>. Vembu recently shifted full-time into R&amp;D and promised more innovations in the future. This means Arattai is likely to keep improving and offering new features.</p><p>If the momentum continues, Arattai could become not just a WhatsApp rival, but a <strong>symbol of India’s digital independence</strong>.</p>			<h4>Conclusion</h4>		<p>This success is a proud moment for India’s tech industry. With more than 7.5 million downloads, a number one ranking on the App Store, and support from government leaders and industry giants, it has proven that Indian apps can compete with global players.</p><p>By focusing on <strong>lightweight design, low bandwidth use, and unique features like Android TV support</strong>, Arattai has carved its own identity. More importantly, it shows how technology can be made <strong>inclusive and accessible for everyone</strong>, not just those with expensive devices or fast internet.</p><p>As Sridhar Vembu says, the team must keep going without getting distracted. If they do, Arattai may well become the <strong>true Made-in-India alternative to WhatsApp</strong>, changing the way millions of people connect and communicate.</p>
