A federal jury in Brooklyn, New York, found Huawei Technologies guilty on 10 of the 12 counts it faced on Tuesday, November 24, 2026. The verdict came after a three month trial in the Eastern District of New York. The jury convicted the Chinese company of bank fraud, wire fraud, money laundering conspiracy, conspiracy to steal trade secrets and violating US sanctions on Iran. It acquitted Huawei of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice. Sentencing is set for **September 14, 2027**.

Three Huawei affiliates were also on trial: Huawei Device Co. Ltd., Futurewei Technologies Inc. and Huawei Device USA Inc. The case dates back to 2018.

## Count by Count: What the Jury Accepted and Rejected

The reports do not give a full count by count breakdown, but they agree on which charges stuck and which did not.

| Charge | Verdict | What it means in plain terms |
|---|---|---|
| Bank fraud | Guilty | Lying to banks to get money or services from them |
| Wire fraud | Guilty | Using phone, email or electronic transfers to carry out a fraud |
| Money laundering conspiracy | Guilty | Agreeing to move money in a way that hides where it came from or what it was for |
| Conspiracy to steal trade secrets | Guilty | Agreeing to take confidential technology from other companies |
| Iran sanctions (IEEPA, the law behind <a href="https://ofac.treasury.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">US Treasury sanctions</a>) | Guilty | Doing business with Iran in breach of US economic sanctions law |
| Racketeering conspiracy | Not guilty | The claim that Huawei ran an ongoing criminal enterprise over many years |
| Conspiracy to obstruct justice | Not guilty | The claim that Huawei worked to block the US investigation |

Racketeering was the charge that tied everything into one long running scheme. Huawei's defence lawyer, Brian Heberlig, argued in closing that prosecutors had picked out separate incidents to build a 20 year conspiracy story. The jury did not accept that framing, yet it still convicted on the individual fraud, sanctions and trade secret counts.

## The Iran Case Built Around Skycom

The fraud and sanctions counts centre on Skycom, which Fox Business describes as a wholly owned Huawei subsidiary that operated in Iran. The Justice Department says Huawei told HSBC and other banks that Skycom was an independent partner, The Next Web reports. Those false statements, prosecutors said, helped Huawei obtain billions of dollars and keep its access to US dollar clearing.

Fox Business reports that Skycom supplied telecom equipment and surveillance technology in Iran. The Next Web says its work helped Iranian authorities monitor and censor internet users.

The case first became public in December 2018, when Huawei's chief financial officer Meng Wanzhou, daughter of the company's founder, was arrested in Canada on a US warrant. She fought extradition for nearly three years and then entered a deferred prosecution agreement in which she acknowledged that Huawei had illegally done business in Iran. Prosecutors showed that statement to the jury. The US put Huawei on a trade blacklist in 2019.

## Five Companies Named in the Trade Secret Count

Prosecutors named five companies whose technology was targeted: T-Mobile, Cisco, CNEX, Quintel and Fujitsu. The Next Web, citing the Justice Department, gives two examples from the trial. Huawei engineers copied source code for Cisco routers and referred to the company as "ocsic" in their notes. In another episode, Huawei gathered details on a T-Mobile phone testing robot called "Tappy" under a non-disclosure agreement, and an employee walked out of a restricted lab with a piece of it.

Huawei says all five disputes were civil matters settled long ago. "No court ever found Huawei liable for willful intellectual property misappropriation," the company said in its statement.

## How Washington and Beijing Reacted

Attorney General Todd Blanche said "No person or company is above the law" in announcing the verdict. Tysen Duva, the assistant attorney general who leads the Justice Department's Criminal Division, said Huawei had built its global business on fraud, theft and deception over more than two decades.

Huawei welcomed the racketeering acquittal and called the other convictions "equally without legal or factual merit." It said it would review the verdict and take legal action to protect its rights.

In Beijing, Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said on Wednesday that China "firmly opposes" what he called US suppression and containment of Chinese companies, Fox Business reports.

Washington is also preparing tariffs on China, the EU and 14 other economies after an investigation into industrial subsidies, as we explained in our piece on the <a href="/us-section-301-tariffs-india-16-economies/">US Section 301 tariffs on 16 economies</a>.

## What Happens Between Now and September 2027

- **Sentencing, September 14, 2027.** A company cannot be jailed, so the penalties are financial: criminal fines, forfeiture and restitution, according to the Justice Department. No amounts have been announced.
- **Legal challenges.** Huawei has said it will take legal action. Companies convicted in US federal court usually file post trial motions before sentencing and can appeal after judgment.
- **Huawei's wider business.** Huawei has spread into new areas and become a central player in China's artificial intelligence push, Fox Business notes. The Next Web points out that it still signs deals with American firms, including a patent agreement with Qualcomm.
