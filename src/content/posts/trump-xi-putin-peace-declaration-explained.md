US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin issued a joint "peace declaration" on Thursday, November 19, after their first ever three-way meeting, held in Shenzhen on the sidelines of the APEC summit. The talks ran for a little over an hour, according to the <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/3-takeaways-from-trump-xi-and-putins-historic-summit" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Associated Press</a>. The text was released by Chinese state media and the Kremlin, and Reuters reported there was no immediate US statement.

The declaration has four points. It does not mention the war in Ukraine, the US-led war with Iran or Taiwan.

## The Four Points, One by One

Outlets reported the wording slightly differently. Here is each point as reported, and what it leaves open.

| Point | What the declaration says | What it does not say |
|---|---|---|
| Oil | The three will raise production and supply of refined petroleum products for countries around the world (Xinhua, via Outlook India) | No volumes, timelines or mechanism |
| Nuclear risk | A nuclear war "cannot be won and must never be fought"; talks on reducing nuclear risk and stopping proliferation | No date or venue for those talks |
| UN reform | As permanent Security Council members, the three commit to reforming the UN | AP notes it gives no detail on the type or extent of reform |
| ICC and immunity | State officials should have immunity; the ICC and similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction | No named cases or proposed changes |

### Why the ICC line stands out

Putin faces an International Criminal Court arrest warrant over the war in Ukraine and has skipped recent G20 summits because of it, Al Jazeera noted. The United States is not an ICC member. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk responded online: "God, protect us from such friends."

## What Each Leader Said

Trump opened by telling the room, "We're making history," and pointed out that the three countries hold the world's three biggest nuclear arsenals. He called Putin "my friend."

Xi, as host, said, as reported by Al Jazeera, that nuclear war "cannot be won and must not be fought," and urged the others to drop a "Cold War mentality." Xinhua quoted him saying there is hope for peace as long as dialogue continues.

Putin called the talks "very frank" and, according to AP, told Xi he did not think anyone expected things to go so well. Separately, Outlook India reported that he said there would be no negotiations after this week's Ukrainian strikes on the Crimean Bridge, and that Moscow would respond. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Ukraine was not the main topic of the three-way talks.

## The Gaps: Ukraine, Iran and Taiwan

**Ukraine.** Trump asked Xi to help end the war, and later dialled into a Berlin meeting where Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and more than 40 European leaders were gathered. US envoy Steve Witkoff said Trump told them he had discussed a Ukraine peace with Xi and Putin. None of that appears in the declaration.

**Iran.** The US attacked Iran in February, and Tehran then closed the Strait of Hormuz, which used to carry about a fifth of the world's oil supply, Al Jazeera reported. AP said there was no sign Trump won help from Xi or Putin on the conflict, though Trump and Xi agree Iran must fully reopen the strait and must not get a nuclear weapon. Our earlier report on <a href="/iran-attacks-kuwait-bahrain-us-bases-gulf/">Iran's strikes on US bases</a> covers the latest fighting.

**Taiwan.** AP reported that Taiwan was not addressed in the agreement.

## What It Means for India

None of the post-summit reports we read mention India or quote an Indian official. Two points still bear on New Delhi.

The first is oil. The pledge to raise refined petroleum supply comes with no volumes, and Hormuz is still restricted, so there is nothing in the text yet that would move fuel prices in India.

The second is the balance of power in Asia. In a preview published before the meeting, Outlook India wrote that a Trump-Xi understanding "could complicate India's position," citing the unresolved India-China border dispute and Beijing's reach in Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka, the Maldives and Myanmar. It also noted that New Delhi faces pressure over its defence and energy ties with Russia. The declaration does not touch any of these. Our report on the <a href="/quad-manila-summit-2026-vs-2024-india/">Quad summit in Manila</a> covers the other side of that picture.

## What Comes Next

Trump hosts the G20 in Miami next month. Al Jazeera reported that Xi is almost certain to attend and that Trump has invited Putin, but Putin has not committed, according to Reuters. No date has been set for the promised nuclear risk talks.
