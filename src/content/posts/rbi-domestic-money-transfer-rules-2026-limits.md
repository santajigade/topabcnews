Someone without a bank account can walk into a bank branch, hand over cash and have up to Rs 50,000 sent by NEFT to another person's account in one transaction. That is one of the limits in the Reserve Bank of India (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, which the RBI issued on November 11, 2026 and which took effect the same day. The directions bring the RBI's rules on small cash-based transfers into one document, including transfers by people without a bank account, such as migrant workers.

| Channel | Limit per transaction | Monthly cap |
|---|---|---|
| Cash deposit at a bank branch, sent by NEFT to a bank account | Rs 50,000 | None stated |
| Cash deposit through a business correspondent (BC), ATM or similar channel | Rs 5,000 | Rs 25,000 per remitter |
| Money sent from a bank account and collected as cash by a person with no account | Rs 10,000 | Rs 25,000 per beneficiary |

The directions (reference RBI/DPSS/2026-27/476) apply to scheduled commercial banks, regional rural banks, urban, state and district central cooperative banks, and authorised payment system operators. They were issued under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007.

## The Three Ways to Send or Collect Cash

### Walk-in cash at a bank branch

If you have cash but no account, a bank branch can accept it and send it by NEFT to the receiver's bank account. The ceiling is Rs 50,000 per transaction. The RBI text gives no monthly limit for this route, but the tax rules on cash deposits still apply.

### Cash through a BC agent or ATM

Business correspondents are the agents who run banking points in shops and villages on behalf of banks. Cash paid in through a BC, an ATM or a similar channel is capped at Rs 5,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month for each person sending money.

### Cash pay-out to someone without an account

Money can also go the other way, from a bank account to a person who has no account and collects it in cash at an ATM, through a BC or another authorised channel. That is limited to Rs 10,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month for each receiver. So if a family member has already collected Rs 20,000 in a month, only Rs 5,000 more can be paid out to them that month. The sending bank must keep a record of the receiver's name and address.

## What You Need Before Sending Money

Anyone sending money this way needs to meet four conditions:

1. Register as a remitter with a verified mobile number.
2. Give a self-certified Officially Valid Document (OVD), as defined in the RBI's 2025 Know Your Customer directions. The money transfer directions do not list the documents; the KYC rules do.
3. Confirm every transaction with an Additional Factor of Authentication (AFA). The RBI does not say which method banks must use.
4. Expect your details to travel with the money: banks must put the sender's details, and a tag marking the transfer as cash-based, into the NEFT or IMPS message.

Banks must also follow the cash deposit provisions of the Income-Tax Act, 2025 and its rules.

## How the Limits Compare With the 2011 Rules

The new directions repeal two earlier circulars: the "Domestic Money Transfer: Relaxations" circular of October 5, 2011, and the "Review of Framework" circular of July 24, 2024. Reporting from <a href="https://www.business-standard.com/article/finance/rbi-increases-domestic-money-transfer-limits-111100600080_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Standard in October 2011</a> shows what the older rules looked like.

| Rule | 2011 framework (as reported) | 2026 directions |
|---|---|---|
| Cash pay-out per transaction | Raised from Rs 5,000 to Rs 10,000 | Rs 10,000 |
| Cash pay-out monthly cap | Rs 25,000 per beneficiary | Rs 25,000 per beneficiary |
| Walk-in cash transfers | Rs 5,000 per transaction to family members' accounts, up to Rs 25,000 per remitter | Rs 50,000 per NEFT transaction at a branch; Rs 5,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month via BC or ATM |
| Card-to-card transfers | Allowed within the same limits | Excluded; covered by the approval for each card product |

The cash pay-out limits are the same as in 2011. The branch NEFT figure of Rs 50,000 does not appear in the 2011 report, and none of the sources we read says when it was first introduced, so it is not clear whether it is new. The 2011 condition that walk-in transfers go to family members does not appear in the 2026 text. The July 2024 circular, as Business Standard reported it then, tightened KYC and brought in the AFA and mobile registration rules, which the new directions carry forward.

## Charges, Speed and Complaints

The RBI has not fixed fees. Banks and non-banks "may fix reasonable charges" to encourage use of the service, so the cost can differ between providers.

Transfers should go through in real time or near real time, and settlement between banks has to use RBI-approved payment systems. Banks must run velocity checks and send alerts on credits, look into sudden spikes in money coming in, and report suspicious transactions to the authorities. For a separate set of RBI payment rules, see our report on its <a href="/rbi-cyber-rules-payment-apps-six-hour-reporting/">cyber incident reporting rules for payment apps</a>.

If something goes wrong, complaints fall under the Reserve Bank Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026. The full text is on the <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13752&Mode=0" target="_blank" rel="noopener">RBI notifications page</a>.

## Domestic Money Transfer: Common Questions

**Can I send money by NEFT without a bank account?**
Yes. At a bank branch you can pay cash and have up to Rs 50,000 per transaction sent by NEFT to the receiver's bank account, after registering with a verified mobile number and a self-certified OVD.

**What is the limit for depositing cash through a BC agent?**
Rs 5,000 per transaction, with a cap of Rs 25,000 a month for each person sending money. The same limits apply to ATMs and similar channels.

**How much cash can a person without a bank account receive?**
Up to Rs 10,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month in total for each receiver.

**Do the new rules cover card-to-card transfers?**
No. Card-to-card transfers are excluded and remain governed by the approvals for each card product.
