McLaren's Lando Norris will start the 2026 Sao Paulo Grand Prix from pole position after a stormy qualifying session at Interlagos on Saturday, November 7. Norris set a time of 1:27.470 on a wet track to beat Mercedes driver George Russell, but a string of grid penalties has reshuffled the order, putting newly crowned world champion Kimi Antonelli alongside him on the front row. The race starts on Sunday at 14:00 local time, which is 10:30 PM IST.

## How Rain Turned Interlagos Qualifying Upside Down

Heavy rain fell on the Interlagos circuit before and during the session, and the red flag came out within about three minutes of Q1. Antonelli and Red Bull's Isack Hadjar complained that their race suits were soaked after brief trips off the track. After a long delay, running resumed, and the session became a test of nerve.

Several drivers struggled. Gabriel Bortoleto, racing at his home event, hit the wall and finished last on times. Franco Colapinto and Pierre Gasly crossed the line together and were knocked out in 17th and 18th. In Q2, the cars switched to intermediate tyres, and a crash by Haas rookie Camara with less than a minute left caused a second red flag. Oscar Piastri and Carlos Sainz were among the drivers who missed the top ten shootout.

Q3 began under dark skies with heavier rain forecast, which put a premium on getting laps in early. Norris did exactly that and took his third pole in a row at Interlagos. "It was about getting two laps in at the beginning and not making any mistakes," he said, according to the <a href="https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/norris-beats-russell-to-pole-position-in-dramatic-rain-affected-sao-paolo-qualifying.2V60rhlQjdS5my1uFrOKUH" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official Formula 1 qualifying report</a>.

## Starting Grid After Penalties

Penalties have changed the front of the grid in a big way. Russell drops five places, Ferrari's Charles Leclerc takes a 10-place drop for a new engine, and Lewis Hamilton and Max Verstappen both start from the back after power unit changes.

## What Is at Stake in Brazil

The drivers' title is already settled. Antonelli clinched the 2026 championship at the Mexico City Grand Prix by finishing third, and at 20 he became the youngest world champion in Formula 1 history, breaking Sebastian Vettel's record of 23 years and 134 days set in 2010, as <a href="https://www.espn.com/f1/story/_/id/50250037/kimi-antonelli-youngest-f1-world-champion-2026-mexico-city-grand-prix" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ESPN reported</a>. His first win came in China in March, the start of a run of five straight victories.

There is still plenty to race for. Mercedes have a chance to wrap up the constructors' championship, and Ferrari's penalties make that task easier. Further down, Haas and Audi are fighting for seventh in the constructors' table, so Bearman starting just behind Hulkenberg sets up a direct battle. Lawson, who starts third, is also fighting to keep his seat for next season, so a podium would be a major boost for him.

Antonelli sounded confident about his chances. "Whether it is wet or dry, I think we have a good chance," he said after qualifying. Russell, meanwhile, admitted the team had "collectively made a misjudgement at the end" of the session.

## Weather and Strategy for Sunday

Rain could again play a big role. Tyre supplier Pirelli put the chance of rain on race day at about 60%, and said a two-stop plan would be the preferred strategy if the track stays dry. A wet race would make Hamilton and Verstappen's climb from the back both harder and potentially more rewarding, since changing conditions often mix up the order.
