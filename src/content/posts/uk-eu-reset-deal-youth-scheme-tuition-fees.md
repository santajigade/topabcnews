The United Kingdom and the European Union closed a package of agreements at a summit in Brussels on Friday, November 20, 2026. It covers a youth scheme for 100,000 young people in each direction, a food and farm trade deal and a link between their carbon markets. "The deal is done. Negotiations have concluded," a UK government spokesperson said, according to <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/20/eu-and-uk-seal-reset-deal" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera and Reuters</a>. What EU students will pay at British universities is still open, and the EU has tied the start of the package to it.

| Item | What was agreed | Expected timing |
|---|---|---|
| Youth scheme | 100,000 places each way, stays of up to 3 years, ages 18 to 32 | Running by January 2030 at the latest |
| Food and farm trade (SPS) | Fewer checks on animal, plant and food goods | After the tuition fee issue is settled |
| Carbon markets (ETS) | UK and EU systems relinked | UK firms' exemption from EU carbon border tax expected in 2028 |
| Tuition fees | Not finalised | Within about 6 months |

## What Burnham and Brussels Signed Up To

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham met European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President <a href="/eu-e5-team-ukraine-peace-talks-costa/">Antonio Costa</a> at the summit. Euronews describes it as only the second bilateral EU-UK summit since Brexit. Burnham opened the talks by calling it "a good day for Britain."

The UK government says the agreements could add 0.5 percent to Britain's economy. Of that, the sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) arrangement, which reduces border controls on animals, plants and related products, is estimated to be worth 5.1 billion pounds (about $6.7 billion). Burnham said on Thursday there was "the possibility of lower food prices," the Irish Times reported, citing the Financial Times.

Under the food deal, Britain will follow EU agri-food standards once it takes effect. British officials will be able to advise on those rules but will not vote on them.

## The Youth Scheme: Who Can Go and for How Long

The scheme lets 100,000 young people from the EU live in the UK, and 100,000 from the UK live in the EU, for up to three years. The Irish Times puts the age band at 18 to 32. Britain's opening offer was 50,000 places. Euronews reports the scheme is expected to be up and running by January 2030 at the latest.

Britain asked to include professional exchanges for young lawyers and accountants, and the EU turned that down, according to the Irish Times.

This scheme is separate from Erasmus+, the EU student exchange programme. Britain had already agreed in December 2025 to rejoin Erasmus+, with participation starting on January 1, 2027. At the time, the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/young-people-from-all-backgrounds-to-get-opportunity-to-study-abroad-as-uk-eu-deal-unlocks-erasmus" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UK government said</a> it would pay about 570 million pounds for the 2027/28 academic year, a 30 percent discount on the default terms.

## Why Tuition Fees Could Delay Everything

Since Brexit, EU students in Britain pay international fees rather than the lower home rate. <a href="https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/20/young-europeans-win-a-route-back-to-the-uk-but-not-cheaper-university-fees-yet" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Euronews</a>, citing Oxford's Migration Observatory, says EU enrolment at UK universities fell 57 percent between 2020/21 and 2023/24.

The two reports read the fee outcome differently. Euronews says the sides failed to settle fees and committed to reaching a deal within six months, and that it is unclear whether EU member states will make final approval depend on it. The Irish Times, citing two EU diplomats, says the EU dropped its demand that EU students pay the same as British students. Brussels would accept a discount that brings fees below current levels while still covering course costs. In that account, the EU will not put the agreements into effect until the UK introduces the fee cut, which is expected within six months.

Either way, the EU will not implement the agreements until the fee question is resolved.

## The Carbon Link and Why Industry Is Unhappy

Relinking the two emissions trading systems, which were split at Brexit, is meant to exempt goods such as steel from each side's border carbon levies. British businesses are not expected to get that exemption from the EU's carbon border tax until 2028, the Irish Times reported. The UK did not secure an earlier exemption based on a political commitment to relink.

UK Steel and Energy UK both voiced concern, the paper said, warning of higher taxes on exports, higher electricity costs and the risk of cheap imports being dumped in Britain during the gap.

## What the Deal Leaves Out

Some issues were left out or are unfinished:

- **University fees:** to be settled within about six months, as above.
- **Made in Europe:** the EU wanted British access linked to matching EU measures against China, including tariffs on electric cars. The communique settles for joint action to protect supply chains.
- **Migration:** both sides promised closer cooperation on irregular migration. Burnham mentioned a "Channel action plan."
- **Say on new EU laws:** France had pushed to limit British input. It softened its stance, and the UK is expected to get rights to be consulted.
- **Rejoining the EU:** Burnham has described it as one possible outcome, but Al Jazeera says it is far from imminent.

Every part of the package still needs legal approval on both sides, and Downing Street says the timelines will be published in a joint statement. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage called Burnham "naive" and said he had been strong-armed by Brussels.

For readers outside Europe, including in India, none of the reports describes any change to UK or EU visa rules for non-EU nationals. The youth places are reserved for young people from the EU and the UK.

## Dates to Watch

First comes the joint statement with the agreed timelines. After that: a tuition fee arrangement within roughly six months, the UK's return to Erasmus+ on January 1, 2027, the carbon border exemption expected in 2028, and the youth scheme opening by January 2030.
