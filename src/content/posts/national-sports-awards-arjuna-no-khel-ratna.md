President Droupadi Murmu handed out the National Sports Awards 2025 at Rashtrapati Bhavan in New Delhi on Friday, November 13, 2026. Seventeen sportspersons received the Arjuna Award, five coaches were given the Dronacharya Award, and one veteran footballer was honoured for lifetime achievement. But for the first time since 2014, nobody received the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna, India's highest sporting honour. The ceremony, which lasted a little over 36 minutes, closed a long and unusual awards cycle marked by a delay and a re-check of the selection panel's recommendations.

## A Short Ceremony After a Long Wait

Union Sports Minister Mansukh Mandaviya and Sports Secretary Hari Ranjan Rao were present as the President gave away the honours. The list itself had been made public by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports on August 18, 2026, as reported by <a href="https://newsonair.gov.in/government-announced-national-sports-awards-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">All India Radio News</a>.

The awards were traditionally presented on August 29, National Sports Day, which marks the birth anniversary of hockey great Major Dhyan Chand. That date has not been followed strictly since 2020, and this year the ceremony slipped to mid November.

## Why There Was No Khel Ratna This Year

None of the reports on the ceremony, including the account by public broadcaster <a href="https://ddindia.co.in/2026/11/president-murmu-gives-away-national-sports-awards-at-rashtrapati-bhavan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DD India</a>, give an official reason for leaving the Khel Ratna empty. What is known is how the process unfolded. A selection panel headed by former Supreme Court judge Arun Kumar Mishra sent its recommendations late last year. The ministry then carried out what reports described as an unprecedented re-evaluation of those names, saying it wanted to "preserve the integrity of the awards." The committee included IOA vice-president Gagan Narang, former badminton star Aparna Popat and hockey Olympian M M Somaya, among others.

The gap is striking when compared with the previous edition, when four athletes shared the Khel Ratna: chess world champion D Gukesh, hockey captain Harmanpreet Singh, para high jumper Praveen Kumar and shooter Manu Bhaker.

### Chess and Badminton Pairs Stand Out

Two sports placed two names each in a way that drew attention. In chess, Grandmaster Vidit Gujrathi was honoured along with Divya Deshmukh, who won the FIDE Women's World Cup in 2025. All India Chess Federation president Nitin Narang said their performances "made the nation proud." In badminton, the women's doubles pair of Treesa Jolly and Gayatri Gopichand were recognised together.

## Coaches Honoured With Dronacharya Awards

Three coaches received the regular Dronacharya Award: Parveer Singh (athletics), Chhote Lal Yadav (boxing) and Neha Chavan (shooting). Chhote Lal Yadav has worked with the women's national boxing camp and was part of Mary Kom's support staff.

Two more were given the lifetime Dronacharya honour: Dharmendra Yadav, coach of the men's national boxing squad, and wrestling coach Virender Kumar. The Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puraskar, which recognises organisations that promote sport, went to the Army Paralympic Node in Pune.

## Key Highlights

- Ceremony held at Rashtrapati Bhavan on November 13, 2026
- No Khel Ratna for the first time since 2014
- 17 Arjuna Awards, each worth Rs 15 lakh
- Only two para-sport names on the Arjuna list, compared with 17 para athletes in the record list of 32 in 2024
- No cricketer on the list; the last cricketer to receive the Arjuna was Mohammed Shami in 2023
- Five Dronacharya Awards in all, including two for lifetime achievement

## Athletes Questioned the Delay

The long wait did not go down well with some of the winners. In April, decathlete Tejaswin Shankar, who eventually made the Arjuna list, wrote on X that the delay was "not just demotivating to athletes and coaches but also a sign of disrespect." His main point, according to an IANS report, was that any review should have happened before the panel's recommendations became public in December, not months later.

The awards also come during a busy period for Indian sports administration. Our recent report on <a href="/pt-usha-loses-ioa-som-election/">the IOA merit poll in which Saina, Anju, Rani and Dola won seats</a> covers another big change in how Indian sport is run.

## What the Smaller List Signals

A thinner Arjuna list and an empty Khel Ratna slot point to a stricter approach by the ministry this cycle. Whether that becomes the new normal will be clearer when nominations open for the next edition. For now, the 17 athletes and five coaches honoured on Friday have their recognition, even if it arrived later than many of them had hoped.
