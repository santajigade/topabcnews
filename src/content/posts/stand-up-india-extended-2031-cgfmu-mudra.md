The Union Cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Wednesday, November 11, extended the Stand-Up India scheme for five more years, up to March 31, 2031. The scheme gives bank loans of above Rs 10 lakh and up to Rs 1 crore to women and Scheduled Caste (SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) entrepreneurs who want to start a new business. At the same meeting, the Cabinet also continued the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU), which backs small loans given under the Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana. Together, the two decisions aim to keep bank credit flowing to first-time and small business owners.

## What the Stand-Up India Extension Includes

According to <a href="https://www.business-standard.com/amp/economy/news/govt-extends-stand-up-india-scheme-till-2031-approves-4-111-cr-corpus-126111101435_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Standard</a>, the government has approved a credit guarantee corpus of Rs 4,111 crore for the next phase. This fund protects banks if a borrower fails to repay, which makes lenders more willing to give loans to people without a long credit history.

Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said the target is to support 2.5 lakh more entrepreneurs over the five years. Since the scheme began in April 2016, loans have been given to more than 2.75 lakh women, SC and ST entrepreneurs, as per the government.

## Who Can Apply and What the Loan Covers

The scheme is meant only for greenfield enterprises, which means a business being set up for the first time. Here are the main terms as shared by the government:

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Who can borrow | Women, SC and ST entrepreneurs |
| Loan size | Above Rs 10 lakh, up to Rs 1 crore |
| Type of loan | Composite loan (term loan plus working capital) or term loan only |
| Share of project cost | Up to 85% |
| Sectors | Manufacturing, services, trading and agri-allied activities |
| Lenders | Scheduled commercial banks |
| Repayment period | 7 years, with a moratorium of up to 18 months |
| Credit guarantee | 85% of the loan amount |
| Guarantee fee | 0.50% (standard) |

Each bank branch is expected to lend to at least one SC or ST borrower and at least one woman borrower. The interest rate is meant to be the lowest rate the bank applies for that category, capped at the base rate (MCLR) plus 3% plus a tenor premium.

### Why the Guarantee Matters

Many new entrepreneurs from these groups struggle to offer property or other collateral. With 85% of the loan amount guaranteed, the bank carries much less risk, so the borrower has a better chance of getting the loan approved.

## CGFMU: Lower Fee for Mudra Loans

The second decision is about Mudra loans, which are smaller business loans of up to Rs 20 lakh. As per the Cabinet note on the <a href="https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-continuation-of-credit-guarantee-fund-for-micro-units/?comment=disable" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PM India website</a>, the CGFMU will continue for five years up to March 31, 2031, covering loans in the 16th Finance Commission cycle from 2026-27 to 2030-31.

Key points of the CGFMU decision:

- A corpus of Rs 5,000 crore has been approved.
- It is expected to support about Rs 12.6 lakh crore of Mudra loans over the next five years.
- The guarantee fee on Mudra loans has been cut from 1% to 0.75% a year.
- The fee for Self Help Group (SHG) loans stays the same: 0.25% in the first year and 0.50% after that.
- The guarantee is given through National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company Limited (NCGTC).
- Besides Mudra loans, it covers overdrafts of up to Rs 10,000 in Jan Dhan accounts and SHG loans where the portfolio is between Rs 10 lakh and Rs 20 lakh.

The CGFMU was launched in January 2016. By September 2026, lending institutions had sanctioned more than 60.36 crore loans worth about Rs 43.34 lakh crore under the Mudra scheme since its launch.

## How the Two Schemes Fit Together

Mudra loans cover micro enterprises with loans of up to Rs 20 lakh. Stand-Up India picks up where the bigger ticket sizes begin, with loans of up to Rs 1 crore for new ventures run by women and SC and ST entrepreneurs. A lower guarantee fee on Mudra loans could also make it slightly cheaper for banks to extend such credit.

Anyone planning to apply should contact a scheduled commercial bank branch, which will check the project plan and eligibility. ## What Comes Next for Borrowers

With both schemes now running till March 2031, new borrowers have a clear five-year window. The bigger test will be whether banks reach the target of 2.5 lakh new Stand-Up India entrepreneurs, given that about 2.75 lakh were covered in the first ten years.
