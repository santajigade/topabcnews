India's cleanest cities will be named this week. President Droupadi Murmu will present the Swachh Survekshan 2025-26 awards on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, at Vigyan Bhawan in New Delhi, the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) said in an official statement on Monday, November 9. This is the 10th edition of the annual cleanliness survey, which is the main ranking tool under the Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban and now covers more than 4,751 cities and towns across the country.

## When and Where the Swachh Survekshan Awards Will Be Given

The ceremony is being organised by the Department of Urban Development under MoHUA. Union Minister of Housing and Urban Affairs and Power Manohar Lal and Minister of State Tokhan Sahu will attend. The <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/allrelease.aspx?reg=3&lang=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Press Information Bureau release</a> announcing the event was issued on November 9.

The theme of this year's edition is "Swachhata Ki Nayi Pehel - Badhayein Haath, Karein Safai Saath", which loosely means a new step for cleanliness where everyone joins hands to keep surroundings clean.

Awards will be given across several categories. The official statement did not list the categories or name any winners in advance. Final rankings will be revealed at the ceremony.

## How Cities Were Assessed This Year

Swachh Survekshan began in 2016 with just 73 urban local bodies (ULBs). Ten editions later, it covers more than 4,751 cities, a sign of how far the exercise has grown.

For 2025-26, ULBs were judged on 43 indicators grouped under 10 assessment areas:

| No. | Assessment area |
|---|---|
| 1 | Segregation of waste |
| 2 | Collection and transportation |
| 3 | Solid waste management |
| 4 | Visible cleanliness |
| 5 | Access to sanitation |
| 6 | Mechanisation of desludging |
| 7 | Ecosystem and institutional parameters |
| 8 | Used water management |
| 9 | Advocacy for cleanliness and welfare of sanitation workers |
| 10 | Citizen feedback and grievance redressal |

More than 3,000 trained assessors visited cities for ground checks, supported by real-time GPS-enabled monitoring. MoHUA said the entire process, from evidence submission by cities to verification, was digital, with quality checks at every stage.

## What Is New in Swachh Survekshan 2025-26

The ministry has added several changes compared with earlier editions:

- **More weight on citizen feedback**, so residents' experience counts for more in the final score
- **Wider coverage of water bodies**, including urban river fronts, coastlines and other water bodies
- **New indicators on open defecation and open urination**, to check whether toilet access is actually being used
- **Cleanliness of tourist and heritage hotspots** in cities
- **Behaviour change work in schools**, aimed at building cleanliness habits early

For residents, the citizen feedback change is the most direct. It means local complaints about missed garbage pickups or dirty public toilets can affect how a city is ranked, which gives municipal bodies a stronger reason to respond.

## Swachh Shehar Jodi: Big Cities Mentoring Smaller Ones

This year's awards will also recognise the best performers under Swachh Shehar Jodi, a mentorship programme launched by Manohar Lal on September 27, 2025. Described as the first urban waste management peer-learning programme of its kind, it pairs 70 mentor cities with 191 mentee cities across 33 states and union territories. All mentor and mentee cities signed a memorandum of understanding at the launch.

According to figures shared by the ministry, mentee cities improved on several measures between Swachh Survekshan 2024 and the 2025-26 edition:

| Indicator | SS 2024 | SS 2025-26 |
|---|---|---|
| Visible cleanliness | 48% | 70% |
| Door-to-door collection | 52% | 74% |
| Source segregation | 16% | 30% |
| Access to sanitation | 11% | 38% |
| Used water management | 32% | 53% |
| Advocacy for cleanliness | 48% | 66% |

The biggest jump was in access to sanitation, which more than tripled. Source segregation, where households separate wet and dry waste, nearly doubled but still remains low at 30%, showing how much work is left.

## Early Signals Ahead of the Results

While the ministry has not named winners, some cities have received invitations. <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/karnal-among-indias-top-3-cleanest-cities-selected-for-presidents-award" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tribune reported</a> that Karnal in Haryana has been invited to receive a President's Award after being placed among the country's top three cleanest cities. Karnal had also received a President's Award last year for a third place finish. The city's journey shows how rankings can change: it was ranked 115th in 2023 before climbing in the two editions since.

Karnal's Municipal Commissioner Saloni Sharma called the selection a historic moment for the city, according to the report.

## Why the Rankings Matter for Indian Cities

Swachh Survekshan has turned cleanliness into a competition between cities, and a good rank brings national recognition for local bodies and their sanitation workers. The survey also measures things residents feel every day, such as garbage collection, toilets and drain cleaning. Cleaner cities also matter for public health in a season when many urban areas battle poor air, as seen in <a href="/delhi-aqi-severe-diwali-health-tips/">Delhi's air quality ahead of Diwali</a>. Results on November 11 will show which cities have made the most progress.
