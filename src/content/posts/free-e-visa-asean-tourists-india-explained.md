Prime Minister Narendra Modi has proposed free electronic visas for tourists from all 11 ASEAN countries, one of nine ideas he put to Southeast Asian leaders at the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit in Manila on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. The plan, announced through a Ministry of External Affairs statement, is meant to deepen people-to-people ties ahead of the 35th anniversary of ASEAN-India relations in 2027. No start date or rules have been released yet, so here is what the offer would change, using India's own published fee schedule.

## A Fee Waiver, Not Visa-Free Entry

The proposal is easy to misread. It does not mean ASEAN citizens could fly to India without a visa. Travellers would still have to apply online through the official <a href="https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Indian e-Visa portal</a>, upload documents and wait for approval. What would go away is the fee.

Gulf News noted that some reports described the move as a firm announcement and others as a proposal. The MEA framed it as one of several proposals to ASEAN leaders, and so far there is no notification from the government with eligibility rules, the types of e-visa covered or a date when it would take effect.

## What ASEAN Tourists Pay for an Indian E-Visa Today

India's official <a href="https://www.indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa/images/Etourist_fee_final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">e-Tourist visa fee schedule</a> sets prices by nationality and by season. The 30-day visa is cheaper if you apply in the off season of April to June. A bank charge of 2.5% is added on top of every fee. This is what the schedule lists for the 11 ASEAN members (amounts in US dollars):

| Country | 30 days (Apr to Jun) | 30 days (Jul to Mar) | 1 year | 5 years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brunei | 10 | 25 | 40 | 80 |
| Cambodia | 10 | 25 | 40 | 80 |
| Indonesia | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Laos | 10 | 25 | 40 | 80 |
| Malaysia | 0 | 0 | 40 | 80 |
| Myanmar | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Philippines | 10 | 25 | 40 | 80 |
| Singapore | 10 | 25 | 25 | 25 |
| Thailand | 0 | 0 | 40 | 80 |
| Timor-Leste | 10 | 25 | 40 | 80 |
| Vietnam | 10 | 25 | 40 | 80 |

Two things stand out. First, several ASEAN nations already pay nothing for at least the short visa: Indonesia and Myanmar for all durations, and Malaysia and Thailand for the 30-day visa. Thai nationals, for example, get a free 30-day double-entry e-Tourist visa valid until December 31, 2027, or until further orders, according to Gulf News. Second, the schedule may not reflect every recent waiver. India announced free e-visas for Filipino tourists in August 2025, after the Philippines allowed Indians visa-free entry, yet the published table still shows fees for the Philippines. Travellers should always check the final amount on the portal before paying.

So in practice, the new offer would matter most for tourists from Brunei, Cambodia, Laos, Singapore, Timor-Leste and Vietnam, who still pay up to $25 for a 30-day visa in peak season and $40 to $80 for longer visas. For a family of four from Vietnam visiting in December, that is $100 plus bank charges saved on 30-day visas alone.

## The Eight Other Proposals Modi Put Forward

The free e-visa was one part of a nine-point package. The other eight, as listed in the MEA statement reported by PTI, are:

1. **A long-term ASEAN-India Maritime Cooperation Plan** covering connectivity, research, disaster management, Coast Guard cooperation, maritime domain awareness and the safety of seafarers.
2. **Joint cyber forensics training** for cybersecurity professionals, including awareness of online scams and cyber fraud.
3. **Technical exchanges in nuclear energy.**
4. **An ASEAN-India Space Hackathon** focused on agriculture, resource management and youth innovation in space technology.
5. **Cooperation in mining and critical minerals.**
6. **ASEAN-India Youth Connect**, with a youth summit, exchanges of young parliamentarians and start-up hackathons.
7. **More scholarships for ASEAN students at Nalanda University.**
8. **An ASEAN-India Creative Partnership Pavilion** at the WAVES 2027 media and entertainment summit.

The MEA said Modi "reaffirmed India's steadfast support for ASEAN unity and centrality," and leaders reviewed progress on their Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. Outlook India reported that Modi told the gathering India's future rests on "cooperation, and not competition."

## How India and ASEAN Got Here

The e-visa idea builds on three decades of steadily closer ties. This timeline, based on the MEA's account, shows how the relationship has been upgraded:

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1967 | ASEAN founded |
| 1992 | India becomes a sectoral dialogue partner |
| 1995 (December) | Full dialogue partnership |
| 2002 | Annual summit-level meetings begin |
| 2012 | Strategic partnership |
| 2022 | Comprehensive Strategic Partnership |
| 2027 | 35th anniversary of ties |

ASEAN now has 11 members after Timor-Leste joined: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste and Vietnam. Modi also pointed to more than 100 initiatives since 2014 to bring people closer and direct flights between India and nine ASEAN countries. For background on the summit itself, read our report on the <a href="/asean-summit-manila-opens-modi-trump-due/">ASEAN Summit opening in Manila</a>.

## What It Could Mean for Indian Tourism and Travellers

For India's travel trade, a zero fee removes a small but visible barrier for budget travellers from Southeast Asia, especially for short trips where a $25 fee is a noticeable share of the cost. It also pairs with the other people-focused proposals, such as Nalanda scholarships and youth exchanges, that aim to bring more Southeast Asians to India.

For Indians, the move does not change anything directly: entry rules for Indians travelling to ASEAN countries are set by each country. The Philippines case, however, shows the reciprocity at work, since India's free e-visa for Filipinos followed Manila's visa-free entry for Indians. Gulf News reported it was not clear whether India is seeking similar steps from other members this time.

## When Could the Free E-Visa Start?

No timeline has been given. The proposals now need follow-up between India and ASEAN members, and the fee change itself would need a formal notice from the Indian government before it applies on the visa portal. Until then, ASEAN travellers planning trips should budget for the fees in the table above, apply only through the official portal, and avoid third-party sites that charge extra service fees.
