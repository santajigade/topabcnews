Asian stock markets staged a sharp recovery on Monday, November 9, after a bruising week for technology shares. Taiwan's benchmark index jumped around 6% and Japan's Nikkei 225 gained about 3.5% as investors returned to chipmakers and other AI-linked stocks following a rebound on Wall Street on Friday. Oil prices eased slightly after US President Donald Trump said fresh attacks on Iran were unlikely while talks continue. In India, however, the Sensex and Nifty gave up early gains as IT stocks came under pressure.

## Why Asian Markets Bounced After Last Week's Selloff

Last week was one of the toughest for global tech stocks in months. The S&P 500 lost 2.7% over the week, its worst weekly showing since April 2025, according to the <a href="https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/11/09/asian-markets-bounce-back-after-a-recovery-for-ai-stocks-on-wall-street/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Associated Press</a>. Part of the selling was linked to worries that Democratic gains in the US midterm elections could slow the building of AI data centres, particularly in Texas, where voters have raised concerns about power bills. Tokyo and Seoul had fallen about 6% after a heavy Wall Street drop on Thursday.

On Friday, US markets steadied. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.1% and the S&P 500 added 0.3%, while the Dow slipped 0.5%. Nvidia shares climbed 2.8% after two days of steep losses. That gave Asian investors a reason to buy back beaten-down chip stocks on Monday.

## Chip Stocks Lead the Gains in Tokyo, Taipei and Seoul

Semiconductor companies did most of the heavy lifting. In Taiwan, chipmaker TSMC rose about 5.6% in early trade, and the wider market climbed more than 6% after its steepest weekly fall in two decades, according to a <a href="https://www.brecorder.com/news/40448089/asian-stocks-bounce-as-ai-mideast-jitters-recede-dollar-firm-on-fed-bets" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Reuters report</a>. In Tokyo, Tokyo Electron gained 6.5% and Advantest 5.1%, while SoftBank Group fell 2.9%.

In South Korea, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix rose 2.7% and 3.9% respectively. MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan rose 2%, after sliding 4.6% last week.

Not every signal was positive. Nvidia shares fell about 3.5% in after-hours trading, according to Reuters, as the company opened an internal investigation into reports that its chief executive had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Investors will watch how that review develops.

## Oil Eases and the Fed Hike Debate Returns

Brent crude slipped to about $98 a barrel after Trump said on Sunday that new strikes on Iran were highly unlikely while negotiations progressed. US crude traded near $91. Lower oil prices are generally good news for importing nations, including India.

At the same time, the strong US labour market is keeping pressure on the Federal Reserve. The October jobs report showed 165,000 jobs added, more than twice what economists expected, as we explained in our <a href="/us-jobs-report-october-2026-165000/">US jobs report coverage</a>. Reuters said markets now price close to 80% odds of at least a 25 basis point rate hike at the Fed's December meeting, based on CME FedWatch. The US dollar index posted its biggest one-day gain in five months, rising to about 102.2.

In China, data showed factory-gate inflation picking up at its fastest pace in more than four years, while consumer inflation cooled in October.

## Sensex and Nifty Lose Early Gains as IT Stocks Drag

Indian markets opened on a positive note, following global cues. The Sensex rose about 328 points to 81,327 and the Nifty gained 90 points to 25,076 at the open, according to Telangana Today. But the rally faded within minutes. By 9:36 am, the Nifty was down about 0.2% at 24,930 and the Sensex was around 80,826, as reported by Upstox.

IT shares led the decline, with TCS and Infosys down about 1% each and HCL Tech down 1.6%. Power Grid, Bharti Airtel, Hindalco and Tata Steel also fell. Banks and pharma held up better, with SBI up 1.8% and ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, Apollo Hospitals and Dr Reddy's also in the green. Market breadth was weak, with only 13 of the 50 Nifty stocks trading higher.

The pause follows a strong run. The Nifty surged 8.6% last week, helped by foreign buying, lower crude prices and better quarterly earnings, as covered in our report on the <a href="/sensex-jumps-1718-points-nifty-25000/">Sensex's 1,718-point jump</a>. Foreign investors ended a six-day buying streak during the Muhurat Trading session, and domestic institutions were net sellers for a seventh straight session.

## What Investors Will Watch This Week

The coming days are packed with events that could move markets. US consumer price inflation data is due on Tuesday and will shape expectations for the December Fed meeting. Earnings from CoreWeave, Applied Materials and Cisco Systems will test whether heavy spending on AI is translating into profits. Developments in US and Iran talks will also matter for oil prices.

For Indian investors, crude prices, foreign fund flows and the direction of US bond yields remain the key factors. The US 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.87% after the jobs data. A softer US inflation reading could revive appetite for emerging market stocks, while a hot number may add to pressure on IT and other export-focused sectors.
