Yemen's Houthi rebels have pushed to within about 60 km of Aden, the southern port city that serves as the interim seat of the internationally recognised government, according to military and government sources who spoke to the AFP news agency on Sunday, November 15. The advance came as the Saudi-backed government said it was pulling its last troops back from the Bab al-Mandab Strait area to strengthen the defence of Aden and nearby provinces. It is the most serious threat to the city since the early months of the war in 2015.

## How Close the Houthis Are to Aden

Four military sources and two government sources told AFP that Houthi fighters had taken the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts in Lahj province and were positioned at the Al-Kharaz camp in Al-Madaribah, roughly 60 km from Aden. The group also seized strategic positions in the Al-Arah area, according to the same report.

A separate government military source, speaking anonymously to China's Xinhua news agency, put the distance at about 70 km. The figures differ slightly, but both accounts describe the same trend: the front line is moving steadily towards the city.

Two weeks earlier, on November 2, four military sources told Reuters the Houthis were about 80 km from Aden after capturing Al-Maqatirah and fighting at a camp at the entrance to Tur al-Bahah district, one of the main approaches to the city. That was already the closest the group had come since 2015, when its fighters briefly entered Aden before being pushed out.

## Why Government Forces Left Bab al-Mandab

The government announced it was completing the withdrawal of its remaining forces from the area around the Bab al-Mandab Strait and redeploying them "where current circumstances require". Officials said protecting Aden and the surrounding provinces was now the priority.

The Xinhua source was blunter. Describing a Saudi-backed push to retake ground near the strait, the source said: "The offensive has failed." Residents of the Al-Buraiqeh district in western Aden told Xinhua they heard explosions and fighting nearby and watched military vehicles and troops returning from the Bab al-Mandab front through Sunday. The source warned that the pullback had weakened defences on the city's western side.

Military officials told AFP on Friday that troops had begun piling sandbags and digging tunnels around Aden. Along the road between Aden and Lahj, dozens of buildings have been destroyed in heavy fighting between the Houthis and local resistance groups, which have received air support from the Saudi-led coalition.

## Attacks on Aden and the Siege of Taiz

The ground advance follows weeks of missile and drone strikes on the city. Government military sources told AFP that an attack on the presidential palace on October 15 killed four people. Houthi strikes have also hit Aden's international airport, the country's busiest, forcing cancellations and diversions, as <a href="https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/30/need-a-plan-b-yemenis-in-aden-prepare-for-another-war-as-missiles-fall" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera reported from Aden</a>, where some families have stockpiled food and fuel in case they need to leave.

Further north, Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city, has been encircled since early October, when the Houthis cut its main road south to Aden. Reuters reported that the siege had displaced more than 100,000 people and caused shortages of food, fuel and medicine. Yemen's army said on Saturday that troops were regrouping to try to break the siege, Anadolu Agency reported.

## Background to the New Offensive

The Houthis, who are backed by Iran, drove the government out of the capital Sanaa in 2014, and a Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 largely halted major fighting until this year. According to Reuters, the current Houthi push began in September, when the group moved down the Red Sea coast and seized territory along the Bab al-Mandab Strait, including the port town of Mocha.

Reuters has described the Yemen fighting as a volatile new front in the wider US-Israeli war with Iran. The Houthis have stepped up attacks on Saudi Arabia, and the coalition has responded with more strikes in Yemen. On November 15, the coalition said it destroyed two Houthi ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh. ## Why the Strait Matters Beyond Yemen

Bab al-Mandab links the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and is one of the world's most important shipping lanes. Reuters notes it is a key route for Saudi oil exports to Asia. With Houthi forces now consolidating control of the area around the strait, the security of tanker and cargo traffic through the Red Sea is likely to stay in focus for importers across Asia, including India, alongside the disruption already seen near the Strait of Hormuz.

### Key highlights

- Houthis are about 60 km from Aden, per AFP sources; a Xinhua source says about 70 km.
- The government has withdrawn its last forces from the Bab al-Mandab area to defend Aden.
- Taiz remains under siege, with more than 323,000 people displaced across Yemen since August, according to OCHA.
- Saudi-led coalition aircraft are supporting local forces on the Aden to Lahj road.

## What Comes Next for Aden

For now, the government's strategy appears to be defensive: shorten its lines, hold Aden and protect nearby provinces. Whether the Houthis try to push into the city, or use the threat to strengthen their position in any future talks, is not yet clear. Neither side's claims about the battlefield have been independently verified, and the situation is changing quickly.
