Prices in the United States rose faster than expected in October 2026. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) climbed 0.4% from September on a seasonally adjusted basis, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) said on Tuesday, November 10. Economists had pencilled in a 0.3% gain. Coming just days after a strong jobs report, the reading has made a Federal Reserve rate hike in December look close to certain and has even revived talk of a bigger half point move.

## What the October CPI Numbers Show

Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, rose 0.3% in the month. That matched September's pace but was above the 0.2% that economists had expected, according to InvestmentNews. The headline figure did cool slightly from September, when prices had risen 0.6%.

Energy was once again a major driver. The BLS said its energy index rose 0.7% in October, with electricity up 0.9% and gasoline up 1.0% on a seasonally adjusted basis. Natural gas prices bucked the trend and fell 0.8%. Food prices also moved higher, climbing 0.5% overall, with groceries up 0.4% and restaurant meals up 0.5%.

Shelter costs, the largest single part of the index, rose 0.3%. Several service categories saw sharper jumps: hospital services and recreation each rose 0.8%, and motor vehicle insurance gained 0.7%. On the other side, used car and truck prices fell 1.2%, and communication costs slipped 0.1%.

## Why There Is No Annual Inflation Figure This Month

Readers looking for the usual year on year number will not find it in this release. The BLS said it cannot publish 12 month percentage changes for most October 2026 series. The reason is last year's US government shutdown, which stopped the agency from collecting October 2025 price data, leaving no base for comparison.

One exception is gasoline, where the unadjusted index showed prices 37.4% higher than a year earlier. Before the release, economists had expected annual headline inflation of about 3.9% and core inflation of about 2.7%, InvestmentNews reported, up from 3.7% and 2.6% respectively in September. The full data tables are on the <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">BLS consumer price index release</a>.

## What the Report Means for the Federal Reserve

The <a href="https://www.federalreserve.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">US Federal Reserve</a> has already turned towards tighter policy this year. In September it raised interest rates for the first time since July 2023, in a unanimous 12 to 0 vote, and in October it held rates steady as inflation stayed high, according to InvestmentNews. The October jobs data, which showed far more hiring than forecast, had already strengthened the case for another move. We covered that report in detail in our story on the <a href="/us-jobs-report-october-2026-165000/">US October jobs report</a>.

After the CPI release, futures pricing tracked by the CME FedWatch tool pointed to an 84.7% chance of a quarter point hike in December, which would take the target range to 4.00% to 4.25%. The odds of a larger half point hike jumped to 15.3% from just 1.4% earlier on Tuesday.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller added to the debate. Speaking in London, he said he would back a quarter point increase in December but could push for half a point if inflation runs hotter than expected, according to CNBC as cited by InvestmentNews.

### What market watchers are saying

Not everyone expects the Fed to go big. Chris Zaccarelli, chief investment officer at Northlight Asset Management, said his firm expects only a 0.25% rise next month, and sees the larger question as whether a second hike follows in January. Daniel Siluk of Janus Henderson described a half point December move as "a high bar" and said the bigger debate is how much more tightening is needed in 2027. CBS News noted that the report was one of the first major economic readings since the US midterm elections, where high prices were a key concern for voters.

## Why US Inflation Matters for India

Decisions taken in Washington tend to travel quickly to Indian markets. When US interest rates rise, dollar assets become more attractive to global investors, which can pull foreign money away from emerging markets such as India and put pressure on the rupee. Higher US rates can also make borrowing costlier for Indian companies that raise funds abroad. Stock and bond markets in India are therefore likely to watch the Fed's December decision closely, along with the next US CPI report.

## The Next Data Points to Watch

The BLS will publish November 2026 CPI figures on Thursday, December 10, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time, which is 7:00 p.m. India time. For now, the October numbers show that price pressures in the world's largest economy have not eased, with energy, food and services all contributing. Unless upcoming data surprises on the soft side, markets are preparing for the Fed to raise rates again before the year ends.
