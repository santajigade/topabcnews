Japan's Self-Defence Forces will use an Indian firing range for live-fire training in 2027, the first time they have done so. The decision came out of the fourth India-Japan 2+2 ministerial dialogue, held in Tokyo on Thursday, November 19. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar met Japan's Defence Minister Shinjiro Koizumi and Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi. The two sides also pushed forward a naval antenna co-production project, a ship repair arrangement and new work on underwater and missile technology.

## The Firing Range Deal and Why Japan Wants It

According to <a href="https://theprint.in/diplomacy/japan-to-use-indian-ranges-for-missile-tests-as-new-delhi-tokyo-move-to-boost-defence-ties/3103282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ThePrint</a>, the two sides signed a Letter of Intent covering live-fire training that includes surface-to-ship missiles. Japan's Ground Self-Defence Force chief, General Masayoshi Arai, said last month that officials had been sent to inspect possible ranges in India. ThePrint linked the interest to Japan's Type-25 surface-to-ship missile, which has a range of roughly 1,000 km.

Koizumi posted on X that the forces would use an Indian range "for the first time" next year, and <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/india-japan-step-up-defence-ties-japanese-forces-to-use-indian-firing-range" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tribune</a> quoted him calling it "a major step strategically".

## Every Item on the Table, in Plain Terms

What each agreed item is and how far it has got, based on reporting by The Tribune and ThePrint:

| Item | What it is | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| Firing range access | Japanese troops train with live weapons on an Indian range | Letter of Intent signed; training planned for 2027 |
| UNICORN antenna | Unified Complex Radio Antenna, a mast that combines several communication systems on one warship (technical name NORA-50); already used on Japanese warships | First co-production deal announced in July 2026; Acceptance of Necessity expected by February 2027 |
| Maritime Ship Repair Arrangement (MSRA) | Lets each navy repair its ships at the other's facilities | Both sides want it concluded early |
| Maritime security memorandum | Cooperation on tracking activity at sea and in the air, search and rescue, and sharing information | Recently signed; now being put into practice |
| Mine countermeasures | Finding and clearing naval mines | Cooperation to be expanded |
| Niche, underwater and missile technology | Joint research and development | Both sides agreed to speed up cooperation |

### What "Acceptance of Necessity" means

In Indian defence buying, Acceptance of Necessity, granted by the Defence Acquisition Council, is the first step of the acquisition process. It records that the armed forces need the equipment. So a February 2027 target means the UNICORN project would still be at an early stage then, and production on Indian warships would come later.

## What This Means for India

For Indian industry, the key line is the "Design with Japan, Make in India" framework Rajnath Singh said he wanted to strengthen. If UNICORN goes ahead as a co-production deal, parts of a system now fitted to Japanese warships would be made in India. Under a ship repair arrangement, Japanese naval vessels could be repaired at Indian facilities, and Indian ships at Japanese ones.

The maritime security memorandum covers domain awareness, which means knowing what ships and aircraft are moving in a given area, along with an arrangement to share that information with Japan.

## Jaishankar's Message on Chokepoints and Coercion

Jaishankar used the meeting to raise worries about sea routes. He referred to recent experiences in the Persian Gulf and warned against using connectivity, resources, supply chains and market access as weapons. The Tribune read this as a reference to China's curbs on critical minerals. ThePrint noted that China has also imposed export restrictions on goods bound for Japan.

He said both countries were committed, with other Quad partners, to a free and open Indo-Pacific. That theme also ran through the recent leaders' meetings in Manila, covered in our report on the <a href="/quad-manila-summit-2026-vs-2024-india/">Quad summit in Manila</a>.

## A Busy Few Months for Delhi and Tokyo

Jaishankar reached Tokyo on Wednesday after two days in Manila with Prime Minister Narendra Modi for the India-ASEAN summit, and he also held the 19th India-Japan Foreign Ministers' Strategic Dialogue with Motegi. On Wednesday, Singh and Koizumi visited the Japanese navy base at Yokosuka and inspected the Mogami-class frigate JS Natori.

Singh and Koizumi had already met in New Delhi in August and in Cebu, Philippines, in October. Japan has raised defence spending to 2% of GDP about two years earlier than planned and has loosened its rules on military exports, ThePrint reported.

The two countries also launched a logo for 75 years of diplomatic ties, which they will mark in 2027, the same year Japanese troops are due to train on an Indian range.
