Delhi woke up to another day of toxic air on Thursday, November 12, with the city's overall Air Quality Index (AQI) at 321 at 7 am, firmly in the "very poor" band. Several monitoring stations came close to 400, and forecasters at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) expect the air to turn "severe" by Friday morning as winds slow down. Stage III curbs under the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) remain in force across Delhi-NCR.

## How Bad the Air Is Right Now

According to Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) data cited by Outlook India, the hourly AQI stood at 314 at 3 am and 321 at 7 am on Thursday, 22 points higher than at the same time on Wednesday. The 24-hour average AQI rose to 313 on Wednesday from 299 on Tuesday.

Readings were much worse in some neighbourhoods. Open Magazine listed these 7 am readings from CPCB stations:

| Chandni Chowk | 387 |
| Pusa (IITM) | 386 |

For comparison, Mumbai's AQI was 146, in the "moderate" range, on the same morning.

## Severe Air Forecast for Friday

IITM's forecast projects an AQI of about 428, driven by fine PM2.5 particles, by 9 am on Friday, November 13. That would place Delhi in the "severe" band of 401 to 500. Weather is a big reason. India Meteorological Department data reported by Outlook shows the mixing height, the layer in which pollutants spread out, falling from 2,000 metres on Thursday to 1,400 metres on Friday. Winds are also expected to weaken from north-westerlies of up to 15 km/h to slower, variable winds of 5 to 10 km/h, which lets pollution build up near the ground.

## What Is Causing the Smog

IITM's source analysis shows the mix of pollution sources has shifted since Diwali:

- **Firecrackers** made up 71.1% of PM2.5 between 1 am and 9 am on November 9, the morning after Diwali, but their share dropped to 7.4% by Wednesday.
- **Stubble burning** in neighbouring states is projected to contribute about 20% of PM2.5 on Thursday, easing to around 11% in the following days.
- **Other sources** on Wednesday included secondary organic particles at 22.7%, household burning of solid fuels at 22.3% and vehicle emissions at 19.3%.

The trouble began even before the festival. The Centre for Science and Environment found that average PM2.5 in the week before Diwali was 236 micrograms per cubic metre, nearly double the level for the same period in 2025. On Diwali day, November 8, Delhi's average AQI reached 376, the worst Diwali reading since 2021, according to figures cited by Open Magazine. We covered the pre-festival spike in our earlier report on <a href="/delhi-aqi-severe-diwali-health-tips/">Delhi AQI nearing severe before Diwali</a>.

## GRAP Stage III Rules in Force

The <a href="https://caqm.nic.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM)</a> invoked GRAP Stage III across Delhi-NCR on November 6, after the AQI touched 382. The rules, which apply on top of Stage I and II measures, include:

- Curbs on non-essential construction and demolition work that creates dust
- Stricter action against polluting vehicles
- Hybrid classes for school students up to Class V
- Work from home arrangements for offices where directed
- Water sprinkling and road dust control by civic agencies

If the AQI stays in the severe band, the CAQM can move to tougher Stage IV measures, which it decides based on actual readings and forecasts.

## How to Protect Your Health

Long exposure to very poor air can irritate the eyes, throat and lungs and can worsen breathing problems, especially for children, older people and those with heart or lung disease. The CAQM has asked residents to use public transport, avoid open burning and wear masks outdoors. Simple steps can also help:

- Limit outdoor exercise, especially in the early morning and late evening when smog is thickest
- Use a well fitting N95 or similar mask if you must go out
- Keep windows closed during peak pollution hours and use an air purifier if available
- Check live AQI readings on the <a href="https://airquality.cpcb.gov.in/AQI_India/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CPCB air quality portal</a> before planning outdoor activity
- See a doctor if you notice breathlessness, chest tightness or a persistent cough

## Relief Depends on the Wind

With festival smoke fading, Delhi's pollution now depends mostly on local emissions, farm fires and the weather. Forecasters expect calmer winds to trap pollutants on Friday, so residents should be prepared for a few more hard days until stronger winds return. Following GRAP rules, cutting unnecessary vehicle trips and avoiding waste burning are the most direct ways citizens can help.
