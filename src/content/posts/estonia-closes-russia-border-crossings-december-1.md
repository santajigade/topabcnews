Estonia has said it will close all of its road border crossings with Russia from December 1, 2026. The decision was announced on Sunday, November 15, a day after eight people were detained over an attempted arson attack on an Estonian drone manufacturer. Estonian officials say Russia's military intelligence service, the GRU, ordered the attack. Moscow has rejected such accusations in the past and called the planned closure a sign of hostility towards Russia.

## The Attempted Attack on Threod Systems

According to Estonia's public broadcaster ERR, the target was Threod Systems, a defence company based in Viimsi, just north of the capital Tallinn. The firm makes long-range reconnaissance and attack drones, and it supplies drones to Ukraine and other countries, the Australian Associated Press reported with German agency dpa.

Eight Latvian citizens were detained after trying to get into the company's property early on Saturday, November 14. ERR said the group planned to set off an explosion and set the building on fire. Estonia's Internal Security Service, known as KAPO, said the operation had been ordered by the GRU.

It is not the first such incident. ERR noted that this was the second attempted arson on an Estonian defence company since August, when Tallinn-based Milrem Robotics, a maker of unmanned ground vehicles, was targeted.

Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said Estonia had "clear evidence" of GRU involvement. Writing on X, he added that "hostile actions against Estonia come with consequences."

## Which Crossings Will Close and When

Estonia currently has three road crossings open with Russia. All three are now set to shut to both pedestrians and vehicles.

| Crossing | Current use | Hours | Status from Dec 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narva | Pedestrians and freight | 7 am to 7 pm | Set to close |
| Luhamaa | Pedestrians and freight | 7 am to 7 pm | Set to close |
| Koidula | Pedestrians and freight | 7 am to 7 pm | Set to close |

ERR reported that the crossings presently operate for 12 hours a day, from 7 am to 7 pm. Narva is especially busy in summer and around New Year, and it is one of the few land routes into Russia still open to travellers in Europe since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Interior Minister Igor Taro is expected to put a formal proposal to the government, which will discuss it next week. Officials have not yet said whether the closure will be temporary or indefinite. Tsahkna said the closures begin on December 1 and did not give an end date.

Estonia also has rail links with Russia at Narva and Koidula. ERR said it was not yet clear whether those would be affected.

### Key highlights

- Closure of all three road crossings planned from December 1, 2026
- Trigger: attempted arson at drone maker Threod Systems on November 14
- Eight Latvian citizens detained; KAPO blames Russia's GRU
- Second attempted arson on an Estonian defence firm since August
- Estonian residents and truck drivers in Russia get about two weeks to return

## What Estonia's Leaders Said

Prime Minister Kristen Michal said on Sunday that he had discussed closing the border with the interior minister and asked officials to prepare the necessary steps. He argued that shutting the crossings would reduce the threat to people in Estonia and send a clear signal that attacks on the defence industry will not be accepted.

Michal said Russia's aim was to make Estonians afraid and hesitant, and that the government's answer would be the opposite. "We will not be intimidated," he said, according to ERR.

Taro said Estonia had already sharply limited border traffic and had warned Moscow that any further escalation could lead to a full closure. He said the move would also cut the risk of people breaking sanctions on Russia.

## How Russia Has Responded

Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova told the state news agency TASS that a full border closure would be "yet another demonstration of Russophobia." Russia has regularly denied European claims that it is behind sabotage operations on the continent.

The dispute comes during a period of high tension in the Baltic region. Last week, residents in parts of Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia were told to take shelter during <a href="/baltic-states-drone-alerts-nato-jets-scrambled/">overnight drone alerts</a> as NATO jets were scrambled. At the same time, US, Ukrainian and Russian officials have been meeting in the Gulf, where the <a href="/abu-dhabi-talks-day-two-prisoner-swap-no-breakthrough/">Abu Dhabi talks</a> produced a reported prisoner exchange but no peace deal.

## What It Means for Travellers and Trade

For people who cross regularly, especially at the busy Narva checkpoint, the closure will end the remaining road option for visits. ERR said Estonian residents and truck drivers who are currently in Russia will be able to come home over the next two weeks, after which the return journey could become long and complicated.

Freight that now moves by road through Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula will also need other routes if the plan goes ahead. Updates on official travel advice are expected from <a href="https://vm.ee/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Estonia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs</a>, and the latest reporting is available from the public broadcaster <a href="https://news.err.ee/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ERR News</a>.

There is no direct India angle for most readers, but Indian nationals in Estonia or planning overland travel to Russia through the Baltics should check official advisories before making plans, since the land route through Narva is expected to shut from December 1.

## What Happens Next

The Estonian government will discuss Taro's proposal in the coming days and decide whether the closure has an end date. Investigators are also continuing to look into the attempted attack on Threod Systems and the eight people detained. With Estonia, a NATO and EU member and a strong supporter of Ukraine, saying it has evidence of Russian state involvement, the border decision is likely to be watched closely by neighbouring countries facing similar incidents.
