The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) says 2026 will end as one of the four warmest years ever recorded. Its State of the Climate update, released on the opening day of the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, also reports record sea levels, record ocean heat and a fast-growing El Nino that is expected to become the strongest on record by December. The findings arrive just as negotiators begin two weeks of talks on how to cut emissions and pay for adaptation.

The update covers data up to September 2026, which means it does not yet capture the full effect of the current El Nino. The WMO will publish its final State of the Global Climate 2026 report in March 2027.

## How Hot 2026 Has Been So Far

From January to September 2026, the global average surface temperature was 1.47 degrees Celsius above the 1850 to 1900 average, with a margin of uncertainty of 0.13 degrees. The WMO combined nine international datasets to reach that figure, including ERA5, NASA's GISTEMP, HadCRUT5 and NOAA's global record.

That puts 2026 close to 2023 (1.46 degrees) and 2025 (1.43 degrees), and just below the record year of 2024 (1.55 degrees). Two larger points stand out. Each of the last 12 years has ranked among the warmest on record, and the last four years, 2023 to 2026, now occupy the top four places.

A brief La Nina cooling effect in early 2026 did not last. It was reversed once El Nino began building in August.

| Year | Temperature above 1850 to 1900 average |
|---|---|
| 2024 (record) | 1.55 degrees C |
| 2026 (January to September) | 1.47 degrees C |
| 2023 | 1.46 degrees C |
| 2025 | 1.43 degrees C |

## Why the Coming El Nino Matters

El Nino is a natural warming of the tropical Pacific Ocean that shifts weather patterns around the world. According to the WMO, it usually adds between 0.1 and 0.2 degrees to global temperatures in a year, and its biggest effect tends to come in the year after it forms. The current event is forecast to continue into early 2027, so the final figure for 2026 is likely to be higher than the January to September average, and 2027 could also run very hot.

WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo explained that El Nino does not create extra heat. "It releases heat from the ocean, which is the warmest ever measured," she said. The report notes that the World Health Organization has, for the first time, classified the expected health effects of an El Nino as a health emergency. Farmers who depend on rain, especially in places that already face food shortages, are seen as most at risk.

## Oceans, Ice and Greenhouse Gases at Record Levels

The report describes a planet that is storing heat faster than before. Earth's energy imbalance, the gap between energy coming in from the Sun and energy going out, reached its highest level since monitoring began in 1960. Oceans absorb about 90 percent of this extra heat, and 2026 is on track to be the tenth year in a row of record ocean warmth.

Key highlights from the update:

- **Sea level:** Global average sea level hit a record high in 2026. June, July and August were the highest monthly readings since satellite measurements began in 1993.
- **Carbon dioxide:** About 426.8 parts per million in 2025, 53 percent above the level of 1750.
- **Methane:** 1,948 parts per billion in 2025, up 167 percent from 1750.
- **Nitrous oxide:** 339.1 parts per billion, up 26 percent.
- **Glaciers:** They lost more than 400 gigatonnes of ice in 2025, roughly equal to 1 millimetre of sea level rise. All 19 glacier regions lost ice for a fourth straight year.
- **Sea ice:** Antarctic sea ice reached its third lowest yearly maximum since 1979, while the Arctic maximum was the lowest or second lowest on record.

Greenhouse gas levels reached a new high in 2025 and kept rising in 2026. The WMO adds that El Nino years usually bring a jump in these concentrations.

## What the Report Says About South Asia

The update lists several extreme events with a direct link to the region. About 146.5 million people in India were exposed to extreme heat during a heatwave in April 2026. In late August, a glacier collapse in the Himalayas triggered a flash flood in Nepal that killed more than 1,300 people and badly hurt the country's economy.

Elsewhere, heatwaves, drought and wildfires in Europe caused more than 30,000 excess deaths, according to a preliminary estimate, and at least eight countries set new national temperature records. Land water storage has also been falling since 2014. In 2025, 42 percent of the assessed land area had below normal water storage, compared with a long-term average of 25 percent.

For India, a strong El Nino is often linked with weaker monsoon rain and hotter summers, which is why the forecast into 2027 will be watched closely by farmers, power planners and health officials.

## Early Warnings and the Message for COP31

There was one positive trend in the report. The number of countries reporting multi-hazard early warning systems rose from 56 in 2015 to 136 in 2025. The WMO says reliable forecasts, backed by steady observations, remain one of the most effective ways to save lives as extremes grow.

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres used the report to press leaders in Antalya. "We have entered a new age of climate extremes," he said, calling for faster emission cuts and a just transition away from fossil fuels. ## A Hotter Year Ahead Unless Emissions Fall

The WMO's numbers leave little room for doubt about the direction of travel. With El Nino expected to peak around the end of the year, 2027 may test the 2024 record again. Whether COP31 produces stronger plans to cut emissions and fund adaptation will shape how severe the years after that become, especially for heat-exposed countries in South Asia.
