India's unemployment rate fell to 5.1% in the July to September 2026 quarter, down from 5.4% in April to June, according to the latest Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) quarterly bulletin released by the National Statistics Office on November 10. The improvement came mainly from villages, where farm employment rose sharply, while joblessness in cities inched up slightly. More women also joined the workforce, pushing overall labour force participation to 55.5%.

## Headline Numbers From the PLFS Bulletin

The PLFS measures jobs using the Current Weekly Status (CWS) approach, which looks at what a person did in the seven days before the survey. All figures below are for people aged 15 years and above.

| Indicator | July-Sept 2026 | April-June 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Unemployment rate, all India | 5.1% | 5.4% |
| Unemployment rate, rural | 4.3% | 4.8% |
| Unemployment rate, urban | 6.8% | 6.7% |
| Labour force participation rate (LFPR) | 55.5% | 54.6% |
| Female LFPR | 34.7% | 33.2% |
| Worker population ratio (WPR) | 52.7% | 51.7% |
| Female WPR | 32.8% | 31.3% |

Using population projections from the Health Ministry, the bulletin estimates that on average about 57.9 crore people aged 15 and above were employed during the quarter, of whom 40.5 crore were men and 17.4 crore were women. The survey covered 5,55,593 people, including 3,16,683 in rural areas and 2,38,910 in urban areas.

## Villages Drive the Improvement

The rural unemployment rate dropped to 4.3%, a clear fall from 4.8% in the previous quarter and slightly lower than 4.4% a year earlier. Rural labour force participation climbed to 58.1% from 56.9%, and the rural worker population ratio rose to 55.6% from 54.2%.

Farming was the biggest change. The share of rural workers in agriculture jumped to 56.7% from 52.9% in April to June, a quarter that covers the monsoon months. At the same time, the share of rural workers in the secondary sector, which includes manufacturing, construction and mining, fell to 21.9% from 24.4%, and the tertiary or services share slipped to 21.4% from 22.7%. Self employment in villages rose to 62.7% of workers from 61.4%.

## Cities See a Small Rise in Joblessness

Urban India told a slightly different story. The urban unemployment rate edged up to 6.8% from 6.7%, even as urban participation rose a little to 50.4% from 50.2%. Compared with a year ago, urban participation was 0.3 percentage points lower. The urban male LFPR stood at 77.3%.

There was some good news on job quality. The share of urban workers with regular wage or salaried jobs rose to 49.6% from 49.3% in the previous quarter. Casual labour, which usually means daily wage work without security, fell in both rural areas (21.7% from 22.5%) and urban areas (10.7% from 11.1%), and was lower than a year earlier in both.

## More Women in the Workforce

Women's participation continued to rise. The female LFPR reached 34.7% against 33.2% in the April to June quarter, and the female worker population ratio improved to 32.8% from 31.3%. The rise came in the same quarter that rural participation and farm employment climbed.

### Key highlights

- All India unemployment: 5.1%, down from 5.4%.
- Rural unemployment: 4.3%, the main driver of the fall.
- Urban unemployment: 6.8%, up marginally from 6.7%.
- Female labour force participation: 34.7%, up 1.5 points.
- About 57.9 crore people estimated to be employed.
- Casual labour share fell in both villages and cities.

## How the Quarterly and Monthly Data Fit Together

Since January 2025, the government has run a redesigned PLFS that produces monthly and quarterly estimates for both rural and urban India. The July to September 2026 bulletin is the sixth quarterly release under this format. The <a href="https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1792060004197_6c3ba64d-1853-4b3d-98fb-d750d5ec8a5d_Monthly_Press_note_PLFS_Sep_2026.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">monthly PLFS note for September 2026</a> from the Ministry of Statistics had already shown the unemployment rate steady at 5.0% in both August and September, with urban unemployment at 6.9% in September. The quarterly number is calculated separately, which is why it does not exactly match the monthly readings.

The jobs data adds to other recent signals on the economy, such as the <a href="/india-services-pmi-october-2026-four-month-high/">services PMI reaching a four month high in October</a>. For a global comparison, the latest <a href="/us-jobs-report-october-2026-165000/">US jobs report</a> showed 165,000 jobs added in October.

## What the Data Means for Workers and Policy

The July to September numbers show a labour market that is broadly improving, led by villages and by more women stepping into work. The weak spot remains urban joblessness, which stayed close to 7%, and the gains depend heavily on farm work in villages. Economists will watch the October to December bulletin to see whether rural gains hold and whether cities can add more regular salaried jobs. The full release is available on the <a href="https://www.mospi.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation website</a>.
