US President Donald Trump said on Monday, November 9, 2026, that the war with Iran is "effectively over" in military terms. He spoke at a surprise White House briefing after closed-door talks with Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, who has acted as the main channel to Tehran. Trump played down the chance of fresh US strikes but made clear that the naval blockade of Iran and a new land blockade will stay in place for now. Oil prices eased on Tuesday as traders read his remarks as a sign that the conflict is cooling.

## What Trump Said After Meeting Qatar's Prime Minister

According to <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/9/trump-calls-iran-war-effectively-over-after-talks-with-qatar-pm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera's report on the briefing</a>, Trump told reporters that Iran "militarily is effectively over". Vice President JD Vance also took part in the meeting with the Qatari leader.

Trump described the US goal as keeping the "status quo", which he linked to requests from Iran's Gulf neighbours. In practice, that means two things:

- the US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues
- Task Force Iron Gate, the recently announced effort to cut off Iran's trade by land and across the Caspian Sea, stays active

The Hill quoted Trump as saying the US has "the greatest military blockade ever". He added that Iron Gate covers land, so pressure is applied "by land and by sea". He also said US forces remain ready to resume fighting if conditions change, and that Gulf allies are safer than they were six months ago.

Trump said Iran is reaching out about negotiations. He told reporters that US military action against Iran is not necessary "at this point", a line that markets picked up quickly. We covered the land blockade in detail in our report on <a href="/us-task-force-iron-gate-iran-land-routes/">Task Force Iron Gate and Iran's land routes</a>.

## Qatar's Role and the Proposal on the Table

Qatar has been the key go-between in the conflict. Sheikh Mohammed posted after the meeting that he was grateful for "positive discussions on reaching a deal". He said the talks looked at ways to move US-Iran negotiations forward.

Al Jazeera reported that the Qatari prime minister carried a two-day-old proposal from Iran on how to end the war. Neither side has made the full terms public.

Iranian officials, according to the same report, want several conditions met before the war ends:

| Iran's demand | What it means |
|---|---|
| Lift US sanctions | Remove economic penalties on Iran |
| Release frozen funds | Unlock Iranian money held abroad |
| Recognise a Hormuz shipping route | Accept a route through the Strait of Hormuz worked out with Oman |
| End the war on all fronts | Includes Israel's attacks in Lebanon |

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Sunday called Iran's demands "outrageous and ridiculous", The Hill reported. Iranian officials, for their part, want the blockade and sanctions lifted before formal talks restart.

## How Tehran Has Responded

Iran's leaders gave mixed signals. Mohsen Rezaei, Secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, called Washington a "desperate gambler" and accused it of spreading false reports about talks that he said were imaginary.

President Masoud Pezeshkian struck a different tone. He said Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei had called for an end to the current situation, which Pezeshkian described as "neither war nor peace". He said Iran is open to more negotiations but will not give in to force.

## How the War Reached This Point

The conflict began on February 28, 2026, when the US and Israel launched attacks on Iran. Iran responded by moving to shut the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway that carries a large share of the world's oil, gas and fertiliser shipments. It also fired missiles and drones at Gulf states including Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and the UAE, with many strikes aimed at US military sites.

The war spread beyond Iran's borders. Saudi Arabia renewed its fight against the Houthis in Yemen, and Israel began occupying southern Lebanon. After almost nine months, US operations have become occasional. The Hill said recent limited strikes hit Iran's Qeshm Island, which the administration calls defensive. The US has also offered a $20 million reward for information on Iran's nuclear programme. Iran denies that it is seeking nuclear weapons.

## Oil Prices Ease on Signs of De-escalation

Energy markets reacted fast. According to <a href="https://www.arabnews.com/energy/oil-falls-as-trump-downplays-prospect-of-escalation-with-iran-3010875" target="_blank" rel="noopener">a Reuters report on Tuesday's oil trade</a>, Brent crude was at $91.88 a barrel early on November 10, down 70 cents, after falling 5.85% in the previous session. US West Texas Intermediate crude stood at $86.07, down 78 cents.

### Key price points

- Brent touched a six-month high of $118 a barrel on October 30
- It has since dropped about 22% from that peak
- Monday's low was Brent's weakest level since August 31

Analysts were cautious. Gregory Brew of Eurasia Group told Reuters that Brent is unlikely to fall below $90 "without more durable signs of de-escalation".

## Why It Matters for India

India relies heavily on imported crude, so a lower Brent price usually helps by easing the import bill and pressure on the rupee and on fuel costs. Any reopening of normal traffic through the Strait of Hormuz would also matter to Indian refiners and shippers that use Gulf routes. However, the blockade remains, no deal has been signed, and both sides are still far apart on terms, so relief for Indian consumers depends on whether talks actually produce an agreement.

## What to Watch Next

The next steps are Qatar's mediation and whether Washington and Tehran agree on a framework for formal talks. Trump's comments point to a pause in large-scale fighting, but the blockade, the sanctions and Iran's list of demands show that the hardest part of ending the war is still ahead.
