Berkshire Hathaway, the conglomerate now led by chief executive Greg Abel, reported a 5.8% fall in third quarter operating earnings on Saturday, November 7, 2026, as a sharp drop in insurance profits outweighed gains at its railroad, energy and manufacturing businesses. At the same time, the company sharply stepped up its share buybacks, spending about $9.6 billion to repurchase its own stock in the July to September quarter. The results are being watched closely by investors worldwide because they show how Berkshire is being run under Abel, who held his first annual meeting as the company's leader in May.

## Berkshire Q3 2026 Earnings in Numbers

According to the <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001067983/000119312526452530/brka-ex99_1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">earnings release filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission</a>, operating earnings came in at $12.705 billion, down from $13.485 billion a year earlier. Net earnings attributable to shareholders fell more steeply, by about 28%, mainly because investment gains were much smaller than in the same quarter of 2025.

| Figure (US$ million) | Q3 2026 | Q3 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating earnings | 12,705 | 13,485 |
| Insurance underwriting | 1,352 | 2,369 |
| Insurance investment income | 2,734 | 3,181 |
| BNSF railroad | 1,729 | 1,449 |
| Berkshire Hathaway Energy | 1,612 | 1,489 |
| Manufacturing, service and retailing | 4,042 | 3,616 |
| Investment gains | 9,407 | 17,311 |
| Net earnings | 22,112 | 30,796 |

On a per share basis, net earnings were $15,502 for each Class A equivalent share and $10.33 for each Class B share, compared with $21,413 and $14.28 a year ago.

For the first nine months of 2026, the picture is better. Operating earnings rose to $37.034 billion from $34.286 billion, and net earnings climbed to $57.885 billion from $47.769 billion. The 2025 figure had included a $3.76 billion impairment charge on Berkshire's Kraft Heinz holding.

## Why Insurance Profits Slumped

Insurance has long been the heart of Berkshire, and it was the weak spot this quarter. Underwriting profit fell about 43%. A Bloomberg News report, republished by <a href="https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/11/09/891933.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Insurance Journal</a>, said car insurer GEICO faced higher losses and a jump in underwriting expenses, even though Berkshire had no major catastrophe losses in the period.

CFRA Research analyst Cathy Seifert described the underwriting decline as "a pretty significant deterioration" in a key business.

Investment income from the insurance arm also fell about 14% to $2.7 billion. Bloomberg said this was mainly because short-term interest rates were lower than a year earlier, which reduces the return Berkshire earns on its huge holdings of US Treasury bills. Interest rate expectations remain in focus, with the latest <a href="/us-jobs-report-october-2026-165000/">US jobs report for October</a> shaping views on the Federal Reserve's next move.

Insurance float, the money Berkshire holds from premiums before paying claims, stood at about $178.5 billion on September 30, up roughly $2.1 billion since the end of 2025.

## Railroad, Energy and Manufacturing Grow

The non-insurance businesses did better:

- **BNSF:** Earnings rose about 19% to $1.73 billion. Bloomberg reported that average revenue per car increased 10.5%, helped partly by higher fuel surcharges.
- **Berkshire Hathaway Energy:** Earnings rose about 8% to $1.61 billion, supported by higher utility sales volumes and customer rates in some regions. Bloomberg noted that about a third of the unit's earnings came from tax credits, including those linked to wind power.
- **Manufacturing, service and retailing:** This large group of companies grew earnings about 12% to $4.04 billion.

The "Other" line, which includes foreign currency effects, fell to $1.236 billion from $1.381 billion, partly because of a $343 million currency loss in the quarter.

## Buybacks and the $377 Billion Cash Pile

The biggest talking point is buybacks. Berkshire bought back about $9.6 billion of its own shares in the third quarter, taking the total for the first nine months of 2026 to about $14.4 billion. Bloomberg said the company restarted repurchases in the first quarter, its first in more than a year, after Abel judged that the shares were trading below their intrinsic value.

Berkshire's cash reached $377.4 billion at the end of September, according to Bloomberg. After subtracting $20.5 billion of Treasury bill purchases that had not yet settled, the figure was about $357 billion. The company was a small net seller of stocks, selling $426 million more in equities than it bought.

Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Matthew Palazola said stock valuations "probably were less attractive to Abel" in the quarter, which may explain why more cash went into buybacks than new stock purchases.

### Key highlights

- Operating earnings down 5.8% to $12.7 billion
- Insurance underwriting profit down about 43% on weaker GEICO results
- BNSF up about 19%, energy unit up about 8%
- Share buybacks of about $9.6 billion in Q3, $14.4 billion in nine months
- Cash pile of $377.4 billion at the end of September

## What Abel's Berkshire Signals for Investors

Berkshire itself warns in its release that investment gains in any single quarter are "usually meaningless" for judging performance, and urges investors to focus on operating results. On that measure, the quarter showed a company whose industrial businesses are growing but whose insurance engine has slowed.

Under Abel, Berkshire has also been more active in deals. Bloomberg listed recent moves including a $6.8 billion agreement to buy homebuilder Taylor Morrison Home Corp and a $930 million investment in Italian company Bending Spoons. For Indian investors who track global markets or hold US stocks through overseas investment routes, Berkshire's growing buybacks and large cash pile are a signal of how one of the world's best known investment companies views current valuations.
