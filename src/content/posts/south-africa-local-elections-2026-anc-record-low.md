South Africa's African National Congress (ANC) has recorded its weakest showing at the ballot box since the end of apartheid. Final results from the November 4 local government elections, announced by the Electoral Commission over the weekend, show the party winning 30.76% of the national vote, down almost 15 percentage points from 2021. The ANC is still the largest single party, but it failed to win an outright majority in any of the country's eight big metropolitan cities, and parties now have a short window to strike coalition deals before councils are formed.

## How the Main Parties Performed

The vote count was completed on Sunday, November 8. According to the final figures reported by South African business outlet BusinessTech, the ANC's share fell sharply while the Democratic Alliance (DA) and newer parties gained ground.

| Party | 2021 share | 2026 share | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANC | 45.59% | 30.76% | down 14.83 points |
| Democratic Alliance (DA) | 21.66% | 24.64% | up 2.98 points |
| uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) | did not contest | 9.77% | new |
| Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF) | 10.31% | 6.22% | down 4.09 points |
| ActionSA | 2.33% | 5.67% | up 3.34 points |

The rest of the vote was spread across hundreds of smaller parties and independent candidates. The MK party, led by former President Jacob Zuma, finished third nationally in its first local election. The DA said it had won the support of more than a million Black South Africans for the first time, according to a summary of the results by Democracy Now.

## DA Leads in Johannesburg, Tshwane and Cape Town

The biggest shift came in the cities. The DA finished first in five of the eight metros and kept its outright majority in Cape Town, while the ANC topped only Buffalo City and, by a very narrow margin, Mangaung.

| Metro | First place | Second place |
|---|---|---|
| Johannesburg | DA 34.79% | ANC 20.25% |
| Tshwane (Pretoria) | DA 34.37% | ActionSA 22.07% |
| Ekurhuleni | DA 30.68% | ANC 22.11% |
| Cape Town | DA 56.42% | ANC 13.95% |
| Nelson Mandela Bay | DA 44.17% | ANC 25.48% |
| eThekwini (Durban) | MK 38.29% | DA 25.45% |
| Buffalo City | ANC 42.02% | UDM 18.27% |
| Mangaung | ANC 33.91% | DA 33.49% |

Apart from Cape Town, no party crossed 50% in any metro, which means coalitions will decide who runs most of South Africa's largest cities, including the economic hub of Johannesburg. One voting station, where ballots were destroyed, will hold its vote again at a later date.

## Why ANC Support Keeps Falling

The ANC has been the country's leading party since the end of apartheid. Its support has been sliding for years as voters grow frustrated with corruption cases, weak delivery of basic services such as water and electricity, and high joblessness. <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/8/south-africas-anc-suffers-setback-in-local-elections" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera reported</a> that unemployment remains above 34%, more than three decades after apartheid ended.

The party's secretary-general, Fikile Mbalula, described the outcome as "catastrophic". He said the ANC would not go begging for positions in coalition talks and that the right response was to go home and put the party's house in order.

## Coalition Talks and the 14 Day Deadline

Parties have 14 days to negotiate before the new city councils sit. The DA has said it will talk to parties that share its stated commitment to clean and accountable government. Its Johannesburg mayoral candidate, Helen Zille, is seen as having a strong chance of becoming mayor if the arithmetic works out.

The talks will not be simple. Political analyst Daniel Silke told <a href="https://www.africanews.com/2026/11/08/anc-leads-south-africas-local-polls-but-records-all-time-election-low-final-results/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Africanews</a> that many voters in Gauteng province would probably favour a DA and ActionSA partnership, but he described the hostility between the two parties as extreme. In Tshwane, those two parties together hold a clear lead, so their relationship could decide the capital's government. In Durban, the MK party will be the main player.

The national picture adds another layer. Africanews noted that existing national coalition arrangements between parties could complicate the local negotiations, since rivals in one city may be partners elsewhere.

### Key highlights

- Voting day: November 4, 2026; final count completed November 8.
- ANC national share: 30.76%, its worst result since apartheid ended.
- DA rose to 24.64% and leads five of eight metros.
- MK party took 9.77% nationally and won eThekwini.
- Only Cape Town has an outright majority winner among the metros.
- Coalition deals must be reached within 14 days.

## What It Means Beyond South Africa

Johannesburg is South Africa's economic hub, and together with Tshwane and Ekurhuleni it forms the country's main business belt in Gauteng province. Who governs these cities will shape how water, electricity and other basic services are run, issues that voters clearly punished the ANC over. Investors and trading partners, including India, will be watching whether stable city governments emerge. For readers following other recent votes around the world, such as the close <a href="/mississippi-alaska-senate-races-still-counting/">US Senate counts in Mississippi and Alaska</a> and the coalition building in <a href="/herzog-taps-eisenkot-form-israel-government/">Israel</a>, South Africa is another example of fragmented results pushing parties into hard bargaining.

## A Fragmented Map for the Next Five Years

The 2026 local elections confirm that South Africa has moved from one party dominance to a crowded, competitive system. The ANC remains the single largest party, but it has lost the big cities and its bargaining power. The DA has gained ground and now faces the test of building stable coalitions, while the MK party has become the leading force in Durban. The deals struck over the next two weeks will show whether South Africa's cities can be governed smoothly, or whether drawn-out bargaining will slow decisions on services that residents say are already falling short.
