Canada and China have agreed to another round of tariff cuts, the latest step in a trade thaw that has moved quickly while Ottawa's relations with Washington remain tense. Prime Minister Mark Carney and President Xi Jinping met on Tuesday, November 17, in Shenzhen as the APEC summit began. China will ease tariffs on about C$400 million of Canadian pork and seafood, and Canada will keep tariff exemptions on some Chinese steel and aluminium for two more years.

The deal is small in money terms, but it matters for what it signals: a G7 country that is in a tariff fight with the United States is widening its trade with China at the same time.

## Who Gave What in Shenzhen

The [Canadian Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/11/17/prime-minister-carney-advances-canadas-strategic-partnership-china) described the package as an extension and expansion of a preliminary arrangement the two sides reached in January. Here is the trade off side by side.

| China's concessions | Canada's concessions |
|---|---|
| Extra tariff relief on about C$116 million of frozen pork cuts | Two year extension of remission measures on certain Chinese steel and aluminium products that were due to expire at the end of 2026 |
| Extra relief on about C$285 million of fish and seafood, including cold water shrimp and prawns, geoduck and other molluscs, sea cucumber and Greenland halibut | Remissions widened to 32 more steel and aluminium tariff lines where Canada says domestic supply is short or missing |
| Relief on canola meal, peas, lobster and crab extended through the end of 2028 (agreed on November 4) | No change to the January electric vehicle quota |
| Market access restored for Canadian beef from 20 registered meat plants (BNN Bloomberg) | |

Alongside the tariff measures, Ottawa announced a large Canadian trade mission to China in 2027, a "Year of e-Commerce" to help small firms sell on Chinese platforms, scholarships worth C$850,000 for 850 Canadian students to study in China, and new Air Canada flights between Vancouver and Guangzhou from May 2027.

Xi told Carney the improved partnership was "the right choice", according to the Associated Press.

## How the Trade Fight Started

The current thaw makes more sense against the two years of tit for tat that came before it.

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| October 1, 2024 | Canada puts a 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles |
| October 15, 2024 | Canada adds a 25% tariff on Chinese steel and aluminium |
| March 20, 2025 | China puts 100% tariffs on Canadian canola oil, meal and peas, and 25% on pork and seafood |
| October 31, 2025 | Carney and Xi meet at APEC in South Korea |
| January 16, 2026 | Preliminary deal in Beijing: EV tariff cut to 6.1% for up to 49,000 cars a year; China cuts canola and farm tariffs |
| November 4, 2026 | Farm and seafood relief extended to end of 2028 |
| November 17, 2026 | Pork and seafood relief added in Shenzhen |

According to the Canadian government, the January arrangement had already restored market access for more than 90% of the trade hit by Chinese tariff action, worth nearly C$7.5 billion in exports. Parliamentary secretary Kody Blois said the new package brings the total to just under C$8 billion.

## The Numbers Behind Canada's China Push

Carney says Canada is ahead of schedule on its goal of lifting exports to China by 50% by the end of the decade. The figures released this week give a sense of scale:

- Two way merchandise trade in 2025: C$125.6 billion, up 5.6% from 2024.
- Canadian merchandise exports to China in 2025: C$35.1 billion.
- Exports in the first half of 2026: C$21.5 billion, up 31.4% year on year, according to Global Affairs Canada figures reported by [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/politics/2026/11/17/pm-carney-xi-set-to-meet-amid-push-for-trade-concessions-and-chinese-investment/).
- Crude oil shipments to China: up about 37% this year.
- China's direct investment stock in Canada: C$43.4 billion, making it the fourth largest investor country.

For Canadian pork producers the relief is especially welcome, since China is one of their most important export markets. Seafood exporters gain from the new shrimp, prawn, mollusc and halibut lines, which make up most of the package by value.

## Why Washington Is Paying Attention

The timing is the real story. Canada's trade talks with the United States broke down in August, and US tariffs have hurt Canadian autos, steel and aluminium, as [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/17/china-canada-expand-tariff-relief-after-carney-xi-meeting) reported. A day before meeting Xi, Carney said Canada could turn to Chinese carmakers if it cannot keep an integrated North American auto industry with the US. He added that any such investment would have to include real manufacturing, not just assembly of imported kits.

White House trade adviser Peter Navarro hit back on social media, asking whether Carney was trying to play China off the US. "You should be far more worried China is going to eat Canada alive," he wrote, as quoted by BNN Bloomberg.

Carney, for his part, says he has spoken with President Donald Trump and senior officials, and that differences remain but progress is being made. He also met Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum in Shenzhen, where both reaffirmed plans to renew the USMCA trade pact.

### Issues the deal does not touch

Canada says closer trade does not mean it has set aside concerns over foreign interference, cybersecurity, human rights and national security. Canadian tariffs on Chinese steel stay in place outside the remission lines, and no new announcement was made on electric vehicles.

## What to Watch After APEC

The bigger test comes on two fronts. First, whether Chinese automakers take up Carney's offer and commit to building plants, which would be a sharp turn for a market tied so closely to the US. Second, whether a Canada US deal emerges, which could shape how far Ottawa is willing to go with Beijing. The APEC week in Shenzhen is also hosting a [planned Trump, Xi and Putin meeting](/trump-xi-putin-trilateral-november-19-confirmed/), so trade and security talks there are closely linked.
