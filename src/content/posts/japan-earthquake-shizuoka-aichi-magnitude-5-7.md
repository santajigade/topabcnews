A magnitude 5.7 earthquake shook central Japan in the early hours of Sunday, November 8, 2026, injuring more than 20 people across Shizuoka and Aichi prefectures. The quake struck at 1:04 am local time (9:34 pm IST on Saturday) with its centre in western Shizuoka Prefecture at a depth of about 40 km, according to the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA). No tsunami warning was issued, and the operator of the nearby Hamaoka nuclear plant reported no problems.

## Where the Shizuoka Earthquake Struck

The epicentre lay in the western part of Shizuoka Prefecture, the coastal region between Tokyo and Nagoya that is home to the city of Hamamatsu. The shaking was felt across a wide stretch of central and western Japan, including Hamamatsu, the historic cities of Kyoto and Nara, Gifu Prefecture and Yamanashi Prefecture, where Mount Fuji stands. It was not felt in Tokyo, <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/8/earthquake-hits-central-japan-injuring-more-than-20-people" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera reported</a>.

TRT World, citing reports from Japan, said it was the strongest quake to hit Aichi Prefecture in about three decades.

## Injuries and Damage in Aichi and Shizuoka

Local authorities in Aichi and Shizuoka reported more than 20 injuries, according to the Kyodo news agency. The Associated Press reported that some people were hurt when they fell on stairs while rushing out of buildings in the middle of the night.

Damage appears to have been limited mostly to broken glass and fallen objects. Store windows shattered and glass covered parts of some streets. In one bookstore, books were thrown from the shelves. In Toyohashi city in Aichi, windows were broken at an imported car dealership, TRT World reported. There were no reports of deaths in the coverage available on Sunday.

The timing made the experience more frightening for many residents. Because the quake hit just after 1 am, most people were asleep when the shaking began, and many hurried out of homes and buildings in the dark. Reports of injuries from falls on stairs show how the rush to get outside can itself be risky during a night time quake. Authorities had not released a full damage assessment by Sunday, and the injury count could still change as local offices complete their checks in the affected towns.

## Shinkansen Delays and Nuclear Plant Checks

Rail operators moved quickly to check their lines. Some local train services were suspended after the quake, and Shinkansen bullet trains ran at slower speeds in the affected area as a precaution. JR Central said Tokaido Shinkansen trains were delayed between Shizuoka and Gifu-Hashima stations while safety checks were carried out.

Attention also turned to the Hamaoka nuclear power plant, which sits on the Shizuoka coast. Its operator, Chubu Electric Power, said no abnormalities were found at the facility.

## JMA Warns of Aftershocks and Landslide Risk

The JMA told residents to stay alert for further strong shaking over the coming week, particularly in the next two to three days. Agency official Ayataka Ebita urged people to watch for "mudslides, falling rocks and the possible effects from rainfall", since ground loosened by shaking can give way more easily in wet weather. Updates and official warnings are published on the <a href="https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/indexe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Japan Meteorological Agency website</a>.

Separately, a smaller magnitude 4.3 quake was recorded in Kumamoto in southwestern Japan on Sunday morning, according to TRT World.

## Why the Nankai Trough Is Being Watched

Any quake in this part of Japan draws attention because of the Nankai Trough, an undersea trench where scientists say a very large earthquake could one day occur. Ebita said the JMA had not detected any apparent change in the Nankai Trough so far after Sunday's quake.

Japan lies on the Pacific "Ring of Fire", a belt of intense seismic and volcanic activity around the Pacific Ocean, which makes earthquakes a regular part of life in the country.

## What Travellers to Central Japan Should Know

Central Japan, including Kyoto, Nara and the Tokaido Shinkansen corridor, is on the itinerary of many foreign tourists, including visitors from India. With the JMA warning of possible strong aftershocks over the next few days, travellers in the region should follow instructions from local authorities and rail operators, keep an eye on train service updates, and know the nearest evacuation point at their hotel.

For now, the quake has caused injuries and travel disruption but no reported deaths, and officials say the key risk in the coming days is further shaking, especially in hilly areas where rain could trigger landslides.
