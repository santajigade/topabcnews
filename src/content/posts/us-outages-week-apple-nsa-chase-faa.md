In the space of six days, a string of outages has knocked out services at Microsoft, the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), JPMorgan Chase, United Airlines, Apple and even a classified network used by the US National Security Agency (NSA). The latest came on Tuesday, November 17, when iCloud sign-in, the App Store and Apple Pay failed for many users late that night in India. US officials say they have found no evidence of a coordinated cyberattack, and every company involved has blamed its own technical fault. Here is what failed, when, and what each one said caused it.

## Apple Was the Latest to Go Down

Users began reporting problems with Apple services around midday in the US on Tuesday. Tech site 9to5Mac flagged the outage at 12:33 pm Eastern Time, which was 11:03 pm IST. People were signed out of iCloud on iPhones and Macs without warning and then could not verify their accounts. The App Store would not load or download apps, some new iPhones could not be activated, and developers lost access to App Store Connect.

According to Digit, reports on Downdetector passed 2,300 each for Apple Support and Apple Pay, with iCloud crossing 1,300 by 11:05 pm IST. Apple later said the trouble began after an internal update and was not a cyberattack. By Tuesday afternoon in the US, most services on <a href="https://www.apple.com/support/systemstatus/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Apple's system status page</a> had turned green again, though iCloud sign-in, Apple School Manager and AppleCare on Device were still flagged for a while.

## Why Officials Say It Was Not a Hack

The White House, the Department of Homeland Security and the NSA have all said there is no sign of an attack. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton put the problems down to system upgrades and handoffs between systems that did not come back as expected, BNN Bloomberg reported. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the faulty FAA router began broadcasting into the network and overloaded it, and that security agencies told him "we weren't hacked." The FAA has paused further router installations and switched off the Juniper routers already put in.

Two cyber incidents did happen during the same week, but officials treat them as separate. Optimizely, a web platform used by clients such as Zoom, BBC and CNN, found malware planted in some clients' code on Monday evening and says the issue is now resolved. BNN Bloomberg also noted a cyberattack on Norwegian energy firm Equinor and a hack of the International Ice Hockey Federation's website.

Not everyone is satisfied. Senator Josh Hawley said people "are in the dark" and demanded answers, while House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said it was not yet clear whether every disruption was purely technical. Senate Democrats have asked for router replacements across the air traffic system to be paused. Speaking in Manila, where he is attending the <a href="/asean-summit-manila-opens-modi-trump-due/">ASEAN summit</a>, President Donald Trump played the problems down, saying "things like that happen. It's fine."

## How to Read a Cluster of Outages

A run of failures in one week naturally looks suspicious, but the explanations so far point to a common pattern rather than a common attacker. Almost every stated cause is a change the company made itself:

- **Upgrades and updates.** Microsoft's network upgrade, Apple's internal update and the FAA's new router were all planned changes that went wrong.
- **Maintenance windows.** The NSA disruption happened during scheduled maintenance, the time when systems are most exposed to human error.
- **Software bugs in old hardware.** Chase pointed to a bug in an on-premise system, not its cloud services.

Ritesh Kotak, a cybersecurity analyst quoted by BNN Bloomberg, called them "isolated incidents" but said forensic audits would be needed to confirm causes. Recent job cuts at some firms have drawn attention, though there is no evidence linking layoffs to these failures.

Investors did react. JPMorgan shares fell nearly 5% over the week, large bank stocks dropped 1% to 2% on both Monday and Tuesday, and United fell about 3% on Tuesday, according to Yahoo Finance. Apple slipped less than 1%.

## What to Do When an App You Rely on Fails

Outages like Apple's hit Indian users too, since iCloud and the App Store are global services. If a major app stops working, these steps help you avoid making things worse:

1. **Check the company's status page first.** Apple, Microsoft and most banks publish live service health pages. Downdetector can show whether others are seeing the same problem.
2. **Do not reset passwords or remove accounts in a panic.** If the fault is on the company's servers, signing out or deleting your account from a phone can lock you out further once service returns.
3. **Hold off on repeated payment attempts.** A failed card or wallet payment during an outage can still show as pending; check your bank statement before trying again.
4. **Ignore "fix" links on social media.** Phishing messages that pretend to restore your account often appear during outages.
5. **Keep a backup** payment method and a second way to reach colleagues.

In India, the RBI has its own rules on how fast payment firms must report cyber incidents, which we explained in our story on <a href="/rbi-cyber-rules-payment-apps-six-hour-reporting/">RBI cyber rules for payment apps</a>.

## What Comes Next

Most of the companies involved have promised fuller reviews. Microsoft's final report on its outage is still pending, the FAA's root cause investigation continues, and pressure is growing in Congress for a public explanation of the NSA failure. Until those reports are published, the official position is that these were separate accidents that happened to land in the same week, not a single attack.
