Indian households paid 42.7% of the country's total health bill out of their own pockets in 2023-24, down from 64.2% a decade earlier, according to the National Health Accounts (NHA) estimates released by the Union Health Ministry on November 15. Over the same period, government spending on health more than tripled, from about Rs 1.30 lakh crore in 2013-14 to Rs 4.14 lakh crore. The report is the 11th round of NHA estimates and is the most complete official picture of who pays for healthcare in India.

## Ten Years of Health Spending in One Table

The NHA is prepared by the National Health Accounts Technical Secretariat at the <a href="https://nhsrcindia.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Health Systems Resource Centre</a>, using the international System of Health Accounts 2011 framework. Here is how the main indicators have moved, based on figures reported by PTI and Upstox News.

| Indicator | 2013-14 | 2023-24 |
|---|---|---|
| Government health expenditure | About Rs 1.30 lakh crore | Rs 4,14,196 crore |
| Out-of-pocket share of total spending | 64.2% | 42.7% |
| Government share of total spending | 28.6% | 43.5% |
| Per person government spending | Rs 1,042 | Rs 2,959 |
| Social security share | 6% | 11% |
| Private health insurance share | 3.4% | 10.2% |

The out-of-pocket share was 43.4% in 2022-23, so the fall in the latest year was just 0.7 percentage points, which suggests the pace of improvement is slowing.

## What Rs 6,801 a Head Really Means for a Family

The headline numbers are large, so it helps to bring them down to the level of one person. Total health expenditure in 2023-24 was Rs 9,52,122 crore, or Rs 6,801 per person, according to Upstox News. Using the official shares, a rough split looks like this:

| Who paid, per person, 2023-24 | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total health spending | Rs 6,801 |
| Paid by government (Centre and states) | Rs 2,959 |
| Paid directly by households (42.66% share) | About Rs 2,900 (our calculation) |

For a family of four, that works out to roughly Rs 11,600 a year paid straight from the household budget at clinics, pharmacies and hospitals, on average. Averages hide a lot: a single hospital stay can cost many times more, while healthy households may spend little.

The rise of insurance is the other big shift. Private health insurance now covers 10.2% of total spending, or Rs 97,167 crore, up from 3.4% in 2013-14. Government-financed insurance schemes accounted for Rs 33,385 crore. Social security spending, which the ministry says includes schemes like <a href="https://pmjay.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY</a>, state insurance schemes and medical costs for government employees, doubled its share from 6% to 11%.

## Where Your State Stands

The national average hides wide gaps between states. These are the state figures reported by Upstox News from the NHA data.

| State | Out-of-pocket share | Government share |
|---|---|---|
| Uttar Pradesh | 67.1% | 27.5% |
| Kerala | 65.2% | 25% |
| West Bengal | 63% | 30% |
| Bihar | 47% | 48.2% |
| Uttarakhand | 26.7% | 66% |

Among other states, Punjab's out-of-pocket share was 60.7%, while Karnataka (28.5%) and Jammu and Kashmir (33.5%) were among the lowest. Assam (59.5%) and Odisha (57.9%) were among the states where government paid the largest share.

Kerala stands out. It has the highest per person health spending in the country at Rs 13,605, followed by Himachal Pradesh at Rs 9,739 and Maharashtra at Rs 9,074, yet households there still pay about 65% of the bill themselves. High spending, in other words, does not automatically mean high public support. Uttarakhand shows the opposite pattern, with the government paying about two thirds of all health costs.

In absolute terms, Uttar Pradesh had the largest total health spending at Rs 1,20,632 crore, followed by Maharashtra at Rs 1,15,238 crore and West Bengal at Rs 68,549 crore.

## The Gap the Report Does Not Close

Government health expenditure in 2023-24 was 1.43% of GDP and 4.83% of total general government spending, according to Upstox News. Total health expenditure, public and private combined, was 3.29% of GDP. States carry most of the public load, accounting for 67.9% of government health spending against the Centre's 32.1%.

The Health Ministry said in its statement that the steady fall in out-of-pocket costs reflects "the expanding role of government-supported healthcare services." It credited programmes such as Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, free drugs and diagnostics, the PM National Dialysis Programme, Jan Aushadhi stores and AMRIT pharmacies. Government spending on primary healthcare alone was about Rs 1.53 lakh crore in 2023-24.

Still, an out-of-pocket share above 40% means households remain the single largest payer for healthcare alongside the government. With the latest annual drop under one percentage point, the next round of NHA data will show whether the downward trend can keep its earlier speed.
