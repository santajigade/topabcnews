Sheikh Hasina, Bangladesh's former prime minister, plans to fly home from India on December 16, her spokesperson said in a statement reported by Reuters on November 24. It is the first time she has named a date. She has asked the Bangladesh government to send national carrier Biman Bangladesh Airlines to bring her from Delhi to Dhaka. But a Bangladeshi war crimes court sentenced her to death in absentia in November 2025, and a minister told Reuters she would be arrested and jailed if she lands.

## What Hasina's Spokesperson Asked For

The statement came from her spokesperson, Syed Mozammel Ali. It said Hasina was flown to India on a Bangladesh aircraft on August 5, 2024, and asked the government to "arrange for her air transport from Delhi back to Dhaka".

December 16 is the anniversary of the end of Bangladesh's 1971 liberation war with Pakistan. Hasina had first spoken of a December return in a July interview with Reuters, and she repeated the plan at a Foreign Correspondents' Club of South Asia event in Delhi on August 5, saying the exact date would come later.

On November 9 her lawyers applied at the Bangladesh embassy in New Delhi for new passports, according to a Reuters report carried by Khaleej Times. The interim government that took over after her fall had revoked both her passport and her national ID.

## Dhaka's Answer: Arrest on Arrival

A minister, who spoke to Reuters on Sunday and is not named in the report, said Hasina would be arrested and jailed on her return. The same minister said there had been no official talks with India about an aircraft.

On the passport request, the government said it does not issue a passport "to a terrorist" and urged New Delhi to hand her over, pointing to the extradition treaty between the two countries. Reuters reports that violence has risen in Bangladesh since she sought the new passport.

The Bangladesh Nationalist Party won the February 2026 general election, and Tarique Rahman, son of former prime minister Khaleda Zia, is now prime minister. The Awami League, which Hasina led, is banned and could not contest that vote.

## The Case Against Her

The death sentence came from a Bangladeshi war crimes court in November 2025. She was tried in absentia and found guilty of crimes against humanity over the crackdown on the 2024 uprising, including incitement, ordering killings and failing to prevent atrocities, as <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/ex-pm-sheikh-hasina-announces-her-plan-to-return-to-bangladesh" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera reported in August</a>. A UN estimate puts the death toll from the crackdown at up to 1,400. Hasina denies the charges.

"They may arrest me on my return, they may even kill me," she told Reuters in July, in remarks carried by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/10/condemned-ex-pm-hasina-plans-december-return-to-bangladesh" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>. She has also asked for the ban on her party to be lifted.

## Why India Is in the Middle

Hasina has lived in India since August 2024. Three questions now sit with New Delhi, and none has a public answer yet:

1. **The extradition request.** Dhaka has asked more than once for her to be handed over. India's foreign ministry said in April that it was examining the request and wanted to engage constructively with the new government. It has not said more since, and it did not respond to Reuters' request for comment in November.
2. **Her public activity.** When she addressed journalists in Delhi in August, foreign ministry spokesman Randhir Jaiswal distanced the government from the event: "The government has no involvement whatsoever in it."
3. **The flight itself.** A Biman aircraft would need Dhaka's approval. With the minister saying no talks have taken place, it is not clear how she would travel, or whether India would allow or block any departure.

India is also dealing with Tarique Rahman's government, and invited him to a BRICS summit in September. Any decision on Hasina will be weighed against that relationship. ## What to Watch Before December 16

Three things will shape the next three weeks: whether Dhaka formally answers the flight request, whether New Delhi says anything new on extradition, and whether her return draws crowds. Reuters and Khaleej Times both note that tension is high and that a large show of support could lead to unrest. Hasina has said she wants to face the courts at home. The government says she will be arrested as soon as she arrives.
