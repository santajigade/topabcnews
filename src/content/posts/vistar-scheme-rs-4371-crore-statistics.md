The Union Cabinet, chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, has approved a new central sector scheme called VISTAR, short for Viksit Bharat Initiative for Statistical Transformation, Analytics and Research. Cleared on Wednesday, November 4, the scheme has an outlay of Rs 4,371.10 crore and will run for five years, from 2026-27 to 2030-31. Its aim is to give India a faster, more detailed and AI-ready system of official statistics, which the government says is needed for better policy making on the road to Viksit Bharat 2047.

## What the VISTAR Scheme Is Meant to Fix

Official statistics such as GDP, inflation, industrial output and employment numbers guide decisions on interest rates, budgets, welfare schemes and investment. They are produced mainly by the <a href="https://www.mospi.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation</a> (MoSPI). VISTAR will cover all of MoSPI's statistical and data management activities under one umbrella.

According to the <a href="https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/cabinet-approves-viksit-bharat-initiative-for-statistical-transformation-analytics-research-vistar-a-central-sector-scheme-for-the-period-2026-27-to-2030-31/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official Cabinet release</a>, the scheme is designed to build a "dynamic, real-time and future-ready" statistical architecture. The focus is on three things: releasing data more quickly, offering finer detail such as district and city figures, and making datasets from different sources work together. The government also wants datasets that are harmonised and ready to be used with artificial intelligence tools.

A single combined scheme also gives the ministry room to shift money between activities as urgent policy needs come up, rather than being tied to rigid separate budget lines.

## The Three Parts of VISTAR

The scheme has three main components.

### 1. Statistical operations and a stronger data ecosystem

This is the core of the plan. MoSPI will conduct 59 surveys during the 16th Finance Commission cycle, up from 43 major surveys earlier. It will digitise statistical processes and use AI and machine learning to process and analyse data.

New indices are also planned, including a Headline Index of Services Production and an Index of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises. The ministry will tap alternate data sources such as mobile phone data, retail scanner data and e-commerce data. This part also covers data harmonisation and capacity building through an internship scheme for students and support for research proposals.

### 2. Support for Statistical Strengthening (SSS 2.0)

States and Union Territories will get funds under a redesigned, deliverable-linked framework called SSS 2.0. Money will be tied to results. The goal is to help states produce granular data, including district-level and city-level estimates, and to compile State and District GDP figures and a state-level Index of Industrial Production (IIP).

### 3. The 8th Economic Census

The third part funds the 8th Economic Census, which MoSPI will conduct with states and UTs. It will count non-farm establishments and the people who work in them, right down to the Gram Panchayat and ward level. The census will help assess the unorganised sector and its contribution to GDP, and will serve as the base for a National Statistical Business Register. A start date for the census fieldwork has not been announced yet.

## How Better Data Can Help Ordinary Citizens

Statistics may seem distant from daily life, but they shape many decisions that affect households. Welfare schemes are targeted using survey data on income, jobs and consumption. Interest rate decisions by the Reserve Bank of India depend on inflation and growth numbers. States use district data to decide where to build schools, roads and hospitals.

If states can produce reliable district-level and city-level estimates, local governments can plan with far more precision. A detailed Economic Census can show how many small shops, workshops and home businesses exist in each area, which matters for credit schemes aimed at micro and small enterprises. New indices for services and unincorporated enterprises could also give a clearer, more regular picture of parts of the economy that are harder to track.

Students may benefit too. The internship scheme and research support under VISTAR can open opportunities for those studying statistics, economics and data science.

## How VISTAR Fits With Other Recent Cabinet Decisions

VISTAR is part of a series of approvals made by the Cabinet in early November. In the same period, the government cleared a [Rs 2,814 crore scheme for critical mineral processing](/critical-mineral-processing-scheme-lithium-nickel/) and a [Rs 14,500 crore viability gap funding plan for energy storage](/energy-storage-vgf-scheme-rs-14500-crore/). Unlike those, VISTAR is not about building physical capacity. It is about improving the data that will be used to measure the results of such programmes. Media coverage of the approval also highlighted the push to use mobile and e-commerce data in official numbers.

## What Comes Next for the Statistics Overhaul

MoSPI will now roll out the scheme from the 2026-27 financial year, with states joining through the SSS 2.0 framework. Key things to watch include the launch dates of the new services and informal sector indices, the schedule for the 8th Economic Census, and how quickly states start publishing district-level GDP estimates. Updates are expected on the official websites of MoSPI and the Press Information Bureau.
