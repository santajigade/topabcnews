Royal Enfield has pulled the covers off its biggest adventure motorcycle yet. The new Himalayan 750 made its global debut at <a href="https://www.eicma.it/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">EICMA 2026</a>, the international motorcycle show in Milan, on November 3, and it sits above the popular Himalayan 450 as the brand's new flagship tourer for long distance and off road riding. It is powered by a new 743cc parallel twin engine and comes loaded with features such as cruise control, traction control and adjustable Showa suspension. Indian prices have not been announced yet, but the bike is expected to make its India debut at the company's Motoverse festival later this month.

For riders who have grown up on the Bullet and Classic, this is a big step. The brand that recently refreshed its smaller models, like the <a href="/royal-enfield-bullet-250-2025/">Bullet 250</a>, is now aiming at buyers who want a serious big touring bike.

## A New 743cc Twin With More Pull

The Himalayan 750 uses a 743cc parallel twin engine. It is based on Royal Enfield's familiar 650cc twin, but the company says about 90 percent of the parts are new. Despite the bigger size, the engine weighs roughly the same as the 650 unit.

The motor makes 55 PS at 7,000 rpm and 63 Nm of torque at 5,000 rpm. More importantly for touring riders, about 90 percent of the peak torque is available from just 3,500 rpm, so the bike should feel strong at low and mid speeds without constant gear changes. Power goes through a six speed gearbox with a slip and assist clutch, which makes the clutch lever lighter and helps during hard downshifts.

### Engine and gearbox at a glance

- Engine: 743cc parallel twin
- Power: 55 PS at 7,000 rpm
- Torque: 63 Nm at 5,000 rpm
- Compression ratio: 9.5:1
- Gearbox: six speed with slip and assist clutch

## Showa Suspension and a New Steel Frame

Underneath, the Himalayan 750 gets an all new steel frame. Suspension comes from Showa, with a 43 mm inverted fork at the front and a linked monoshock at the rear. Both ends are adjustable. Wheel travel is 190 mm at the front and 175 mm at the rear, slightly less than the 200 mm at both ends on the Himalayan 450.

Braking is handled by twin 280 mm discs up front and a 255 mm disc at the rear, with dual channel cornering ABS. The bike rides on a 19 inch front wheel and a 17 inch rear wheel. Buyers can choose between tubeless spoke wheels and alloy wheels.

At 224 kg, the 750 is about 30 kg heavier than the Himalayan 450, so it is clearly built more for highway touring with luggage than for tight trail riding.

## Electronics and Touring Features on the Himalayan 750

This is the most feature rich Royal Enfield so far. A 4 inch round TFT screen shows navigation through Google Maps, along with call and SMS alerts, and it can receive over the air software updates. The screen is controlled with a small joystick on the left switch cube, while the cruise control buttons sit on the right.

A ride by wire throttle brings five riding modes: Road, Road Sport, Rain, Trail and Custom. There is also lean sensitive traction control that can be set to Low, Medium or High, or switched off. Other features include full LED lighting, tyre pressure monitoring, two USB Type C charging ports and self cancelling indicators.

## Variants and Colours

Royal Enfield will offer the Himalayan 750 in three versions, as shown at EICMA:

- **Top variant:** comes with Givi panniers, a top box, handguards and heated grips as standard
- **Spoke wheel variant:** in Siberia White or Viking Bronze
- **Alloy wheel variant:** in Aztec Green or Tectonic Black

The full colour list is Antarctic Silver, Siberia White, Aztec Green, Viking Bronze and Tectonic Black. Spoke wheel bikes run on Vredestein Centauro XR tyres, while the alloy wheel version uses Ceat Crossrad RT tyres.

## Himalayan 750 India Launch and Price Expectations

In the UK, the Himalayan 750 starts at GBP 6,999. Royal Enfield has not yet revealed Indian prices. The bike is expected to be shown to Indian buyers at Motoverse, the brand's annual riding festival, towards the end of November 2026, according to Indian auto media reports. Prices should become clear around that time.

Once launched, it will take on middleweight adventure and touring bikes such as the Honda NX500 and Kawasaki Versys 650. Details on bookings and dealer arrivals are expected on the official <a href="https://www.royalenfield.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Royal Enfield website</a> closer to launch.

With a strong twin engine, proper touring electronics and Royal Enfield's wide service network, the Himalayan 750 could give Indian riders a new option in the big adventure bike space. Its success will depend heavily on how aggressively the company prices it.
