Meta is expanding its artificial intelligence based age estimation system to users around the world, starting this month. The company announced on Monday, November 9, 2026, that the tool will look for people it suspects are teenagers, even if they signed up with an adult birthday, and move them into its Teen Account protections. Instagram gets the change first, with Facebook and Threads to follow later in 2026. The move comes after large legal settlements in the US and growing pressure from governments that want platforms to keep young users safer.

## How Meta's AI Age Estimation Works

Many teens type in a false birth date when they create an account. That lets them skip the limits and parental controls meant for younger users. Meta's system tries to close that gap.

According to <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/9/meta-rolls-out-automated-age-checks-for-teens-globally" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera's report on the announcement</a>, the AI studies how a person behaves on the app and what they post to judge their likely age. Meta said it uses the technology "to find people we suspect to be teens, even if they use an adult birthday."

TNGlobal reported that the system relies on profile and account signals to estimate whether someone is between 13 and 17. If the tool decides a user is probably a teen, the account is shifted into age-appropriate settings.

In Australia, Meta also uses Apple's age range signal. A parent sets up a child's iPhone, Apple's system assigns an age range, and that range is shared with apps when they are downloaded. Meta said it uses this signal voluntarily and keeps its own AI estimation as a backstop.

## What Changes for Teen Accounts

Teen Accounts limit who can contact a young user, what kind of content they see and how long they spend on the app. Based on the Al Jazeera report, the main features include:

- Children under 13 are not allowed on the apps
- Teens are prompted to leave Instagram after 60 minutes of activity
- Notifications are muted at night
- A two-hour daily usage limit applies
- Features that drive "social comparison", such as seeing likes on other teen accounts, are blocked

Meta said it tested the age estimation tool in several countries, including the US, the UK, Canada and Australia, before taking it global. The company first described those trials in <a href="https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/meta-parents-new-technology-enroll-teens-teen-accounts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">its April 2025 post on enrolling more teens into Teen Accounts</a>, which also noted that users placed in teen settings by mistake can change them. Parents in the US who set up Instagram for their teens will be told about the new measures this month.

## The $18 Billion Settlement Behind the Push

The global rollout follows major legal trouble in the US. In August 2026, Meta agreed to pay $18 billion to US states and to change how teens use its platforms. The states had alleged that Meta's apps encourage addictive use, fail to check ages properly and push harmful content.

### Key settlement terms

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Daily limit | Under 18 users capped at two hours a day |
| Night curfew | No use from midnight to 6 am |
| School hours | Push notifications blocked from 8 am to 3 pm |
| Break prompts | After every 15 minutes of continuous use, and at 60 and 90 minutes of daily use |
| Parent alerts | When a teen first messages an adult, plus activity updates |

As part of that deal, Meta also agreed to get better at identifying minors. TNGlobal reported that Meta is strengthening its systems to find accounts that may belong to children under 13 under an agreement with 52 US state and territorial attorneys general.

## Questions Over Accuracy

Meta says early results are encouraging, but it has not published accuracy rates, false positive figures or country-level rollout numbers, according to TNGlobal. There is also no independent review of the tool so far. That leaves open questions about how often adults may be wrongly flagged as teens, and how often teens will still slip through.

Meta has called on other major platforms, including TikTok and YouTube, to adopt similar checks. We have earlier looked at the wider regulatory pressure on short video apps in our piece on <a href="/whats-next-for-tiktok-in-the-us/">what's next for TikTok in the US</a>.

## What It Means for Users in India and Elsewhere

Governments across Asia Pacific and other regions are bringing in or weighing stricter online safety and age assurance rules, TNGlobal noted. Approaches differ: some countries want broad identity checks, while others let platforms choose how to verify age. Australia has gone furthest, with a law that bars under-16s from social media. Reddit and the Digital Freedom Project have filed a legal challenge against that law this month.

Since the rollout is global, Indian teens on Instagram who registered with an adult birth date could also be moved into Teen Account settings once the tool reaches them. Meta has not given a country-by-country timeline. For parents, the change adds another layer of protection, but it does not replace setting up supervision tools and talking to children about how they use social media.

## The Road Ahead for Meta

Meta says the expansion should cover "millions more teens" once fully in place. With Facebook and Threads next in line, the company is betting that automated age checks can satisfy regulators and parents. Whether the system works as well as Meta claims will become clearer once outside researchers and regulators can test it.
