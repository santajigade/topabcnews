A three-storey building in the Flatbush neighbourhood of Brooklyn, New York, collapsed into a neighbouring construction pit just after 4 pm local time on Wednesday, November 11 (around 2:30 am IST on Thursday). At least two people were killed and 17 were injured, seven of them children, according to New York City officials. <a href="https://abcnews.com/US/rescue-operations-underway-after-building-collapses-brooklyn-new/story?id=138059620" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Rescue operations</a> continued through the night because officials still do not know how many people were inside when the building came down.

## What Happened on Flatbush Avenue

According to <a href="https://abc7ny.com/post/flatbush-brooklyn-building-collapse-leaves-2-dead-17-injured-search-continues/20035354/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ABC7 New York</a>, the building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue fell into an open construction site at 2625 Bedford Avenue, close to the junction of the two avenues. Workers were digging at the site when the collapse took place.

The structure was a mixed-use building, with apartments on the upper floors and businesses at street level. A one-storey building on the neighbouring plot had earlier been demolished to make way for a new project, and the collapsed building had been standing at the edge of that excavated lot. When it fell, bricks and wood filled the pit.

A building next door at 1323 Flatbush Avenue was also damaged. One of its outer walls was torn away, leaving apartments open to the street, but the building itself remained standing.

## Casualties and the Injured

City officials gave the following breakdown of the injured on Thursday:

| Condition | Number of people |
|---|---|
| Killed | 2 |
| Critical condition | 4 |
| Serious, not life-threatening | 4 |
| Minor injuries (including one firefighter) | 9 |
| Total injured | 17 |

FDNY Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore said seven of the victims were children. Officials have not shared the condition of the children. Several children and a pregnant woman were among those pulled from the debris. Most of the injured were taken to Kings County Hospital, while others were sent to nearby hospitals.

One resident, Anderson Velez, told the Associated Press that his wife and two young children had been trapped under the rubble. He later learned that all three had been rescued alive.

## Inside the Rescue Operation

About 250 firefighters worked at the scene on Wednesday evening, according to an AP report. Crews dug through broken brick and timber by hand and with power saws, and used search dogs and a drone to look for survivors. Around 6:30 pm, firefighters lifted a living person out of a hole in the debris and carried them to an ambulance on a stretcher.

By Thursday, rescuers had brought in hand tools, buckets, a suction machine and vacuum trucks. Officials said the crews were moving slowly on purpose, because careless digging could cause the remaining debris or nearby walls to shift.

Bonsignore called the work "dangerous and perilous" and said every move was carefully planned. FDNY Chief of Department John Esposito said the first firefighters reached the site in under a minute and also removed people trapped in the damaged building next door. He added that officials did not know how many people were missing overall. FDNY officials said the search could go on for several days.

### What the Mayor Said

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani described the incident as "an incredibly challenging and fast-moving situation". He also praised the fire department, saying this type of rescue is exactly what the FDNY trains for.

## Construction Permit and the Investigation

The cause of the collapse has not been officially determined. New York City Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said construction workers were doing excavation work next to the building at the time it fell.

The city's Department of Buildings (DOB) said the developers of the neighbouring site held an active permit for excavation and foundation work. The DOB has now issued a full stop-work order on that site, and its inspectors are checking the safety of other buildings nearby. Reports also mention a violation at the collapsed building in April that had been marked as resolved, though officials have not shared details.

The names of the property owner, the architect and the general contractor have not been made public by officials. The AP said it tried to contact them but did not get a response.

## Help for Displaced Families

New York City Emergency Management opened a reception centre with the American Red Cross at the Flatbush YMCA on Flatbush Avenue for families forced out of their homes. The Red Cross said it had registered 11 adults and two children from six households for emergency help, including temporary places to stay.

Police asked people to stay away from the Bedford Avenue and Flatbush Avenue area because of road closures and traffic delays. Updates for residents are being shared through <a href="https://www.nyc.gov/site/em/index.page" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NYC Emergency Management</a>.

### Key highlights

- Building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue collapsed into a construction pit just after 4 pm on November 11.
- Two people killed and 17 injured, including seven children and one firefighter.
- Around 250 firefighters took part in the rescue, which continued overnight.
- Stop-work order issued on the neighbouring excavation site, which had an active permit.
- Cause still under investigation by city officials.

## Why Excavation Next to Old Buildings Is Under Scrutiny

The disaster has turned attention to the risks of deep digging beside older buildings in a crowded city. Investigators are expected to look at how the excavation was carried out and whether the collapsed building was properly supported. Until the search is complete, officials have said the toll could change.
