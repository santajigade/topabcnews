Anthropic, the US artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, has set a price range for what could be one of the largest stock market debuts ever. In an updated filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, reported on Tuesday, November 10, the company said it plans to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT." At the top of the range, the offering would raise about $64.35 billion and value the company at up to roughly $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. Trading is expected to begin as early as November 19.

## The Offer at a Glance

| Detail | What the filing and reports say |
|---|---|
| Price range | $70 to $78 per share |
| Shares on offer | 825 million Class A shares |
| New shares | 801.4 million, with 23.6 million sold by existing holders |
| Maximum raise | About $64.35 billion at the top of the range |
| Exchange and ticker | Nasdaq, ANT |
| Expected timing | Pricing around November 18, trading from November 19 or 20 |
| Fully diluted valuation | Roughly $1.3 trillion to $1.45 trillion |

The underwriters also have an option to buy an extra 123.75 million shares, according to Decrypt's reading of the filing. CNBC, cited in that report, said about 20% of shares could go to retail investors, well above the usual share in a US IPO, and that the order book was already covered at the proposed range.

## Valuation Lower Than Earlier Hopes

The proposed range is a big jump from the $965 billion valuation Anthropic received in a private funding round in May. But it is well below some Wall Street estimates that reached $2 trillion in early October, <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/anthropic-seeks-14-trillion-valuation-in-ipo-130300928.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Yahoo Finance reported</a>.

The timing matters. Investors have become more careful about the huge valuations of AI companies. Last week's market turmoil, as traders weighed the Democratic win in the US midterm elections and the cost of building AI data centres, has made buyers more price sensitive. Bloomberg reported that some prospective investors discussed a range of $1.2 trillion to $1.3 trillion, with a few considering as low as $1.1 trillion.

For comparison, SpaceX's IPO earlier this year raised $85.7 billion and gave it a market value of $1.9 trillion. Readers following the wider AI listing race can also see our earlier piece on <a href="/openai-ipo-soon-chatgpt/">OpenAI's own IPO plans</a>.

## Chip and Cloud Partners Are Buying In

Alongside the public offering, seven companies that supply Anthropic with computing power have agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting Class N shares at the IPO price. They are Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD and Micron. Because this is a private placement, these shares will not face the usual six-month lock-up after listing.

Decrypt reported that, at the midpoint of the range, Google and Nvidia would each buy about $10 billion of these shares. Combined net proceeds from the IPO and the placement are expected to go towards general corporate purposes, including compute and infrastructure.

## Fast Revenue Growth, Heavy Spending

The filing shows a business growing at an unusual pace while spending enormous sums.

### Key highlights

- Revenue rose about 1,206% to $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026
- Revenue was $16.3 billion in the first six months, nearly 14 times the same period a year earlier, <a href="https://abcnews.com/Business/anthropic-seeks-70-78-share-new-ipo-filing/story?id=138007081" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ABC News reported</a>
- The company posted a net loss of $80.7 billion in the first half, according to ABC News
- Committed future spending runs to hundreds of billions of dollars, mostly on compute and infrastructure
- Decrypt reported that most of the loss came from a large non-cash charge linked to convertible notes and other instruments

These spending commitments are the main reason investors are debating the price. They show how much capital frontier AI developers now need for chips and data centres.

## Unusual Risk Warnings and Governance

ABC News noted that around 90 of the roughly 300 pages in the preliminary prospectus deal with risks, which it described as unusual for an IPO filing. The document warns that AI models could pose a "catastrophic or existential risk to humanity" and admits its safeguards are incomplete.

Control of the company will also stay concentrated. Anthropic will be a public benefit corporation, and its Long-Term Benefit Trust will elect a majority of the board. According to Decrypt, a single Class F share held by an entity owned by the seven co-founders carries 50.1% of the voting power on most matters, making Anthropic a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules. Ordinary Class A shareholders will have limited say over major decisions.

## What to Watch Before the Listing

The final price will be set after the investor roadshow, likely around November 18. The key questions are whether demand pushes pricing towards the top of the range, and how the stock trades in its first days given recent swings in AI shares. A strong debut could lift sentiment across technology markets, including the Asian chip stocks that have been moving with AI news, as seen in the recent <a href="/asian-stocks-rebound-taiwan-nikkei-ai-rally/">rebound in Taiwan and Japan</a>. A weak one could cool the rush of AI companies planning to go public.
