PM Vidyalaxmi is the Centre's education loan scheme for students who get a merit seat at one of India's top-ranked colleges. The Union Cabinet approved it on 6 November 2024 and the common loan portal went live on 25 February 2025. Banks lend without collateral or a guarantor, the government guarantees 75% of loans up to Rs 7.5 lakh, and students from families earning less than Rs 8 lakh a year get a 3% interest subsidy on up to Rs 10 lakh during the course and one year after it.

| Item | What the scheme offers |
|---|---|
| Who qualifies | Merit admission to one of 1,425 Quality Higher Education Institutions (QHEIs) |
| Collateral or guarantor | Not needed; no upper cap on the loan amount |
| Credit guarantee | 75% on loans up to Rs 7.5 lakh |
| Interest subsidy | 3% on loans up to Rs 10 lakh, family income below Rs 8 lakh, 1 lakh fresh slots a year |
| Interest rate cap | Bank's EBLR plus 0.5% |
| Repayment | Up to 15 years, after a moratorium of course period plus one year |
| Budget | Rs 3,600 crore for 2024-25 to 2030-31 |

This post first appeared in September 2025. We have corrected two points from the original version. The full interest waiver for families earning up to Rs 4.5 lakh comes from a separate, older scheme (CSIS, explained below), not from PM Vidyalaxmi itself. And while the Cabinet release said the scheme would support over 22 lakh students, the interest subsidy itself is limited to 7 lakh fresh students over seven years.

## Which Colleges Count as QHEIs

You qualify only if your college is on the QHEI list and you got the seat on merit. Management quota and NRI quota seats are excluded. An institution makes the list if it is:

- in the top 100 of the NIRF rankings (overall, category or domain list),
- a state or UT government institution in the top 200 of NIRF, or
- any other central government institution.

Foreign universities and their campuses, in India or abroad, are not covered. You can use the subsidy and guarantee only once, for one undergraduate, postgraduate or integrated course. From the second year you need satisfactory marks to keep the subsidy, and you lose it if you drop out or are expelled.

## PM Vidyalaxmi or CSIS: Which Subsidy Will You Get

Many students mix these two up, and it decides whether you get part of the interest paid or all of it. Both pay only during the moratorium (course plus one year), and both cover the first Rs 10 lakh of principal even if your loan is bigger.

| Point | PM Vidyalaxmi | PM-USP CSIS (since 2009) |
|---|---|---|
| Family income limit | Below Rs 8 lakh a year | Up to Rs 4.5 lakh a year |
| Subsidy | 3% of interest | Full interest |
| Courses | Any course at a QHEI | Technical or professional courses at accredited institutes |
| Seats | 1 lakh fresh students a year | No slot limit |

So a student from a family earning Rs 4 lakh in an engineering course at an accredited college should look at CSIS first, because it covers the whole interest. A student from a family earning Rs 7 lakh, or one doing a BA or BCom at a QHEI, falls under PM Vidyalaxmi's 3%. You cannot stack either subsidy on top of another central or state scholarship, fee reimbursement or interest scheme.

Even without any subsidy, interest paid on an education loan can be claimed under Section 80E of the Income Tax Act once you start earning.

## How to Apply on the PM Vidyalaxmi Portal

1. Go to pmvidyalaxmi.co.in and register with your Aadhaar.
2. Fill in the common education loan application once. The same form works for all banks.
3. Choose a bank. Public sector, private, regional rural and cooperative banks are on the portal: 12, 20, 25 and 7 respectively, as of July 2026.
4. Upload your admission letter, fee structure, ID and family income proof, then track the status online.
5. After the loan is disbursed, claim the interest subsidy on the portal. It lands in your Digital Rupee wallet and then moves to your loan account.
6. Use the portal's grievance option if the bank delays or rejects without reason.

The loan can cover tuition plus related costs such as hostel, mess and a laptop. Banks may give up to 1% extra rebate if you pay interest during the study period.

## Where the Loans Are Going

A Ministry of Education reply in the Lok Sabha in March 2026 gave a bank-by-bank picture for 25 February 2025 to 2 March 2026. The portal received 1,09,139 applications across 51 banks. Of these, 68,537 were sanctioned, 61,436 disbursed and 8,799 rejected, while 27,105 were closed, withdrawn or pending with students.

State Bank of India took the bulk, with 57,279 applications and 36,508 sanctions. Uttar Pradesh sent the most applications (14,344), followed by Tamil Nadu (12,004) and Maharashtra (9,505). About one in four applications was closed, withdrawn or left pending with the student, so keep your documents ready before you apply. ## PM Vidyalaxmi: Common Questions

**Is there a maximum loan amount under PM Vidyalaxmi?**
No. The scheme sets no upper cap. The bank decides based on fees and related costs, but the guarantee covers up to Rs 7.5 lakh and the subsidy covers up to Rs 10 lakh.

**What is the income limit for the PM Vidyalaxmi interest subsidy?**
Family income must be below Rs 8 lakh a year for the 3% subsidy. Families earning up to Rs 4.5 lakh may get full interest relief under CSIS instead.

**Can I get PM Vidyalaxmi for study abroad?**
No. Foreign institutions and their campuses are outside the scheme.

**What interest rate will the bank charge?**
The rate is capped at the bank's external benchmark lending rate (EBLR) plus 0.5%, so it varies by bank.
