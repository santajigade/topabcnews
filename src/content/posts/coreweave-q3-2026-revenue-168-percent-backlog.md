CoreWeave, the US company that rents out Nvidia-powered computing capacity to artificial intelligence developers, reported third quarter results on November 10, 2026 that came in ahead of expectations. Revenue for the three months to September 30 rose 168% from a year earlier to $3.66 billion, and the company raised its forecast for the full year. Shares rose sharply in premarket trading on Wednesday, November 11, after a rough month for AI infrastructure stocks.

The results matter beyond one company. CoreWeave is often treated as a test of whether the huge spending on AI data centres is turning into real, paying demand. A strong quarter eased some of the worry that the AI boom is slowing down, though the company's growing losses and heavy borrowing remain points of debate.

## CoreWeave's Q3 2026 Numbers at a Glance

According to the company's <a href="https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Reports-Strong-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official third quarter results release</a>, here is how the main figures compare with the same quarter of 2025:

| Metric | Q3 2026 | Q3 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3,661 million | $1,365 million |
| Operating income | $207 million (6% margin) | $52 million (4% margin) |
| Adjusted operating income | $379 million (10% margin) | $217 million (16% margin) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $2,256 million (62% margin) | $838 million (61% margin) |
| Net loss | $709 million | $110 million |
| Net interest expense | $846 million | $310 million |
| Loss per share | $1.27 | $0.22 |

For the first nine months of 2026, revenue reached $8.31 billion, while the net loss for the period stood at about $2.08 billion.

Chief executive Michael Intrator described it as a "breakout quarter for CoreWeave", pointing to sequential revenue growth of more than $1 billion and double digit adjusted operating margins arriving a quarter earlier than planned.

## Why the $132.5 Billion Backlog Stands Out

The figure investors watched most closely was the revenue backlog, which is the value of signed contracts still to be delivered. CoreWeave said this stood at about $132.5 billion as of September 30. That is many times its expected annual revenue, and it suggests customers have committed to buying computing power for years ahead.

The company added new customers during the quarter, including Canva, Substack, Wayve, Serve Robotics, Mirendil and Parallel Web Systems. It also expanded existing deals with OpenAI, Perplexity, Cohere, Jane Street, Hippocratic AI and the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative.

On the government side, CoreWeave listed agreements with defence contractor Leidos for a secure AI cloud aimed at defence and intelligence work, and with the US research agency DARPA for a managed AI cloud supporting biological research.

### Power capacity keeps growing

Data centres are limited as much by electricity as by chips. CoreWeave said its active power capacity crossed 1.7 gigawatts after it added more than 200 megawatts in the quarter, roughly doubling its footprint since the start of the year. Contracted power, which includes capacity not yet switched on, rose by more than 900 megawatts to over 4.6 gigawatts.

## Raised Guidance for Full Year 2026

CoreWeave lifted several targets for the full year:

- **Revenue:** $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion
- **Adjusted operating income:** $1.275 billion to $1.375 billion, about 26% higher at the midpoint than its earlier guidance
- **Capital expenditure:** $37 billion to $39 billion, with the low end raised from $35 billion
- **Annualised run-rate revenue at the end of 2026:** $20 billion to $21 billion
- **Active power by year end:** more than 1.85 gigawatts, unchanged

TheStreet noted that the revenue outlook was above the LSEG analyst consensus of $12.86 billion, and the operating income range was above the Visible Alpha consensus of $1.07 billion. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-crwv-q3-earnings-report-2026.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CNBC reported</a> that the beat and higher guidance helped ease concerns about an AI slowdown.

## The Debt Question Behind the Growth

The strong revenue comes with a big bill. CoreWeave spent about $9.44 billion on property and equipment in the third quarter alone, and its net interest expense nearly tripled from a year earlier. The company expects interest expense of $1.05 billion to $1.15 billion in the fourth quarter.

To fund its build-out, CoreWeave said it raised about $4.2 billion through convertible senior notes and closed a $2.6 billion delayed draw term loan facility. It also expects around $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months, which would reduce how much it needs to borrow.

Investors have been nervous. TheStreet reported that the stock had fallen more than 40% over the past month as money rotated out of AI infrastructure names, and that yields on some of CoreWeave's bonds had recently gone above 16%. In premarket trade on November 11, the shares were up about 17% at around $57, according to the same report. The wider market was also recovering after a sell-off on Tuesday linked to hotter-than-expected US inflation data and a fall in Nvidia shares.

## What It Means for India

CoreWeave said more than 1.3 gigawatts of its contracted power is now outside the United States, in India, Indonesia and Spain. That places India within the company's international expansion plans at a time when global and domestic firms are racing to build AI-ready data centres in the country. A recent industry estimate expects <a href="/india-data-centre-capacity-7-5-gw-2030/">India's data centre capacity to grow about five times to 7.5 GW by 2030</a>.

For Indian investors who track US technology stocks, CoreWeave's results are also a signal for the wider AI trade, which includes chipmakers and AI developers heading to public markets, such as <a href="/anthropic-ipo-price-range-70-78-nasdaq/">Anthropic with its planned IPO</a>.

## Key Highlights

- Q3 2026 revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% year on year
- Net loss widened to $709 million as interest costs rose
- Revenue backlog of about $132.5 billion
- Full year revenue guidance raised to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion
- Capex plan for 2026 now $37 billion to $39 billion
- Over 1.3 GW of contracted power outside the US, including India

## Strong Demand, Heavy Spending

CoreWeave's quarter shows that demand for AI computing remains strong, with a contract book that runs well into the future. The open question for the market is whether revenue can keep growing fast enough to cover rising interest costs and tens of billions in yearly spending. The next few quarters, and how quickly the company turns its contracted power into working capacity, will shape that answer.
