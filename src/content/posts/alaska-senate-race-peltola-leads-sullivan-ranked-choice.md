More than ten days after Americans voted in the November 3 midterm elections, the US Senate race in Alaska is still officially undecided. Former Democratic congresswoman Mary Peltola holds a clear lead over Republican Senator Dan Sullivan, with about 98% of the vote counted according to the Associated Press. But because she is just short of 50% of first choice votes, the result will be settled by Alaska's ranked choice count, which state officials plan to run on November 18.

Peltola has already claimed victory. Sullivan, who has held the seat since 2015, has not conceded, and the AP has not called the race. When we last looked at this contest in our report on the <a href="/mississippi-alaska-senate-races-still-counting/">uncalled Mississippi and Alaska Senate races</a>, the result was still open. It remains uncalled, but Peltola has added to her lead as more ballots were counted.

## Where the Alaska Count Stands

The latest updates show Peltola close to the majority mark. Figures reported on Saturday, November 14, put her at about 49.8% to 49.9% of first choice votes, against roughly 46% for Sullivan.

Saturday's update added 4,290 ballots, according to Alaska Public Media. Overseas voters still have time for their ballots to arrive, which is why the final ranked count has been scheduled after their deadline.

## How Alaska's Ranked Choice System Decides the Winner

Alaska uses an open primary in which the top four candidates move on to the general election. In November, voters rank those candidates in order of preference. If no one wins more than half of the first choice votes, the candidate in last place is removed and their voters' next choices are shared out. This repeats until someone crosses 50%.

The <a href="https://www.elections.alaska.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Alaska Division of Elections</a> will run this tabulation on November 18, after the deadline for overseas ballots. A preliminary analysis by the election reform group FairVote, using the cast vote record released on November 13, estimated that Peltola would finish ahead by about 51.5% to 48.5% once backup choices are counted, a margin of about 8,034 votes. That is only an estimate until the official count is run.

Voters also rejected Ballot Measure 2 in this election, which means Alaska's current voting system, including ranked choice voting, stays in place.

## The Two Dan Sullivans on the Ballot

One unusual feature of this race was a second candidate named Dan Sullivan. Daniel J. Sullivan, a former Forest Service employee who is not related to the senator, appeared on the ballot as an independent. Senator Sullivan described that candidacy as a "sham." State officials first ruled the namesake ineligible, but a state court put him back on the ballot in June.

According to Alaska Public Media, Peltola's lead is now more than double the namesake's vote total, and she has received more votes than both Sullivans combined. A federal prosecutor has opened a criminal investigation into how Daniel J. Sullivan was recruited, and Governor Mike Dunleavy has said he is also looking into it. Peltola has denied any knowledge of the recruitment.

There is also legal action around the count. A Justice Department lawsuit filed on October 22 sought to block certification if Sullivan lost. That case was dismissed, and the department is appealing, but Alaska Public Media reports no decision is expected before the new Congress is sworn in.

## What a Peltola Win Would Mean for the US Senate

If Peltola's lead holds, she would become the first Alaska Native elected to the US Senate. She previously represented Alaska in the House of Representatives from 2022 to 2025.

Her win would also add to the size of the Democratic majority. Alaska Public Media reports that Peltola would join a 56 member Democratic majority, compared with 55 if Sullivan keeps his seat. Control of the chamber does not depend on this race, but every seat matters for passing legislation and confirming appointments. More details on how the <a href="https://www.senate.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">US Senate</a> works can be found on its official website.

Alaska is one of several close contests still being counted after the midterms. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom defeated Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, as we reported in our story on the <a href="/scott-colom-wins-mississippi-senate-race/">Mississippi Senate result</a>. Elsewhere, Republican Representative Lauren Boebert lost her House seat in Colorado, according to Anadolu Agency.

## Other Alaska Results From the Midterms

Alaska voters also chose a governor and a member of the US House:

- **Governor:** Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins leads Republican Bernadette Wilson by about 50.9% to 28%, and the AP called the race on Friday.
- **US House:** Republican Representative Nick Begich leads independent Bill Hill by about 47.7% to 41.7%. That race has not been called.

## What Happens Next in Alaska

The next date to watch is November 18, when the ranked choice tabulation is expected to give a final, unofficial result for the Senate seat. State certification is scheduled for November 25. Until then, both campaigns are waiting on the last overseas ballots, and the official winner will be confirmed only after the count is complete.
