Hyderabad startup Cosmoserve Space opened Base 1 on Thursday, November 5, 2026. It is a 14,800 square foot facility at VSD Tech Park in Gachibowli where the company will design, build and test robots meant to capture space junk and service satellites in orbit. The opening came with a USD 6 million seed round, which takes Cosmoserve's total funding past USD 9 million. The company says it wants to finish its technology demonstrations by 2027 and start commercial operations by 2028.

Union Minister of State for Science and Technology Dr Jitendra Singh joined virtually from New Delhi, and Telangana IT and Industries Minister D. Sridhar Babu attended in person. Cosmoserve calls Base 1 India's first private space debris removal facility and the world's sixth with space vision effects and microgravity simulation.

## Inside Base 1

According to <a href="https://ddindia.co.in/2026/11/jitendra-singh-inaugurates-hyderabad-space-robotics-facility-outlines-debris-free-space-vision-for-2030/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DD India's report on the inauguration</a>, the building brings design, manufacturing, testing and mission operations together. It has:

- an orbital rendezvous and capture lab, where engineers test how a spacecraft approaches and grabs another object;
- a space robotics innovation lab for the arms and mechanisms that hold objects in space;
- a microgravity simulation platform, so hardware can be tried under conditions close to orbit before it flies;
- an ISO Class 8 clean room for assembling and integrating satellites;
- a mission control centre to run missions once hardware is in space.

Base 1 will also support work on repairing and refuelling satellites in orbit, Inc42 reported.

## Who Is Funding Cosmoserve and What Went Wrong on Its First Flight

Founder and CEO Dr Chiranjeevi Phanindra spent close to 15 years at ISRO before starting Cosmoserve in June 2025, Inc42 reported. The seed round was led by Rebright Partners, with Aranya Holdings and Naandi Ventures joining and earlier backers AUM Ventures and Shakti VC investing again. The valuation has doubled since a pre-seed round in September, though the figure was not disclosed.

Mission EMBRACE went up in July 2026 on Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket to test soft robotic petals meant to wrap around and hold an object. The petals did not open as planned. Cosmoserve told Inc42 it has traced the problem to the thermal release mechanism and the effect of low temperatures, and that it has fixed both. An upgraded EMBRACE-2 flight is expected in the coming months. As of 24 November 2026 we found no announced launch date for it.

Cosmoserve works with Pixxel on satellite systems, Dhruv Aerospace Netherlands on propulsion and Skyroot on launches. It wants to raise its local sourcing above the current 60% or so and rely less on imported optics, avionics and space-grade electronics.

Cosmoserve says it already holds letters of intent from four satellite constellation operators: one in the US, one in Europe and two in India. Its full-scale demonstration aims to get within 10 metres of a dead satellite, which the company says would be a world record.

The business plan rests on two spacecraft working together. A larger Orbital Service Station acts as a supply hub and a smaller Autonomous Service Vehicle visits satellites one after another. Cosmoserve told Telangana Today that a pair could service up to 100 satellites and bring the cost per satellite down from about USD 10 million to under USD 1 million. Those are the company's own projections, and nothing has been tested in orbit yet.

## Why Debris Is Now a Business

Space debris is the dead satellites, spent rocket stages and broken fragments left in orbit. At around 10 km per second, even a small piece can wreck a working satellite. <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/explainer-isros-mission-possible-debris-free-space" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tribune's explainer on space debris</a>, citing the European Space Agency, said low Earth orbit holds more than 30,000 pieces larger than 10 cm. Every new satellite constellation adds to the collision risk.

Dr Phanindra put it plainly at the launch: "Debris is becoming the limiting factor for every nation's space ambitions." He said Base 1 is about keeping orbits usable for Gaganyaan, the planned Bharatiya Antariksh Station and future satellites.

## Where Base 1 Fits in India's Debris-Free 2030 Goal

India announced its Debris-Free Space Missions (DFSM) initiative in Bengaluru on April 16, 2024. Under the plan set out on <a href="https://www.isro.gov.in/Debris_Free_Space_Missions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ISRO's DFSM page</a>, every Indian space operator, government or private, should fly debris-free missions by 2030. The main rules:

- rocket stages and satellites must re-enter in a controlled way or move to orbits from which they fall back within 5 years;
- safe end-of-mission disposal should succeed more than 99% of the time;
- satellites should stay trackable and controllable throughout their life;
- the 400 km band used for human spaceflight should be kept clear.

These rules aim to stop new junk. Active removal, Cosmoserve's line of work, deals with what is already up there. Speaking at the event, Jitendra Singh said India is "emerging among the early global pioneers in space debris removal", PTI reported. He put India's space economy at nearly USD 9 billion, said it is projected to reach USD 40 to 45 billion over the coming decade, and noted that space startups have grown from single digits to nearly 450.
