Hyderabad based startup Cosmoserve Space has opened Base 1, a facility where it will design, build and test robots that can catch space junk and service satellites in orbit. The facility at VSD Tech Park in Gachibowli was inaugurated on Thursday, November 5, 2026. Union Minister of State for Science and Technology Dr Jitendra Singh joined the event virtually as chief guest, while Telangana IT and Industries Minister D. Sridhar Babu attended as guest of honour.

The launch comes as India pushes towards its goal of making all its space missions debris-free by 2030.

## What Is Inside Cosmoserve's Base 1

Base 1 spans about 14,800 square feet, as reported by Daily Excelsior. It brings design, manufacturing, testing and mission operations under one roof. India.com described it as the country's first privately owned facility for space vision and microgravity simulation.

The facility has four main parts:

- **Orbital Rendezvous and Capture Lab:** Tests how a spacecraft approaches and grabs another object in orbit.
- **Space Robotics Innovation Lab:** Develops robotic arms and systems that hold and move objects in space.
- **ISO Class 8 clean room:** A dust-controlled room for assembling and integrating satellites.
- **Mission Simulation and Control Centre:** Plans and runs missions once hardware is in space.

Robots built here can be tested under simulated space conditions before they fly, which cuts risk and cost for future missions.

## Funding and the First Test in Space

Cosmoserve is led by its founder and CEO Dr Chiranjeevi Phanindra. According to India.com, the company has raised USD 6 million in a seed round, taking its total funding to more than USD 9 million.

The startup has already flown hardware. Its first payload, Mission EMBRACE, went to space in July 2026 on Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket. It tested soft robotic petals designed to wrap around and capture objects. India.com reported that the petals did not open as planned, but the flight still produced useful data for the next version.

Cosmoserve works with other Indian space firms, including Pixxel for satellite systems, Dhruv Aerospace for propulsion and Skyroot for launch services. The company currently sources close to 60% of its mission components from Indian suppliers and wants to raise that share.

## Why Space Debris Is a Growing Problem

Space debris means dead satellites, spent rocket stages and small broken pieces left in orbit. These fragments move at around 10 km per second, so even a tiny piece can damage a working satellite. <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/explainer-isros-mission-possible-debris-free-space" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tribune's explainer on space debris</a>, citing the European Space Agency, noted that low Earth orbit holds more than 30,000 pieces larger than 10 cm.

As more countries and private firms launch satellite constellations, the risk of collisions rises. That is why debris removal and in-orbit servicing, such as refuelling or repairing satellites, are becoming a serious business worldwide.

Dr Phanindra summed up the concern at the launch: "Space debris is becoming the limiting factor for every nation's space ambition."

## How It Fits India's Debris-Free Space 2030 Goal

India announced its Debris-Free Space Missions (DFSM) initiative at an international space debris meeting in Bengaluru on April 16, 2024. Under the plan explained on <a href="https://www.isro.gov.in/Debris_Free_Space_Missions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ISRO's official DFSM page</a>, all Indian space players, both government and private, should carry out debris-free missions by 2030. Key rules include:

- Rocket stages and satellites must either re-enter in a controlled way or move to orbits where they fall back within 5 years.
- The success target for safe end-of-mission disposal is above 99%.
- All satellites should stay trackable and controllable throughout their life.
- The 400 km band used for human spaceflight should be kept clear.

At the Cosmoserve event, Dr Jitendra Singh spoke about the vision of a debris-free space by 2030 and said India's work on active debris removal shows its growing role in space technology, according to Daily Excelsior. He also urged domestic and foreign investors to put more money into the space sector and called for more philanthropy in science research.

## What This Means for India's Space Startups

India's private space sector has grown fast since it was opened to startups. Companies now build rockets, imaging satellites and, with Cosmoserve, robots for cleaning up orbit. Facilities like Base 1 let these firms test hardware at home instead of depending on labs abroad. Telangana minister Sridhar Babu said such facilities show why Hyderabad is leading in space innovation.
