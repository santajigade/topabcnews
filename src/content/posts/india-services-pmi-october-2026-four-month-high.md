India's services sector grew at its fastest pace in four months in October 2026, according to the HSBC India Services Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) compiled by S&P Global. The index rose to 55.8 from 55.2 in September, helped by strong domestic demand, while price pressure on businesses eased to its lowest level in more than six years. The data, released on November 4, came two days after the manufacturing survey showed factory growth slowing slightly to 54.7.

## What the October PMI Numbers Show

A PMI reading above 50 means activity is expanding, while a reading below 50 means it is shrinking. Both of India's main surveys remain comfortably in growth territory.

| Index | October 2026 | September 2026 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Services PMI | 55.8 | 55.2 | Four-month high |
| Composite PMI Output | 56.2 | 55.9 | Four-month high |
| Manufacturing PMI | 54.7 | 55.1 | Slight slowdown |

The composite index, which combines services and manufacturing output, climbed to 56.2, its best reading in four months, as reported by <a href="https://newsonair.gov.in/hsbc-pmi-rises-to-55-8-last-month-from-55-2-in-preceding-month/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">All India Radio News</a>.

## Domestic Demand Drives Service Providers

The services sector, which includes banking, insurance, transport, hotels, telecom, information technology and consulting, makes up the largest part of India's economy. That is why the services survey is closely watched by economists as an early signal of how consumers and companies are spending, well before official growth figures for the quarter are released by the government.

The pickup in services was led by orders from within India. New business rose at its fastest rate since June, and companies credited new client wins and successful advertising. All four broad service categories covered by the survey reported quicker growth in both output and new orders, with finance and insurance firms leading the way.

There were softer spots, however. Growth in new export orders for services slowed to its weakest since February 2023, suggesting overseas demand for Indian services is cooling. Hiring also continued, but the pace of job creation was the slowest since June. Service providers said they were cautiously optimistic about business over the next 12 months.

## Inflation Pressure Eases to a Six-Year Low

The most eye-catching number in the services survey was on costs. Input prices rose at their slowest pace since August 2020, more than six years ago, according to <a href="https://www.ibef.org/news/india-s-services-pmi-rises-to-55-8-in-october-as-input-cost-inflation-eases-to-six-year-low" target="_blank" rel="noopener">IBEF</a>, the trust set up by the Commerce Ministry. Firms also raised the prices they charge customers at the slowest rate since June.

Manufacturers reported a similar trend. Their input cost inflation was the lowest since February 2026, and the increase in factory selling prices was the weakest in nearly four years. Softer price rises are good news for households, since they suggest less pressure on the cost of everyday goods and services in the coming months.

## Factories Slow Slightly but Exports Pick Up

The manufacturing PMI dipped to 54.7 from 55.1, but it still stayed above its long-term average. Factory output rose sharply, though at a slower pace than in September. The bright spot was exports: new export orders grew at the fastest pace in six months, with demand coming from markets including the US, the UK, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Indonesia, Oman and Canada.

Factories kept hiring for a second straight month, though only modestly. They also built up stocks quickly, with input inventories rising at the fastest rate in eight months. Business confidence among manufacturers fell from September and sat below its long-term average, with about 18% of firms expecting output to rise over the next year.

### Key highlights

- Services PMI: 55.8 in October, up from 55.2, a four-month high
- Composite PMI: 56.2, also a four-month high
- Manufacturing PMI: 54.7, down from 55.1 but above the long-term average
- Services input cost inflation at its lowest since August 2020
- Factory export orders at a six-month high; services export orders weakest since February 2023

## What It Means for Markets and the RBI

Strong growth together with easing inflation is a combination that markets usually welcome. Lower cost pressure gives the Reserve Bank of India more room when it weighs its next interest rate decision, although the central bank also looks at official inflation data, the rupee and global conditions. For businesses and workers, the mixed signals on hiring and services exports are worth watching. If domestic demand stays firm through the festive season, the next PMI release in early December will show whether October's momentum carried into November.
