The United States has opened a new front in its pressure campaign against Iran, this time on land. US Central Command (CENTCOM) announced on Saturday, November 7, 2026, that it had formed Task Force Iron Gate, a unit meant to stop Iran from moving weapons, sensitive technology and money across its land borders and the Caspian Sea. On Sunday, President Donald Trump said the task force was "in full force and effect". The move extends a US naval blockade of Iranian ports that has been in place since July, and comes as Tehran warns it will hit back hard if American attacks resume.

## What Task Force Iron Gate Is Meant to Do

According to CENTCOM, the task force was ordered by its commander, Admiral Brad Cooper. <a href="https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/us-sets-up-task-force-to-block-irans-weapons-supply-routes-1.500739720" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gulf News reported</a> that it has about 20 civilian and military staff and is based in Tampa, Florida.

The unit will work with other US government agencies to block four kinds of flows:

- advanced weapons systems
- dual-use technologies that have both civilian and military uses
- sanctioned materials
- illegal revenue streams

Cooper said the effort would build on what he called the success of the "ironclad blockade" at sea. CENTCOM has not published a budget or a detailed operating plan, and the reports did not name the specific border crossings or neighbouring countries involved.

Michael Mulroy, a former US deputy assistant defence secretary for the Middle East, told Al Jazeera that the task force would map where weapons enter Iran, how those flows could be cut, and how the country moves its energy. He said any escalation was likely to focus on economic and infrastructure targets.

## How the Sea Blockade Has Worked So Far

The land effort follows months of tight control over shipping. The naval blockade, announced in July, covers Iranian ports on the Gulf and the Gulf of Oman and aims to halt imports and exports except humanitarian aid. CENTCOM says it has kept Iran from exporting more than 25 billion dollars of crude oil in recent months.

| Blockade measure | Figure reported by CENTCOM |
|---|---|
| Commercial vessels redirected since July | 147 |
| Merchant ships disabled for refusing to comply | 5 |
| Crude exports blocked in recent months | Over 25 billion dollars |

The most recent case was the St Kitts-flagged cargo ship M/V Huda, which CENTCOM said it disabled in the Gulf of Oman by firing precision munitions at its stern.

## Strikes, Mines and Rising Tension in Hormuz

The announcement came during a tense weekend. Trump confirmed new US strikes on Qeshm Island on November 7 and described them as "minor". Iran's state news agency IRNA reported two explosions on Qeshm and several more in Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy said two oil tankers struck sea mines near the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday. On Sunday, Iranian state media said another tanker caught fire after hitting a mine east of the strait. In each case, Iran said the ships had switched off their identification systems and entered unsafe or restricted waters. These accounts could not be independently confirmed.

## Tehran's Warning and the State of Talks

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi responded on Sunday with a sharp warning. In a post on X, he said his "2-day proposal stands" and set out Iran's demands, including an end to the war on all fronts. He said that if Washington chose war instead, Iranian forces would "rain down hell", as <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/8/iran-threatens-to-rain-down-hell-if-us-escalates-war" target="_blank" rel="noopener">reported by Al Jazeera</a>. Araghchi also said negotiations had been slowed by the US midterm elections.

Trump, speaking to CNN by phone, said renewed strikes were "highly unlikely" if talks on Iran's nuclear programme and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz made progress. He added that he would strike again if needed. Talks mediated by Qatar are continuing, according to Al Jazeera.

## Why Oil Markets and India Are Watching

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most important routes for crude oil, so every incident there moves prices. On Monday, November 9, Brent crude slipped 64 cents to about 97.67 dollars a barrel and US crude (WTI) fell to about 90.51 dollars, according to The Nation, after Trump's comments raised hopes that fresh strikes could be avoided.

For India, a large importer of crude oil, prices near 100 dollars a barrel matter for fuel costs, inflation and the rupee. Any further disruption to shipping through Hormuz could push prices up again, while progress in the Qatar-mediated talks could ease them. ## What Could Happen Next

The new task force signals that Washington wants to tighten economic pressure on Iran from every direction while talks continue. Tehran, for its part, is warning of a severe response to any new attacks. Whether the coming days bring a deal or a fresh round of strikes will shape not only the region's security but also global energy prices, which households in India and elsewhere feel directly.
