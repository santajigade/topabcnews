Transgender persons living in Delhi, along with their families, will now be covered under Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY) with a health cover of up to Rs 10 lakh a year. The Delhi Cabinet, chaired by Chief Minister Rekha Gupta, cleared the proposal on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, according to an official statement reported by PTI. The aim, the government said, is to remove the cost of treatment as a barrier for a community that often struggles to get care.

The decision matters because a central scheme for transgender health already exists, and many people are unsure which card covers what. Below we explain the new Delhi cover, how it compares with the existing national option, and what a person needs in hand to qualify.

## What the Delhi Cabinet Approved

The cover is worth up to Rs 10 lakh per eligible family every year. Half of it, Rs 5 lakh, comes from the Centre under Ayushman Bharat, and the other Rs 5 lakh is added by the Delhi government.

Treatment is cashless at hospitals empanelled under PM-JAY anywhere in India, not just in Delhi. The scheme covers 1,961 treatment packages across 27 medical specialities. As per the statement reported by Outlook Money, this includes hospitalisation, diagnostics, medicines, follow-up and tertiary care, plus expenses from 3 days before admission and 15 days after discharge.

The government expects about 5,656 transgender persons in Delhi to benefit in 2026. That estimate is projected from the 4,213 transgender persons counted in Delhi in the 2011 Census. The extra cost to the Delhi budget is put at about Rs 1.19 crore a year.

Gupta said the cashless facility would let people get treatment "with dignity and without discrimination."

## Who Is Eligible

The statement sets out two conditions:

- The person must hold a valid transgender identity certificate issued by the District Magistrate concerned.
- Beneficiaries will be identified from a verified list maintained by the Delhi government's Revenue Department.

Family members of the eligible person are covered within the same family limit. The government has not yet published a separate enrolment form, portal link or start date, so residents should watch for a notice from the Delhi health department or their district office.

## New Delhi Cover vs the Existing TG Plus Card

A national option has been in place since 2022. On 24 August 2022, the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment signed an agreement with the National Health Authority to launch the Ayushman Bharat TG Plus card, as announced in a <a href="https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1854141" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PIB release on the TG Plus card</a>. Here is how the two compare, based only on what each government has published:

| Feature | Central TG Plus card (2022) | Delhi PM-JAY cover (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | Ministry of Social Justice | Centre Rs 5 lakh + Delhi Rs 5 lakh |
| Cover amount | Rs 5 lakh per beneficiary per year | Up to Rs 10 lakh per family per year |
| Who is covered | The transgender person | Transgender person and family |
| Proof needed | Certificate from the National Portal for Transgender Persons | Valid DM-issued transgender identity certificate, plus name on Revenue Department list |
| Where usable | Any PM-JAY empanelled hospital in India | Any PM-JAY empanelled hospital in India |
| Special packages | Includes gender-affirming surgery packages | Not specified in the Delhi statement |

Two details from the 2022 release are worth knowing. First, the TG Plus card was meant for people not already getting similar benefits from another central or state scheme. Second, it added packages for sex reassignment surgery and related treatment to the regular PM-JAY list. The Delhi statement does not say whether those special packages carry over, so anyone planning such treatment should ask the hospital's Ayushman desk before admission.

## How to Get the Certificate You Need First

The identity certificate is the gateway to both schemes. It is issued through the Ministry's <a href="https://transgender.dosje.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Portal for Transgender Persons</a>. According to the portal's own FAQ, the steps are:

1. **Register** on the portal and log in. Text and video manuals are available there.
2. **Fill one application** that covers both the Certificate of Identity and the Identity Card.
3. **Upload documents**: any address proof (or an affidavit if you have none), a photo ID, your signature or thumb impression, and the affidavit in Form-2 and/or a medical certificate.
4. **Track the status** online. The FAQ says there is no fee and that processing can take up to 30 days, depending on district verification.
5. **Download and print** the certificate and card once approved.

People who cannot fill the form alone can get help at the nearest Social Welfare Department office. The portal lists helpline 14427 for administrative queries.

One caution: Parliament amended the transgender rights law in 2026. ThePrint reported in April that the amendment replaced self-identification with verification by a medical board. The portal FAQ may not yet reflect every new step, so applicants should check the current form on the portal or with the district office before applying.

## What Delhi Residents Should Do Now

If you already have a transgender identity certificate, keep a copy ready along with your Aadhaar and address proof, and check with your district Revenue office whether your name is on the verified list. If you do not have a certificate yet, start the portal application now, since it is required before any health card can be issued. For a wider look at how much Indians still pay from their own pockets for treatment, see our report on the [National Health Accounts 2023-24](/national-health-accounts-2023-24-out-of-pocket/).

The Delhi move doubles the money available compared with the central card and extends it to families, which could make a real difference for costly hospital stays. How quickly the verified list is drawn up and the cards are issued will decide how soon that promise reaches people.
