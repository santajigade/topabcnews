The first draft of the main political deal at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, was released on Monday, November 16, with four days of talks left. The 18 page text, put out by Australia as President of Negotiations, asks rich countries to triple yearly adaptation finance for developing nations to at least $120 billion by 2035 and calls for "accelerated action" to move away from coal, oil and gas. Nearly 200 countries must agree on a final version by consensus before the summit closes on November 20.

Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen, who chairs the negotiations, told reporters in Antalya that "not all parties will agree" with some parts of the draft, according to <a href="https://www.aa.com.tr/en/turkiye/cop31-draft-decision-calls-for-tripling-adaptation-finance-to-120b-speeding-shift-away-from-fossil-fuels/4126715" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Anadolu Agency's report on the draft</a>.

## What the Antalya Draft Asks Countries to Do

The document, known as a cover decision, is meant to sum up what the summit agrees on and set the direction for the next few years. Its main proposals:

- **Adaptation money:** at least $120 billion a year by 2035 for developing countries, with developed countries asked to present a roadmap, milestones and a way to monitor delivery.
- **Fossil fuels:** a "just, orderly and equitable" transition away from fossil fuels, plus a phase out of inefficient fossil fuel subsidies.
- **Clean power:** tripling global renewable energy capacity and doubling the yearly rate of energy efficiency gains by 2030, and lifting electricity to 35% of final energy use by 2035.
- **Methane:** better leak detection and an end to routine flaring and venting.
- **New work streams:** a programme on mountains and climate change, and a study of the science of overshooting 1.5°C and then bringing temperatures back down.
- **Roadmap to COP32:** an "Antalya to Addis Ababa Roadmap" on adaptation and loss and damage funding, with a report due at COP32 in Ethiopia in 2027.

The text also voices "deep concern" that the 1.5°C limit of the Paris Agreement is close to being breached, and recognises the central role of the IPCC's science, wording that Europe and climate vulnerable nations had pushed for, AFP reported.

## How the $120 Billion Goal Compares With Earlier Promises

Climate finance targets have piled up over the last five summits, and they are easy to confuse. This table puts them side by side.

| Summit | Goal | Target year | Status in Antalya |
|---|---|---|---|
| COP26, Glasgow (2021) | Double adaptation finance from about $18.8 billion (2019 level) to about $40 billion | 2025 | Taken as the base for "tripling" |
| COP29, Baku (2024) | At least $300 billion a year in overall climate finance for developing countries | 2035 | Adaptation money is meant to count inside this |
| COP30, Belém (2025) | Triple adaptation finance, no baseline stated | 2035 | Usually read as about $120 billion |
| COP31 draft, Antalya (2026) | At least $120 billion a year, with a roadmap and milestones | 2035 | Under negotiation |

The key change in the Antalya draft is that it puts an explicit dollar figure and a delivery pathway on the tripling pledge made a year ago in Brazil. According to an analysis by the <a href="https://www.wri.org/insights/tripling-adaptation-finance-goal" target="_blank" rel="noopener">World Resources Institute</a>, the COP30 text never stated what was being tripled, which left room for different readings.

## Why Rich and Poor Nations Are Still Split

The money fight has been running since the first day. The African Group of Negotiators says adaptation finance today is only around $40 billion a year, and wants the tripling tied to the Global Goal on Adaptation, which tracks concrete targets such as water and food security. The European Union and other developed nations prefer to handle the issue in a broader climate finance work programme and have pledged only to "pursue" tripling, the South African outlet <a href="https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-11-15-africa-battles-eu-delaying-tactics-at-cop31-over-120bn-climate-adaptation-lifeline/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Daily Maverick reported</a>. One negotiator quoted by the outlet warned the EU approach would "dilute" the language.

Civil society groups also point out that even $120 billion is small next to the need. The UNEP Adaptation Gap Report 2026 estimates developing countries will need about $305 billion a year by 2035. International public adaptation finance was between $28.2 billion and $31.7 billion in 2024, covering only 20 to 29% of that need, according to figures cited by The Week.

Fossil fuels are the other sticking point. AFP called them among the most contentious issues, with a conflict driven energy crisis lowering expectations.

## Where India Stands on the Draft

India has not publicly commented on the text yet. But its stated priorities, set out by Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav before the summit, show where New Delhi will look closely:

1. **Who pays:** India says finance from developed countries is an obligation under Article 9.1 of the Paris Agreement and should be predictable, mostly public and concessional so it does not add to debt. A firm $120 billion figure with milestones fits this demand.
2. **No strings on adaptation:** India wants indicators under the Global Goal on Adaptation to stay voluntary, so they do not become conditions for getting money. How the draft links finance to these indicators will matter.
3. **No top down targets:** India opposes new emission obligations imposed from above and wants mitigation work to stay "party driven" and non punitive. Strong fossil fuel wording could test this.
4. **Farmers first:** India plans to link adaptation to agriculture, food security and smallholder livelihoods, which is where most of its own adaptation spending goes.

On Monday, Yadav met UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell and Bowen in Antalya. He said India assured the Presidency of a constructive role and discussed mitigation, adaptation and climate finance, <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/cop31/environment-minister-bhupender-yadav-meets-unfccc-executive-secy-cop31-presidencys-negotiations-chief-in-antalya" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Tribune reported</a>. India works in the talks with the G77 and China, the Like Minded Developing Countries and the BASIC group of Brazil, South Africa, India and China. BASIC worked alongside the African Group to keep the $120 billion figure in the text.

## What Happens Before Friday's Deadline

Negotiators now go line by line through the draft, and new versions are expected over the coming days. Watch three things: whether the $120 billion figure survives with a clear 2035 date and roadmap, whether "accelerated action" on fossil fuels stays in or is softened, and whether adaptation money is ring fenced or simply folded into the wider $300 billion goal. A final text is due by Friday, November 20.
