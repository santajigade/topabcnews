Five people, including children, were killed in central Gaza on Sunday evening, November 8, 2026, when a sand embankment softened by heavy rain collapsed onto tents housing displaced families in Deir el-Balah. The deaths came as the first winter storm of the season swept across the Gaza Strip, flooding tens of thousands of tents and exposing how fragile shelter remains for most of the territory's population more than a year after a ceasefire took effect.

## How the Deir el-Balah Collapse Happened

The tents had been pitched next to a raised mound of sand and soil at a shelter site south of Deir el-Balah. After days of rain and strong winds, the mound gave way in the dark, about an hour after evening prayers, burying the families living below it.

Palestinian Civil Defence officials said five people died. Accounts of who the victims were differ slightly between outlets. <a href="https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/09/storm-killed-five-in-gaza-as-sandstone-slab-collapsed-onto-tents" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Euronews reported</a> that three children and two women were killed, while Al Jazeera said the dead were four children and a woman. Al Jazeera also reported that two people were still missing.

Civil defence spokesman Mahmoud Bassal said rescue teams were continuing to search for those unaccounted for. According to The National, rescuers worked for more than two hours and pulled more than 10 injured people out of the debris. Before specialist teams arrived, young men from the camp dug with their hands and shovels, and video shared online showed dozens of people trying to lift a heavy slab.

One displaced man who helped with the digging told The National that seeing people "buried alive" was among the hardest things he had ever witnessed.

## Scale of the Flooding Across Gaza

The collapse was the deadliest single incident of a storm that began on Friday, November 6, and brought widespread flooding over the weekend. Gaza's government media office, which is run by Hamas, said about 70,000 tents sheltering displaced families were flooded.

Dozens of cracked walls and roofs also collapsed, injuring people, some critically, according to the media office. Over the weekend, hundreds of families left camps in parts of Gaza City and elsewhere after their shelters were destroyed by floodwater. In Jabalia in northern Gaza, one father of a family of eight told The National that the camp had no proper drainage and that requests for sturdier tents and tarpaulins had gone unanswered for months.

## Why Rescuers Struggled to Respond

Ismail Al Thawabta, head of the Gaza government media office, said civil defence crews could answer only a small fraction of emergency calls because they lacked staff and equipment. Bassal said the shortage of heavy rescue machinery may have contributed to the deaths, and blamed Israeli restrictions on bringing such equipment into Gaza.

Al Jazeera's correspondent in Deir el-Balah, Tareq Abu Azzoum, said the victims had been living in thin tents on open ground with no protection from the rain and no chance to escape when the embankment fell.

The civil defence agency has warned displaced people not to pitch tents or stay close to sand masses and raised soil mounds during rainfall, describing them as a real threat to life. It has also asked humanitarian organisations to supply the equipment its teams need.

## Gaza Authorities Blame Global Inaction

In a statement on Monday, November 9, the Gaza Government Media Office condemned what it called global inaction. As <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/9/gaza-authorities-slam-global-inaction-after-mudslide-kills-five-people" target="_blank" rel="noopener">reported by Al Jazeera</a>, the office held Israel, the United States and the international community responsible, and accused them of blocking shelter supplies, building materials, caravans, fuel and spare parts for municipal and civil defence equipment. It also criticised the Board of Peace and the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza for failing to act.

The aid group Save the Children has said more than half a million children in Gaza live in "critical or catastrophic" shelter conditions. It says Israeli authorities have repeatedly blocked the entry of caravans, the mobile homes that were meant to replace tents under the ceasefire arrangements. The reports reviewed for this article did not include a response from Israeli authorities to these specific accusations.

## The Ceasefire Backdrop

The ceasefire in Gaza took effect on October 10, 2025, after two years of war that began with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on October 7, 2023. It was reached through mediation by Egypt, Qatar and the United States. Euronews noted that the Israeli army has continued air strikes and ground operations despite the truce.

According to figures cited by Al Jazeera, more than 74,600 people have been killed in Gaza since the war began, including more than 1,600 Palestinians killed since the ceasefire started. Most of Gaza's population has been displaced at least once and many families still live in tents or makeshift shelters, which leaves them exposed each time bad weather arrives. Israeli politics is also in flux, with <a href="/herzog-taps-eisenkot-form-israel-government/">President Herzog asking Gadi Eisenkot to form a new government</a>.

## What Winter Could Bring for Displaced Families

The weekend storm is the first of the winter, and officials and residents fear more to come. Families at the Deir el-Balah site had already repaired their flooded tents with whatever materials they could find before the embankment gave way. Without stronger shelters, drainage and heavy rescue equipment, civil defence officials warn that each new round of rain carries the risk of further collapses, flooding and injuries for hundreds of thousands of displaced people.
