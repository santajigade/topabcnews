Delhi's air has turned dangerous just days before Diwali. On Friday morning, November 6, 2026, the city's overall Air Quality Index (<a href="https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/11/06/delhi-air-quality-to-stay-severe-ahead-of-diwali-aqi-hits-389-at-several-stations-despite-grap-measures.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AQI</a>) stood at 391, right at the edge of the "severe" category, as per The Week. About 30 of Delhi's 59 monitoring stations were already in the severe zone at 9 am. Forecasts say the air could stay severe through the Diwali weekend, so doctors' usual advice on masks, outdoor activity and indoor air has become important for every family in Delhi NCR.

## How bad is the air in Delhi right now

Delhi has been in the "very poor" band (AQI 301 to 400) for several days in a row. Outlook and The Week reported how the city's AQI climbed through this week:

| Day | Delhi AQI (as reported) | Category |
|---|---|---|
| Monday, Nov 2 | 324 | Very poor |
| Tuesday, Nov 3 | 350 | Very poor |
| Wednesday, Nov 4 | 377 | Very poor |
| Thursday, Nov 5 (4 pm) | 369 | Very poor |
| Friday, Nov 6 (9 am) | 391 | Very poor, near severe |

Some areas were much worse. The Week said Rohini recorded an AQI of 475 and IIT Delhi 472 on Friday morning. On Wednesday, <a href="https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/delhi-pollution-aqi-nears-400-ahead-of-diwali-stubble-burning-drives-worsening-air-quality-565800-2026-11-05" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Today</a> reported readings of 458 at IIT Delhi and 448 at both Jahangirpuri and Punjabi Bagh. Just a week earlier, on October 30, rain linked to Cyclone Montha had pushed the AQI down to 60, which is "satisfactory", showing how quickly conditions changed.

Nearby cities were also affected. On Wednesday morning, Noida recorded 330 and Gurugram 322, both very poor, according to Business Today.

## Why Delhi's air is getting worse

Several factors are coming together at the same time:

- **Stubble burning:** The Decision Support System of the Ministry of Earth Sciences estimated that farm fires contributed 20.2% of Delhi's pollution on Wednesday, Business Today reported. Outlook put the estimated share at 15.6% on Thursday and 19.2% on Friday.
- **Vehicles:** Transport was estimated to add about 18% on Friday and over 21% on Saturday, as per Outlook.
- **Farm fire count:** Satellites picked up 467 farm fires across six states on November 4, including 151 in Punjab.
- **Weather:** Slow winds, morning mist and clear skies trap pollutants close to the ground.
- **Firecrackers:** Diwali crackers could add to the already heavy load.

## Forecast for the Diwali weekend

The Air Quality Early Warning System expects Delhi's AQI to cross 400 and stay severe from Friday to Sunday, according to The Week. Outlook reported that severe levels are expected from November 6 to 8, and air quality may move between severe and very poor through the following week. Diwali falls on Sunday, November 8.

<a href="https://caqm.nic.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GRAP</a> Stage II curbs have been in force since November 2, as per CareerIndia. These include tighter dust control at building sites, mechanical road sweeping, water sprinkling and stricter checks on open burning and polluting vehicles. Stage III, which brings curbs on non essential construction and some goods vehicles, can be invoked if the AQI stays in the 401 to 450 range.

## Green cracker rules for Diwali

CareerIndia reports that under the Supreme Court rules for this year, only certified green crackers can be burst, and only on November 7 and 8. The allowed time windows are 6 am to 7 am and 8 pm to 10 pm. Crackers are not allowed within 300 metres of hospitals and silence zones, and the full ban returns from November 9. Buyers should pick only PESO certified products.

## Health tips to protect yourself and your family

Children, older people and those with breathing or heart problems need the most care when the air is this bad. Based on advice reported by Business Today and CareerIndia, here are simple steps to follow:

1. **Wear a well fitting N95 or FFP2 mask** when you step out.
2. **Limit outdoor exercise**, especially early morning and evening, when pollution often peaks. Shift walks and workouts indoors for a few days.
3. **Keep windows closed during smoggy hours.** Open them for a short time around midday, when air is usually a little better.
4. **Use an air purifier with a HEPA filter** in the room where you spend most time, if you can.
5. **Carry prescribed inhalers and medicines** if you or a family member has asthma or another lung condition.
6. **Use the Metro or buses and share rides** to cut both your exposure and vehicle smoke.
7. **Check the AQI before going out** on official sources such as aqi.delhi.gov.in.

If anyone at home feels unwell or finds it hard to breathe, consult a doctor without delay.

A healthy routine also helps the body cope better. Read our guide on [daily exercise for long term health](/daily-exercise-key-to-longevity-vitality/), and remember to move workouts indoors on high pollution days.

## Celebrating Diwali With Cleaner Air in Mind

Delhi is heading into Diwali with some of the worst air of the season, and forecasts offer little relief for the next few days. Following the green cracker rules, cutting down on firecrackers and protecting the most vulnerable members of the family can make a real difference. Small steps like wearing a good mask and keeping indoor air clean will help everyone celebrate more safely.
