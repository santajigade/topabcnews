Delhi went into Diwali with some of its worst air of the season. At 8 am on Friday, November 6, 2026, the city's Air Quality Index stood at 389, in the "very poor" band and close to "severe", with about 28 monitoring stations already in the severe range and IIT Delhi at 473, Business Today reported from CPCB data. Two days earlier, on November 4, the 24 hour average had been 377. The Air Quality Early Warning System expected severe conditions from November 6 to 8.

That forecast mostly held. Here is what followed, what each AQI level means for health, and what the Health Ministry advises.

## Update (23 November 2026)

**Diwali day was the worst since 2021.** Delhi's average AQI on Diwali, Sunday, November 8, was 376, according to CPCB data reported by PTI. Earlier Diwalis for comparison:

| Year | Diwali day AQI |
|---|---|
| 2021 | 382 |
| 2022 | 312 |
| 2023 | 218 |
| 2024 | 328 |
| 2025 | 345 |
| **2026** | **376** |

**The day after was not much better.** On Monday, November 9, the hourly average was 371 at 10 am, and 14 of 51 reporting stations were severe, with Delhi Technological University worst at 439. The official 4 pm bulletin put the day's AQI at 344, Outlook reported.

**GRAP was tightened, then eased.** The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) invoked Stage III of the Graded Response Action Plan on November 6, when the AQI was 380. Air quality slowly improved, and on November 17, with the AQI at 204, CAQM <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2337619&reg=48&lang=2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">revoked Stage III with immediate effect</a>, according to a PIB release. Stage I and Stage II measures remain in force, and agencies were told to step them up so Stage III is not needed again.

**Late November: poor, not yet clean.** On the morning of November 22, CPCB readings reported by Outlook showed Delhi in the "poor" band, in the high 230s to 240s, with Sirifort the worst station at 326 at 6 am. IITM's early warning system expected "poor" air to continue at least until Tuesday, November 24. For the mid month spell, see our report on [Delhi's AQI and the IITM severe forecast of November 13](/delhi-aqi-very-poor-severe-forecast-november-13/).

## How the Air Got Worse Before Diwali

On October 30, rain linked to Cyclone Montha had pushed the AQI down to 60, which is "satisfactory". Within a week the city was near severe.

| Date | Delhi AQI | Category |
|---|---|---|
| October 30 | 60 | Satisfactory |
| October 31 | 117 | Moderate |
| November 1 | 261 | Poor |
| November 2 | 324 | Very poor |
| November 3 | 350 | Very poor |
| November 4 (24 hour average) | 377 | Very poor |
| November 5 (9 am) | 367 | Very poor |
| November 6 (8 am) | 389 | Very poor |

CAQM imposed GRAP Stage II on November 2, when the AQI reached 324, PTI reported. On November 4, 22 of Delhi's 59 stations were severe, led by IIT Delhi at 458 and Jahangirpuri and Punjabi Bagh at 448 each.

Farm fires were a large part of the problem. The Decision Support System estimated that stubble burning made up 20.2 percent of Delhi's pollution on November 4 and transport 15.5 percent. Satellites counted 467 farm fires across six states that day, 151 of them in Punjab, Business Today reported.

The Supreme Court allowed only CSIR-NEERI approved green crackers on November 7 and 8, between 6 am and 7 am and between 8 pm and 10 pm, with no fireworks within 300 metres of hospitals, nursing homes, old age homes or silence zones. The full ban returned from November 9.

## What Each AQI Level Means for Your Health

India's National Air Quality Index, launched in 2014, covers eight pollutants including PM2.5. The <a href="https://ies.gov.in/arthapedia/concept/national-air-quality-index" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official AQI bands</a> and the health impacts listed by CPCB are:

| AQI | Category | Likely health impact |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 50 | Good | Minimal impact |
| 51 to 100 | Satisfactory | Minor breathing discomfort for sensitive people |
| 101 to 200 | Moderate | Breathing discomfort for people with lung disease, asthma or heart disease |
| 201 to 300 | Poor | Breathing discomfort for most people on prolonged exposure |
| 301 to 400 | Very poor | Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure |
| 401 to 500 | Severe | Affects healthy people and seriously impacts those with existing disease |

GRAP stages follow the same bands:

| GRAP stage | AQI range | Examples of what it brings |
|---|---|---|
| Stage I | 201 to 300 (Poor) | Basic dust control and limits on open burning |
| Stage II | 301 to 400 (Very poor) | Mechanical road cleaning, water sprinkling, anti smog guns, higher parking fees |
| Stage III | 401 to 450 (Severe) | Halt on most construction and demolition, curbs on BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel cars in Delhi |
| Stage IV | Above 450 (Severe plus) | The strictest emergency curbs |

CAQM can act on forecasts: this year it invoked Stage III at an AQI of 380.

## Health Ministry Advice on Masks, Purifiers and Exercise

The Union Health Ministry's <a href="https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/1-ADVISORY-ON-AIR-POLLUTION-AND-HEALTH.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">advisory on air pollution and health</a> sets out these steps:

1. **Pick the right mask.** A disposable N95 or N99 mask helps for short trips outside if it fits snugly over nose and mouth. Cloth masks, paper masks, scarves and handkerchiefs do not protect against fine particles.
2. **Move exercise indoors.** Skip morning and late evening walks and runs on poor, very poor and severe days, since pollution is usually highest then.
3. **Time your ventilation.** Keep doors and windows shut in the early morning and late evening. If you need fresh air, open them between 12 pm and 4 pm.
4. **Use purifiers carefully.** Follow the maker's instructions on filter changes, and avoid purifiers that produce ozone, which adds to indoor pollution.
5. **Cut indoor sources.** Wet mop instead of sweeping, run air conditioners on recirculate mode, and avoid mosquito coils, incense or smoking in closed rooms.
6. **Protect those most at risk.** Older people, children under five, pregnant women and anyone with asthma, lung or heart disease should stay indoors with low activity on very poor and severe days and keep prescribed medicines at hand.
7. **Know when to see a doctor.** Breathlessness, dizziness, cough, chest pain or discomfort, and red or watery eyes all need prompt medical advice.

For indoor options on bad air days, see our guide on [daily exercise for long term health](/daily-exercise-key-to-longevity-vitality/).

## What to Watch for the Rest of November

Delhi is in the "poor" range with GRAP Stage II in force, and IITM expects little change for now. Above 300 the city slips back to very poor; the Stage III band starts at 401. Check the CPCB reading each morning and follow the steps above whenever it is poor or worse.
