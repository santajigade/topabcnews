OpenAI has given its AI agents a way to pay. The company launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, November 13, letting its always-on "dots" agents complete online purchases and hotel bookings using a saved credit card. Built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, the feature is limited for now to ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States, and every payment needs the user's fingerprint or face approval before it goes through.

## What ChatGPT Wallet Does

Dots are OpenAI's autonomous agents, introduced in late September, that run on their own cloud computer and can carry out tasks in the background. Until now they could search for products and compare options, but stopping at the checkout page. Wallet lets them finish the job on a merchant's own website.

The feature only works inside conversations with a dot. It is not available in ordinary ChatGPT chats or in ChatGPT Work, according to <a href="https://www.ibtimes.com/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-ai-assisted-purchases-3809730" target="_blank" rel="noopener">IBTimes</a>. Users add a card once through a secure form rather than typing it into the chat.

### Key highlights

- **Launch date:** November 13, 2026
- **Who can use it:** ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and above with a US billing address
- **Partners:** American Express, Mastercard and Visa
- **Cards:** Credit cards only, debit cards are not supported
- **Approval:** Face ID, Touch ID or fingerprint for every purchase
- **Fees:** OpenAI takes no commission during the initial rollout

## How the Tokenised Card Keeps Your Number Hidden

The main safety feature is tokenisation. When a dot reaches checkout, it shows the user the merchant, the items, the total amount and the card it plans to use. Nothing happens until the user approves it with a biometric check.

Once approved, the card network issues a payment token, a stand-in card with its own number, expiry date and security code. According to Notebookcheck, OpenAI says neither the merchant nor the agent ever sees the real card number. Full card details stay with payment service providers, and OpenAI receives only tokenised credentials and limited card information.

Cardholders keep their card's rewards, purchase protections and dispute rights. Merchants remain the sellers, so returns, cancellations and delivery problems go to the shop, while unauthorised charges and billing disputes go to the card issuer.

## Limits and Risks Users Should Know

OpenAI has been open that agents can make mistakes, and it advises users to check details carefully before approving. Some practical points stand out from the company's guidance as summarised by Notebookcheck:

- **Approval is not an order.** A "Ready to check out" status only means the user approved. Only the merchant's confirmation proves the order went through.
- **Retries can duplicate orders.** Trying again after an unclear result may place a second order.
- **Subscriptions keep running.** If a user agrees to a subscription, the merchant may store the token and keep charging, and that has to be cancelled with the merchant.
- **Removing a card stops new payments only.** It does not cancel orders already placed.

Wallet also cannot guarantee that every store will accept an agent's purchase. Merchants and card issuers can still reject transactions.

## Why Shops and Platforms Are Pushing Back

The launch lands in the middle of a wider fight over whether AI agents should be allowed to shop on other companies' websites. IBTimes reported that United Airlines said this week it would block AI agents from booking tickets through its systems. Amazon has tried to keep outside agents off its platform, and in March it won a court order restricting Perplexity's shopping agent.

OpenAI published guidance for merchants alongside the launch, explaining how websites can accept signed agent requests and adjust fraud filters. American Express innovation head Luke Gebb said the limited release lets the partners watch performance before expanding.

This is also OpenAI's third attempt at shopping inside ChatGPT. It launched Instant Checkout in September 2025 and then reworked shopping in March 2026 into a discovery and comparison tool. Chaya Nayak, OpenAI's product lead for payments, said the near-term focus is building ties with "merchants and our network partners" rather than earning revenue from transactions.

## Will ChatGPT Wallet Come to India?

For now, there is no sign of it. OpenAI has announced no timeline for cheaper plans or for markets outside the US, and the feature requires a US billing address. Indian ChatGPT users, including those on paid plans, cannot use it.

Any future launch in India would also need to work with local payment systems and regulators, and OpenAI has not said anything about Indian card networks or UPI. Users who want the official eligibility rules can check OpenAI's own <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001654-making-purchases-with-chatgpt-wallet" target="_blank" rel="noopener">help page on ChatGPT Wallet purchases</a>.

The scale of the opportunity is clear, though. ChatGPT had more than 1.2 billion weekly active users and nearly 100 million paying users at the end of the third quarter, according to CNBC figures cited by IBTimes. Only a small slice of them can try Wallet today.

## What This Means for AI Shopping

ChatGPT Wallet is a careful first step. By keeping it to US Pro users, requiring biometric approval for every payment and using tokenised cards, OpenAI is testing agent payments with guardrails firmly in place. Pre-approved spending limits and categories are planned, which would let agents buy without asking each time, but no date has been given.
