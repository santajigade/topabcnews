Retiring EPF members will be able to take their provident fund money in regular instalments instead of one lump sum, after the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation's board approved a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) on Thursday, November 19. At the same meeting in New Delhi, the Central Board of Trustees cut the minimum balance members must keep in their PF account from 25% to 10%, and brought the waiting period for final withdrawal after losing a job down from 12 months to 2 months.

None of these changes is live yet. The EPF Scheme has to be amended and notified first, and the tax treatment of the new payouts is still being discussed with the Finance Ministry, according to the <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2338667&reg=48&lang=2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official PIB release on the 240th CBT meeting</a>, which Labour Minister Mansukh Mandaviya chaired.

## Old Rules vs New Rules at a Glance

| Rule | Before November 19 | Approved by CBT |
|---|---|---|
| Retirement payout | Lump sum | Lump sum, or instalments under SWP (optional) |
| Minimum balance kept in PF account | 25% of contributions | 10% |
| Share of balance available for partial withdrawal | Up to 75% | Up to 90% |
| Wait for final PF settlement after job loss | 12 months | 2 months |
| Wait for final pension (EPS) withdrawal | 36 months | 36 months (unchanged) |
| Status | In force | Needs scheme amendment and notification |

The 75% and 90% figures follow directly from the minimum balance rule: if a quarter of the money must stay in the account, three quarters can be withdrawn, and the new 10% floor leaves nine tenths. Business Standard and Deccan Herald both report the new ceiling as 90%.

## How the Systematic Withdrawal Plan Would Work

The SWP is for retiring members who would rather not take their PF savings in one go. The PIB release says members can pick monthly, quarterly or half-yearly payouts, and that the money left in the account keeps earning returns while it stays invested. In its words, "The facility would be entirely voluntary", so anyone who wants the full lump sum can still take it.

<a href="https://www.business-standard.com/finance/investment/epfo-okays-systematic-withdrawal-plan-raises-partial-pf-withdrawal-to-90-126111901374_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Standard reported</a> more detail from the board papers:

- Payouts could also be annual, and the plan could run for up to 20 years or another period the government notifies.
- Members would choose between three routes: the whole eligible corpus in instalments, a part lump sum with the rest paid out over time, or only the interest, with the principal left untouched.
- The remaining balance earns the prevailing EPF interest rate.
- Members can stop the plan at any time and withdraw what is left.
- The first phase covers statutory EPF members. Exempted PF trusts, which run their own funds, are outside it.

If a member dies while drawing instalments, the nominee or dependant can either withdraw the remaining balance or keep receiving the payouts, the PIB release says.

### Who benefits first

EPFO estimates that about 6.32 lakh members aged above 55 with PF savings of more than Rs 5 lakh will be the main users in the first phase.

## What the 90% Rule Means in Rupees

For a working member taking money out for an approved purpose, the lower floor means a bigger withdrawal. The examples below are simple arithmetic on the 75% and 90% limits; actual eligibility still depends on the purpose and service conditions under EPF rules.

| PF balance | Partial withdrawal at 75% | Partial withdrawal at 90% | Extra money available |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rs 2 lakh | Rs 1.5 lakh | Rs 1.8 lakh | Rs 30,000 |
| Rs 5 lakh | Rs 3.75 lakh | Rs 4.5 lakh | Rs 75,000 |
| Rs 10 lakh | Rs 7.5 lakh | Rs 9 lakh | Rs 1.5 lakh |

The 25% floor came from the board's October 2025 overhaul. As <a href="https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/epfo-allows-up-to-100-withdrawals-for-members" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PTI reported at the time</a>, that meeting merged 13 withdrawal provisions into three groups (essential needs such as illness, education and marriage; housing; and special circumstances), cut the minimum service for partial withdrawals to 12 months, and set aside 25% of contributions as a balance that could not be touched. The board has now lowered that floor to 10%.

## Job Loss Rule Rolled Back

The October 2025 changes also raised the wait for full PF settlement after unemployment from 2 months to 12 months. The board has now reversed it, Deccan Herald reported. Once notified, a member who stays unemployed for 2 months can apply to settle the PF account in full. The 36-month minimum wait for final withdrawal from the pension scheme stays as it is, according to Business Standard.

## Other Decisions From the Meeting

The board also approved:

- **Alternative Investment Funds:** in-principle approval to put up to 3% of fresh accretions into eligible AIFs, within EPFO's existing investment ceiling and initially only through government-backed entities.
- **EDLI relief:** a six-month window for establishments to renew expired insurance exemptions, with relief from contributions, interest and penal damages for a verified transitional period.
- **Exempted trusts:** 27 establishments surrendered their exemption and 4 had it cancelled, moving about Rs 5,996 crore belonging to 1,44,437 members to EPFO.
- **Faster claims:** auto-processed claims rose from 39% to 71%, the rejection ratio fell from 23% to 7.82%, and average settlement time dropped from 11.15 days to 2.57 days.

## When the Changes Could Start

EPFO has not given a start date. The scheme amendments need ministry approval and notification, and the Income Tax Act angle is being taken up with the Finance Ministry. People familiar with the process told Business Standard the SWP could take effect in around two months. Until a notification is issued, existing rules apply to every claim, so members planning a withdrawal should check the current limits on the EPFO portal before applying.

## EPFO Withdrawal Changes: Common Questions

**Can I withdraw 90% of my PF now?**
Not yet. The board approved cutting the minimum balance to 10%, but the change applies only after the EPF Scheme amendment is notified. Until then the 25% minimum balance applies.

**Is the systematic withdrawal plan compulsory for retirees?**
No. The PIB release says it is entirely voluntary, and a member can still take the full amount as a lump sum.

**How soon can I close my PF account after losing my job?**
Under the approved change, after 2 months of unemployment instead of 12 months, once the rule is notified.

**Will SWP instalments be taxed?**
That is still open. EPFO is consulting the Finance Ministry on the tax treatment of SWP withdrawals and interest.
