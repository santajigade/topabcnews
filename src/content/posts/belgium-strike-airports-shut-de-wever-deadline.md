Almost no planes will take off or land in Belgium on Monday, November 23. The country's three main union confederations have called a national day of action against the government's spending cuts, and staff at skeyes, the air traffic control company, are taking part. Skeyes said it cannot run normal services at Belgian airports or in Belgian airspace from 07:00 to 21:00 local time, which is 11:30 am to 1:30 am (Tuesday) in India. Only emergency and safety-critical flights are guaranteed.

The strike lands on the day King Philippe set as the deadline for Prime Minister Bart De Wever's coalition to agree a budget. The government is trying to save about 10 billion euros, and the unions say workers and public services should not pay for it.

## What Is Happening at Brussels and Charleroi

Brussels Airport said most departing and arriving flights would have to be cancelled, and Lufthansa Group confirmed the same for its flights to and from Brussels, according to Brussels Morning. Cancellations appear on the airport's departure board only after each airline registers them, so passengers should check with their airline rather than rely on the board, AeroTime reported.

At Charleroi, the main base for Ryanair in Belgium, nothing will take off or land during the 14-hour window. The airport called it a "complete shutdown of all the country's airports for an entire day" and criticised the "last-minute decision," <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/23/belgiums-airports-set-to-shut-down-due-to-anti-austerity-strike" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera reported</a>. About 3,400 people work at the airport.

Airlines said they will contact affected passengers directly about rebooking or refunds. Travellers can also follow updates on <a href="https://www.brusselsairport.be/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Brussels Airport's website</a>. Anyone with family or colleagues flying into or through Brussels from India on Monday evening IST should check with the airline before leaving for the airport.

## Eleven Strikes in Eleven Months

The Board of Airline Representatives in Belgium, which speaks for about 25 carriers, said Monday's walkout is the 11th strike to hit Belgian airspace in 11 months. Brussels Morning counts it as at least the 11th national day of union action since De Wever's government took office.

The unions' complaints cover purchasing power, pensions, social security, working conditions and public services. In a joint statement they said the savings should not fall on "workers, benefit recipients and public services."

## How the Budget Crisis Reached Monday

De Wever, leader of the N-VA, heads a five-party coalition that has been unable to agree how to split about 10 billion euros of savings between spending cuts and tax increases. The goal is to bring down the deficit and meet EU fiscal rules by 2029, according to Brussels Morning.

The sticking point is tax. Coalition partner Vooruit wants a tax on the financial assets of millionaires, including shares and crypto. It has lowered the amount it wants such a tax to raise from 1.5 billion euros to 1.1 billion euros. If no deal is reached, the King can either give more time or accept De Wever's resignation, Brussels Morning reported.

## Life on the Ground in Brussels

Protesters gather at Brussels-North station from 10:00 and march towards Brussels-South from 11:00. Police expect traffic disruption in the centre from 09:00 to 15:00, and household waste collection in the Brussels-Capital Region is also expected to be hit. Schools and government offices may be affected too, Al Jazeera said.

Rail travel is the exception, with SNCB trains expected to run normally. The Brussels metro, tram and bus network STIB is heavily disrupted, De Lijn is running only about six in ten services in Flanders, and TEC in Wallonia has cut some trips.

## What Happens When the Deadline Passes

The air traffic halt is due to end at 21:00 Belgian time, 1:30 am IST. The political deadline has no such end point. By the close of Monday, De Wever's coalition either has a budget, or King Philippe must choose between granting more time and accepting the resignation he refused on November 6.
