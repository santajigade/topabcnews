United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres gave world leaders a blunt warning at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. He said the climate crisis is "outrunning us" and that the current course is a path to "mutually assured destruction". His speech opened about two days of addresses by heads of state and government, as negotiators from nearly 200 countries try to agree on a plan to speed up the use of electricity and revive stalled efforts to move away from coal, oil and gas.

The leaders' segment comes in the first week of a two-week meeting that runs from November 9 to 20. Turkey hosts the talks, while Australia leads the negotiations under a co-presidency deal. We covered the <a href="/cop31-opens-antalya-climate-finance-dispute/">early fight over the climate finance agenda on day one</a>; this report looks at what leaders heard on Wednesday and the main proposals now on the table.

## What Guterres Told COP31 Leaders

According to Reuters, Guterres said science is not asking the world to give up but to "fight harder". He pointed out that the last four years have been the hottest on record and, as reported by Al Jazeera, that emissions in 2025 reached an all-time high.

He also made an economic argument. Three quarters of humanity, he said, live in countries that import fossil fuels, which leaves them exposed to sudden price shocks. He linked recent conflicts to a cost-of-living crisis, an apparent reference to the jump in oil prices since the war involving Iran disrupted Middle East supplies. The Associated Press has reported that the war has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, a key route for oil shipments.

Guterres accused the fossil fuel lobby of delaying action and said coal, oil and gas must eventually go "the way of the dinosaurs". He also asked Turkey and Australia to push forward a roadmap for moving away from fossil fuels before the summit ends.

## The 35 by 35 Electrification Goal and Why It Is Disputed

Turkey, as COP31 president, wants the final deal to include a voluntary target to raise electricity's share of global energy use to 35% by 2035. Australia's climate minister Chris Bowen, who is leading the negotiations, told ABC News Australia that electricity now makes up about 23% of global energy use, so the target would need the pace of electrification to rise more than fourfold.

Bowen said the world has no chance of holding warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius without reaching that level.

Critics have a different worry. The draft text does not require the extra electricity to come from clean sources. If new power is generated partly from coal or gas, they argue, the goal could lock in fossil fuel use instead of reducing it. The European Union has insisted that electrification must be "clean". Analysts cited by ABC estimate that meeting the 35% goal with clean power alone could trim about 0.3 degrees Celsius from projected warming, which is at least 2.6 degrees under current policies.

### Rooftop solar pledge gains new members

A separate, voluntary pledge backed by Australia aims to double the number of homes with rooftop solar to at least 300 million by 2030. Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria, Palau and Vanuatu joined it this week. Pakistan and Indonesia's state power utility PT PLN have signalled support. The pledge is not binding and asks countries to ease permits and improve access to finance for households.

## A Year of Extreme Weather Behind the Talks

The summit follows a year of severe climate disasters. Western Europe faced extreme heatwaves in 2026, and a glacier collapse in Nepal triggered a flood that killed more than 1,450 people, with thousands still missing, according to Reuters. A UN report last month found the world is on course to break the 1.5 degree limit set under the Paris Agreement. Our earlier report on <a href="/wmo-2026-among-four-warmest-years-cop31/">2026 ranking among the four warmest years</a> explains the latest temperature data.

<a href="https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168763" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UN Climate Change chief Simon Stiell</a> has said no single conference can close the gap between science and reality, and the COP31 presidency has framed this meeting around putting existing promises into practice rather than writing new ones.

## Why It Matters for India and Other Importing Nations

India's leaders were not among the heads of government listed as speaking on Wednesday, and none of the reports reviewed mentioned an Indian announcement on the rooftop solar pledge. Still, two of the themes in Antalya matter for Indian readers. Guterres's point about fuel importers being hostage to price shocks applies to large oil buying economies, and the electrification debate will shape global rules on how clean new power must be.

The next steps will play out in the second week, when ministers take over the detailed bargaining. Whether the 35% target survives, and whether it carries a clean energy condition, is likely to be one of the clearest tests of what Antalya can deliver.
