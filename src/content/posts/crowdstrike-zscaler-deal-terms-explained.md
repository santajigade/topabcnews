CrowdStrike agreed on Monday, November 23, to buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal that values the cloud security company at about $43.9 billion. Zscaler shareholders will get 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share they hold, worth about $260.41 based on Friday's prices. That is roughly 34% above Zscaler's closing price on November 20. Both boards approved the deal unanimously, and the companies expect it to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which begins in February 2027.

The two companies announced the deal alongside their quarterly results. CrowdStrike moved its earnings call forward from December 1 to the morning of November 23.

## The Deal in Numbers

| Term | Figure |
|---|---|
| Enterprise value | About $43.9 billion |
| Payment | All stock, no cash |
| Exchange ratio | 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share |
| Implied price | About $260.41 per Zscaler share |
| Premium | About 34% over the November 20 close |
| Ownership after closing | CrowdStrike holders 84.1%, Zscaler holders 15.9% |
| Combined annual recurring revenue | More than $10 billion |
| Expected closing | Second half of CrowdStrike fiscal 2028 |

The deal still needs approval from Zscaler shareholders and from regulators. Zscaler founder and chief executive Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, who own about 34% of Zscaler's shares, have signed agreements to vote for it. Until it closes, the two companies will keep operating separately. After closing, Chaudhry will keep running the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board, according to the <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001535527/000110465926130785/tm2630454d1_ex99-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">joint press release filed with the US SEC</a>.

## Why CrowdStrike Wants Zscaler

The two companies cover different parts of corporate security. CrowdStrike's Falcon platform protects endpoints: laptops, servers and the workloads running on them. Zscaler sells "zero trust" network security, which sits between users and the internet or company apps and checks every connection. Zscaler says it protects half of the Fortune 500, runs more than 200 service locations and handles more than 750 billion transactions a day.

The companies already share more than 2,000 customers and have 16 product integrations. CrowdStrike counts about 8,000 Zscaler customers that do not yet buy from it. It is targeting about $1 billion a year in extra revenue within 30 months of closing, and about $500 million a year in cost savings within 18 to 24 months. It says the combined market it can sell into will be worth more than $185 billion in 2029.

Both chief executives tied the deal to AI agents, software that can act on its own inside company systems. "Today, safety is required to let AI in," CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz said. Chaudhry said AI agents "are rapidly becoming the new weakest link."

## How It Compares With Google-Wiz and Palo Alto-CyberArk

This is the third very large cybersecurity takeover in about 20 months, and the biggest of the three.

| Deal | Value | How it is paid | Announced | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CrowdStrike buys Zscaler | About $43.9 billion | All stock | November 23, 2026 | Needs shareholder and regulatory approval |
| Google buys Wiz | $32 billion | All cash | March 2025 | Closed March 11, 2026 |
| Palo Alto Networks buys CyberArk | About $25 billion | $45 cash plus 2.2005 shares per CyberArk share | July 2025 | Closed February 11, 2026 |

The other two deals took between seven months and a year to close. Google's purchase of Wiz needed US clearance in November 2025 and EU approval in February 2026. <a href="https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/company/press/2026/palo-alto-networks-completes-acquisition-of-cyberark-to-secure-the-ai-era" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Palo Alto Networks completed its CyberArk purchase</a> in February, about seven months after it first announced the plan. CrowdStrike's own target of the second half of fiscal 2028 points to a similar or longer wait.

Because this deal is paid in stock, the $260.41 figure is not fixed. Zscaler holders receive a fixed number of CrowdStrike shares, so what they finally get moves with CrowdStrike's share price until closing. Google paid a fixed cash price for Wiz.

## What the Share Prices Said

CrowdStrike shares fell more than 8% before Monday's opening bell, while Zscaler rose 17%, AP reported. <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/crowdstrike-reaches-44-billion-cybersecurity-deal-for-zscaler" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Bloomberg also reported the $44 billion agreement</a> on Monday. Other security stocks also moved up in premarket trade, with Palo Alto Networks up 4.1%, Fortinet up 4.4% and Okta up 5%, according to Investing.com. Before the deal, CrowdStrike was worth about $234 billion and Zscaler about $32 billion.

CrowdStrike's own results were strong. Revenue for the quarter rose 28% to $1.58 billion, above estimates of $1.53 billion. It raised its full-year revenue forecast to about $6.08 billion, from an earlier range of $5.99 billion to $6.01 billion. Zscaler reported quarterly revenue up 20% to $949.3 million and annual recurring revenue of $3.88 billion.

CrowdStrike's existing shareholders will own about 84.1% of the combined company. Indian investors who hold US tech stocks or global technology funds were already watching a weak US market, after the <a href="/sp-500-correction-nifty-rally-fii-inflows-november/">S&P 500 slipped into a correction</a> this month.

## The India Link

Zscaler has offices in India. When it reopened its offices after the pandemic in May 2022, it named Bangalore, Pune and Mohali among its locations, according to a Zscaler blog post from that year. Neither company has said anything about jobs, offices or pricing in India after the merger, and both will run separately until the deal closes.
