Ukrainian and Russian negotiators met for a second straight day in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday, November 11, in talks mediated by the United States. The first session on Tuesday ran for about four hours and, according to Ukrainian sources, produced an understanding on a large exchange of prisoners of war. But none of the three sides has reported progress on the central questions of territory and post-war security, and officials in Washington and Europe have urged patience.

## A Second Day Focused on Humanitarian Issues

Wednesday's programme opened with a three-way session between the US, Russian and Ukrainian delegations, followed by a separate meeting between Kyiv's team and European partners. Rustem Umerov, head of Ukraine's Foreign Intelligence Service and Kyiv's chief negotiator, posted on Telegram that the second day had begun. Wednesday was the last day of scheduled meetings.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who is not attending, described the opening day as "constructive and quite substantive." He said the discussions centred on humanitarian matters, including prisoner releases, the return of Ukrainian civilians held in Russia and children taken from Ukraine, as well as security guarantees.

This is the first direct meeting between the two warring sides since February. We covered the run-up to this round in our earlier report on <a href="/abu-dhabi-ukraine-russia-us-peace-talks-resume/">the resumption of talks after a nine-month pause</a>.

## The Reported Prisoner Exchange

Ukrainian outlet Ukrainska Pravda, citing sources on Kyiv's negotiating team, reported that the two sides agreed on a "large-scale exchange of prisoners of war in the near future." A senior Ukrainian official told the Associated Press that details would be worked out in the coming days.

Moscow has been more guarded. A source in the Russian delegation, quoted by state television channel Rossiya 1, cautioned against getting ahead of events. No official numbers have been announced.

Prisoner swaps have been one of the few areas where the two sides have cooperated during the war. Talks in Geneva in February led to an exchange of 500 prisoners from each side, carried out in two phases in March. According to Ukrainian sources, at least 78 exchanges have taken place since February 2022, bringing home nearly 10,000 Ukrainians.

Representatives of the UK, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission met the Ukrainian team separately. The Kremlin said its delegation had no contact with the Europeans and none was planned. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said President Vladimir Putin was being briefed constantly.

## Territory and Security Still Divide the Sides

The core disagreements remain where they were. Russia controls about one-fifth of Ukraine, including Crimea and parts of the eastern Donbas region. Moscow continues to demand that Ukraine hand over all of Donbas, give up its NATO bid and accept limits on its military. Zelenskyy has refused to give up land that Ukrainian troops have died defending, and Kyiv wants an immediate ceasefire before wider negotiations.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said Moscow prefers to agree a broad European security framework before dealing with specifics of the war, and he rejected a truce. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen took the opposite view, saying wider security talks should come only after a ceasefire.

Kushner said the aim was a "new framework" and that there were "some good ideas" to bridge differences. An unnamed US official told reporters before the talks that both sides had privately shown some flexibility on occupied land, and that any territorial deal would most likely come last, after security guarantees.

### Key highlights

- Talks held on November 10 and 11 in Abu Dhabi, with the US as mediator
- Tuesday's three-way session lasted about four hours
- A large prisoner of war exchange was reported agreed, with details pending
- No breakthrough on territory, ceasefire or security guarantees
- Neither Putin nor Zelenskyy attended

## Fighting Continues as Diplomats Meet

The talks have not slowed the war. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile on the eve of the talks. Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi said Russia struck six large electricity substations in four central regions, as Moscow targets the power grid ahead of winter. In overnight attacks before Wednesday's session, seven civilians were wounded in Zaporizhzhia and Kharkiv, the AP reported.

Russia's defence ministry said its air defences downed 627 Ukrainian drones overnight, including 150 over the St. Petersburg region. A drone attack caused a fire at Ust-Luga, one of Russia's main oil export ports, injuring three workers, according to the regional governor.

## How Washington and Europe Are Reading the Talks

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking from the <a href="https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/11/09/g7-talks-in-paris-try-to-tackle-global-crises-with-uncertainty-high-over-the-us-approach/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris</a>, called the first day "very productive" but said a solution was not imminent. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he did not have overly high expectations. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said there could be no solution without Europe.

Analysts quoted by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/10/russia-ukraine-and-us-to-meet-as-zelenskyy-claims-battlefield-successes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a> said a deal was unlikely from this round because the two positions remain far apart. They noted that the US administration may push for a result before the new Congress, elected in the November 3 midterms, takes office in January.

## What Comes After Abu Dhabi

Russian negotiator Medinsky was expected to speak to the press after the second day. No date has yet been set for another round. For now, the most concrete result appears to be on the humanitarian track: if the reported prisoner swap goes ahead, it would be the first tangible outcome of the renewed US effort. For India, which has called for a peaceful resolution and has an interest in safe Black Sea trade routes, any steady progress would be welcome, but the gap on territory suggests a long road ahead.
