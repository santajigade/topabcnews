<p>ITR filing deadline is the most important topic. The <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/">Income Tax Department</a> has announced an <strong>extension of the deadline for filing Income Tax Returns (ITRs)</strong> for the <a href="https://topabcnews.com/category/finance/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">financial </a>year 2024–25 (Assessment Year 2025–26).</p><p>Taxpayers who do not require their accounts to be audited can now file their returns by <strong>September 15, 2025</strong>.</p><p>The pace of return filing has been much slower compared to last year. As of <strong>September 4, 2025</strong>, only <strong>4.56 crore returns</strong> had been filed, which is lower than the previous year’s numbers.</p><p>Here are the details of current ITR filing status, important deadlines, penalties, and what taxpayers should do next.</p><h4>Current Filing Status (as of September 4, 2025)</h4><p>According to data from the Income Tax Department:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Total ITRs filed</strong>: Over <strong>4.56 crore</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>E-verified returns</strong>: Over <strong>4.33 crore</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>Returns processed</strong>: Around <strong>3.17 crore</strong></p><p> </p></li></ul><p>This means that while a large number of taxpayers have completed filing, but a substantial share is still pending. In fact, more than <strong>3 crore people</strong> are yet to file their returns, with only a little over a week left before the September 15 deadline.</p><h4>Comparison With Last Year</h4><p>Looking at last year’s filing numbers shows just how much the pace has slowed.</p><ul><li><p>By <strong>July 31, 2024</strong>, around <strong>7.28 crore ITRs</strong> were already filed for AY 2024–25.</p></li><li><p>On that single day (July 31, 2024), nearly <strong>70 lakh taxpayers</strong> filed their returns.</p></li></ul><p>This comparison highlights two things:</p><ol><li><p>Last year saw a record surge in last minute filings.</p></li><li><p>This year, even after the extension, the numbers are lagging behind.</p></li></ol><p>This raises the question, will we see another rush in the final days, or will many taxpayers still miss the deadline?</p><p>Why to wait? Please file your returns before ITR filing deadline.</p><h4>Who Needs to File by September 15?</h4><p>The extended deadline of <strong>September 15, 2025</strong> applies only to taxpayers whose accounts <strong>do not require auditing</strong>. This typically includes:</p><ul><li><p>Salaried individuals</p></li><li><p>Pensioners</p></li><li><p>Freelancers with simple income structures</p></li><li><p>Taxpayers with income only from one or two sources</p></li></ul><p><br />For taxpayers whose accounts need to be audited (such as business owners or professionals with complex accounts), the deadline is <strong>October 31, 2025</strong>. So do not wait for ITR filing deadline.</p><h4>Penalties for Missing the Deadline</h4><p>If you miss the September 15 deadline, there are consequences:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Late Fee (Section 234F)</strong></p><ul><li><p>A late filing penalty of up to <strong>₹5,000</strong> will be charged.</p></li><li><p>If your total income is below ₹5 lakh, the late fee will be reduced to <strong>₹1,000</strong>.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Interest on Pending Tax (Section 234A)</strong></p><ul><li><p>If you have tax dues, an interest of <strong>1% per month</strong> will be charged until payment is made.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Loss of Certain Benefits</strong></p><ul><li><p>You cannot carry forward certain losses (like capital losses) if you miss the deadline.</p></li></ul></li></ol><h4>Belated Returns: A Second Chance</h4><p>Taxpayers who miss the September 15 deadline still have the option to file a <strong>belated return</strong>.</p><ul><li><p>The last date for filing a belated return is <strong>December 31, 2025</strong>.</p></li><li><p>However, this comes with penalties (up to ₹5,000) and loss of some benefits.</p></li><li><p>Also, refunds may get delayed if the return is filed late.</p></li></ul><p>So while this option provides a backup, it is not advisable to rely on it.</p><h4>Why Filing Early Is Better</h4><p>Many taxpayers wait until the last moment to file returns, but this creates problems. Here’s why filing early is always better:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Avoids Website Glitches</strong>: The Income Tax e-filing portal often slows down due to heavy traffic in the final days.</p></li><li><p><strong>Faster Refunds</strong>: Early filers usually get refunds processed more quickly.</p></li><li><p><strong>Peace of Mind</strong>: Filing on time avoids penalties, interest, and last-minute stress.</p></li><li><p><strong>Error Checking</strong>: Early filing allows time to correct mistakes, if any, in income details or deductions.</p></li></ul><h4>Why Are Filings Slower This Year?</h4><p>Several reasons may explain why fewer returns have been filed so far this year:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Extended Deadline Announced Early</strong></p><ul><li><p>The government announced the September 15 extension in May itself. This may have made taxpayers more relaxed, thinking they had more time.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Waiting for Form 26AS and AIS Updates</strong></p><ul><li><p>Many taxpayers wait until their Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS are updated fully before filing.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Hopes for Further Extension</strong></p><ul><li><p>Some taxpayers are waiting in the hope that the government may extend the deadline once again.</p></li></ul></li></ol><h4>Will the Deadline Be Extended Again?</h4><p>At this stage, the government has not indicated any further extension beyond September 15. Whether it will extend again depends on:</p><ul><li><p>The pace of filings in the next 7–10 days</p></li><li><p>Whether technical glitches affect the portal</p></li><li><p>Pressure from taxpayer associations and industry groups</p></li></ul><p>If filings surge in the final week (as seen last year), another extension is unlikely.</p><h4>Practical Tips for Taxpayers</h4><p>If you haven’t filed yet, here are some steps to make the process smoother:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Collect Documents Early</strong></p><ul><li><p>Salary slips, Form 16, bank statements, interest certificates, investment proofs.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Check AIS and Form 26AS</strong></p><ul><li><p>Verify income details and TDS deductions to avoid mismatches.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Use Pre-Filled Forms</strong></p><ul><li><p>The e-filing portal now provides pre-filled data, which reduces errors.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Don’t Delay Refunds</strong></p><ul><li><p>If you are eligible for a refund, filing late only means waiting longer to receive it.</p></li></ul></li><li><p><strong>Seek Help if Needed</strong></p><ul><li><p>Use a Chartered Accountant or online tax filing platforms if you find the process confusing.</p></li></ul></li></ol><h4>Key Deadlines for AY 2025-26</h4>										<figure>
