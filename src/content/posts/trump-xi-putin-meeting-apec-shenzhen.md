US President Donald Trump has said he expects to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin together when Asia-Pacific leaders gather in Shenzhen, China, on November 18 and 19. Speaking to reporters on Sunday, November 8, before flying back to Washington from Florida, Trump said the talks would cover denuclearisation and ending the war in Iran. China's foreign ministry has confirmed it is in contact with the parties involved, but has not said whether a three-way meeting will actually happen.

If it does take place, it would be a rare moment in world diplomacy. According to TRT World, citing AFP, the three leaders have each met the other two separately, but have never held a joint summit.

## What Trump Said About Meeting Xi and Putin

According to Reuters, Trump told reporters that he would travel to China for the summit and that one of his meetings there would likely include both Putin and Xi. "That'll be interesting," he said of the possible encounter.

He linked the talks to two issues: nuclear arms reduction and the conflict involving Iran. In October, Trump had already said he was open to three-way arms control talks with the Chinese and Russian leaders aimed at denuclearisation.

Separately, in an interview with CBS's 60 Minutes, Trump said he believed a deal to end the Russia-Ukraine war was close. He suggested Ukraine could come out of any settlement in a "little bit different shape," but did not give details. The Wall Street Journal has reported that Putin himself proposed a trilateral meeting to discuss ending the war in Ukraine.

## How Moscow and Beijing Have Responded

The idea of a three-way summit has been building for weeks, mostly from the Russian side.

- **Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov** first said in September that a meeting at APEC was possible. After Putin met US special envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg on October 21, Ushakov said none of the three sides had ruled it out.
- **Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov** told the Financial Times that "work is under way on organising such a meeting." At a briefing in early November, he said Moscow hoped it would be finalised in the coming days and described China, as host, as the main organiser.
- **China's foreign ministry** has been more cautious. Spokesperson Mao Ning said Beijing was "maintaining communication with relevant parties." Spokesperson Guo Jiakun later said China was in contact about a possible meeting of the three heads of state on the sidelines of APEC, but had no further information to share.

The Financial Times reported that China had not yet decided whether to go ahead, and that it was unclear whether officials from the three countries were meeting together or whether Beijing was talking to Washington and Moscow separately. One source told the paper that the US and Russia were pressing China to agree.

A source close to Kremlin thinking told the FT that Moscow does not expect detailed Ukraine negotiations at the meeting itself.

## Ukraine and Europe Raise Concerns

Not everyone welcomes the idea. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned last month that such a summit could hand Moscow "another major diplomatic advantage." He compared it to the 1945 Yalta conference, where Allied leaders divided spheres of influence in Europe, and said Ukraine did not want a repeat. "Russia wants this meeting more than anyone," he added.

Germany's president, who met Xi in late October, has stressed that Europe must be involved in any Ukraine talks, according to The Moscow Times.

The Kyiv Independent has noted that US-led peace efforts have largely stalled despite repeated visits to Moscow by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, with Russia and Ukraine still far apart on territory.

## Why a Three-Way Meeting Matters

Relations between the three powers shape global security, trade and energy markets. Several threads run through the possible talks:

- **Arms control:** Trump has repeatedly said he wants denuclearisation talks that include China, not just Russia.
- **Ukraine:** Moscow and Washington are both looking for a way to end the war, though on very different terms from Kyiv.
- **Iran:** Trump has tied the meeting to ending the conflict with Iran, where the US recently moved to tighten pressure, as covered in our report on <a href="/us-task-force-iron-gate-iran-land-routes/">Task Force Iron Gate and Iran's land routes</a>.
- **US-China ties:** Xi and Trump have already met twice this year, most recently during Xi's White House visit in September, according to TRT World.

## What Happens Next in Shenzhen

For now, the meeting remains a possibility rather than a confirmed event. Beijing, as host, holds the deciding vote, and its statements so far have stopped short of agreeing to a joint session. Peskov said on November 2 that Putin's APEC schedule was still being drawn up.

Over the next week, watch for a formal schedule from China's foreign ministry, any White House announcement on Trump's travel plans, and Kremlin confirmation of Putin's attendance. Even if the three leaders do meet, officials on the Russian side have played down hopes of a breakthrough on Ukraine at the summit itself.
