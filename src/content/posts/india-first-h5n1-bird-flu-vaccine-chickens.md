Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan is set to dedicate India's first home-grown H5N1 bird flu vaccine for chickens on Friday, November 20, at the ICAR-National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases (ICAR-NIHSAD) in Bhopal. It is an inactivated vaccine, made by scientists at the institute, against the clade 2.3.2.1a strain of highly pathogenic avian influenza. According to a government release carried by <a href="https://ddnews.gov.in/en/shivraj-singh-chouhan-to-dedicate-indias-first-indigenous-h5n1-chicken-vaccine-on-november-20/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DD News</a>, this is the strain behind most chicken outbreaks in the country, and no commercial vaccine for it exists today.

The launch comes a week after a confirmed H5N1 outbreak in Tamil Nadu's Cuddalore district, where tens of thousands of birds died in a fortnight.

## What the Vaccine Is, in Plain Terms

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Indigenous | Developed in India by ICAR-NIHSAD, Bhopal, a laboratory of the <a href="https://icar.org.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Indian Council of Agricultural Research</a> |
| Inactivated | Made from virus that has been killed, so it cannot cause infection; it trains the bird's immune system to recognise H5N1 |
| H5N1 | The bird flu subtype that, with H5N8, has caused more than 500 outbreaks in India since 2006 |
| Clade 2.3.2.1a | A genetic branch of H5N1. The government says it causes most chicken outbreaks in India |
| For chickens | Built for poultry. It is not a human vaccine |

The release says clade 2.3.2.1a has also been found in several other animal species and has been linked to three human cases, though it does not say where those cases occurred.

## How the Cuddalore Outbreak Reached NIHSAD

When birds start dying, state samples go to NIHSAD in Bhopal for confirmation. This is how it went in Cuddalore this month:

1. **October 30:** Tamil Nadu's Animal Husbandry Department gets reports of sudden poultry deaths at a farm in Thamanampatti village.
2. **First tests:** Samples go to the Poultry Disease Diagnostic Laboratory in Namakkal.
3. **November 5:** As deaths spread to nearby farms, samples are sent to NIHSAD, Bhopal.
4. **November 13:** NIHSAD confirms H5N1 at a farm in Kattiyankuppam village, Kurinjipadi taluk, DT Next reported.
5. **Response:** Culling within a 1 km radius, deep burial, disinfection and nine rapid response teams. By then 35,647 birds had died and 1,550 had been culled.
6. **November 14:** District Collector Sibi Adithya Senthil Kumar asks farmers not to restock for three months.

Cuddalore was not the only scare this year. In February, The Federal reported H5N1 in more than 1,000 dead crows in and around Chennai, poultry culls in Andhra Pradesh's Annamayya and Chittoor districts, and a 10 km surveillance zone in Bihar's Bhagalpur.

## The Cost of an Outbreak, by the Government's Numbers

The official release makes the case for the vaccine with these figures:

- A major outbreak could pull India's GDP growth down by as much as 1.5%.
- More than 20% of the country's poultry could be at risk if the disease spread unchecked.
- The 2006 outbreak cost an estimated Rs 2,200 crore to Rs 3,000 crore in direct and indirect losses.
- One outbreak in Manipur caused losses equal to 0.5% of that state's GDP.
- Worldwide, the WHO has counted 1,003 human H5N1 cases and 479 deaths in 25 countries, India among them, since 2003.

A ministry statement quoted by IANS says the vaccine should strengthen India's ability to control bird flu and reduce what poultry farmers lose.

## What a Poultry Farmer Should Know Right Now

Dedicating the vaccine to the nation is not the same as having it on sale at the local veterinary store. The official releases leave several practical points open:

- **Availability:** No date has been given for commercial supply, and no manufacturing partner has been named.
- **Doses and schedule:** The releases do not say how many shots a bird needs or at what age.
- **Who can vaccinate:** No guidance yet on whether vaccination will be open to all farms, limited to high-risk zones, or run by state animal husbandry departments.
- **Existing rules still apply:** Outbreak control still relies on culling around infected farms, movement bans and disinfection, as in Cuddalore. Farmers who see sudden bird deaths should report them to the district animal husbandry office.

Details on rollout are likely to come from ICAR and state animal husbandry departments, so those are the notices to watch.

## What to Watch After the Bhopal Launch

The next step is rollout: who makes the vaccine, how it reaches farms, and whether states use it in outbreak zones such as Cuddalore. None of that has been announced.
