India's bowlers turned a below-par total into a series-clinching win on Sunday, November 15, as the visitors beat New Zealand by 22 runs in the fifth and final ODI at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui. Bowled out for 209 in 42.1 overs, India dismissed the hosts for 187 in 48.1 overs to take the five-match series 3-1. The result ended New Zealand's long unbeaten run in bilateral ODI series at home and gave India their first away ODI series win under head coach Gautam Gambhir.

## How India Defended 209 at Bay Oval

On paper, 209 looked short on a ground known for high scores. India's top order did not help. According to <a href="https://www.outlookindia.com/sports/cricket/india-vs-new-zealand-5th-odi-live-score-squad-ind-vs-nz-scorecard-bay-oval-mount-maunganui-latest-updates" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Outlook India's match coverage</a>, both Rohit Sharma and Virat Kohli fell for single-digit scores, leaving the middle order to rebuild.

Ishan Kishan and Axar Patel did most of that work, each scoring 46. Kishan made his runs off 58 balls before Jacob Duffy removed him. Naman Dhir added 22 off 31 balls, but wickets kept falling. Yash Thakur, playing his first ODI, was out for a duck.

The innings got a late lift from an unlikely place. Batting at number 11, Arshdeep Singh hit two sixes in a quick 14 off just four balls. He and Mohammed Siraj added 28 for the last wicket, and those runs proved to be close to the final winning margin. Arshdeep joined Venkatesh Prasad and Mohammed Shami as the only Indian number 11 batters to hit two sixes in an ODI innings.

For New Zealand, Duffy and captain Mitchell Santner took three wickets each. Santner removed Dhir, Auqib Nabi and Thakur.

## Arshdeep, Axar and Siraj Squeeze the Chase

New Zealand needed 210, but India's attack never let them settle. Siraj, playing his first game of the series, struck early by dismissing Tim Seifert and later removed Finn Allen, who made only 1.

The chase had a stop-start feel. Dean Foxcroft top-scored with 31 off 42 balls and survived an early let-off when Kohli dropped a chance at second slip off Arshdeep's very first delivery. Thakur eventually removed Foxcroft for his maiden ODI wicket. Nick Kelly (24 off 45), Will Young (22 off 42) and Michael Bracewell (19 off 33) all got starts but scored too slowly to ease the pressure. Nabi accounted for Bracewell.

Santner threatened to drag his side close with 24 off 31 balls, but a catch by Dhir to remove him was seen as the turning point. The lower order fought on, and the match finally ended when Kishan stumped Blair Tickner for 14, with New Zealand still 22 runs short.

Arshdeep finished with three wickets, while Axar and Siraj took two each.

## How the Series Unfolded Match by Match

This was a series shaped heavily by the weather. India, led by Shubman Gill, won a rain-hit opener in Auckland by five wickets after bowling New Zealand out for 185. In Wellington, they then edged the second game by four runs under the DLS method, as reported by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/11/7/india-new-zealand-second-odi-rain-dls-method-win-four-runs" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>. You can read our full report on that match in [India's DLS win in Wellington](/india-beat-new-zealand-2nd-odi-dls-wellington/).

The third ODI in Hamilton was abandoned without a ball being bowled because of persistent rain, keeping India 2-0 ahead. New Zealand won the fourth game to keep the scoreline alive, which made Sunday's match the decider for the final margin.

- Match 1, Auckland: India won by five wickets
- Match 2, Wellington: India won by four runs (DLS)
- Match 3, Hamilton: No result, rain
- Match 4: New Zealand won
- Match 5, Mount Maunganui: India won by 22 runs

## Why This Series Win Matters for Gambhir's India

New Zealand had not lost a bilateral ODI series at home for seven years, a stretch covering 11 series. Breaking that record on their soil is a real achievement, especially in conditions that often favour seam bowling.

It is also a milestone for Gambhir, whose side had not won an ODI series away from home since he took charge. The batting, though, will worry the team management. India were bowled out for 209 with almost eight overs unused, and senior batters struggled in the final game.

Fielding was another weak area. Outlook India counted 14 misfields and eight dropped catches by India across the series, numbers that could have cost them in a tighter contest.

The bowling unit, on the other hand, came out of the tour with plenty of credit. Arshdeep led from the front, Siraj made an immediate impact on his return, and newcomers Thakur and Nabi both picked up wickets in the decider.

## What Comes Next for India in New Zealand

The tour is not over. India stay in New Zealand for the Test leg, and the squad has already seen changes, with pacer Gurnoor Brar added and spinner Kuldeep Yadav released. Read more in our story on [the updated India Test squad](/gurnoor-brar-india-test-squad-kuldeep-released/).

The switch to red-ball cricket will test a different set of skills, but India will travel to the first Test with a series trophy already in hand. For New Zealand, the challenge is to regroup quickly after losing a home ODI series for the first time in seven years.
