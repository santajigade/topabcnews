The United States is preparing new tariffs on India and 15 other economies after an investigation, opened in March, into what Washington calls industrial excess capacity. The Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday, November 24, that US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could publish his findings as early as this week, according to a PTI report carried by <a href="https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/us-planning-to-hike-tariffs-against-india-15-other-countries-report-126112401230_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Standard</a>. The final rates are not yet known, but the report said they would come on top of tariffs already in place.

What is known so far:

- **Who is covered:** 16 economies, including India, China, the European Union, Japan and Vietnam.
- **Legal route:** Section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974, which allows tariffs against trade practices the US finds unfair.
- **When it began:** the probe was opened on March 11, 2026.
- **Rates:** not announced. Indian media reports citing the Journal said India and Vietnam may face higher rates than the EU, Japan and South Korea, which are expected to get 15% or less, and that China could face some of the highest duties.
- **Existing duties:** a separate forced labour probe completed in July found grounds for tariffs of 10% to 12.5% on dozens of partners, including all 16 in this probe.

## Why the US Turned to Section 301

The US Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) did not let the President impose tariffs. The administration had to withdraw those tariffs on February 24, according to law firm White & Case. It then imposed a temporary 10% global tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act, which can last no more than 150 days. India's rate also moved to that temporary 10% at the time, a White House official told News On AIR in February.

Section 301 gives USTR a separate route, based on an investigation of each country's trade practices. On March 11, USTR opened the excess capacity investigations into 16 economies and said it would also start forced labour investigations covering about 60 partners. The tariffs now being prepared come from the excess capacity probe.

## What USTR Said About India in Its Notice

The March 17 <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-03-17/html/2026-05214.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Federal Register notice</a> sets out the case against each economy. For India, it makes these points:

- India ran a **$42 billion goods trade surplus with the US in 2025**.
- India has global trade surpluses in textiles, health, construction goods and automotive goods.
- The notice says India "has created significant excess capacity in petrochemicals, steel, and other industries."
- It singles out solar modules, saying India's module manufacturing capacity is nearly three times its annual domestic demand.

The notice does not name any specific Indian subsidy scheme. Its list of practices, such as subsidies, subsidised lending, state-owned enterprise activity and weak labour or environmental rules, is stated for all 16 economies together.

## How India's Surplus Compares With Other Targets

USTR's notice gives bilateral goods surplus figures with the US for several of the economies. The years differ, as shown.

| Economy | Goods surplus with the US (per USTR notice) | Year |
|---|---|---|
| China | $361 billion | 2024 |
| European Union | $237 billion | 2024 |
| Vietnam | $178 billion | 2025 |
| Japan | $57 billion | 2024 |
| India | $42 billion | 2025 |

The other economies under investigation are Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, South Korea, Taiwan, Bangladesh and Mexico. India's surplus is the smallest in the table, but India sits alongside Vietnam among the economies reportedly in line for steeper rates.

## Indian Export Sectors on the US List

USTR named 21 manufacturing sectors as examples: aluminium, automobiles, batteries, cement, chemicals, electronics, energy goods, glass, machine tools, machinery, non-ferrous metals, paper, plastics, processed food and beverages, robotics, satellites, semiconductors, ships, solar modules, steel and transportation equipment.

Several match areas the notice flags for India, notably steel, automobiles, chemicals and solar modules. It is not yet clear whether the final tariffs will apply to all goods from a country or only to certain sectors. The <a href="https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/march/fact-sheet-ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-structural-excess-capacity-and-production" target="_blank" rel="noopener">USTR fact sheet</a> says action could take the form of tariffs or other measures.

## Where the India-US Trade Talks Stand

India and the US are negotiating an interim trade agreement after the framework agreed in February went back for further discussion, Business Standard reported. India wants lower US tariffs on its goods than competitors such as Bangladesh, Vietnam and Cambodia, all three of which are also in this probe.

Under Section 301, if USTR makes an affirmative finding, action normally follows within 30 days, though this can be extended, according to White & Case. USTR also usually invites public comment on proposed remedies before they take effect.
