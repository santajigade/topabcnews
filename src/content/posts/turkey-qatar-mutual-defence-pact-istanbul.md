Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement in Istanbul on Friday, November 13, promising to take a unified stance against any external armed attack on either country. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Qatar's Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani signed the deal after talks at Dolmabahce Palace. The pact comes as the war involving Iran keeps the Gulf on edge, with fresh attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz.

## What the Turkey-Qatar Defence Pact Says

According to a joint communique reported by Reuters and published by <a href="https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/11/turkey-qatar-sign-mutual-defence-agreement-pledge-unified-stance-against-attack" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al-Monitor</a>, the two governments pledged to adopt a "unified stance against any external armed attack" targeting either side. The stated aims are to strengthen collective defence, protect each country's sovereignty and territory, and support a more stable regional order.

The communique also listed areas of cooperation:

- Consultation on threats to their security, on regional and global security issues and on defence policy
- Joint military exercises
- Defence industry cooperation
- Exchanges for training military personnel
- Energy cooperation, especially trade in liquefied natural gas (LNG)

The full text of the agreement has not been published. Reports did not say what each country must do if the other is attacked, whether troop commitments are involved, or how long the pact will last.

## Five Agreements Signed in Istanbul

The deal was signed during the 12th meeting of the Turkey-Qatar Supreme Strategic Committee, a regular top-level forum between the two countries. The New Arab reported that five documents were signed in total:

| Agreement | Signed by |
|---|---|
| Mutual defence pact | President Erdogan and Sheikh Tamim |
| Education cooperation | Turkish Education Minister Yusuf Tekin and Qatari Minister of State Mohammed bin Abdulaziz Al-Khulaifi |
| Consumer protection and market inspection | Turkish Trade Minister Omer Bolat and Qatari minister Sheikh Faisal bin Thani Al Thani |
| Peace, reconciliation and conflict resolution | Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan and Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani |
| Joint declaration of the 12th committee meeting | Fidan and Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman |

The leaders also discussed the situation in Gaza, Syria's efforts to normalise relations and developments in Libya, according to The New Arab.

## What Erdogan and Sheikh Tamim Said

Writing on X, the Turkish presidency quoted Erdogan as saying the two countries would keep working together to prevent war from returning to the Gulf region, encourage dialogue and push for peace. His office said the agreement would add a new dimension to relations between Ankara and Doha.

Sheikh Tamim described the deal as a strategic partnership. In a post on X, he said the talks looked at how to build on past achievements and set priorities for joint action.

Erdogan also said a trade and economic partnership agreement that came into force last year was helping both sides move towards their trade targets, and that defence industry ties would keep growing.

## Why the Pact Matters Now

The agreement builds on an already close relationship. Turkey and Qatar have signed several defence and military cooperation deals in the past, and Turkey keeps a military base in Qatar.

The timing is linked to the wider conflict in the region. <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/13/turkiye-and-qatar-sign-mutual-defence-agreement" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a> connected the pact to the war between the US and Israel on one side and Iran on the other. Security analyst Yusuf Alabarda told the network that Gulf states, especially Qatar, had concluded that US security guarantees were not working as expected. He suggested Turkish weapons and air defence systems could be deployed to Qatar in the near term. These are the analyst's views and have not been confirmed by either government.

On Saturday, Iran's navy said another tanker had hit a naval mine near the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, the third such incident in 24 hours, according to Al Jazeera's live coverage. Iran accused the vessel of entering prohibited areas. We reported earlier on <a href="/hormuz-lng-tanker-hits-mine-irgc-says/">the LNG tanker that hit a mine in Hormuz</a>.

## A Growing Web of Regional Security Deals

The Turkey-Qatar pact is the latest in a series of security arrangements involving Ankara:

- **Mecca Joint Defence Agreement (August):** Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan agreed that an attack on one would count as an attack on all. Turkey's parliament ratified it the week before the Istanbul meeting, clearing the way for Ankara to send military support to Saudi Arabia, which has faced attacks by Yemen's Houthi rebels. Turkey says the pact is not aimed at any country and is open to others, including Egypt.
- **Badr memorandum (October):** Qatar, Indonesia, Pakistan and Turkey signed a multilateral defence cooperation memorandum in Doha.

## Key Highlights

- Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement in Istanbul on November 13, 2026.
- Both pledged a unified stance against any external armed attack on either country.
- The deal covers joint exercises, defence industry ties, training and LNG trade.
- It was one of five agreements signed at the 12th Supreme Strategic Committee meeting.
- Turkey already has a military base in Qatar.

## What to Watch Next

Key details, such as what military support each side would give in a crisis, are still unknown, and neither government has released the agreement's text. Observers will watch whether new Turkish air defence or troop deployments follow in Qatar, and how the pact fits with the Mecca agreement. With LNG trade named in the communique and tanker attacks continuing near Hormuz, energy security is clearly part of the picture.
