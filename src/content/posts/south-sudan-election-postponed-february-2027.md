South Sudan has pushed back its first national election once more. The National Elections Commission (NEC) announced a revised timetable that moves the presidential and parliamentary vote from December 22, 2026 to February 11, 2027. The new date was confirmed on Saturday, November 14, the same day voter registration opened across the country. The commission did not give a reason for the delay, which adds roughly seven weeks to a vote that South Sudanese have been waiting on since independence in 2011.

## Why This Election Keeps Slipping

South Sudan, often called the world's youngest country, broke away from Sudan in 2011 but has never held a national election. A civil war between forces loyal to President Salva Kiir and those backing his rival Riek Machar ran from 2013 to 2018 and killed at least 400,000 people, according to <a href="https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/south-sudan-postpones-first-ever-national-elections-until-february" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera's report on the new poll date</a>.

A peace agreement signed in 2018 ended the war and set up a transitional unity government, with elections first expected in 2022. Since then the vote has been postponed at least three times. Reports from Qatar News Agency point to repeated disputes over how to carry out the peace deal and its transitional arrangements, along with security problems, as the background to each delay.

## The New Election Timetable

The revised calendar, published by the NEC and reported by Anadolu Agency, sets out each stage of the process in detail.

| Stage | Dates |
|---|---|
| Voter registration | November 14 to 28, 2026 |
| Provisional voter register published | November 29, 2026 |
| Objections to voter register | November 30 to December 6, 2026 |
| Candidate nominations submitted | December 14 to 20, 2026 |
| Campaign period (45 days) | December 27, 2026 to February 9, 2027 |
| Final voter register published | January 9, 2027 |
| Final list of candidates | January 18 to 21, 2027 |
| Silence day | February 10, 2027 |
| Polling day | February 11, 2027 |

Final results are due in mid-February. The timetable also sets aside 148 days, from February 17 to July 15, 2027, for resolving election disputes.

## How Voter Registration Will Work

NEC chairman Gabriel Bol Deng said registration would take place at 3,161 centres spread across the country's 10 states and three administrative areas. More than 22,000 staff have been hired and trained for the exercise. Election material had reached state capitals but not yet every individual centre, and officials said a logistics plan would cover delivery over the following days.

Proving identity is a major hurdle. About 90 percent of South Sudan's roughly 15 million people have no state-recognised ID, Al Jazeera reported, citing the World Bank. Applicants are asked for a national ID card, passport or other official document showing their age and identity. Where people have none, traditional and community leaders will help them fill out a special identification form so they can still register.

### Key highlights

- First general election since independence in 2011 moved to February 11, 2027
- Original date was December 22, 2026
- No official reason given for the latest change
- Voter registration runs for two weeks from November 14
- 45-day campaign begins on December 27

## Political Tensions Behind the Vote

The delay comes after a turbulent few months in Juba. In September, President Kiir dissolved the transitional unity government and named a caretaker administration. That step allowed the election process to go ahead without first finishing a permanent constitution and a national census, which had been tied to the transition under the peace deal.

Critics said the move broke up the power-sharing arrangement at the heart of the 2018 agreement and concentrated authority with the president. Yasmin Sooka, who chairs the UN Commission on Human Rights in South Sudan, described it in October as a "constitutional coup through legislative reforms", according to Al Jazeera.

Machar, the former first vice president, has been under house arrest since March and faces charges of crimes against humanity. Government troops and fighters loyal to him have clashed in recent months.

## What UN Investigators Have Warned

In August, the UN rights commission cautioned that holding a vote without basic safeguards could deepen the conflict. According to <a href="https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168173" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UN News coverage of the commission's findings</a>, almost half of the key provisions of the 2018 peace deal, including the unification of the country's security forces, had not been carried out. The commission said fighting had resumed in Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity states, and it recorded 407 ceasefire violations between August 2025 and January 2026.

Investigators also said most of the warning signs listed in the UN framework for assessing the risk of atrocity crimes were present in South Sudan and growing at the same time. Ethnic tensions and disputes over cattle raiding add further strain in one of the world's poorest countries.

## What Comes Next for South Sudan

The next two weeks of voter registration will be the first real test of whether the NEC can run a nationwide exercise in a country with limited roads, low ID coverage and active conflict in several regions. Observers will also watch whether the candidate nomination window in December draws a broad field, given Machar's detention and the dissolution of the unity government.

For now, the February 11 date gives the commission a little more time. Whether it brings South Sudan closer to a credible first election, or becomes another entry in a long list of delays, will depend on how registration, security and political talks unfold over the coming weeks.
