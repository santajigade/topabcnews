China's Shenzhou-24 spacecraft is due to lift off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 8:18 am Beijing time on Wednesday, November 25. That is **5:48 am IST** (5:18 am in Pakistan, 00:18 GMT). On board is Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old Pakistan Air Force pilot who will be the first Pakistani in space and, according to the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA), the first foreign national to visit the Tiangong space station.

| Detail | What is planned |
|---|---|
| Launch time | Nov 25, 8:18 am Beijing / 5:48 am IST |
| Rocket and site | Long March-2F, Jiuquan, northwest China |
| Crew | Tang Hongbo (commander), Wu Yi, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali (payload specialist) |
| Ali's stay | About one week, returning on Shenzhou-23 |
| Docking | Forward port of the Tianhe core module, after about 3.5 hours of autonomous rendezvous |

## Who Is Flying on Shenzhou-24

Commander Tang Hongbo, 51, flew on Shenzhou-12 and Shenzhou-17. This trip will make him the first astronaut to board Tiangong three times, the Associated Press reported. Wu Yi, 39, comes from China's third batch of astronauts and is making his first flight. Chinese and Pakistani outlets differ on his title: Al Jazeera calls him flight engineer and Dawn calls him mission pilot.

Ali is the payload specialist. His job is to install experiment samples and research equipment and run the scheduled experiments, Geo News reported. His backup, Khurram Daud, trained alongside him. Ali told reporters that learning Chinese was the hardest part of the preparation. He also said, "I want to assure you that I am not flying for myself," in remarks carried by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/24/pakistans-zeeshan-ali-set-for-historic-flight-to-chinas-space-station" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera's report from Islamabad</a>.

## How Pakistan Got a Seat on Tiangong

Pakistan's space agency SUPARCO and CMSA signed an agreement in February 2025 to select and train Pakistani astronauts for Chinese station missions. Candidates went through three rounds of screening. On April 22 this year CMSA named Ali and Daud as reserve astronauts, and on April 24 they joined the Astronaut Center of China in Beijing. Dawn reported, citing a China Daily video, that a training plan meant to last a year was squeezed into seven months.

The training covered spacecraft and station systems, experiment procedures, emergency drills and Chinese commands and technical terms. The Astronaut Center of China prepared Chinese-English operating manuals and halal space food for the mission, according to Geo News. A final full-system rehearsal took place at Jiuquan on November 21.

Pakistan's Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal called Ali "Pakistan's first astronaut" on X, and Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar also posted his congratulations.

## What the Crew Will Do in Orbit

CMSA said the mission will run around 90 experiments. Chinese state broadcaster CCTV said these include four joint science projects with Pakistan in biotechnology and materials, plus an experiment with cyanobacteria, the microorganisms that photosynthesise. Chinese institutions picked the Pakistani experiments from more than 300 proposals that universities across Pakistan sent to SUPARCO, with a focus on food security and climate change. SUPARCO told Al Jazeera that finishing the four experiments will take 6 to 12 months, so the work will continue after Ali comes home.

Ali will return after about a week on Shenzhou-23, together with Lai Ka-ying, Hong Kong's first astronaut, and Zhang Zhiyuan. Zhu Yangzhu of the Shenzhou-23 crew will stay on Tiangong for about a year, the first full-year stay in China's programme.

## Tiangong Opens to Foreign Crew

China has been shut out of the International Space Station since 2011, when a US law barred NASA from working with China. China built Tiangong on its own, and until now only Chinese astronauts have flown there. CMSA spokesperson Zhang Jingbo said China hoped to explore space with more countries and regions. SUPARCO said Pakistan could now share its experience with other countries and that a lunar mission with China is tentatively planned for 2028. China itself aims to land astronauts on the Moon by 2030.

## Pakistan's Route Compared With India's

Shubhanshu Shukla flew to the ISS on a commercial Axiom mission in 2025, while India builds its own crew vehicle at home. Pakistan's seat is part of a government agreement with China.

| | India | Pakistan |
|---|---|---|
| Recent or upcoming flight | Shubhanshu Shukla, Axiom-4 to the ISS | Zeeshan Ali, Shenzhou-24 to Tiangong |
| Dates | Launched June 25, 2025; splashdown July 15, 2025 | Launch Nov 25, 2026; about one week aboard |
| Partner | Axiom Space, SpaceX Dragon, NASA's ISS | CMSA, China's own station |
| Science | Seven microgravity experiments | Four joint projects, part of about 90 on the mission |
| Home-built crew vehicle | Gaganyaan, first uncrewed flight G1 pending | None announced |

Gaganyaan's schedule is still moving. ISRO chairman V Narayanan had said the uncrewed G1 flight, carrying the half-humanoid robot Vyommitra on the human-rated LVM3 rocket from Sriharikota, was set for December 2026, Business Today reported on November 19. A day later NewsBytes, citing India Today, reported that G1 is now unlikely before February 2027 because of further technical fine-tuning and simulations, with about 80% of the work done. ISRO plans at least three uncrewed flights before the first crewed one. ## Launch and Docking Times in IST

Liftoff is set for 5:48 am IST on Wednesday, November 25. Docking at the Tianhe core module follows about 3.5 hours of autonomous rendezvous, which puts it at roughly 9:20 am IST if the flight goes to plan.
