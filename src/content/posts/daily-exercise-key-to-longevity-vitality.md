The closest thing we have to a pill for a longer, healthier life is regular movement, and the dose is smaller than most people think. Health agencies ask adults for **150 minutes of moderate activity a week**, such as brisk walking, or **75 minutes of vigorous activity**, such as jogging, plus **muscle-strengthening work on at least 2 days**. That works out to about 30 minutes a day on 5 days.

Most people are not getting there. The World Health Organization says **31% of adults worldwide (about 1.8 billion people)** were insufficiently active in 2022. In India the figure is worse: **49.4% of adults**, according to WHO's 2024 country factsheet.

| Who | Aerobic activity per week | Strength work | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults | 150 min moderate, or 75 min vigorous, or a mix | 2 or more days, all major muscle groups | More than 150 min brings more benefit |
| Adults 65 and above | Same as adults | 2 or more days | Balance activities every week |

## What the Research Says About Exercise and a Longer Life

The WHO links low activity to heart attacks, strokes, type 2 diabetes, dementia, and cancers such as breast and colon cancer. Exercise works on several of these at once, which is why it shows up in almost every study of healthy ageing.

A useful recent piece of evidence is about steps, because steps are easy to count on a phone. A review of 57 studies, published in The Lancet Public Health in July 2025 and led by Professor Melody Ding of the <a href="https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2025/07/24/rethink-the-10000-a-day-step-goal-study-suggests.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">University of Sydney</a>, compared people walking 7,000 steps a day with those walking 2,000:

- **Risk of death from any cause:** 47% lower at 7,000 steps, almost the same as at 10,000.
- **Dementia:** 38% lower at 7,000 steps, with only a further 7% gain at 10,000.
- **Type 2 diabetes:** 22% lower at 10,000 steps and 27% lower at 12,000.

So the old 10,000 step target is not a magic line. Much of the benefit comes earlier, which is good news for anyone starting from a low base.

## India's Inactivity Problem, Year by Year

WHO's India factsheet tracks the share of adults who do not meet the activity guideline. The rise over two decades is steady and has not reversed:

| Year | All adults | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 22.3% | 19.6% | 25.0% |
| 2010 | 33.7% | 28.9% | 38.7% |
| 2015 | 40.1% | 34.1% | 46.4% |
| 2020 | 46.8% | 39.7% | 54.2% |
| 2022 | 49.4% | 42.0% | 57.2% |

Three things stand out. Indian women are far less active than men, a gap of more than 15 points. Teenagers are in worse shape still: the factsheet puts 74% of Indians aged 11 to 17 below the activity level they need. And the Lancet Global Health study behind these numbers projected that about 60% of Indian adults could be insufficiently active by 2030 if the trend holds, Onmanorama reported.

Globally, <a href="https://www.who.int/news/item/26-06-2024-nearly-1.8-billion-adults-at-risk-of-disease-from-not-doing-enough-physical-activity" target="_blank" rel="noopener">WHO data</a> places South Asia (45%) among the least active regions, behind only high-income Asia Pacific (48%). The global goal is a 15% cut in inactivity by 2030, and WHO says the world is off track.

India does have programmes aimed at this, including the Fit India Movement launched in 2019 and Khelo India for grassroots sport. But the factsheet notes there is no stand-alone national physical activity action plan, and no workplace activity promotion policy in place.

## A Week That Meets the Guideline Without a Gym

You do not need equipment or a membership. Here is one way to reach the 150 minute and 2 strength day targets set by WHO and the CDC:

| Day | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Brisk walk | 30 min |
| Tuesday | Brisk walk, then squats, push-ups against a wall, stair climbs | 30 min + 15 min |
| Wednesday | Cycling to work or the market | 30 min |
| Thursday | Rest or light stretching or yoga | |
| Friday | Brisk walk | 30 min |
| Saturday | Bodyweight strength work for legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms | 20 min |
| Sunday | Outdoor game with family, or a longer walk | 30 min |

That adds up to 150 minutes of aerobic activity and two strength sessions. The CDC says you can split the time into shorter bouts across the day, so three 10 minute walks count the same as one 30 minute walk. If you are over 65, add balance work each week. The CDC suggests simple moves like walking heel to toe or standing up from a chair without using your hands.

Other options that fit a busy day:

- Walk or cycle to nearby places instead of taking a vehicle.
- Dance to music at home.
- Take the stairs.
- Do short stretches at your desk if you sit for long hours.

On days when the air in your city is very poor, move the session indoors. Our guide to <a href="/delhi-aqi-severe-diwali-health-tips/">protecting your lungs during high AQI</a> explains the risks.

## Why Starting Small Still Counts

"Some physical activity is better than none," the <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CDC guidelines</a> say. Adults who sit less and add any amount of moderate or vigorous activity get some health benefit, even before they reach 150 minutes.

The usual reason for skipping exercise is lack of time. But the target is about 30 minutes on 5 days, and it can be broken into pieces. A simple plan has three parts:

1. **Start small.** Ten minutes of walking is a real start. Build up from there.
2. **Keep a routine.** The same time each day makes it easier to stick with.
3. **Pick something you like.** A sport, a walk with a friend or a dance class is easier to keep doing than a workout you dread.

## Daily Exercise: Common Questions

**How much exercise do I need to live longer?** WHO and CDC recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, plus strength work on 2 days. Doing more than that brings extra benefit.

**Is 7,000 steps a day enough?** In the 2025 Lancet Public Health review, 7,000 steps a day was linked to a 47% lower risk of death compared with 2,000 steps, nearly the same as 10,000 steps.

**Can I split my exercise into short sessions?** Yes. The CDC says activity can be spread through the week and broken into shorter sessions.

**What should older adults add?** People aged 65 and above should follow the adult targets and also do balance activities every week.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or have not exercised in a long time, check with a doctor before starting a new routine.*
