Japan's most famous monster is breaking records in North America again. Godzilla Minus Zero, the sequel to the Oscar winning Godzilla Minus One, opened in US and Canadian cinemas on Friday, November 6, 2026, and is on course for an opening weekend of around $30 million to $33 million. If those projections hold, it will be the biggest North American debut ever for a foreign language live action film, well ahead of the previous best. The weekend also has an Indian connection, with Ramayana placing in the top five.

## How Big the Godzilla Minus Zero Opening Is

Godzilla Minus Zero is playing in 3,254 theatres across North America in Japanese with English subtitles. It earned $6.7 million from Thursday preview shows alone, according to <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/timlammers/2026/11/06/godzilla-minus-zero-projected-to-earn-30-million-plus-in-opening-weekend/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Forbes' weekend box office report</a>. Deadline projected an opening of $30 million to $33 million, while The Hollywood Reporter estimated about $32 million.

A $32 million start would comfortably beat the $17.8 million record set by the Chinese martial arts film Hero in 2002, the long standing benchmark for a foreign language live action release. It would also be the biggest opening for GKIDS, the North American banner releasing the film for Toho.

Collider noted the projected start is roughly three times the $11 million North American debut of Godzilla Minus One in 2023. Official weekend estimates are due later on Sunday in the US, so the final figure could move up or down.

### Key highlights

- Release: Friday, November 6, 2026, in 3,254 North American theatres
- Thursday previews: $6.7 million
- Projected opening weekend: about $30 million to $33 million
- Previous foreign language live action record: $17.8 million (Hero, 2002)
- Rotten Tomatoes score: 90 percent, Certified Fresh, as reported by Collider

## A Sequel Set in 1949 With Godzilla in New York

Takashi Yamazaki returns as writer and director. The film stars Ryunosuke Kamiki and Minami Hamabe. AFP reported that the story is set in 1949, two years after the events of Minus One, and follows the Shikishima family, with Godzilla this time causing destruction in New York.

At a CinemaCon presentation in Las Vegas, Yamazaki said "Japan was driven down to minus by Godzilla's attack", AFP reported.

Godzilla Minus One became a surprise global hit. It earned about $115 million worldwide and won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects, the first Oscar in the franchise's history. Yamazaki has said that film cost under $15 million, and that he expected the sequel's budget to be larger, Forbes reported. The new film has not disclosed its budget.

In Japan, Collider reported that Minus Zero earned about 910 million yen on its opening day, roughly triple what Minus One managed.

## The Cat in the Hat Settles for Second Place

Warner Bros. Animation's The Cat in the Hat, with Bill Hader voicing the Dr. Seuss character, is projected to finish second. The Hollywood Reporter estimated about $18 million from 4,003 theatres, below earlier tracking. Deadline put the film's production budget at $86 million before marketing costs, which makes the opening a tough start. For comparison, the 2003 live action version starring Mike Myers opened to $38.3 million in North America.

## Ramayana's Place in the North American Top Five

For Indian viewers, the most interesting line on the chart is Ramayana. Nitesh Tiwari's film, released in North America as Ramayana: Rise of the Legend and distributed there by Sony Pictures Entertainment, was projected at about $7.3 million from 1,433 theatres, good enough for fifth place. That is a strong number for an Indian film in a crowded weekend.

At home, the film has already crossed Rs 100 crore net in just two days. You can read our <a href="/ramayana-box-office-day-2-collection/">Ramayana Day 2 box office report</a> for the India numbers.

## Why Japanese Films Are Doing Well Abroad

The success of Minus Zero shows that subtitles are no longer a barrier for many cinema goers in North America. Deadline's <a href="https://deadline.com/2026/11/box-office-godzilla-minus-zero-cat-in-the-hat-1237215161/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">box office coverage</a> described the film as roaring toward a record opening. A low budget Japanese sequel beating a big Hollywood animated release on its first weekend is unusual, and it suggests audiences will follow a story they trust even with subtitles. For Toho, a big overseas opening also strengthens the case for more Godzilla films made in Japan for a global audience.

## What to Watch After the Weekend

The final Sunday estimates will show whether Godzilla Minus Zero tops $30 million. With strong reviews and a holiday season ahead, its second weekend hold will decide how close it can get to, or beyond, the worldwide total of its Oscar winning predecessor.
