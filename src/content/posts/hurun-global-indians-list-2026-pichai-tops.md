Google and Alphabet chief Sundar Pichai has taken the top spot in the 2026 HSBC Hurun Global Indians List, moving ahead of Microsoft's Satya Nadella. The list, released on November 12, ranks Indian-origin leaders who run companies headquartered outside India. This year it counts 236 such leaders at the helm of 200 companies with a combined value of about $15 trillion, a jump driven largely by the artificial intelligence boom.

## How the Hurun List Ranks Global Indians

The ranking is not about personal wealth. Hurun measures the value of the company each person leads, using valuations as of June 25, 2026. Only companies based outside India qualify, and the leader must be of Indian origin.

The bar for entry has also gone up sharply. The minimum company value needed to make the list doubled to $2.5 billion, from $1.2 billion in the previous edition two years ago. Even so, 76 companies are new to the list this year, according to The Hans India.

Of the 200 companies, 127 are publicly listed and 73 are privately held. Together they employ close to 53 lakh people and earned about $2.5 trillion in revenue in 2025, with total net profit of $416 billion.

## Top Five Leaders on the 2026 List

| Rank | Leader | Company | Company value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sundar Pichai | Alphabet | $4.2 trillion |
| 2 | Satya Nadella | Microsoft | $2.6 trillion |
| 3 | Sanjay Mehrotra | Micron Technology | $1.4 trillion |
| 4 | Neal Mohan | YouTube | Not separately given |
| 5 | Thomas Kurian | Google Cloud | Not separately given |

The top five companies alone are worth $9.1 trillion, about 61% of the list's total value, while the top ten add up to nearly 70%.

Pichai's rise reflects a strong run for <a href="https://abc.xyz/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Alphabet</a>, whose value has climbed 98% since 2024, according to Business Today. Microsoft's value, by contrast, fell 17% over the same period, which helped Pichai move past Nadella.

The most dramatic gain belongs to <a href="https://www.micron.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Micron Technology</a>, led by Sanjay Mehrotra. Its value jumped 1,174% to $1.4 trillion on huge demand for memory chips used in AI systems, lifting Mehrotra to third place.

## AI Drives a 53% Jump in Value

The combined value of companies on the list rose 53% to $15 trillion, up from $9.9 trillion in 2024. AI is the clear reason. Hurun counted 103 companies that are AI-driven or have built AI into their business, together worth $10.7 trillion, or about 71% of the list's total value.

For the first time, AI also appears as a separate industry category, with 18 companies valued at $227 billion. In the startup section, AI coding tool maker Cursor, co-founded by Aman Sanger, leads with a value of $60 billion, followed by Prometheus at $41 billion and Perplexity at $20 billion. Seven of the ten most valuable startups on the list work in AI, and all of them are based in the US.

By sector, software and services leads with 53 companies, followed by financial services with 31 and healthcare with 21. In all, 49 startups on the list are worth a combined $398 billion.

The AI theme is also visible in other big tech news this week, including <a href="/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-colette-kress-interim/">the leadership change at Nvidia</a>, the world's leading AI chip company.

## Where Global Indian Leaders Are Based

The United States dominates, with 177 entrants, and more than a third of all leaders are based in California. San Francisco has the most entrants of any city with 40, followed by Dubai with 15, and Palo Alto and London with 13 each. The UAE and the UK each have 15 entrants in total.

### Key highlights

- **Leaders on the list:** 236 Indian-origin leaders across 200 companies
- **Total value:** About $15 trillion, up 53% from $9.9 trillion
- **Number one:** Sundar Pichai, Alphabet ($4.2 trillion)
- **Women:** 16 leaders, led by Jayshree Ullal of Arista Networks ($208 billion)
- **Background:** 53% professional managers, 44% first-generation entrepreneurs, 3% second-generation leaders
- **Top Indian college:** IIT Delhi, with 15 undergraduate alumni on the list
- **Top foreign college:** Stanford University, with 19 postgraduate alumni

## From Brain Drain to Brain Bridge

The list says a lot about India's link with its talent abroad. According to Business Today, 58% of the leaders completed their undergraduate studies in India, which shows how much Indian colleges continue to feed top global companies.

Anas Rahman Junaid, founder and chief researcher of Hurun India, described the shift as moving away from the old idea of a "brain drain" towards a "brain bridge". He also said the reach of global Indians is something most people experience without realising it.

For Indian students and young professionals, the list highlights how engineering and management degrees from India, often combined with study abroad, have become a path to the top of global companies. It also shows how much of that success is now tied to AI and cloud computing.

## What the 2026 Ranking Tells Us

The 2026 HSBC Hurun Global Indians List shows Indian-origin leaders running some of the most valuable companies on the planet, with AI pushing their combined value to about $15 trillion. With Pichai at the top, Mehrotra's rapid climb and a fresh wave of AI startups on the list, the fortunes of these companies are now closely tied to the path of the AI industry.
