India's foreign exchange reserves rose by $2.49 billion to $719.547 billion in the week ended November 6, 2026, according to data released by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Friday, November 13. The increase came mostly from a sharp rise in the value of the country's gold holdings. It reversed part of the previous week's fall and comes after reserves slipped steadily from a record high reached in early September.

## How the Reserves Moved in the Week to November 6

The latest gain followed a drop of $2.691 billion in the week ended October 30, when reserves had fallen to $717.056 billion. The new figure puts the total back above $719 billion. The RBI publishes these numbers every Friday in its <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Weekly Statistical Supplement on the RBI website</a>.

All four parts of the reserves went up during the week, though by very different amounts.

| Component | Week ended Nov 6, 2026 | Weekly change |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign currency assets | $591.350 billion | Up $232 million |
| Gold reserves | $104.796 billion | Up $2.205 billion |
| Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) | $18.610 billion | Up $52 million |
| Reserve position with the IMF | $4.791 billion | Up $1 million |
| **Total reserves** | **$719.547 billion** | **Up $2.49 billion** |

## Gold Did Most of the Heavy Lifting

Gold accounted for nearly 90% of the week's rise. The value of the RBI's gold stock climbed by $2.205 billion to $104.796 billion. Weekly changes in gold reserves usually reflect movements in global gold prices as much as any new buying, so a rally in the metal lifts the reported figure even if the central bank does not add a single ounce.

Readers who follow bullion will know that gold prices in India have been at elevated levels this year, as seen in our coverage of the [gold rate around Dhanteras 2026](/gold-rate-today-dhanteras-2026/).

Foreign currency assets, the largest part of the reserves, rose only $232 million to $591.350 billion. These assets are counted in US dollars but include holdings in other currencies such as the euro, pound and yen. That means a stronger or weaker dollar against those currencies changes their dollar value, independent of any RBI buying or selling.

## Why Reserves Are Below the September Record

India's reserves hit an all-time high of $785.706 billion in the week ended September 4, 2026. That peak followed a special concessional swap window for diaspora deposits, through which banks raised a record $133 billion, bringing in a large inflow of foreign currency. Since then, reserves have trended lower, and the latest level is about $66 billion below the record.

Neither Business Standard nor ETV Bharat gave a specific reason for the week's increase beyond the gains in gold and foreign currency assets. Weekly reserve data does not show how much the RBI bought or sold in the currency market; that information comes later in separate monthly releases.

## What Forex Reserves Mean for Ordinary Indians

Foreign exchange reserves are the savings India keeps in foreign currencies, gold and other assets. They act as a cushion for the economy in several ways:

- **Paying for imports:** India buys large amounts of crude oil, electronics and gold from abroad. Strong reserves reassure markets that the country can pay its import bills.
- **Steadying the rupee:** The RBI can sell dollars from its reserves to slow a sharp fall in the rupee. A weaker rupee makes imported goods and foreign travel or education more expensive.
- **Investor confidence:** Large reserves help India borrow on better terms and signal stability to foreign investors.

The SDR line in the table is a reserve asset created by the <a href="https://www.imf.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">International Monetary Fund</a>, while the reserve position is India's quota-linked holding with the IMF. Both are small compared with currency assets and gold.

The reserve data comes at a sensitive time for the economy. Retail inflation rose above the RBI's tolerance limit in October, as explained in our report on [India's October CPI reading of 6.09%](/india-retail-inflation-october-2026-six-percent/). Higher crude oil prices have also weighed on markets this week.

### Key highlights

- Reserves rose $2.49 billion to $719.547 billion in the week ended November 6.
- Gold reserves jumped $2.205 billion to $104.796 billion.
- Foreign currency assets rose $232 million to $591.350 billion.
- The previous week saw a fall of $2.691 billion.
- The record high remains $785.706 billion, set in the week ended September 4.

## What to Watch in the Coming Weeks

The next weekly update from the RBI, due on Friday, will show whether the recovery continues or reserves resume their slide from the September peak. Gold prices, the dollar's strength against other major currencies and any RBI action in the currency market will decide the direction. For now, at over $719 billion, India's reserves remain a large buffer against external shocks.
