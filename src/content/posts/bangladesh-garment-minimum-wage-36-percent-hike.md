Bangladesh's government-appointed garment wage board raised the minimum monthly pay for entry-level garment workers by 36% early on Wednesday, November 25, 2026. The floor moves from 12,500 taka to 17,000 taka, about $136, according to the <a href="https://abcnews.com/International/wireStory/bangladesh-raises-minimum-wage-garment-workers-after-weeks-138432725" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Associated Press</a>. The board approved it after nearly 12 hours of talks, following weeks of protests that shut factories around Dhaka. The new rate applies from December, so workers will see it in the salaries paid in January.

Workers had asked for much more, and it is not yet clear whether workers will accept the deal and go back to work.

## Old Wage, New Wage and What Workers Wanted

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Previous entry-level minimum | 12,500 taka a month (about $100) |
| New entry-level minimum | 17,000 taka a month (about $136) |
| Increase | 4,500 taka a month, or 36% |
| New pay grades (Reuters) | 4 grades, from 17,000 taka (Grade 4) to 21,500 taka (Grade 1) |
| Board vote (AP) | 4 to 2, with both worker members backing 18,000 taka |
| Takes effect | December 2026, first paid in January 2027 |

Reports differ slightly on the headline number. AP and Reuters put the entry wage at 17,000 taka, and 36% on top of 12,500 taka comes to exactly that. AFP, in a report carried by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/25/bangladesh-garment-workers-win-36-pay-rise-after-weeks-of-protest" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>, gave the figure as 17,500 taka. We have used 17,000 taka because it matches the 36% rise every outlet reported.

On the demand side, AFP said workers had sought 30,000 taka ($240). AP said they wanted the minimum doubled. Reuters reported that worker representatives began at 32,000 taka and came down to 18,000 taka during the talks. The six-member board has two members each from workers and employers, plus two others.

## Why the Increase Came After Three Years, Not Five

The last revision, in 2023, set the 12,500 taka floor. Babul Akhter, a labour representative on the board, told AFP that wages used to be revised once every five years and that this time the revision came after three. "Although we are not completely satisfied, the outcome is not entirely bad either," he said, and asked workers to return to their factories.

Reuters described the 36% rise as the smallest in more than a decade.

## What the Protests Cost Factory Owners

The unrest grew through November. AP reported that workers clashed with police and attacked factories for weeks, and that on November 18 protesters blocked the Dhaka-Mymensingh highway in Gazipur, an industrial area near the capital.

The Bangladesh Garment Manufacturers and Exporters Association (BGMEA) put a price on the disruption on November 22, as reported by bdnews24:

- More than 600 factories were disrupted.
- A survey of 317 member factories found production losses of $428 million in the first 18 days of November. That works out to roughly $1.35 million per surveyed factory on average.
- Buyers moved orders for 59 million garments, worth about $254 million, to other countries.
- Some affected factories stayed shut for up to 16 days.

The BGMEA said the survey covers only part of the industry and that real losses are likely to be "considerably higher". Its statement did not say which countries received the diverted orders. Any claim that a particular rival, India included, picked up that business is not backed by the BGMEA figures.

Reuters reported that 397 factories could not operate on Tuesday, November 24, and that 84 stayed closed under a "no work, no pay" rule.

## Bangladesh's Garment Sector in Numbers

AFP says the sector earns roughly $38 billion a year, employs about four million people, many of them women from rural areas, and accounts for about four-fifths of the country's exports. Bangladesh is the world's second largest garment exporter after China, and most of its clothing goes to the United States and Europe.

## What Happens Next

These are the dates and open questions to watch:

1. **Return to work:** Unions have not said whether they accept 17,000 taka. Two of the six board members voted for a higher figure.
2. **December 1:** The new wage structure is due to take effect, according to Reuters.
3. **January 2027:** Workers receive the first salary at the new rates.
4. **Orders:** Exporters will be watching whether buyers who moved orders bring them back once production is steady.

Factory owners told Reuters that any further rise would add to costs and hurt Bangladesh's ability to compete. Labour groups say pay has to keep up with living costs.
