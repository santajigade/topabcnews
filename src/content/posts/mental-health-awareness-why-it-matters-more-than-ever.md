More than 1 billion people around the world live with a mental health condition, according to the World Health Organization's latest estimates released in September 2025. In India, about 11 in every 100 adults have a diagnosable mental disorder, and most of them get no proper treatment. A free, round the clock government helpline, Tele-MANAS (14416 or 1-800-891-4416), exists for exactly this gap, yet many people still do not know about it.

## Update (20 November 2026)

When this article first appeared in August 2024, it gave general advice without numbers. Since then, WHO has published new global data and the Indian government has expanded Tele-MANAS, including a video consultation option that went nationwide on 16 June 2025. We have rewritten this piece with those figures and a practical guide to getting help.

## What Mental Health Means

Mental health is how we think, feel and cope with daily life: our emotions, our thoughts and the way we handle stress. The body can fall ill, and so can the mind. That is normal, and it can be treated.

WHO says anxiety and depression are the most common conditions among both men and women, and together they are the second biggest cause of long-term disability. They also cost the world economy an estimated US$1 trillion a year. Suicide took about 7,27,000 lives in 2021, and WHO warns that the world is on track for only a 12% fall by 2030, against a target of one third.

## The Treatment Gap in India

India's figures come from the National Mental Health Survey 2015-16, carried out by NIMHANS. It found that:

- about 10.6% of adults had a diagnosable mental disorder;
- about 15% had mental health issues that needed some intervention;
- lifetime prevalence was 13.7%;
- prevalence was 13.5% in urban areas against 6.9% in rural areas.

The <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2188003" target="_blank" rel="noopener">government's November 2025 backgrounder</a> puts the treatment gap at 70% to 92%. It names three reasons: low awareness, stigma and too few professionals. With 0.75 psychiatrists for every 1,00,000 people, India has a quarter of the minimum WHO recommends.

The problem is not only Indian. Globally, governments spend a median of just 2% of their health budgets on mental health, a share that has not moved since 2017, and there are only 13 mental health workers for every 1,00,000 people, the <a href="https://www.who.int/news/item/02-09-2025-over-a-billion-people-living-with-mental-health-conditions-services-require-urgent-scale-up" target="_blank" rel="noopener">WHO report</a> says. Cost is a real barrier for families here too: our report on <a href="/national-health-accounts-2023-24-out-of-pocket/">India's out-of-pocket health spending</a> shows how much of the medical bill households still pay themselves.

## Warning Signs You Should Not Ignore

These signs, in yourself or someone close, are worth taking seriously when they last for weeks:

- feeling tired or sad most of the time;
- losing interest in things you used to enjoy;
- trouble sleeping, or eating much more or much less than usual;
- worrying all the time;
- avoiding people or activities.

One sign alone on a bad day is not a diagnosis. A pattern that does not lift is a reason to talk to someone.

## How to Use Tele-MANAS, Step by Step

Tele-MANAS is the Union Health Ministry's tele mental health service. It was launched on World Mental Health Day (10 October) in 2022 and now covers all 36 states and union territories.

1. **Dial 14416 or 1-800-891-4416.** Both numbers are toll-free and work 24 hours a day.
2. **Choose your language.** The service offers support in up to 20 languages, the minister of state for health told the Rajya Sabha in July 2025.
3. **Speak to a trained counsellor.** You can describe what you are going through. The service also makes follow-up call-backs.
4. **Ask about a video consultation if needed.** A pilot started on 10 October 2024 in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Jammu and Kashmir, and the option went nationwide on 16 June 2025.
5. **Get referred to local care.** Mental health services are part of the National Mental Health Programme. The District Mental Health Programme now covers 767 districts with 10 inpatient beds in each, and more than 1.77 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs include mental health in their primary care package. Services are also available at all AIIMS institutes.

Use is growing fast. Monthly callers rose from about 12,000 in December 2022 to more than 90,000 by May 2024, according to <a href="https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2022057" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PIB</a>, and total calls crossed 28 lakh by October 2025.

## Small Habits That Help Every Day

- **Talk it out.** Share how you feel with a friend or family member you trust.
- **Stay active.** Exercise helps the brain as well as the body.
- **Sleep well.** A rested mind copes better with stress.
- **Cut back on screen time.** Social media invites comparison that rarely helps.
- **Ask for help early.** A counsellor or doctor can make a large difference, and Tele-MANAS is free.

## Why Stigma Still Keeps People Silent

Fear of being judged is still the biggest reason people hide what they are going through, and the government itself lists stigma as a cause of India's treatment gap. A mental health problem is not a weakness. Listening without judging, and pointing a friend to 14416, are simple things anyone can do.

## Mental Health: Common Questions

**What is the Tele-MANAS helpline number?**
Dial 14416 or 1-800-891-4416. Both are toll-free and available 24 hours a day across India.

**Is Tele-MANAS free?**
Yes. It is a Union Health Ministry service and the numbers are toll-free.

**How common are mental health problems in India?**
The National Mental Health Survey 2015-16 found about 10.6% of adults had a diagnosable mental disorder, and 70% to 92% of them were not getting proper treatment.
