Poland's Magda Linette has won the 2026 Chennai Open, beating top seed and defending champion Janice Tjen of Indonesia 5-7, 6-1, 6-4 in a final that had to be pushed back by a day. The WTA 250 title match was played on Monday, November 9, at the SDAT Tennis Stadium after rain and poor conditions stopped play on Sunday. The week also gave Indian tennis a landmark: Vaishnavi Adkar and Rutuja Bhosale became the first all-Indian pair ever to reach a WTA Tour doubles final.

## How Linette Came Back Against Tjen

The final lasted 2 hours and 43 minutes. Tjen, who had won all nine of her previous matches in Chennai, took a tight first set with the only break of serve, converting at 5-5 after Linette had saved five break points earlier in the set.

Linette then took control. She broke twice to win the second set 6-1. In the decider she broke for 2-1, Tjen broke back for 3-3, and the 34 year old broke once more for 4-3 before serving out the match to love. According to <a href="https://www.tennismajors.com/wta-tour-news/wta-chennai-open/linette-ends-tjens-chennai-reign-to-win-first-title-since-2024-866264.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Tennis Majors</a>, Linette saved 11 of the 13 break points she faced, while Tjen served five double faults to her opponent's one.

## A Hard Week That Ended With a Trophy

Linette did it the long way. She came from a set down in three straight matches and spent more than 12 and a half hours on court across five rounds. Her first round win over qualifier Aliona Falei lasted 3 hours and 33 minutes, reported as the longest WTA main draw match of the season.

This is the fourth WTA singles title of Linette's career, after The Bronx in 2019, Hua Hin in 2020 and Prague in 2024. It also made up for a painful memory in the same city: she was runner-up in Chennai in 2022, losing a three set final to Linda Fruhvirtova.

For Tjen, the loss ended her bid to become the first player to win the Chennai title twice. Her 2025 win here had made her the first Indonesian woman to win a WTA singles title since 2002. She reached this year's final with a straight sets semifinal win over American Robin Montgomery, 6-3, 6-4.

## Adkar and Bhosale Write a New Chapter for India

The Indian highlight came in doubles. In Saturday's semifinal, Adkar and Bhosale upset the second seeds, Weronika Falkowska of Poland and Alana Smith of the US, 6-2, 2-6, 10-8 in a match tie-break. Adkar sealed it with a forehand down the line, even though she had needed a medical timeout for a strapped left thigh during the match.

That win made them the first all-Indian team to reach a final at WTA Tour level. As PTI noted in its report carried by <a href="https://theprint.in/sport/karamoko-waltert-combination-too-strong-for-indians-in-chennai-open-final/3093704/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Print</a>, earlier Indian success in women's doubles came mostly with foreign partners. Sania Mirza won 43 doubles titles but none with an Indian partner, and Ankita Raina and Karman Thandi's 2018 title came at the WTA 125 level, one step below the main tour.

### A tough final against the Swiss pair

In Sunday's final, the Indians ran into Switzerland's Naima Karamoko and Simona Waltert, who won 6-2, 6-0. Karamoko was strong at the net and Waltert steady from the baseline, and the Indian pair struggled on serve, with Bhosale and Adkar each dropping service games in the second set. It was the first tournament the Swiss players had entered as a team, and they left with the trophy, having also beaten the top seeds, Momoko Kobori and Peangtarn Plipuech, in the quarterfinals.

### Key highlights

- Singles champion: Magda Linette (Poland), third seed.
- Final score: 5-7, 6-1, 6-4 over top seed Janice Tjen.
- Final moved to Monday, November 9, after Sunday's play was stopped.
- Doubles champions: Naima Karamoko and Simona Waltert (Switzerland).
- Vaishnavi Adkar and Rutuja Bhosale: first all-Indian pair in a WTA Tour final.

## What the Week Means for Indian Tennis

A runners-up finish might look modest, but reaching a tour level final together is something no Indian women's team had done before. It shows that home tournaments like the Chennai Open can give Indian players a real platform. It is another positive moment in a strong month for Indian athletes, following results such as India's medal run at the <a href="/youth-olympics-2026-india-medal-tally-taniya-silver/">Youth Olympics 2026</a>. The challenge now is to turn one breakthrough week into regular appearances in the later rounds of bigger events.
