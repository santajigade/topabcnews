Delegations from Ukraine, Russia and the United States sat down in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday, November 10, for their first three-way peace talks since February. The two-day round, which runs until Wednesday, ends a nine-month pause in the US-led effort to stop the war. It comes as winter approaches and Russia steps up attacks on Ukraine's power grid. A day earlier, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy spoke to Prime Minister Narendra Modi and said India was ready to help set up food corridors through the Black Sea.

## Who Is at the Table in Abu Dhabi

Each side has sent senior officials, according to the Kyiv Independent.

| Delegation | Led by | Other members named |
|---|---|---|
| Ukraine | Rustem Umerov, Foreign Intelligence chief and chief negotiator | Kyrylo Budanov, Serhii Kyslytsia, David Arakhamia, Vadym Skibitsky |
| Russia | Vladimir Medinsky, presidential aide | Sergei Ryabkov, Mikhail Galuzin, Igor Kostyukov, Yury Zhigarlovsky |
| United States | Envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner | |

National security advisers from France, Germany, the UK and Italy, along with NATO and EU officials, were also expected in Abu Dhabi. They are holding separate consultations with Ukrainian and US officials outside the three-way format. Zelenskyy said on November 6 that questions about Europe's security must be discussed with Europeans present.

## What Each Side Wants From the Talks

Umerov said the agenda covers key questions on ending the fighting and moving closer to peace. Kyiv hopes to discuss prisoner exchanges, a possible ceasefire covering shipping and energy targets, and groundwork for a future summit of leaders. Budanov said humanitarian issues, including faster exchanges of prisoners of war, would be the main focus.

The US envoys have said the meetings could open the way to talks on wider security arrangements in Europe and the postwar concerns of both Kyiv and Moscow.

Ukrainian lawmaker Arakhamia told Deutsche Welle he did not expect a breakthrough in the first round but was more hopeful about a second. He added that "simply resuming the talks is already an achievement."

## Territory Remains the Biggest Obstacle

The positions of the two warring sides remain far apart, and the talks have been stuck on land for most of the year.

- **Russia's demands:** Ukraine's withdrawal from the Donbas region, a ban on Ukraine joining NATO, limits on its armed forces, and the removal of Ukrainian restrictions on the official use of the Russian language and on the Moscow-linked Ukrainian Orthodox Church.
- **Ukraine's position:** No withdrawal from territory it holds, no formal recognition of Russian occupation and no caps on its military. Kyiv wants an unconditional ceasefire first, which the Kremlin has repeatedly rejected.
- **Security guarantees:** Zelenskyy wants continued Western military support, a European-led reassurance force and guarantees modelled on NATO's Article 5. He says Russia's idea of guarantees likely means pulling back NATO forces from Central and Eastern Europe.

US President Donald Trump told CBS's 60 Minutes on November 8 that he believed the war would end soon. On November 5 he said he had met Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev privately and spoke of a "big agreement" involving Ukraine, Russia and Europe, though no details have been released.

## Zelenskyy's Call With Modi and the Black Sea Question

Zelenskyy phoned Modi on Monday, November 9, to brief him on preparations for Abu Dhabi. According to Anadolu Agency, Zelenskyy wrote on X that India "is ready to mediate in establishing food corridors" and that Ukraine was counting on that support. He said Russia's blocking of Black Sea grain shipments was hurting "hundreds of millions of people" worldwide. He also said Modi had told the Russian side directly that now is the time to end the war.

Modi's own statement was more general. His office said the leaders discussed bilateral cooperation and that Modi repeated India's view that the conflict must be settled through dialogue and diplomacy, along with India's readiness to support all efforts for peace. As The Wire noted, Modi's post did not mention the Black Sea or food corridors. External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar spoke to Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov later the same day.

## Why the Black Sea Matters for India

The Black Sea route is a major channel for grain and other food exports. The UN and Turkey brokered the <a href="https://www.un.org/en/black-sea-grain-initiative" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Black Sea Grain Initiative</a> in July 2022, which helped ship nearly 33 million tonnes of food before Russia pulled out about a year later. Ukraine later opened its own corridor, but attacks on ports and ships have continued.

India has a direct stake. On November 3, Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said five Indian nationals had been killed in attacks on commercial vessels in the Black Sea and two were missing. In early October, Jaishankar said India was talking to both Moscow and Kyiv about shipping, grain and energy exports, including possible understandings not to attack such targets. Because Indian seafarers have already lost their lives on these routes, any pause in strikes at sea would matter to Indian families as well as to food importers.

The talks also feed into a busy diplomatic calendar. Trump has said he expects to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, as we reported in our story on the <a href="/trump-xi-putin-meeting-apec-shenzhen/">expected APEC meeting in Shenzhen</a>. Official updates from Kyiv are posted on the <a href="https://www.president.gov.ua/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ukrainian President's website</a>.

## What to Watch After the First Round

The Abu Dhabi round is scheduled to end on Wednesday, November 11. Officials on all sides have played down hopes of a quick deal on territory. The more likely early results, if any, are progress on prisoner exchanges and talks on limiting strikes on ships and energy sites. Whether those smaller steps can hold through the winter will shape the chances of a second round and any leaders' summit.
