BepiColombo, the joint mission of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), went into orbit around Mercury on November 21, 2026, nearly eight years after it left Earth. Its four chemical thrusters began firing at 18:24 UTC, which was 11:54 pm IST. The burn ended early because of a software glitch and delivered 77% of the planned change in speed. The spacecraft is now in a 75-hour orbit instead of the planned 58-hour one. ESA says the mission is safe and that the next big steps, starting with the separation of its two orbiters on December 9 and 10, are not affected.

## What Went Wrong During the Burn

The burn was planned to last 37 minutes, starting about 635 km above Mercury. Before it began, the spacecraft turned into position, which cut off contact with Earth as planned. Controllers expected to hear from it again at 22:37 CET, or 3:07 am IST on November 22.

When contact came back, BepiColombo was in safe mode and the burn had stopped early. According to European Spaceflight, ESA confirmed internally at about 01:00 CET (5:30 am IST) that Mercury had captured the spacecraft, but contact was not publicly confirmed until 03:02 CET (7:32 am IST). ESA disclosed the short burn on the evening of November 22.

ESA has traced the fault to software. European Spaceflight reports it affects orientation and thruster control, and the investigation is still going on. Ignacio Tanco, ESA's flight operations director, said: "Upon finding Bepi in safe mode, it was like flipping a switch."

## What the Wider 75-Hour Orbit Changes

The shortfall left BepiColombo in a wider orbit, taking 75 hours to go around Mercury instead of 58. ESA says it has adapted the remaining manoeuvres so no extra thruster burns are needed to make up the gap, and that the separation of the two orbiters and the European orbiter's move to its science orbit are not affected. In ESA's words, "The mission is safe and on track for the separation."

Gizmodo noted that ESA has not said whether the wider schedule could still change.

ESA also used a method called weak stability capture to enter orbit. ESA says this is the first time the method has been used to enter orbit around another planet. Until now only some spacecraft heading to Earth's Moon had used it.

## Two Spacecraft, One Ride

BepiColombo is really two orbiters that travelled stacked together. ESA calls it the first mission to orbit Mercury with two spacecraft.

| Orbiter | Built by | Job after separation |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) | ESA | Drops to its own science orbit to study the surface and interior |
| Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio) | JAXA | Stays in an elliptical polar orbit to study the magnetic environment |

A third part, the Mercury Transfer Module, carried the stack across the solar system with electric propulsion and was dropped on September 3, 2026. On September 15, every instrument on the European orbiter was switched on together for the first time since that separation, and its laser altimeter BELA fired more than 700,000 pulses over 21.5 hours.

## Timeline From Launch to First Science

Times are in IST where ESA gave an exact clock time.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| October 20, 2018 | Launch on an Ariane 5 rocket |
| April 2024 | Electrical problem cuts thruster power; thrust restored to 90% by May 7 |
| December 2025 | Original arrival date, missed after the revised route |
| September 3, 2026 | Transfer module separates |
| September 24, 2026 | Orbiter thrusters fire for 8 min 42 s, an 11.4 m/s correction |
| November 21, 2026, 11:54 pm IST | Orbit insertion burn begins |
| November 22, 2026 | ESA reports the short burn and the 75-hour orbit |
| December 9 to 10, 2026 | MPO and Mio separate |
| December 16, 2026 | MPO drops Mio's sunshield and interface structure |
| About March 10, 2027 | MPO reaches its final orbit, according to Gizmodo |
| April 2027 | Science operations begin |

The 2024 problem is why BepiColombo is a year late. Its transfer module could not supply enough power to the electric thrusters before a planned manoeuvre. Engineers got thrust back to 90%, but ESA had to choose a new route, which pushed arrival back by almost a year.

## What Scientists Want to Learn

Mercury is the smallest planet and the one closest to the Sun. BepiColombo's two orbiters will study its surface, interior and magnetic field, each from its own orbit.

The next dates to watch are the December 9 to 10 separation and the start of science in April 2027.
