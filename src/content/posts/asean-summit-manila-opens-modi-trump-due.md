Southeast Asian leaders opened the 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila on Sunday, November 15, with trade, energy security and the long-running South China Sea dispute at the top of the agenda. Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., this year's chair, launched the plenary at the Philippine International Convention Center. The meetings run until November 18 and will draw a wide circle of world leaders, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi from Monday and US President Donald Trump on Tuesday.

## Marcos Sets the Tone at the Opening Plenary

In his opening remarks, Marcos said the Philippines had built its chairship around three ideas: "peace and security anchors, prosperity corridors, and people empowerment." He promised that all 19 of the bloc's priority economic deliverables would be completed before Manila hands over the chair, according to <a href="https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2026/11/15/2570290/marcos-opens-asean-plenary-south-china-sea-code-likely-miss-target" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Philstar</a>.

One of those deliverables is already done. ASEAN's economic ministers formally signed the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement on Saturday, November 14. The Inquirer also reported that ASEAN plans a Manila-based maritime centre to serve as a regional knowledge hub, and that free trade talks between ASEAN and Canada had reached a "substantial conclusion."

Marcos urged fellow leaders to turn political goodwill into practical cooperation and to build on the ASEAN Outlook on the Indo-Pacific as a basis for open engagement.

## South China Sea Code of Conduct Likely to Miss 2026 Target

The biggest unfinished item is the code of conduct for the South China Sea, which ASEAN and China have been negotiating for years. ASEAN foreign ministers set a 2026 deadline three years ago, but Marcos gave no timeline in his speech, and talks are now expected to spill into 2027, when Singapore takes over the chair.

Officials have given slightly different readings of how far the talks have come:

- Philippine spokesperson Dominic Xavier Imperial said last week that negotiations were at "70%," as reported by Philstar.
- Foreign Secretary Theresa Lazaro put the figure at 80 per cent, according to an AFP report carried by The Star.
- A diplomat quoted by AFP did not expect a signing at this summit.

The main sticking points are the code's geographic scope, definitions of sensitive terms, and whether it should be legally binding. China has resisted a binding code, while the Philippines and Vietnam argue one is needed. Siew Mun Tang, formerly of the ISEAS ASEAN Studies Centre, told AFP a signing would be "a small miracle."

## Myanmar, Energy and Security on the Table

Myanmar's military government remains barred from high-level ASEAN meetings, a policy in place since the 2021 coup. The country is represented in Manila by a foreign ministry official. AFP reported that Thailand wants Myanmar's foreign minister readmitted, while Lazaro said there would be "no easing" of the bloc's approach.

Leaders are also discussing an energy crisis linked to the Iran war, including possible fuel-sharing schemes, along with haze from Indonesia. About 20,000 police have been deployed around Metro Manila, and activist groups are planning protests during Trump's visit.

## What Modi's Manila Visit Means for India

India is not an ASEAN member but is one of its key dialogue partners. The Ministry of External Affairs said Modi will travel to Manila from November 16 to 18 for the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit, according to <a href="https://aninews.in/news/world/asia/pm-modi-to-visit-manila-for-asean-summit-maritime-security-trade-on-agenda20261115180530/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ANI</a>. The trip includes a dinner hosted by Marcos and bilateral meetings.

| India-ASEAN item | Details reported |
|---|---|
| Bilateral trade | About USD 122 billion |
| Trade pact | India wants a faster review of the ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement (AITIGA) |
| Defence | Philippines bought BrahMos batteries in 2022 under a USD 375 million deal and wants more |
| South China Sea | India says any code must follow UNCLOS |
| Myanmar | India says isolating Myanmar is "not the right approach" |

MEA Secretary (East) Rudrendra Tandon said the AITIGA review needs more time because of complex negotiations. India hopes the revised deal will help address its trade deficit with the bloc.

A possible Quad leaders' meeting in Manila is also being watched closely. AFP reported that a Quad sideline meeting with India, Japan and Australia is expected during Trump's visit, which would be his first of his second term. A US official told ANI that Washington is working to formalise a Quad Leaders' Summit at ASEAN. The MEA, however, said it had no information on a Quad meeting and did not confirm a Modi-Trump meeting.

## Trump's Arrival and the Shenzhen Link

Trump is due in Manila on Tuesday, near the end of the summit. From there, attention shifts to China, where Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin are set to meet on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. We covered that plan in [our report on the Trump, Xi and Putin meeting](/trump-xi-putin-trilateral-november-19-confirmed/).

Other leaders in Manila include Brazil's Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, Canada's Mark Carney and Australia's Anthony Albanese, who is expected to sign a new defence pact with the Philippines. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres and IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva are also attending.

## What to Watch in the Final Days

The next three days will show whether ASEAN can deliver on trade and digital goals while managing tensions at sea. For India, the key questions are progress on the AITIGA review, deeper maritime ties with the Philippines, and whether a Quad leaders' meeting takes place. A firm South China Sea code, by most accounts, will have to wait until 2027.
