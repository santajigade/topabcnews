Pope Leo XIV has returned to Peru, the country where he spent about two decades as a missionary priest and bishop, and told officials in Lima that he was coming back to his homeland. Huge crowds lined the streets of the capital on Wednesday and Thursday, and on Friday, November 13, he flies north to Chiclayo, the city where he served as bishop before his election as pope. The Peru visit is the final and most personal leg of a three-nation South American tour that began in Uruguay and Argentina.

## A Record Welcome in Lima

The pope landed at the Callao Air Base on Wednesday, November 11, after flying in from Buenos Aires. CBS News reported that the Vatican estimated about 3 million people lined his motorcade route, with some people breaking through security to run after his car on the way to the presidential palace. Reuters, in a report carried by ThePrint, described it as a record crowd.

At the Government Palace he met privately with President Keiko Fujimori and then joined her and a children's choir in singing the national anthem. In his first speech, reported by <a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/pope-leo-peru-homeland-south-america-trip/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CBS News</a>, he said his Peruvian citizenship, taken in 2015, was the sign of a bond that started long before. Reuters reported that he urged Fujimori to face the country's turbulent past and work to rebuild public trust. Fujimori won a narrow victory earlier this year in an election that saw accusations of fraud, according to the same report.

## Messages to Bishops and Young People

Thursday, November 12, was packed with six events, according to the official <a href="https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/travels/2026/documents/uruguay-argentina-peru-6-17novembre2026.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Vatican programme for the journey</a>. He began with a meeting with Peru's bishops at the archbishop's residence, where, according to America Magazine, he used the image of the Qhapaq Ñan, the ancient Inca road network, to describe the church as a path of communion.

He asked the bishops to stay close to their people, avoid division and act as bridge builders in a divided society. He also addressed clergy abuse, calling for truth, justice and prevention, and for what Reuters quoted as "an ever more resolute commitment" to protecting victims.

Later in the day he spoke at a meeting on the challenges and hopes of the Peruvian people near Lima's cathedral and attended an evening prayer vigil with young people and university students at the National Stadium. Reuters reported that crowds filled the historic centre for miles as he rode in an open popemobile.

## Who Is Pope Leo XIV

Born Robert Prevost in Chicago, the pope holds both US and Peruvian citizenship and is the first pope from either country. He worked in Peru for more than 20 years, first as a missionary and later as bishop of Chiclayo, a post Reuters says he held from 2015 to 2023. He has often said Peru taught him what it means to be a pastor, and that history explains the emotional scenes in Lima, where cafes served coffee decorated with his face and a 46-foot statue of him was unveiled in Callao, according to CBS News.

## The Rest of the Peru Schedule

The Vatican has released a detailed timetable for the remaining days. All times below are Peru local time, which is 10.5 hours behind India.

| Date | Place | Main events |
|---|---|---|
| Friday, Nov 13 | Chiclayo | Mass near Pampas de Pimentel, meetings with clergy and with the Santo Toribio de Mogrovejo Catholic University |
| Saturday, Nov 14 | Chiclayo and Santa Cruz de Succhabamba | Coronation rite at Chiclayo cathedral, Mass in Santa Cruz, prayer closing the Jubilee Year of Santo Toribio |
| Sunday, Nov 15 | Cusco and Pucallpa | Meeting at Saqsaywaman, Angelus in Cusco, meeting with Amazonian peoples and Mass in Pucallpa |
| Monday, Nov 16 | Lima and Callao | Visit to a home for elderly people, Mass at Las Palmas Air Base, farewell ceremony and flight to Rome |

He is due to land in Rome on Tuesday, November 17.

## Issues the Pope Is Expected to Raise

CBS News reported that the Amazon stop in Pucallpa is meant to honour Indigenous peoples, whose communities are affected by illegal gold mining, coca growing, violence and deep poverty. The Cusco visit has symbolic weight too: the city's main square is where the Indigenous leader Tupac Amaru II was executed in 1781 after leading the largest rebellion against Spanish colonial rule.

Rising crime is another concern. Reuters spoke to residents who said their joy at the visit was mixed with worry about organised crime and weak state institutions. The pope's call for trust and unity in his first speech is likely to frame how Peruvians read the rest of the trip.

## Why the Trip Matters Beyond Peru

The pope chose to end his first South American tour in the country that shaped his ministry, and the scale of the welcome shows how strongly Peruvians identify with him. For the church, the visit is also a test of his message on abuse, poverty and the environment in a region where those issues are sharply felt. For Peru's government, which faces questions over trust and security, the coming days offer a rare moment of national unity, though the hard political and economic problems raised by residents will remain after he flies back to Rome on Monday.
