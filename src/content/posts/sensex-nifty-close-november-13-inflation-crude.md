Indian shares ended the week on a weak note on Friday, November 13, as worries about rising inflation and a rebound in crude oil prices pulled the benchmarks lower. The BSE Sensex fell 318.56 points, or 0.39%, to close at 81,592.45, snapping a three-day winning run. The NSE Nifty 50 dropped 155.05 points, or 0.61%, to settle at 25,144.30. Despite the Friday slide, both indices still closed the week higher, their third weekly gain in a row.

## Why the Market Fell on Friday

Two worries dominated trading. The first was inflation. India's retail inflation rose to 6.09% in October, crossing the Reserve Bank of India's upper tolerance limit of 6%. That has raised expectations that the RBI could tighten policy again, a risk we explained in our report on [India's October retail inflation](/india-retail-inflation-october-2026-six-percent/). Higher food prices were the main driver of the jump.

The second was oil. Brent crude traded around $95.7 per barrel on Friday, up from about $92 at Thursday's close, as attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and disruption in the Strait of Hormuz kept supply fears alive. India imports most of its crude, so higher oil prices hit the trade deficit, the rupee and company costs at the same time. Rising bond yields in India and the US added to the caution towards riskier assets such as equities.

Vinod Nair of Geojit said strong corporate earnings and steady foreign buying were still offering support, which helped limit the fall.

Market breadth on the BSE was negative, with 1,763 stocks advancing, 2,328 declining and 167 unchanged. The Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100 each lost about 0.25%.

### Top Nifty losers and gainers

- **Losers:** Shriram Finance (down 3.37% to Rs 970), Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles (down 3.31% to Rs 287.40), Grasim Industries (down 2.60% to Rs 3,034), Hindalco Industries (down 2.12% to Rs 943.60) and Adani Enterprises (down 1.95% to Rs 2,688).
- **Gainers:** Eicher Motors (up 1.34% to Rs 7,650), Asian Paints (up 0.53% to Rs 2,550), Bharti Airtel (up 0.48% to Rs 2,106), ONGC (up 0.22% to Rs 225.50) and Nestle India (up 0.18% to Rs 1,263.20).

Outside the Nifty, quarterly results drove sharp moves. Amber Enterprises rose as much as 12.4% after reporting 60% year-on-year revenue growth for the September quarter, and FACT gained up to 5.32% after an 88% jump in net profit. SpiceJet climbed 4.9% as its board prepares to consider a fresh fundraising proposal on November 18. On the other side, Hinduja Global Solutions fell over 4% after a quarterly loss, and Gillette India slipped 2.15% as raw material costs hurt margins.

## Foreign Investors Keep Buying Indian Shares

One bright spot was foreign money. Foreign investors bought shares worth Rs 7,831 crore on Thursday, their ninth straight session of net buying, according to HDFC Sky. Foreign portfolio inflows in November have already crossed $8 billion, the highest monthly total since December 2020. This steady buying is a key reason the market has held up even as inflation and oil prices rise.

The rupee, however, weakened. It closed at 94.24 against the US dollar, down 21 paise from 94.03, pressured by higher crude prices and US Treasury yields. A weaker rupee makes imports such as fuel and electronics more expensive. Official reference rates are published by the <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Reserve Bank of India</a>.

## How the Week Ended for Sensex and Nifty

Over the full week, the Sensex gained 0.7% and the Nifty 50 rose 0.5%. Smaller companies did much better: the Nifty Smallcap index jumped 3.6% and the Nifty Midcap index gained 2.1%. Thirteen of 16 major sectoral indices ended the week higher.

The big exception was technology. Nifty IT fell 3.2% for the week, ending a six-week winning streak in which it had gained 17.1%. Readers following daily moves can compare this with our earlier [market close report from November 9](/sensex-nifty-close-november-9-psu-banks/).

## Levels to Watch Next Week

Sudeep Shah of SBI Securities sees support for the Nifty in the 24,900 to 24,850 zone, with a possible slide towards 24,600 if that breaks. On the upside, he sees resistance at 25,424. Live index data is available on the <a href="https://www.nseindia.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Stock Exchange</a> website.

The near-term direction will depend on three things: where crude prices go as tensions in the Gulf continue, whether food inflation cools, and what global investors expect from interest rates. With the RBI's December policy meeting approaching, rate-sensitive sectors such as banking, auto and real estate will be in focus.

## Friday Dip Leaves the Rally Intact for Now

Friday's fall was modest after a strong run, and steady foreign inflows plus solid quarterly earnings have so far cushioned the market against inflation and oil shocks. Investors will watch crude and inflation data closely before taking fresh positions.
