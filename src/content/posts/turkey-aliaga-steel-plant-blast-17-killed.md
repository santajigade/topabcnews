At least 17 workers were killed and around a dozen injured when an explosion tore through a steel plant in Aliaga, a coastal industrial town north of Izmir in western Turkey, around midday on Monday, November 9. The blast hit the HABAS Demir Celik facility while maintenance work was under way. President Recep Tayyip Erdogan said judicial and administrative investigations had been opened to find out who was responsible and whether negligence played a part.

## What Happened Inside the HABAS Plant

According to Izmir Governor Suleyman Elban, the explosion happened during a boiler replacement operation. An overhead crane collapsed and fell into a furnace used for casting liquid steel, setting off the blast and a fire at the site. The company that runs the plant told the <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/world/europe" target="_blank" rel="noopener">BBC</a> that the accident took place during "scheduled maintenance work" on one of its electric arc furnaces.

A 52-year-old worker told AFP that a crane rope snapped and roughly 120 tonnes of molten steel fell onto the furnace. In his account, the hot metal struck water-cooled panels and caused a hydrogen explosion. This version has not been confirmed by officials, and investigators are still looking at the equipment involved in the maintenance job.

Witnesses said the blast felt like an earthquake. Thick black smoke rose over the plant, and one eyewitness described the burning roof as melting and flowing. About 200 workers were reportedly on site at the time, fewer than the usual 300, because production had been paused for the maintenance.

## How the Death Toll Rose to 17

Erdogan first announced that 16 people had died and that 13 others were receiving treatment, two of them in critical condition. Health Minister Kemal Memisoglu later raised the toll to 17 after one of the critically injured workers died. The BBC reported that all those killed and injured were employees of the plant.

Governor Elban said some people were believed to still be under the rubble as search and rescue work continued on Monday. Officials have not yet released the full list of victims.

## Arrests, Investigations and Questions Over Negligence

BBC Turkish, citing local media, reported that at least five people had been detained in connection with the blast. Authorities have not publicly named them or said what role, if any, they had at the plant.

Erdogan said investigators would establish the cause and whether negligence was involved. HABAS is a privately owned conglomerate and one of Turkey's biggest steel producers. It declined to answer questions from AFP about the explosion.

## Anger Among Workers and Unions

The disaster has triggered anger among labour groups, who say the plant has a poor safety record. The Labour Party, a small leftist group, said it was the third time in three weeks that workers had died at HABAS. The daily Evrensel reported that at least 11 workers had died at the plant over the past decade. The pro-Kurdish DEM party also said negligence had caused several earlier worker deaths this year.

Two members of the Aegean Workers' Collective were detained outside the hospital in Aliaga while trying to make a statement about workplace safety, AFP reported. The group blamed the company's pursuit of profit for the disaster and demanded accountability. Workers planned a protest in a square in Aliaga on Tuesday evening under the slogan "Enough is enough, we do not want to die!"

The atmosphere outside the factory and hospital was described as very tense. AFP reported that a man said to be the brother of one of the victims set himself on fire outside the factory before onlookers stepped in and he was taken to hospital.

## Why Workplace Safety Is a Recurring Issue in Turkey

The independent Health and Safety Labour Watch, which tracks workplace deaths in Turkey, has said such accidents remain a chronic problem in the country. Turkey has seen other deadly industrial blasts in recent years, including a December 2024 explosion at an <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/12-people-killed-explosives-factory-blast-turkey-rcna185311" target="_blank" rel="noopener">explosives factory that killed 12 people</a>.

Aliaga is a heavy industry hub on the Aegean coast, home to steel mills, a refinery and ship recycling yards, and labour groups say tougher enforcement of safety rules there is overdue.

The tragedy comes as Turkey hosts world leaders and negotiators at the [COP31 climate summit in Antalya](/cop31-opens-antalya-climate-finance-dispute/), further south on the same coast. Steel is one of the hardest industries to make cleaner and safer, and the sector's conditions in Turkey are now under fresh scrutiny.

## What Happens Next in Aliaga

Investigators will now examine the crane, the furnace and the maintenance procedures that were being followed. Their findings will decide whether criminal charges follow and whether the plant faces penalties or a longer shutdown. Families of the dead are waiting for answers, and workers' groups say they will keep up pressure until those responsible are held to account. Official updates are expected from the Izmir governor's office and the Turkish Health Ministry as the probe moves ahead.
