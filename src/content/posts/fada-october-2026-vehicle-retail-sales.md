Vehicle dealers across India sold 31,72,466 vehicles in October 2026, according to data released on 6 November by the Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations (FADA). That is 23.8% lower than October 2025, when 41,63,555 vehicles were sold. The fall looks steep, but dealers say it is mostly a calendar effect: last year Navratri, Dhanteras and Diwali all fell in October, while this year Dhanteras (6 November) and Diwali (8 November) have moved into November. Compared with September 2026, sales actually rose by about 25%.

The month also brought one clear bright spot. Electric vehicle retail touched a record of about 3.55 lakh units, nearly 45% higher than a year ago.

## Why October Sales Look Weak on Paper

October 2025 was an unusual month. It combined the full festive season with pent-up demand after the GST rate cuts that took effect on 22 September 2025. That created a very high base for this year to be compared against.

FADA President Sai Giridhar called the year-on-year decline "almost entirely optical" in the federation's <a href="https://www.fada.in/images/press-release/16aed49cca8e14FADA_Press_Release_Oct_26.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official October 2026 retail data release</a>. He pointed to the 25% jump over September as a better reading of real festive demand. FADA also noted that October 2026 was still the second highest October on record for total retail.

## Segment-Wise Vehicle Sales in October 2026

Two-wheelers took the biggest hit, while commercial vehicles, three-wheelers and tractors grew. Here is how each segment performed, based on FADA figures:

| Segment | October 2026 | October 2025 | Change (YoY) | Change (vs Sept) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-wheelers | 23,21,419 | 32,39,949 | -28.35% | +29.67% |
| Three-wheelers | 1,41,420 | 1,36,025 | +3.97% | +6.68% |
| Passenger vehicles | 5,07,768 | 5,92,032 | -14.23% | +18.86% |
| Commercial vehicles | 1,15,140 | 1,12,561 | +2.29% | +11.19% |
| Tractors | 79,285 | 76,898 | +3.10% | +3.09% |
| Construction equipment | 7,434 | 6,090 | +22.07% | +14.62% |
| **Total** | **31,72,466** | **41,63,555** | **-23.80%** | **+25.05%** |

According to <a href="https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/auto/auto-retail-sales-fall-23-8-in-october-to-3-172-million-units-fada-126110600528_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Business Standard's report on the FADA data</a>, commercial vehicles, three-wheelers and tractors each posted their best ever October.

## Two-Wheeler Slowdown Hits Rural India Harder

Two-wheelers are the largest part of the Indian vehicle market, so their 28% fall pulled the overall number down. Rural two-wheeler sales dropped by about 34% from last year, while urban sales fell by about 21%.

FADA does not see this as lost demand. Its view is that rural buyers have simply pushed their purchases to the Dhanteras and Diwali days in November, which are traditionally considered lucky days to bring home a new vehicle. The federation expects rural and northern markets to lead growth this month.

## EV Sales Touch a New High

Electric vehicles had their strongest month so far. About 3.55 lakh EVs were sold in October, and their share of total vehicle retail rose to about 11.5%, compared with roughly 6% a year earlier. Business Standard reported that electric two-wheelers, three-wheelers, passenger vehicles and commercial vehicles all had their best month on record.

EV share within each segment in October 2026, as per FADA:

- **Three-wheelers:** 62.63% of sales were electric
- **Two-wheelers:** 9.65%
- **Passenger vehicles:** 7.30%
- **Commercial vehicles:** 4.29%

Giridhar said that for EVs, availability rather than demand was the main limit during the month. In simple words, more people wanted electric vehicles than dealers could supply. Another sign of change: CNG, hybrid and electric vehicles together made up 41.83% of sales, slightly ahead of petrol at 41.6%, according to Business Standard.

## Dealer Stock and the November Outlook

Passenger vehicle inventory at dealerships stayed at around 43 to 45 days, the same as September. FADA expects this to come down to around 30 days by the end of the calendar year as festive buying clears stock.

Dealers are cautiously hopeful about November. In FADA's survey:

- 51.65% of dealers expect growth in November
- 34.71% expect sales to stay flat
- 13.64% expect a decline

For the three months from November 2026 to January 2027, about half of dealers (50.41%) expect growth. FADA listed some risks too, including weak underlying demand, a possible lull after the festive season, supply and model gaps, and higher financing costs. Around 31% of dealers said a recent rise in the repo rate is affecting loan costs for buyers.

## What This Means for Car and Bike Buyers

For buyers, the numbers suggest that festive demand is now arriving in November instead of October. Popular models, especially EVs, may have waiting periods because supply is already tight. At the same time, passenger vehicle stock at dealers is still above normal, which can mean year-end offers on some cars.

The real test of the 2026 festive season will come with the November figures, which FADA is expected to release early next month. If the shift in the festival calendar is the main reason for October's fall, November should show a strong jump over last year.
