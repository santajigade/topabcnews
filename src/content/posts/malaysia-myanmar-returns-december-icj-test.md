Malaysia plans to send 6,803 Myanmar nationals home in December, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told parliament on Tuesday, November 24. Three navy ships will carry 1,500 people on December 6 and 7, and another 5,303 will be flown back during the month, with Myanmar's government paying for the flights. The announcement came five days after the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya and told it to allow the "safe, voluntary and dignified return" of refugees.

## What Anwar Told Parliament

According to AFP, Anwar said the foreign minister had approved three naval ships for the sea transfers. He told lawmakers the asylum seekers were "victims of atrocities carried out by the Myanmar regime," Al Jazeera reported, but said Malaysia could no longer afford to host refugees and that waiting for resettlement in third countries had not worked.

He also defended his government's contacts with Myanmar's leadership. Anwar said that engagement was meant to protect Malaysia's interests and was in line with the ICJ ruling, which he described as a guarantee of safe and dignified return.

Malaysia has long sheltered people fleeing Myanmar. It also says hosting them is a financial burden and a security risk.

## Rohingya or Migrants: The Sources Differ

Reports do not agree on who exactly is being sent back. Al Jazeera's headline and opening line describe the group as Rohingya refugees. AFP's report, carried by the Philippine Daily Tribune, calls them "Myanmar migrants" and "Myanmar citizens." Anwar's own quoted words refer to Myanmar citizens and do not give an ethnic breakdown.

The ICJ case was about the Rohingya, while Malaysia also hosts many other people from Myanmar. UNHCR's latest published breakdown, for the end of 2024, counted about 170,530 registered refugees and asylum seekers from Myanmar in Malaysia. Of those, about 111,670 were Rohingya, about 27,700 were Chin and 31,150 belonged to other groups. Until Malaysia says how many of the 6,803 are Rohingya, it is not possible to say how far the December returns touch the people covered by the court's ruling.

## How the Plan Lines Up With the ICJ Order

Our [explainer on the ICJ genocide verdict](/icj-myanmar-genocide-rohingya-verdict-explained/) covers what the court found on November 19. On returns, its order uses three words: "safe," "voluntary" and "dignified." This is what the reports say against each.

- **Safe:** In late October, Malaysia said it would ask Myanmar to explain reports that hundreds of people sent back earlier were being held in military detention. Al Jazeera noted that Anwar did not mention that request on Tuesday. No outcome of the request has been reported.
- **Voluntary:** Neither report says whether those on the December list agreed to go, or how they were chosen.
- **Dignified:** The reports give the transport plan and who pays for the flights. They do not say who pays for the navy transfers or what happens to people on arrival.

Rights groups criticised the September round, which Al Jazeera described as an opaque scheme, and warned that returnees could be in danger. They also objected when Malaysia hosted Min Aung Hlaing, the former junta chief who is now Myanmar's president, in October, saying the visit lent legitimacy to his government.

## How Many People Malaysia Hosts

Al Jazeera, citing UNHCR, put the registered refugee and asylum seeker population at 225,740 in August 2026, up from about 192,240 at the end of 2024 on UNHCR's own figures page. UNHCR has said the rise in registered numbers reflects its registration work rather than new arrivals, and that refugees report no major new arrivals since 2013.

Against that base, the December plan covers about 3% of the registered population. Counting the September round, Malaysia will have returned around 8,300 people to Myanmar in roughly three months if the December transfers go ahead as announced.

## Open Questions Before the Ships Sail

The navy ships are due to sail on December 6 and 7. Malaysia has not said how many of those on the list are Rohingya or whether they agreed to go, and no reply from Myanmar has been reported to Malaysia's question about the earlier returnees reported to be in detention.
