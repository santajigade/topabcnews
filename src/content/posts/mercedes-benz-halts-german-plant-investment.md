Mercedes-Benz has stopped all further investment at its factories in Germany and is now seriously considering closing plants, after talks with worker representatives on cutting labour costs ended in deadlock. The decision was shared with employees in an internal memo on Thursday, November 12, according to Reuters. The move shows how heavy the cost pressure on Germany's car industry has become.

## What Mercedes Told Its Workers

Production chief Michael Schiebe said in the memo that management had "jointly decided to halt all further investments in our German locations." He linked the move to high manufacturing costs and the failure to agree on savings with the works council and the IG Metall union, Reuters reported.

Board member Britta Seeger, who is responsible for human resources, struck a cautious note. "We all want to avoid plant closures at all costs," she wrote. But in comments reported by German media and cited by <a href="https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/12/mercedes-benz-halts-new-investment-in-german-plants-considers-closures" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Euronews</a>, she added that closures now have to be pursued more seriously than before. She also said a quick agreement with employee representatives was not in sight.

The company's aim, according to Seeger, is a package that delivers noticeable savings while still being socially acceptable for staff.

### Key highlights

- **Decision:** All new investment at German sites suspended for now
- **Why:** High labour and site costs, and no deal with unions on cuts
- **Possible closures:** Two German plants, one vehicle assembly plant and one powertrain plant
- **Earlier warning:** Schiebe first raised the closure risk in September
- **Cost gap:** An hour of labour in German plants can cost up to five times more than in Eastern Europe, per Euronews
- **Plants named:** Not yet disclosed

## Which German Plants Could Be Affected

Mercedes has not said which sites are at risk. Schiebe had warned weeks earlier that two plants could close if cost cuts did not happen: one that assembles vehicles and one that makes powertrains, the engines, motors and related parts that drive a car.

The company runs vehicle assembly plants in Germany at locations including Sindelfingen, Rastatt and Bremen. Its powertrain network includes Stuttgart-Untertuerkheim, Affalterbach, Berlin, Hamburg and Kamenz, along with sites in Thuringia, according to Euronews. Until the company names the sites, workers across these locations face uncertainty.

## Why Talks With IG Metall Have Stalled

The works council and <a href="https://www.igmetall.de/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">IG Metall</a>, Germany's powerful metalworkers' union, are trying to protect jobs and benefits that were agreed through collective bargaining. Management has put forward several ideas to bring costs down, according to German reports cited by Euronews:

1. Longer working hours without any extra pay
2. Possible cuts to special payments such as profit sharing
3. Possible cuts to holiday bonuses and Christmas bonuses

These proposals have already triggered protests by employees. According to Seeger, the talks are deadlocked, and no quick agreement is expected.

## A Wider Crisis in German Carmaking

Mercedes is not alone. Volkswagen and Audi have also announced savings programmes and job cuts. Mercedes itself has struggled with falling sales and weaker profits over the past few years, Euronews noted.

German automotive expert Ferdinand Dudenhoeffer told Bild that without a basic change in cost structures, Germany's car industry risks losing even more of its competitiveness. The labour cost gap with Eastern Europe, where an hour of work can be up to five times cheaper, is a big part of that problem.

## What It Could Mean for Mercedes Buyers and India

For now, the freeze affects investment plans in Germany, not existing production or the cars already on sale. There has been no announcement linking the decision to Mercedes-Benz operations in other countries, including India, and no change in model plans has been announced.

Indian buyers have been seeing a steady flow of new Mercedes models, such as the recently launched <a href="/mercedes-glc-electric-launched-india/">Mercedes GLC Electric in India</a>. Any longer term shift in where Mercedes builds cars and parts would play out over years rather than weeks, and the company has not given details on how production might move if German plants close.

More details may come from <a href="https://group.mercedes-benz.com/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Mercedes-Benz Group</a> once the talks progress or a decision on the two plants is made.

## High Stakes Talks Ahead for Mercedes and Its Workers

The investment halt turns a long running cost dispute into an open standoff. Management says it wants to avoid closures, but has now made clear that shutting two plants is a real option if no deal is reached. The coming weeks of talks with the works council and IG Metall will decide whether Mercedes can cut costs through a negotiated package, or whether one of Germany's best known industrial names will close factories at home.
