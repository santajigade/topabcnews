The Supreme Court of India has told WhatsApp and other online platforms to spell out exactly how they plan to stop "digital arrest" scams, in which fraudsters pose as police or investigating officers on video calls and frighten people into transferring money. The direction came in an order uploaded after the court's October 29 hearing and reported on November 10, 2026. The platforms must also share their proposals with the Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C), and the case will be heard again on November 23.

## What the Supreme Court Has Asked Platforms to Do

A bench of Chief Justice of India Surya Kant with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana is hearing the matter on its own, in a suo motu case titled *In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents*. In its latest order, the bench asked WhatsApp LLC, which is owned by Meta, and "other concerned intermediaries" to tell the court what measures they propose to prevent digital arrest frauds and related cyber crimes.

The order adds that these companies "shall also simultaneously submit their proposals to the I4C," the Ministry of Home Affairs unit that coordinates the country's response to cyber crime. This means the plans will be examined by the government's own experts and not only by the court.

Senior advocate Kapil Sibal, appearing for WhatsApp, told the bench that the platform had already acted against 9,400 accounts. He said WhatsApp would file an affidavit listing its steps and is building software to flag suspicious accounts and warn users, according to PTI reports.

## How Big Is the Digital Arrest Problem?

During the hearing, it was stated that cyber crimes had fallen by about 50%, yet victims have still lost around Rs 800 crore so far this year, according to reports of the proceedings. Attorney General R Venkataramani, appearing for the Centre, said substantial work had been done to improve detection of suspicious transactions and to strengthen safeguards in the banking system.

Earlier this year, a Ministry of Home Affairs status report told the court that WhatsApp had begun a multi week investigation in January 2026 after concerns raised by I4C, the IT ministry and the Department of Telecommunications, as reported by LiveLaw. WhatsApp told a government committee that most scam operations targeting Indian users are run from Southeast Asia, especially Cambodia.

## Banks Told to Act as a "Protective Umbrella"

The court has also turned its attention to banks. On October 29, the bench said that if an investigation shows a fraud happened because a bank or financial institution failed to exercise due diligence, the institution should compensate the victim with an amount "not less than the amount lost by the customer." The court wants the Reserve Bank of India's rules to include enforceable provisions on compensation, preventive action and accountability.

CJI Surya Kant described banks as a "protective umbrella" for vulnerable customers. The bench suggested practical safeguards such as temporarily holding unusual transactions, alerting the nearest police station and physically verifying flagged payments. The RBI must also give the court a list of all authorised financial institutions before the next hearing.

The bench will first examine the proposed RBI KYC (Amendment) Directions, 2026, which are expected to be notified by November 30 and come into force by December 31.

## Police Across States Asked to Report on FIRs

The order covers law enforcement too. Police in every state and Union Territory must report the FIRs registered in digital arrest cases and the progress of investigations, and suggest ways to prevent, detect and prosecute these crimes. Directors General of Police have been made responsible for compliance. The reports will go to an inter departmental committee that coordinates the government's response, with copies filed in court.

## How to Protect Yourself From a Digital Arrest Scam

No genuine police officer, CBI official or court will place anyone under "arrest" through a video call or ask for money to settle a case. If you receive such a call, disconnect and do not share personal or bank details. Victims of online financial fraud should immediately call the national cyber crime helpline 1930, which <a href="https://ps.keralapolice.gov.in/ernakulamruralcyber-ps/notifications/National-Cyber-Crime-Reporting-Helpline-No-1930-3338" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Kerala Police notes is available 24 hours a day</a>, or file a complaint on the <a href="https://cybercrime.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Cybercrime Reporting Portal</a>. Reporting quickly gives investigators a better chance of freezing money before it moves.

## What Happens at the November 23 Hearing

When the court sits again on November 23, it is expected to look at WhatsApp's affidavit, the proposals from other platforms, the RBI's list of institutions and the draft KYC changes. The outcome could shape how much responsibility tech companies and banks must carry when ordinary Indians are tricked by fraudsters, and whether victims can expect faster refunds in the future.
