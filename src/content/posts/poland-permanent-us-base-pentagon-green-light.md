Poland says the United States military has given its support to a permanent American base on Polish soil, a long-standing goal for Warsaw. Deputy Prime Minister and Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz made the claim after meeting US Secretary Pete Hegseth at the Pentagon on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. The final word rests with President Donald Trump, and Polish officials expect a White House decision in early December. The Pentagon's own summary of the meeting did not mention a base, so for now the plan remains a Polish account of a positive signal rather than a signed deal.

## What Poland Says Happened at the Pentagon

According to a statement from the <a href="https://www.gov.pl/web/national-defence/deputy-prime-minister-kosniak-kamysz-poland-is-not-only-a-model-ally-of-the-us-but-also-became-their-strategic-partner" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Polish Ministry of National Defence</a>, Kosiniak-Kamysz described Hegseth's response on the base as positive. He quoted the US secretary as saying "everything is going in a good direction" and called this a green light from the Pentagon. He also said a permanent US troop presence is Poland's "absolute priority."

The Kyiv Post, citing TVP World, reported that the minister called the talks a breakthrough. Both Prime Minister Donald Tusk's government and President Karol Nawrocki support the project, despite their political rivalry.

The Pentagon readout, as reported by Stars and Stripes, focused instead on a new agreement to expand defence industry cooperation between the two countries.

## Where the Base Could Be and How Big

No site has been chosen yet. Polish officials have put forward four cities, all west of the Vistula river: Bydgoszcz, Poznan, Szczecin and Wroclaw. Breaking Defense reported that this western location fits US preferences.

| Detail | What has been reported |
|---|---|
| Permanent US troops | About 5,000 |
| Total people at the site | Up to 25,000, including families and support staff |
| Candidate cities | Bydgoszcz, Poznan, Szczecin, Wroclaw |
| Poland's offer | 15 to 17 billion zloty (about $3.8 to $4.4 billion) for infrastructure |
| Final decision | President Trump, expected early December |

A permanent base would need family housing, schools and other services that current sites lack, so construction would likely take several years. Today most American personnel in Poland are there on rotation, and the Kyiv Post put the current number at about 10,000. The Army already runs a small permanent garrison, Camp Kosciuszko in Poznan, set up in 2023.

## Why Warsaw Wants US Troops to Stay

Poland borders both Ukraine and Russia's Kaliningrad exclave, and it formally proposed the new base in May as a deterrent against possible Russian aggression. Warsaw's stated aim is roughly 10,000 rotational troops plus 5,000 permanent personnel.

The push also comes at a moment of uncertainty about the US role in Europe. Earlier this year the Pentagon said it would cut 5,000 troops from Germany and cancelled a planned brigade rotation to Poland, which angered Polish officials. Trump later ordered 5,000 additional troops to Poland. A force posture review ordered by Hegseth, covering roughly 80,000 American troops in Europe, is expected to finish in the coming weeks.

In August, Polish deputy defence minister Pawel Zalewski told <a href="https://polskieradio.pl/395/7784/Artykul/3724930,polandus-talks-on-permanent-bases-gather-pace-officials" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Polskie Radio</a> that the US presence "doesn't have to be one base" and could be spread across several sites. He also said Poland does not want nuclear warheads stationed on its territory.

## Other Deals From the Washington Visit

The visit produced several other outcomes:

- A statement of intent to expand and speed up defence industry cooperation
- A "positive recommendation" from the Pentagon on Poland's request to buy Tomahawk cruise missiles, though the US Congress has the final say on the sale
- Confirmation of Poland as host of a service centre for PAC-3 interceptors used in Patriot air defence systems, the first such facility outside the United States
- A plan by Kosiniak-Kamysz to ask EU defence ministers to make equipment built by Polish-American firms eligible for European funds

The talks also fit what Breaking Defense described as the Trump administration's "NATO 3.0" idea, under which European allies take primary responsibility for their own conventional defence.

## The Wider European Picture

The base question is unfolding alongside other security worries on NATO's eastern flank, including the recent <a href="/baltic-states-drone-alerts-nato-jets-scrambled/">drone alerts in Lithuania and Latvia</a>, and the US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks. Earlier, Hegseth warned allies that deployments could change depending on how closely they line up with Pentagon priorities, saying some countries would "fail" the review.

## What to Watch Before December

The next step is the White House decision. If Trump approves the plan, the two governments will still need to agree on a location, cost sharing and a timeline. If he does not, Poland has said it will keep pressing. Either way, the outcome will be read across Europe as a signal of how Washington plans to balance its commitments on the continent.
