Motorists in Mumbai and its neighbouring cities are paying more for CNG from today. Mahanagar Gas Limited (MGL) has raised the price of compressed natural gas by Rs 1.50 per kg, taking it to Rs 91.50 per kg. The new rate came into force from midnight on November 14, so it applies from Sunday, November 15. The company says the hike is needed because it is getting less domestic gas and has to buy more costly imported gas.

This is the eighth increase in CNG prices in Mumbai in 2026, and it comes just two weeks after the previous one. For the lakhs of auto-rickshaw and taxi drivers who run on CNG, the change adds to running costs that have already climbed steadily through the year.

## How Much CNG Costs in Mumbai Now

The latest revision takes CNG to its highest level this year. In January, a kilo of CNG cost Rs 80.50 in Mumbai. With today's hike, the total increase in 2026 has reached Rs 11 per kg, or close to 14%. Just over half of that rise, Rs 5.50 per kg, has come in the past two and a half months.

### Key highlights

- **New price:** Rs 91.50 per kg in Mumbai and adjoining areas
- **Increase:** Rs 1.50 per kg
- **Effective from:** Midnight of November 14 (applies from November 15)
- **Previous price:** Rs 90 per kg, set from October 31
- **Price in January 2026:** Rs 80.50 per kg
- **Number of hikes this year:** Eight
- **Vehicles affected:** About 12.93 lakh CNG vehicles in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region

The revision covers CNG. Gujarat Samachar reports that the price of domestic piped natural gas (PNG) for households has not been changed this time.

## Why Mahanagar Gas Raised Prices Again

<a href="https://www.mahanagargas.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Mahanagar Gas</a>, the city gas distributor for Mumbai, Thane and nearby areas, has given three main reasons for the increase.

First, supply of cheaper domestically produced natural gas has dropped. Second, to make up the shortfall, the company is buying more regasified liquefied natural gas (RLNG), which is imported and priced against international markets. Third, tensions in the Middle East have pushed up global gas prices, which raises the cost of every cargo of imported LNG.

MGL says the revision will recover only part of its higher costs and help it keep supply running without interruption. City gas firms in India get an allocation of domestic gas under rules set by the <a href="https://mopng.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas</a>, and when that allocation falls short, they have to fill the gap with imports at market rates.

## What It Means for Auto and Taxi Drivers

The biggest impact falls on commercial drivers. Auto-rickshaws and kaali-peeli taxis in Mumbai run almost entirely on CNG, so each hike cuts directly into daily earnings unless fares are raised.

Fares have already gone up once this year. The minimum auto-rickshaw fare in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region was increased to Rs 27 in September. According to Gujarat Samachar, auto unions now want another Rs 2, which would take the minimum fare to Rs 29 for the first 1.5 km. UNI also reported on Sunday that auto unions have demanded a fare revision after the latest increase.

After the previous hike on October 31, auto union leader Thampi Kurien told the Free Press Journal that the union could seek a fare revision if fuel and other costs pushed overall operating expenses up sharply. Any change in fares has to be approved by the transport authorities, so passengers will not pay more until a decision is taken.

### Who else feels the pinch

- **Private car owners** with CNG cars will pay more at every refill. A car that takes about 10 kg of gas will now cost Rs 15 more per fill than it did yesterday.
- **Fleet and cab operators** running app-based taxis on CNG face higher costs across hundreds of vehicles.
- **Public transport** operators using CNG buses will see their fuel bills rise.

## Does CNG Still Make Sense for Car Buyers?

CNG cars have been popular in Mumbai because gas has long been cheaper per kilometre than petrol. Repeated hikes this year have narrowed that gap, which may make some buyers think harder before choosing a CNG variant, especially since these cars usually cost more upfront than petrol versions.

For people who drive long distances every day, CNG can still lower running costs, but the savings now depend more on how far they drive and how prices move in the coming months. With MGL linking its prices to the cost of imported LNG, further changes will depend on global gas markets and on how much domestic gas the company receives.

Rising fuel costs are part of a wider squeeze on household budgets. India's retail inflation rose above the RBI's upper limit last month, as we reported in our story on <a href="/india-retail-inflation-october-2026-six-percent/">October's CPI data</a>, and everyday items such as <a href="/mother-dairy-milk-price-hike-delhi-ncr-november-12/">milk have also become costlier</a> this month.

## What Mumbai Commuters Should Watch Next

The key question for the next few weeks is whether the transport authorities accept the demand for higher auto fares. Commuters should also keep an eye on further CNG revisions, since MGL has changed prices twice in November alone. Drivers who use CNG for work may want to track their fuel spend closely as the new rate kicks in.
