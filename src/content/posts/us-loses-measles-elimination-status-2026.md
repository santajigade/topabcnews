The United States has lost the measles elimination status it held since 2000, according to a Wall Street Journal report on Friday, November 13, that was repeated by Reuters. The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), the World Health Organization's regional office for the Americas, reportedly found that the virus had spread continuously inside the country for more than a year. The news came on the same day state health departments pushed the number of measles-linked deaths in 2026 to 10.

Neither PAHO nor the US Department of Health and Human Services had officially confirmed the decision at the time of the reports, and both did not immediately respond to requests for comment, Reuters said.

## What Losing Elimination Status Means

A country is considered to have eliminated measles when it goes at least 12 months without continuous local transmission. Before this, measles still reached the US through travellers, and imported cases sometimes caused local outbreaks. But those chains of infection were eventually broken.

That is no longer the case. Reports say the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) submitted evidence on whether the same strain had circulated without a break for a year or longer. A person familiar with the CDC findings told Reuters the agency concluded that it had. The CDC report itself has not been made public.

PAHO's review was first planned for April and was later pushed to November, Live Science reported. To win back elimination status, the US will now need to go 12 months without continuous transmission.

Canada, Spain and the UK have also recently lost their elimination status after reviews by WHO experts, according to the Reuters report.

## Measles Cases and Deaths in 2026

This year's numbers are far above normal. The CDC counted 5,412 cases in 2026 across 45 states as of its Friday update, an increase of 230 in one week. For comparison, the country averaged about 180 cases a year between 2000 and 2024, <a href="https://abcnews.com/amp/Health/measles-cases-nationwide-top-5400-outbreaks-spread-us/story?id=138108176" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ABC News reported</a>. The 2026 total is already more than double the 2,289 cases recorded in 2025.

State health departments now report 10 measles-associated deaths this year:

| State | Deaths in 2026 | Total cases |
|---|---|---|
| Pennsylvania | 7 | 1,542 |
| Wisconsin | 2 | 651 |
| New York | 1 | Not given |

The CDC's own count stood at six deaths after its latest update, and the agency says its figure is subject to change. At least four of this year's victims were children.

### The Latest Two Deaths

According to <a href="https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/measles/2-more-people-die-measles-complications-us-cases-top-5400" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CIDRAP at the University of Minnesota</a>, a child in Grant County, Wisconsin, died of measles. More than a third of Wisconsin's cases have come from that county, and a baby in the state had died the week before. Pennsylvania reported its seventh death of the year, an unvaccinated resident of Montour County.

Wisconsin recorded 174 new cases in the week, the most of any state. Ohio (372 cases), Indiana (171) and Minnesota (160) also reported fresh infections.

## Why Measles Came Back

Experts point mainly to falling vaccination rates. Measles is extremely contagious, and coverage of about 95 percent is needed to stop large outbreaks. Kindergarten coverage in the US slipped from 95 percent in 2019 to 92 percent in 2026, and roughly 300,000 kindergartners started school this year without the vaccine, ABC News said. Only 10 states were above the 95 percent mark for 2025-26.

The gap is clear at state level. West Virginia, which does not allow religious or philosophical exemptions, had 98.9 percent kindergarten MMR coverage and only two cases this year. Wisconsin, at 85.1 percent, is now the fastest-growing hotspot.

About 96 percent of reported US cases this year were in people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccine status was unknown. Around 11 percent of all patients needed hospital care.

Experts quoted by Live Science also blamed cuts to public health funding and staff, falling trust in vaccines, and confusion over changes to federal vaccine guidance.

Noel Brewer of the University of North Carolina, who chairs an independent panel reporting to PAHO on US measles, said the disease is "fully preventable" and called the case numbers unacceptable. CDC director Erica Schwartz told Reuters before the decision that the agency would keep working with states to encourage immunisation.

## Key Highlights

- The US reportedly lost measles elimination status after 26 years, following a PAHO review.
- 5,412 cases have been confirmed in 2026 across 45 states.
- States report 10 measles deaths this year, seven of them in Pennsylvania.
- Kindergarten vaccine coverage has fallen to 92 percent, below the 95 percent needed.
- The US must go 12 months without continuous spread to regain the status.

## What Travellers and Families Should Know

Two doses of the MMR vaccine, given at 12 to 15 months and again at 4 to 6 years, are about 97 percent effective at preventing infection. Measles can stay in the air and on surfaces for up to two hours after an infected person leaves a room, and it can cause pneumonia, brain swelling and death, especially in young children.

For Indian students, workers and families heading to the US, the practical step is simple: check vaccination records before travel and speak to a doctor if a dose is missing. The US case count is still climbing, and health experts quoted by Reuters expect large outbreaks to continue unless vaccination rates recover.
