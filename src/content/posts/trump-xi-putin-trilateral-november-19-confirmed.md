US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin will hold their first ever three-way meeting on Thursday, November 19, 2026, in the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen. The White House announced the talks on Friday, and the Kremlin confirmed the date on Saturday. The meeting will take place on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, which runs on November 18 and 19, with nuclear weapons and the Iran war expected to top Washington's agenda.

The three leaders have met each other separately many times, but they have never sat down together in a formal trilateral setting.

## From "Possible" to Confirmed in a Week

When we first reported on the plan, <a href="/trump-xi-putin-meeting-apec-shenzhen/">Trump said he expected to meet Xi and Putin at APEC</a>, while Beijing would only say it was in contact with the parties. That changed over the last two days.

- **November 6:** China's Foreign Ministry said discussions were under way, but no agreement had been announced.
- **November 8:** Trump said he expected to meet both leaders at the summit.
- **November 13 (Friday):** White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters the three leaders would meet in Shenzhen next week.
- **November 14 (Saturday):** Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told the Russian news agency Tass that "an agreement has been reached" to hold the meeting on the 19th.

Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov had said last month that Moscow, Beijing and Washington were ready in principle. He also said no separate Trump and Putin meeting had been agreed on the sidelines, and the White House schedule so far confirms there is no one-on-one with Putin planned.

## Nuclear Arms Reduction Leads the US Agenda

According to <a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-putin-xi-jinping-meeting-china-us-russia-talks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CBS News</a>, Wales said Trump will discuss "the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world" among other topics. She added that the president views nuclear proliferation as very dangerous.

US officials, speaking on condition of anonymity, said Trump also intends to raise the war with Iran during the trilateral session.

Beijing's view on arms control is a key unknown. China has previously described three-way nuclear disarmament talks as unrealistic for now, as noted in an analysis by <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/11/6/what-a-trump-xi-putin-summit-would-mean-and-what-it-wouldnt" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>. Chinese officials have not publicly set out an agenda for Thursday.

## Why Ukraine and Chip Controls Are Off the Table

Two big issues are not expected to come up in the three-way format:

- **Ukraine:** US officials said the war in Ukraine is being handled on a separate track with Kyiv and Moscow. A senior US official said China "is not really a part of that track." That track has included the recent rounds of talks in Abu Dhabi, which we covered in our report on <a href="/abu-dhabi-talks-day-two-prisoner-swap-no-breakthrough/">the prisoner swap and the lack of a peace breakthrough</a>.
- **Export controls:** A second US official said Washington does not negotiate American export controls, signalling that limits on sensitive chip technology sales to China will not be discussed.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has warned that Shenzhen could turn into a modern version of the 1945 Yalta conference, where wartime allies divided Europe into spheres of influence, according to an AFP report published by Dawn.

The Thursday session with Xi will be their fourth meeting since Trump returned to office. Tech billionaire Elon Musk will travel with Trump to China but will not take part in the meetings with Xi and Putin, officials said.

## What Analysts Expect From Shenzhen

Opinions differ on how much the meeting can deliver. Speaking to Al Jazeera, Moscow based analyst Alexander Bratersky said the event would mainly serve as a photo opportunity that raises Putin's standing, with its real value becoming clear only if results follow in the weeks after.

Inderjeet Parmar of City St George's, University of London, told the same outlet that Trump wants a dramatic display of deal making for audiences at home. He warned that a three-power format could sideline US allies, though he said fears of a formal "G3" were overstated. Russian lawmaker Pyotr Tolstoy has openly compared the idea to Yalta.

## Why the Shenzhen Meeting Matters Beyond the Three Powers

Washington, Beijing and Moscow are each central to the issues the meeting touches, from nuclear arsenals to the Iran war, and all three are major nuclear powers. Any agreement, even a broad statement on reducing nuclear arsenals, would be watched closely by governments across Asia and Europe. Equally, a meeting that ends without results would show the limits of leader level diplomacy among rivals.

For now, the confirmed facts are the date, the place and a short agenda. The details of what, if anything, the three leaders agree on will only emerge after Thursday's talks in Shenzhen.
