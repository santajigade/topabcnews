NASA has opened applications for its next class of astronauts, saying the new recruits will help the United States return to the Moon, build a Moon Base and prepare for the first crewed missions to Mars. The call went out on Saturday, November 7, and the window closes at 11:59 pm US Eastern Time on Thursday, December 3, 2026. The agency expects to name the chosen candidates in late 2027.

The recruitment drive comes as NASA's Artemis programme heads towards a planned crewed lunar landing in 2028, and as the agency says it needs more trained flyers than it currently has.

## Why NASA Is Hiring More Astronauts Now

NASA currently has 32 active astronauts, according to figures reported alongside the announcement. With Artemis missions, a planned lunar base and longer-term Mars goals on the agenda, the agency wants a bigger pool of crew members.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman put the opportunity plainly. "The people we select could walk on the Moon, live and work at the Moon Base," he said, as quoted from the agency's <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-seeks-next-generation-of-explorers-to-join-astronaut-corps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official recruitment announcement</a>.

Artemis III commander Randy Bresnik was quoted as saying, "We have far more work than we have astronauts."

## Who Can Apply for the NASA Astronaut Class

The basic rules are strict. Applicants must be US citizens, which means most international readers, including those in India, cannot apply through this route.

The main requirements are:

- **Education:** A master's degree in a science, technology, engineering or maths (STEM) field. Two years of doctoral study, a medical degree, or completion of a test pilot school programme can also qualify.
- **Experience:** At least three years of related professional experience after the qualifying degree. Medical residency training can count towards this.
- **Pilot route:** Pilots can meet the experience rule through flying hours instead.
- **Timelines:** Test pilot school and any medical residency must be finished by July 2027.
- **Medical fitness:** Every candidate must pass NASA's long-duration astronaut flight physical.

### Flight hour rules for pilots

| Aircraft type | Total hours | Pilot-in-command hours |
|---|---|---|
| High-performance jet aircraft | Not specified | 750 |
| Other fixed-wing aircraft | 1,500 | 750 |
| Rotary-wing aircraft | 1,000 | 300 |

Meeting these rules only makes a person eligible. Final selection depends on NASA's assessments and on how each applicant compares with the rest of the field. NASA looks at technical knowledge, leadership, communication, teamwork and physical fitness.

## How Tough the Competition Is

Recent numbers show just how hard it is to get in. In the previous round, which closed in 2024, more than 8,000 people applied and only 10 were chosen as astronaut candidates in 2025.

Since the first class was picked in 1959, NASA has selected 370 astronaut candidates in total. Chief astronaut Scott Tingle chairs the Astronaut Selection Board at the Johnson Space Center in Houston.

### Key highlights

- **Applications opened:** November 7, 2026
- **Deadline:** 11:59 pm EST, December 3, 2026 (10:29 am IST, December 4)
- **Eligibility:** US citizens only
- **Selection announcement:** Expected in late 2027
- **Training start:** Expected by early 2028
- **Last round:** 10 selected from more than 8,000 applicants

## What New Astronauts Will Train For

Once picked, candidates face extensive training covering spacecraft operations, lunar exploration, geology, robotics and other technical skills, according to Science Times.

NASA is targeting 2028 for its first crewed lunar landing under the Artemis programme, after a demonstration mission planned for 2027. The architecture pairs the Space Launch System rocket and Orion capsule with commercial landers. SpaceX and Blue Origin are both developing human landing systems aimed at crewed landings in 2028.

The new class is not expected to fly on that first landing. Instead, its members could be among those who later live and work at a permanent base on the Moon.

NASA has also been reaching out to younger space enthusiasts. Space.com reported that the agency launched a $1 million Moon Base Student Challenge offering cash prizes for ideas on lunar bases.

## The Road From Application to the Moon

The next few weeks are the only chance for eligible Americans to enter this selection cycle. After December 3, NASA's selection board will assess the applicants and is expected to announce its final picks in late 2027. Those chosen will begin training by early 2028, just as the agency aims to put astronauts back on the lunar surface.
