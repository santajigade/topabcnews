A tanker carrying liquefied natural gas (LNG) struck a naval mine and suffered an explosion in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, November 13, according to a statement from the navy of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) carried by Iranian state media. The IRGC said the ship had been using an "unauthorized and dangerous route." The incident came a day after Iranian media reported that four oil tankers had been hit by explosions in the same waterway, and it adds fresh doubt to hopes that shipping through one of the world's most important energy routes is returning to normal.

## What Iran Said About the LNG Tanker Blast

The IRGC navy statement gave few details. According to <a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/iran-says-tanker-carrying-lng-hit-mine-suffered-explosion-hormuz-2026-11-13/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Reuters reporting on the LNG tanker incident</a>, no information about the vessel's name, owner, flag or crew was immediately available. There was also no word on casualties or on how badly the ship was damaged. The claim that the tanker hit a mine has not been independently confirmed.

Iran's framing is consistent with its recent statements. Tehran has repeatedly said that vessels moving through the strait must follow routes it approves, and it has described ships that ignore its warnings as entering restricted areas.

## Four Oil Tankers Hit a Day Earlier

On Thursday, November 12, Iranian media said four oil tankers had been struck by explosions in the strait after disregarding warnings. Just Security's daily briefing, summarising Reuters reports, said two supertankers were disabled on Wednesday and two more hit mines on Thursday in the eastern part of the strait.

The pace of attacks has been high for weeks. Figures from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, cited by Reuters, show:

| Period | Ships attacked |
|---|---|
| October 2026 | 37, the highest monthly total since the conflict began in late February |
| November 1 to 10, 2026 | At least 10 |

Earlier incidents give a sense of the range of tactics. Maritime sources said that on October 30 a small boat attached two limpet mines to a tanker's hull, causing an engine room fire. Bloomberg also reported at the start of November that Pakistan negotiated with Iran for safe passage of the LNG carrier Al Mayeda after it was attacked near the strait.

## Talks, Threats and a Radar Strike

The latest blasts come against a tense diplomatic backdrop. Iran's top negotiator and Parliament Speaker, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, told Al Jazeera on Wednesday that navigation in the strait would not go back to pre-war conditions. He rejected the idea of "free waters" in Hormuz and argued that the 1982 UN Convention on the Law of the Sea does not apply there, saying the two coastal states govern the passage.

In Washington, President Donald Trump said on Thursday, "We don't need a deal with Iran," adding that the United States has "their Strait." According to The New York Times, as cited by Just Security, Trump also said he had removed Vice President JD Vance from potential peace talks.

Military exchanges have continued too. The IRGC said it destroyed a US early warning radar in Bahrain after a US strike on a telecommunications pole in the Iranian port of Sirik, <a href="https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-bahrain-radar-strait-hormuz-5d73a23b5b8647d2515c35b267b36c08" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the Associated Press reported</a>. Separately, AP reported that Iran told the International Atomic Energy Agency, in a confidential report, that talks on nuclear monitoring cannot go ahead while it faces military threats.

Only days ago, Trump had described the war as "effectively over" after meeting Qatar's prime minister, as we reported in our story on <a href="/trump-iran-war-effectively-over-qatar-talks/">the Qatar talks and the state of the Iran war</a>. The latest tanker attacks show how far the situation at sea remains from that description.

## LNG Traffic Was Recovering Before the Latest Attacks

The blasts are a setback for gas shipping, which had been slowly picking up. Ship tracking data from Kpler, cited by OilPrice.com, shows:

- **31 LNG tankers** exported through Hormuz in October, the highest since February
- **23 LNG tankers** in September
- **Over 2.7 million tonnes** of LNG loaded at Qatar's Ras Laffan in October
- **Nearly 6.2 million tonnes** exported by Qatar in February, before the war, showing output is still well below normal

Reuters noted that attacks are continuing even as overall traffic has rebounded and as markets have priced in some de-escalation.

## Why Oil and Gas Prices Matter for India

So far, oil markets have reacted calmly. OilPrice.com reported that Brent crude briefly touched $96 a barrel after Trump's comments on Iran, while prices were on course for a second straight weekly loss despite the new attacks. Its market data on Friday showed Brent at about $96.20 and the Indian Basket of crude at around $91.15 a barrel.

For India, which depends heavily on imported crude oil and LNG, the strait is a key artery. Any sustained rise in attacks on tankers could push up freight and insurance costs and feed into fuel and gas prices at home. That risk has added weight now that India's retail inflation has already crossed the RBI's upper limit, as covered in our report on <a href="/india-retail-inflation-october-2026-six-percent/">October's CPI data</a>.

## What to Watch Next

Key questions now are whether the vessel hit on Friday is identified, whether its crew is safe, and whether shipping companies respond by again cutting traffic through Hormuz. The path of US-Iran talks, and Iran's insistence on controlling routes through the strait, will decide whether the recent rebound in energy shipments can hold.
