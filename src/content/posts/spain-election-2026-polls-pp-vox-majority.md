Spain goes to the polls on Sunday, November 29, 2026, in a snap general election that most surveys say the right is set to win. Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez called the vote in early October, months ahead of the July 2027 deadline, after parliament threw out his housing measures. With 12 days to go, private polls show the conservative People's Party (PP) and the far-right Vox together above the 176 seats needed for a majority, even as Sanchez's Socialists have narrowed the gap in some surveys. Here is what is at stake and how to read the numbers.

## How a Housing Vote Brought Down the Government

Sanchez leads a minority coalition of his Socialist Party (PSOE) and the left-wing Sumar, which depended on smaller regional parties to pass laws. On October 2, the lower house rejected two housing decrees. According to <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/4/spain-protests-flare-after-housing-bill-rejected-will-it-cause-snap-poll" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera</a>, one would have halted evictions of vulnerable people and tightened short-term leases, while the other would have renewed rental contracts automatically when they expired.

The PP, Vox and the Catalan party Junts voted against them. Junts argued the rules would push owners to pull homes off the rental market. The defeat came during large protests over rents, which Al Jazeera reported have almost doubled in 10 years. The election decree followed on October 5. We covered the wider anger in our report on the <a href="/spain-general-strike-housing-crisis-4-million/">Spain general strike over housing</a>.

## What the Latest Polls Show

Reuters reported on November 16 that three new polls still showed the PP and Vox winning a majority, though the Socialists gained ground. The table below sets recent surveys against the last result in July 2023.

| Poll (published) | PP | PSOE | Vox |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 2023 election result | 138 seats (33.3%) | 121 seats (31.7%) | 33 seats (12.4%) |
| El Pais / Cadena SER (Nov 13) | 129 seats (31.5%) | 118 seats (29.4%) | 61 seats (18.8%) |
| El Mundo (Nov 13) | 129 seats (30.8%) | 105.5 seats (25.3%) | 66.5 seats (19.6%) |
| CIS, state pollster (Nov 12) | 102 seats (25.5%) | 138.5 seats (33.3%) | 61 seats (18.4%) |

Seat figures from the newspaper polls are averages of projected ranges, as compiled by the Catalan daily ARA. Earlier in November, Reuters said Sigma Dos for El Mundo gave the PP and Vox 199 seats combined, and SocioMetrica for El Espanol put them at about 200.

The outlier is the state run CIS, which puts the Socialists first by almost eight points. Even in that survey, though, the PP and Vox reach 163 seats, short of a majority, while the left would still need regional allies. Most private polls show the opposite picture.

### Three things the numbers say

- **Vox has roughly doubled.** It won 33 seats in 2023 and is now polling around 60 to 70. <a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/12/far-right-surges-as-crises-threaten-sanchez-could-vox-lead-spain" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Al Jazeera has reported</a> that anti-immigration sentiment is drawing young voters to Santiago Abascal's party.
- **The PP needs Vox.** No poll gives Alberto Nunez Feijoo's party a majority on its own.
- **The left is split.** Reuters reported that hard-left parties failed to agree on a joint list, and that Podemos risks losing its seats altogether.

## How 350 Seats Turn Into a Government

Spain's system is often misread by comparing national vote shares. Here is how it works:

1. **Congress has 350 seats.** A party or coalition needs 176 for an absolute majority.
2. **Seats are won province by province.** There are 52 districts: 50 provinces plus Ceuta and Melilla. Each province gets at least two seats, with the rest shared out by population.
3. **Closed lists and the D'Hondt formula.** Voters pick a party list, not a person, and seats are divided by the D'Hondt method, with a 3% threshold of valid votes.
4. **The Senate is elected separately.** Voters also choose 209 of the 267 senators directly on an open list, but the prime minister is confirmed by Congress.

This is why a combined PP and Vox total matters more than who finishes first. In 2023, the PP came first with 138 seats, yet the Socialists, with 121, ended up leading the government with the support of Sumar and smaller regional parties.

## Issues to Watch Before Polling Day

Reuters said housing, immigration and corruption are expected to dominate the campaign. The fallout from a deadly train crash in October, which led Transport Minister Oscar Puente to resign, is also in play. A PP government relying on Vox would mark a clear shift for Madrid, while a narrow or hung result could mean weeks of coalition talks or even another election.
