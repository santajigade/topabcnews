European Union leaders met in Dublin on Friday, November 13, for what was planned as the bloc's first summit focused on artificial intelligence. Instead, the war in Ukraine took over the agenda. The shift came after US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner flew from talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow to brief Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy outside the Irish capital, and said any peace deal would need Europeans at the table. EU leaders now have to decide who should represent them.

## The Late-Night Briefing at Casement Aerodrome

On the evening of Thursday, November 12, Witkoff and Kushner landed at Casement Aerodrome, a military airbase at Baldonnel west of Dublin, where the Irish government arranged a meeting with Zelenskyy. The Kyiv Independent reported that Ukraine's chief negotiator Rustem Umerov was also present. Details of the discussion were not made public.

The envoys had come from the Kremlin, following two days of US-brokered talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi that ended on November 11 without a major breakthrough. You can read our earlier report on <a href="/abu-dhabi-talks-day-two-prisoner-swap-no-breakthrough/">the second day of the Abu Dhabi talks</a> for background.

According to <a href="https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2026/11/13/ukraine-dominates-agenda-as-europes-leaders-come-to-dublin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Irish Times</a>, Kushner described the Putin meeting as "detailed and productive" and said the US side had argued for including Europeans. He said a deal could not be concluded without them. Zelenskyy said the next round would include Europe in the negotiating team. The Kremlin, however, has played down the idea of European participation, Euronews reported.

## What Putin Proposed, According to Russian Media

Russian state media reported that Putin suggested a temporary "humanitarian mission" in Kostiantynivka, a contested city in eastern Ukraine's Donbas region. Putin claimed Russian forces control the town and said Moscow was ready to pause fighting so civilians could leave. These claims have not been independently confirmed. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said the issues would be discussed further at a planned meeting between Putin and US President Donald Trump at the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

Ukraine's delegation told allies that the Abu Dhabi talks gave no sign that Russia had softened its main demands, The Irish Times reported.

## Who Should Speak for Europe: Leaders Disagree

The question of a single European voice split the room. Here is how key figures have positioned themselves, based on reports from Euronews and The Irish Times:

| Leader | Position |
|---|---|
| Giorgia Meloni, Italy | Wants one representative with a clear mandate; said the EU seemed "all over the place" |
| Antonio Costa, European Council President | Said European security cannot be discussed without Europe |
| Kaja Kallas, EU foreign policy chief | Has pitched herself alongside Costa, citing an earlier understanding |
| Luc Frieden, Luxembourg | Backs Costa as the natural representative |
| Donald Tusk, Poland | Played down Poland's exclusion from the E3 format |
| Micheal Martin, Irish Taoiseach | Called representing Europe a "team effort" that includes the UK, Canada and Norway |

The Kyiv Independent noted that French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz had not endorsed a Costa and Kallas arrangement. If no decision is reached in Dublin, the issue moves to a special European Council on November 26 and 27, which will also cover the EU budget and Ukraine's financing.

## Starshield Restrictions and Air Defence Worries

Zelenskyy also raised concerns about Ukraine's air defence. Restrictions on Starshield, the military version of Starlink, have been reintroduced less than three weeks after similar limits were lifted, hampering the interception of Shahed drones, according to <a href="https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/13/eu-leaders-gather-in-dublin-to-decide-who-will-speak-for-europe-in-peace-talks" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Euronews</a>. He said Russia launched 115 missiles in the first week of November and that Ukraine is short of anti-ballistic interceptors. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte also met Zelenskyy in Dublin.

### Key highlights

- Dublin hosted the European Political Community summit on November 12, followed by an informal EU leaders' meeting on November 13
- The AI agenda, with Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch and Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton invited, was overshadowed by Ukraine
- 43 countries at the European Political Community summit signed a declaration that European security matters must be decided by Europeans
- The European Commission plans to present a paper on Ukraine's 2027 funding needs and a 22nd sanctions package at the late November summit
- The UK announced new sanctions on Russia's energy and shipping sectors on November 12
- A "Coalition of the Willing" meeting on Ukraine is due in Berlin next week, Zelenskyy said

## Absences, Protests and the Road to Late November

Several leaders were missing. Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten and Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever stayed home because of domestic political turmoil, and Spain's Pedro Sanchez skipped the meeting ahead of a snap election on November 29. Outside, about 1,000 people protested in Dublin, and Irish police arrested two men for public order offences.

## Why the Talks Matter Beyond Europe

Any settlement, and the decision on whether the EU adopts a 22nd sanctions package by the end of the year, will shape energy trade and security far beyond the continent. Several European leaders also worry that Moscow wants wider security talks that could weaken NATO's eastern flank, The Irish Times reported. For now, the Dublin meeting has shown that Europe agrees it wants a seat at the table but has not yet agreed on who will sit in it. The next test comes at the special European Council on November 26 and 27.
