British novelist M John Harrison has won the 2026 Booker Prize for his novel *The End of Everything*. The result was announced on Monday, November 9, at a ceremony at Old Billingsgate in London. At 81, Harrison is now the oldest writer ever to win the prize, and he did it with his first ever Booker nomination after more than five decades of writing science fiction and fantasy. He receives £50,000, roughly $67,000.

## A Strange Story Set on the English Channel

*The End of Everything*, published by Serpent's Tail, is set in a Britain that has been shaken by an unexplained crisis. According to the <a href="https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/books/the-end-of-everything" target="_blank" rel="noopener">official Booker Prize book page</a>, the government barely works, strange new creatures have appeared in the sea, and Europe seems to have slipped out of reach.

The main character, Phillip Tennent, makes a living by collecting objects that wash up on the Channel coast. One day he pulls a creature out of the water that keeps changing shape, and he has to carry it inland. That journey begins to unsettle everything he thought he understood. Harrison has said the setting draws on the small art towns along England's east coast, and that the book's satire responds to current affairs.

## What the Booker Judges Said

The 2026 jury was chaired by historian and broadcaster Mary Beard. The other judges were poet Raymond Antrobus, Pulp frontman and broadcaster Jarvis Cocker, critic Rebecca Liu and novelist Patricia Lockwood.

The panel said the book could be read in many ways: as a thriller, a mystery, a satire, a dystopia, a road novel or a work of realism. In its citation the jury wrote that whatever a reader wants can be found in it, "told at a slant and with a twist". Earlier, when the book made the shortlist, the judges had called it "a book not just about everything but about now".

Critics have been just as warm. Reviews quoted by the Booker Prize Foundation include praise in the Financial Times, which found the novel "unexpectedly compassionate", and in The Observer, where writer Helen Macdonald said she doubted she would read a better book that year.

## A Rare Win for Science Fiction

Harrison is described by the Booker Prize Foundation as an acknowledged master of science fiction. As <a href="https://www.khaleejtimes.com/lifestyle/books/british-author-m-john-harrison-oldest-ever-winner-booker-prize-2026?amp=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Khaleej Times reported</a>, this may be the first science fiction novel to take the prize, although the 2024 winner, *Orbital*, set aboard a space station, is harder to classify.

His long career includes the *Viriconium* stories, which he began in 1971 and continued until 1984, set in a city on a far future Earth. Between 2002 and 2012 he completed the *Kefahuchi Tract* trilogy, a series of space novels. *The End of Everything* is his 13th novel.

## The 2026 Booker Shortlist

Six novels reached the final round, chosen from a longlist of 13 titles. Two of Harrison's rivals were former Booker winners.

| Book | Author | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The End of Everything | M John Harrison | Winner |
| The Disappearers | Marlon James | Won the Booker in 2015 |
| John of John | Douglas Stuart | Won the Booker in 2020 for Shuggie Bain |
| The Things We Never Say | Elizabeth Strout | Shortlisted in 2022 |
| Black Bag | Luke Kennard | Third novel by the poet |
| May We Feed the King | Rebecca Perry | Debut novel |

Each shortlisted author receives £2,500 in addition to the main prize going to the winner.

### Key highlights

- Winner: M John Harrison, *The End of Everything*.
- Announced: November 9, 2026, at Old Billingsgate, London.
- Age 81: the oldest Booker winner in the prize's history.
- Prize money: £50,000 for the winner, £2,500 for each shortlisted author.
- Jury chair: Mary Beard.
- First Booker nomination of Harrison's career.

## Why the Booker Still Matters to Readers

The Booker is one of the most watched literary awards in the English speaking world, with a following among readers in India as well as in Britain. For Harrison, the win brings fresh attention not only to *The End of Everything* but also to a back catalogue stretching from the 1970s to the 2010s, which many new readers may now pick up. For fans of genre stories on screen, such as the upcoming <a href="/stranger-things-other-worlds-prequel-netflix/">Stranger Things prequel</a>, this year's result is a reminder that strange, speculative stories can also win literature's biggest honours.

## A Late Honour for a Genre Pioneer

Harrison's victory caps a career that has quietly shaped British science fiction for more than 50 years. By choosing a book that mixes the weird with the political, the 2026 jury has given the prize to a writer who had never before been nominated for it. *The End of Everything* is likely to reach a much wider audience in the coming months, both in the UK and among readers abroad.
