MS Dhoni has played his last game of cricket. Chennai Super Kings (CSK) announced on Tuesday, November 17, that the 45 year old former India captain has retired as a player and will stay with the franchise as team mentor from the 2027 season. The news came only hours before the 6 pm IST deadline for IPL teams to submit their retention lists, and Dhoni's name duly appeared among CSK's released players.

For fans, it ends a 19 year run that began when CSK bought him at the very first IPL auction in 2008. For CSK, it closes one chapter and opens a practical question: how the team rebuilds at the mini auction in Goa on December 16.

## Why the Announcement Came Now

Dhoni quit international cricket back in 2020 but kept playing in the IPL, mostly batting lower down the order in recent years. According to [Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/cricket/news/13613181/ms-dhoni-former-india-captain-retires-from-playing-role-with-ipls-chennai-super-kings-aged-45), he missed the whole of the 2026 season through injury, which in effect signalled the end. The National reports that his last match was against Gujarat Titans in IPL 2025.

Timing also mattered. Ahead of IPL 2025, CSK had retained him as an uncapped player for Rs 4 crore under the new retention rules, a cheaper slot available because he had long since left international cricket. With the November 17 retention deadline approaching, CSK had to decide whether to keep paying for a player who had not taken the field in a full season. Releasing him and naming him mentor settles that question without ending the relationship.

CSK owner N Srinivasan summed up the club's view in a statement on the franchise website: "There is no CSK without MS Dhoni."

## Dhoni's IPL Career in Numbers

The table below brings together the figures reported by Sky Sports and The National. The overall numbers include the two seasons he spent with Rising Pune Supergiant while CSK were suspended.

| Record | Figure |
|---|---|
| IPL debut season | 2008 (bought by CSK at the first auction) |
| Seasons with CSK | 17 of 19 IPL seasons |
| Other IPL team | Rising Pune Supergiant (2 seasons) |
| Total IPL matches | 278 |
| Total IPL runs | 5,439 at an average of 38.30 |
| Matches for CSK | 272 (most by any CSK player) |
| Runs for CSK | 5,314 (second highest for the franchise) |
| IPL titles as captain | 5 (joint most by any captain) |
| Champions League T20 titles | 2 |
| Last IPL match | 2025, against Gujarat Titans |

Outside the IPL, he led India to world titles in both the 20 over and 50 over formats, which is why he was known as "Captain Cool" long before Chennai fans started calling him "Thala", the Tamil word for leader.

## What the Mentor Role Means

CSK has not published details of the job. The franchise has said only that Dhoni will guide players from the 2027 season. Co-owner Rupa Gurunath told [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/sport/cricket/2026/11/17/ipl-ms-dhoni-retires-but-will-always-be-a-part-of-chennai-super-kings-identity/) that he will help shape the next generation during a phase of transition, adding: "A player like MS Dhoni comes along once in a lifetime."

CSK managing director KS Viswanathan, quoted by Sky Sports, said the club was glad Dhoni would keep guiding its players. A mentor at an IPL franchise usually works alongside the captain and coaching staff on strategy, player development and match situations, but the exact split of duties at CSK will depend on how the club sets it up. What is clear is that the franchise wants his voice in the dressing room even without his name on the team sheet.

## How His Release Changes CSK's Auction Plans

Releasing Dhoni frees up his salary slot ahead of the December 16 auction in Goa. According to the retention lists published on November 17, CSK kept 19 players and released 11, including Dhoni, Matt Henry, Matthew Short, Spencer Johnson and Zak Foulkes. That leaves CSK with Rs 28.05 crore to spend.

Here is where CSK stands against the other nine teams, based on the reported remaining purse:

| Team | Players retained | Purse left |
|---|---|---|
| Lucknow Super Giants | 18 | Rs 47.45 crore |
| Kolkata Knight Riders | 17 | Rs 41.65 crore |
| Delhi Capitals | 16 | Rs 39.50 crore |
| Sunrisers Hyderabad | 18 | Rs 35.15 crore |
| Mumbai Indians | 16 | Rs 32.60 crore |
| Chennai Super Kings | 19 | Rs 28.05 crore |
| Rajasthan Royals | 19 | Rs 22.05 crore |
| Punjab Kings | 19 | Rs 18.60 crore |
| Royal Challengers Bengaluru | 20 | Rs 14.15 crore |
| Gujarat Titans | 22 | Rs 3.75 crore |

CSK sits in the middle of the pack. Five teams will walk into Goa with deeper pockets, so CSK is unlikely to win a bidding war for every big name. Its retained core includes Ruturaj Gaikwad, Sanju Samson, Shivam Dube, Noor Ahmad, Dewald Brevis and Liam Livingstone, which means the auction is more about filling gaps than rebuilding from scratch. One gap is obvious: CSK has to plan for life without the wicketkeeper and finisher who defined the side for nearly two decades.

### Key dates for fans

- **November 17, 2026:** Retirement announced; retention lists submitted by 6 pm IST.
- **December 16, 2026:** IPL 2027 mini auction in Goa.
- **IPL 2027 season:** Dhoni's first season as CSK mentor.

## A Farewell Without a Final Match

The one thing missing is a goodbye on the field. Because Dhoni did not play in 2026, Chepauk never got a planned last innings. He will still be in yellow next season, just in the dugout rather than behind the stumps. For a team that faces its own reset at the Goa auction, having him there may be worth more than any single signing.
