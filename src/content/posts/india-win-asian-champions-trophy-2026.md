The Indian men's hockey team are champions of Asia once again. On Thursday night, November 5, 2026, India came from a goal down to beat Japan 4-1 in the final of the Men's Asian Champions Trophy at the Olympian Balbir Singh International Hockey Stadium in Mohali. The win gave India a record sixth Asian Champions Trophy title and their third in a row, after the 2023 and 2024 editions. India also finished the tournament without losing a single match, in front of home fans.

## How the final was won

Japan made a dream start. In just the third minute, Kazumasa Matsumoto found space on the left and beat the Indian goalkeeper with a reverse hit. Japan defended well after that and held the 1-0 lead into half time, which made the home crowd nervous.

The turning point came in the third quarter. In the 42nd minute, Aditya Lalage sent a flick pass into the circle and Shilanand Lakra finished from close range to make it 1-1. Japan protested that the pass was too high, but the TV umpire checked the replay and allowed the goal.

The final quarter belonged to India. Dilpreet Singh put India ahead around the 48th minute. Jugraj Singh then converted a penalty corner with a powerful dragflick to make it 3-1. Moments later, Vivek Sagar Prasad struck a half volley into the net to seal a 4-1 win. India scored three goals in the last quarter, while Japan had no answer.

## India's unbeaten road to the title

India played their matches in Jalandhar and Mohali and won every game they played. Here is how their campaign went:

- Beat Bangladesh 13-0
- Beat China 5-2
- Beat Japan 4-1 in the pool stage
- Beat Malaysia 4-2
- Beat South Korea 8-1 in the semi-final, with Lakra and Dilpreet Singh scoring two goals each
- Beat Japan 4-1 in the final

Japan reached the final after a goalless draw with Malaysia in their semi-final, which they won 5-3 in a penalty shootout. Malaysia later beat South Korea 5-2 on the final day to finish third.

## Heartbreak again for Japan

For Japan, this was another painful final. They were playing only their third final in the history of the competition and are still waiting for their first title. Their early goal and strong defending in the first half showed they had a plan, but they could not handle India's pressure after the break. Japan had also lost 4-1 to India in the pool stage, and the final ended with the same scoreline.

## What this win means for Indian hockey

Winning a continental title at home, without a single defeat, is a big confidence boost for the Indian team. The way the side recovered from an early goal in the final shows maturity and fitness, which will matter in bigger tournaments ahead. The goals were shared across the team, with Lakra and Dilpreet Singh scoring in both the semi-final and the final, and Jugraj Singh and Vivek Sagar Prasad also finding the net when it mattered most. Hosting the event in Jalandhar and Mohali also gave fans in Punjab, a traditional hockey heartland, a chance to watch the team win at home.
